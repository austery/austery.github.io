There is a little pattern in the tape that has become almost funny. Every OpenAI launch triggers the same reflex: the demo drops, the timeline melts, and Google trades down like it just lost another limb. If you think every OpenAI launch means Google is dying, you’re trading the surface story. OpenAI and Google aren’t even valued on the same axis anymore. One is drifting toward frontier infra plus agent layer economics. The other still earns its keep as the world’s biggest attention marketplace. The tape reacts as if OpenAI is taking Google’s lunch; in reality they are barely playing the same game. And yet Google is not some cornered incumbent. It runs its own stack end-to-end: silicon, models, cloud, distribution. When the frontier moves, Google isn’t just a victim of the headline. It’s also a beneficiary of the underlying economy the model boom feeds: more tokens, more workloads, more infra demand, including on its own cloud. The real question for investors has nothing to do with “coolness” or who grabbed the news cycle. It’s whether the market is still pricing Google purely as an ads machine, or whether you’re getting its entire AI optionality for free every time panic selling hits the tape. If you can tell the difference between a narrative drawdown and an actual earnings threat, you’re at least playing the stock on the right dimension.

Forget Fed's rate cut and Powell's script. In the Trump–Fed knife fight he is basically a caretaker on borrowed time. If you want to know whether risk still has a bid, you watch Chicago Fed’s NFCI and ANFCI, not the press conference. Both indices are sitting around −0.5. Zero is “normal,” positive is tight, negative is loose. NFCI, a composite index of risk, credit and leverage that maps the real liquidity regime. NFCI < 0 already tells you there is no broad credit crunch, no systemic “funding winter.” ANFCI is even more negative, which means that after you strip out the macro cycle, financial conditions are still looser than the economy alone would justify. Translate that into plain portfolio language: high policy rates do not equal tight financial conditions. As long as NFCI and ANFCI stay below zero, the system is still willing to fund stories with long duration cash flows and heavy capex. The AI capex wave, the four-to-five-hundred-billion-a-year data center buildout, high-multiple semis and software names all sit on top of that liquidity bedrock. So my playbook for tomorrow is simple. After the meeting, I am less interested in whether Powell sounds “hawkish” or “dovish” and more interested in where NFCI and ANFCI drift over the next few weeks. If they stay negative while the Fed is already cutting, then monetary easing is not being neutralized by markets, it is being amplified. That is how you extend an equity boom, not how you kill it.
