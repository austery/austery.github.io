TL;DR: A macro-architecture upgrade made trainable, proving the bottleneck is still bandwidth and memory.” DeepSeek just dropped a systems paper, not an “algo tweak.” Think of training as building a long highway. HC turns each layer into a multi-lane interchange: more routing freedom, more power, but it can destabilize deep training. mHC adds strict “traffic rules” so the mixing stays conservative and trainable at scale, while keeping the marginal overhead around ~6.7%. The real signal: the race in training isn’t over, and the bottleneck is still bandwidth + memory (the memory wall).

今年过节不收礼，收礼只收DeepSeek的“大红包” 人家过节发钱，DS梁文峰发论文。这不是小修小补修个函数算法，这次是又一次震撼性的系统级的训练方面大升级 我尽可能浅显易懂讲清楚拆解出核心的对于26年AI训练和半导体意味什么？搞研究搞投资必读啊 以前训练大模型，有点像修一条很长的高架。 每一层高架只有一个接口，一辆车一路往前开，稳定是稳定，但你能做的路况调度很有限。 论文呢想法很激进： 把每一层高架改成多层立交，直接开四个车道，允许车在不同车道之间换道分流。信息流动更自由，理论上模型更聪明。 但自由是要付代价的。 立交越复杂，越容易“失控”：有的层把车流放大成拥堵，有的层把车流抽干成断流，层层叠加，训练就容易崩。 这篇论文最关键的价值，是给这套“多车道立交”加了一套硬规则：每次分流只能按比例分配，不能凭空把车变多，也不能把车搞没。结果就是两件事同时成立：训练更稳，效果也更好。 更重要的是，它没有停在“理论很美”那一步。这么搞整体训练成本只多了大约 6.7%，但换来的，是可训练性和性能上的提升。 这篇论文背后的信号是什么： 首先模型训练的竞赛远没有停滞。推理更重要但训练还在继续抬高上限可能性。 算法的时代结束了吗？没有。算法敢不敢设计得更激进，工程能不能把它撑住。谁能把“想法”变成“跑得起来的训练吞吐”，谁就拿到下一轮领先。 再次验证了一个老结论：今天最大的瓶颈依旧不是算力本身，而是带宽和内存。 对应到现实，就是高带宽内存、互联、通信、内核优化这些硬东西。 所以，这篇论文并不会让 HBM 需求消失。相反，它在告诉你：架构越进化，带宽和内存越成为核心约束，软件和硬件会继续互相逼迫、互相推动，形成滚动式升级。 把它当成 2026 的第一份开年礼就行。 它预示的不是“又一个模型更聪明了”，而是更大的趋势： AI 的天花板还在上移，而且这次上移的方式是系统工程把算法托上去。 新年第一份论文红包，收下。 今年 AI 还会继续把高度往上拔。

People keep turning $NVDA Groq’s SRAM story into a meme: “More SRAM = no more HBM.” - Wrong, Why? Quick question: have you ever cooked and wondered why you’re “slow”? I t’s rarely the stove. It’s the constant trips to the fridge. SRAM is the prep counter next to the stove. Put the hot ingredients and tools within arm’s reach, and the workflow becomes smoother, more predictable. That’s the real Groq signal: not “less thinking”, but less waiting inside the system. But a bigger prep counter doesn’t eliminate the fridge. HBM is the fridge + pantry: the capacity and bandwidth you need to serve many customers, long contexts, and high concurrency. So no, SRAM doesn’t “kill” HBM. SRAM buys determinism. HBM buys scale. In the inference era, if anything, scaling real deployments can make HBM even more important. $MU I turned this into a simple visual. Link in first reply.

The regime changed quietly: efficiency stopped being a virtue. It became a liability. Taipei. An Ivy campus. Australia. Different contexts, same structure. High-order systems are now colliding with unmodelable, individual tail shocks. Not because the world “lost control.” Because we optimized control into fragility. For three decades, we engineered the mean. We centralized supply chains, standardized processes, compressed redundancy, and celebrated utilization. It worked, until the tail started pricing itself into daily life. Deglobalization is not a mood. It is risk budgeting. Political consolidation is not stability. It is correlation. Identity and belief hardening into tribes is not culture. It is narrative volatility. Semiconductors are the cleanest mirror. The world concentrated critical compute, advanced packaging, and geopolitical exposure into too few places. In the mean, it looks like dominance. In the tail, it is a single-point failure with a global blast radius. This is where Intel matters, even if you dislike the stock. Not as “the next TSMC,” but as a second pole. A partial US-based stack changes the distribution: more routes, more optionality, less world-wide correlation to one island and one strait. In a tail-driven regime, partial redundancy is not a nice-to-have. It is a valuation input. The market is slowly repricing from growth premium to resilience premium. Not because resilience is inspiring. Because it is scarce, measurable, and survivable. The question is no longer whether a shock happens. The question is whether you still have options when it does. Company resilience is redundancy, multi-path supply, and onshore capability. Portfolio resilience is liquidity, hedges, position sizing, and detoxing from single-node dependence. Mean returns are easy to model. Tails decide careers.
