## 2017年的信任危机：Fyre音乐节

请和我一起回到2017年。那时候你过得很开心，因为我们谁都不知道什么是新冠大流行。舒适的复古高腰牛仔裤重新流行起来，而你在2008年买的比特币也终于开始暴涨。（笑声）

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Come back with me a moment to 2017.
You're having a great time because
none of us know what a pandemic is.
Comfy mom jeans are back in style,
and the Bitcoin you bought back in 2008,
it's finally taking off.
(Laughter)
</details>

你正在刷Instagram，这感觉挺棒的，因为他们当时才刚刚开始使用算法信息流，所以你看到的依然主要是你真正认识的人发的梗图和生活照合集。（笑声）

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
You're scrolling on Instagram,
and that's pretty cool
because they only just started
with their algorithmic feed,
so you're still kind of seeing
memes and photo dumps
from people you actually know.
(Laughter)
</details>

那些橙色方块到底是怎么回事？从Kendall Jenner、Hailey Baldwin，到Emily Ratajkowski和Ja Rule，一众网红和名流都在发帖宣传这场即将在巴哈马举办的高端音乐节。（笑声）

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
What's up with all these orange squares?
Influencers and famous faces
from Kendall Jenner and Hailey Baldwin,
Emily Ratajkowski and Ja Rule
are posting about this high-end
music festival coming to the Bahamas.
(Laughter)
</details>

白沙滩、豪华住宿、Migos、Major Lazer，号称十年一遇的文化体验。网红和明星驱动的营销在48小时内就卖出了95%的门票。有些人甚至掏出高达10万美元买一个名额，还有大学生也去那里度春假。期末考前这样放松一下挺不错的，对吧？

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
White-sand beaches, luxury accommodations,
Migos, Major Lazer,
the cultural experience of a decade.
Influencer and celebrity-driven marketing
sells out 95 percent
of tickets in 48 hours.
Some folks are shelling out
up to $100,000 for a spot,
and college students
are also going for spring break.
Not a bad way to unwind for finals, right?
</details>

只不过它根本没有举办。Fyre音乐节作为“从未发生过的最盛大派对”载入了史册。满心期待五星级服务的购票者到了现场，看到的只有面包奶酪三明治和联邦紧急事务管理局（FEMA）的救灾帐篷。承诺的艺人一个都没演出，而主要组织者最终进了联邦监狱。（笑声）

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Except it never took place.
Fyre Festival has gone down in history
as the greatest party that never happened.
Ticket holders expecting five-star service
showed up to bread and cheese sandwiches
and FEMA tents.
None of the promised artists performed,
and the main organizer
ended up in federal prison.
(Laughter)
</details>

## 亲和力、可信度与信息核查的技能鸿沟

虽然这件事有很多令人震惊的地方，但真正触动我的是，它本质上是一个关于信任的故事。很多在场的人都感到非常失望。Netflix和Hulu倒是拿到了绝佳的素材。但成百——不，是成千上万聪明、富裕的人，在网上看到这些光鲜亮丽、精心策划的内容后，集体认定这是真的，而且物有所值。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
While so much of this was shocking,
what really resonated with me
was how this was really
a story about trust.
A lot of people here
were left disappointed.
Netflix and Hulu,
they got some great fodder.
But hundreds -- no
no, thousands of smart, well-off people
saw this glamorous, curated content online
and collectively decided
this is real, and it’s worth the cost.
</details>

人类总是会被自己喜欢的人吸引。当一个人让人觉得有亲和力时，我们会本能地更信任他。但亲和力和可信度是两件截然不同的事。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Humans gravitate to people that we like.
When someone feels relatable,
we instinctively trust them more.
But relatability and credibility
are two very different things.
</details>

在传统新闻编辑室工作多年后，我曾以为自己了解人们如何与新闻互动，从而形成我们的世界观。直到后来我也开始与新媒体创作者合作，我才意识到，我们在剖析信息时所使用的框架，差异究竟有多大。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
After years working in legacy newsrooms,
I thought I understood
how people interact with news
to inform our worldviews.
And it wasn't until
I later started working
also with new-media creators,
that I realized just to what extent
we are not all dissecting information
using the same framework.
</details>

在我的传播职业生涯中，我一直站在前排，亲眼见证不断演变的媒体格局如何塑造我们的民主。而记者用来核实信息的习惯，不应该成为行业机密。它们并不难，也并非遥不可及，但在新闻编辑室或新闻学院之外，却几乎没有人教。在如今这个由社交媒体和人工智能驱动的信息环境里，这种技能鸿沟或许是我们面临的最大公民问题。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Throughout my career in communications,
I’ve had this front-row seat
to the ways that
our evolving media landscape
is shaping our democracy,
and the habits journalists use
to verify information
shouldn't be trade secrets.
They're not difficult or unattainable,
but they're not taught outside
of newsrooms or journalism schools.
And in today's social media,
AI-powered information environment,
that skill gap might be
the biggest civic problem we have.
</details>

## 新闻编辑室的核查机制与媒介素养缺失

记者会花多年时间学习一门专业训练：为了追求真相而质询信息。这是一门植根于伦理与道德推理的技艺。新闻编辑室内部为了确保准确性所做的大量工作，并不是每个人都了解，仅仅因为我们从未身处过那个环境。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Journalists spend years
learning a professional discipline
of interrogating information
in pursuit of the truth.
It's a craft rooted in ethics
and moral reasoning.
There's so much that happens
inside of a newsroom
to ensure accuracy
that not everybody realizes,
simply because we've never been
in that environment.
</details>

事实不会凭空出现。记者、编辑、事实核查员和法务团队齐心协力，才能梳理出真相。这包括收集信息、核实事实、审查信源、检查偏见，以及在整体上保持客观，以确保内容值得信赖。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Facts don't just materialize.
Reporters, editors,
fact-checkers and legal teams
are all working together
to parse out truth.
That looks like gathering information,
verifying facts, vetting sources,
checking for bias
and overall maintaining objectivity
to ensure trustworthiness.
</details>

我们获取信息的渠道比历史上任何一代人都多。然而在许多情况下，我们的独立思考却比以往任何时候都少。社交媒体和人工智能算法把热门内容推到顶部。但这并不能保证准确性，也不能保证我们获得了形成自己知情观点所需的全部相关事实。这种脱节正在影响我们的媒介素养。而它正发生在民主最需要媒介素养的时候。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
We have more access to information
than any generation in history.
Yet in so many cases, we're thinking
less independently than ever before.
Social media and AI algorithms
push trending content to the top.
But that doesn't guarantee accuracy,
or that we're getting
all of the relevant facts
that we need to make
our own informed opinions.
That disconnect
is affecting our media literacy.
And it’s happening right when
democracy needs [it] most.
</details>

一个强大民主制度的支柱，一半是新闻自由，另一半是知情且积极参与的公众。但在目前的美国，并没有全国性的媒介素养教育标准。这几乎就像是我们的媒体生态系统演变的速度，远远超过了我们的公民教育体系。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
The backbone of a strong democracy
is in equal parts a free press
and an informed, engaged public.
But in the US right now,
there are no national
media literacy education standards.
It's almost like our media ecosystem
has evolved faster
than our civic education system.
</details>

今天出生的孩子，不会默认去想《纽约时报》、福克斯新闻或PBS。他们想到的是Instagram、TikTok和YouTube。86%的Z世代通过社交媒体获取新闻。因此，当我们从早上睁开眼睛的那一刻起就被信息轰炸时，我们所有人都有权获得记者用来解读世界的知识和技巧。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
A kid that's born today
won't default to thinking
in terms of “The New York Times,”
Fox News or PBS.
But they're thinking in terms
of Instagram and TikTok and YouTube.
86 percent of Gen Z are turning
to social media to get their news.
So when we're living
with information bombarding us
from the moment we open
our eyeballs in the morning,
we all deserve access
to the knowledge and techniques
journalists use to interpret the world.
</details>

媒介素养让我们能够识别人工智能、深度伪造和未经核实的新闻。它还能让我们识别文章或网红内容中可能影响叙事框架的偏见。它意味着理解娱乐元素在广播电视新闻中所扮演的角色，并剖析标题和视觉画面背后的意图。它意味着批判性地思考语气和措辞，并追问：“资金是如何影响这条信息的？”以及“这个报道里缺少了什么，为什么？”

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Media literacy is what
enables us to identify AI,
deepfakes and unverified news.
It's also recognizing bias
in articles or influencer content
that could affect story framing.
It's understanding the role
that entertainment plays
in broadcast news,
and it's unpacking the reasoning
behind headlines and visuals.
It's thinking critically
about tone and word choice
and asking, “How does funding
influence this information?”
as well as, "What's missing
from the story and why?"
</details>

## 传统媒体与新媒体的互学及全球实践

我并不是说新闻业是完美的。记者和所有人一样也会犯错，我也不是说人工智能、社交媒体和网红都应该被抛弃。事实上，我认为传统新闻编辑室和新媒体应该互相学习，最终建立一个更快、更准确的新闻基础设施，更好地满足当今受众的需求。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I'm not saying that journalism is perfect.
Journalists, like all humans,
make mistakes,
and I'm not saying that AI
and social media and influencers
should all be left in the dust.
I actually think
that traditional newsrooms and new media
should learn from one another
to ultimately build a faster,
more accurate news infrastructure
that better meets the needs
of today's audience.
</details>

好消息是，世界各地的人们都在努力寻找解决这些问题的办法。澳大利亚禁止16岁及以下儿童使用社交媒体，丹麦也正在效仿。德国从小学就开始开展媒介素养教育。一些课堂甚至会邀请记者走进教室，教学生如何审查信源、识别国家支持的虚假信息，以及在新闻引发情绪反应时，先停下来缓一缓再转发。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
The good news is,
people all over the world
are working on solutions to all of this.
Australia banned social media
for kids 16 and under,
and Denmark is following their lead.
Germany begins media literacy
education in primary school.
Some classrooms will even invite
journalists into the class
to help teach students how to vet sources,
recognize state-backed disinformation,
and also just to pause and take a beat
whenever news stokes an emotional reaction
before sharing it out.
</details>

芬兰是标准化媒介素养教育的另一个绝佳例子。自一个多世纪前芬兰从俄罗斯独立以来，识别宣传和质疑信息就一直是芬兰文化的一部分。因此如今，媒介素养成为国家课程的核心支柱，从K到12年级开始，并一直延续到大学和成人教育。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Finland is another incredible example
of standardized media literacy education.
Identifying propaganda
and questioning information
have been part of Finnish culture
ever since the country gained independence
from Russia more than a century ago.
So today, media literacy is a core pillar
of the national curriculum,
beginning K through 12 and carrying
through college and adult learning.
</details>

赫尔辛基的教育工作者曾登上CBS和半岛电视台，介绍媒介素养是如何融入所有学科的。在文学课上，学生会学习核实新闻、审查信源和批判媒体的基础知识。在数学课上，他们会更多关注算法和统计学。在美术课上，他们会观察图像并学习图像是如何被编辑的。而在历史课上，他们可能会研究二战期间发生的一次空袭，并思考站在战争不同立场的国家会如何以不同的方式教授这段历史。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Educators in Helsinki
have been on CBS and Al Jazeera,
describing how media literacy
is integrated across all subjects.
So in literature classes,
students will learn
the basics of verifying news,
vetting sources and critiquing media.
Then in math, they'll focus more
on algorithms and statistics.
In art, they'll take a look at images
and learn how they can be edited.
And then in history,
it might be taking a look
at an air raid that happened
during World War II,
and considering how
it could be taught differently
depending [on] which side of the war
a country fought on.
</details>

一些芬兰学生甚至玩过一款名为“Troll Factory”的电脑游戏，他们在这个平台上花一周时间，故意扮演网络喷子，尽可能多地传播虚假信息，目标是让这个账号获得尽可能高的曝光度。一名学生总结道：“当你传播很多令人不快的东西时，你会收获很多粉丝。”

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Some Finnish students have even played
this computer game “Troll Factory,”
where they spend a week on this platform,
intentionally acting as an internet troll
to spread as much
disinformation as they can,
with the goal of gaining
as much visibility
for the account as possible.
One student summed this up as:
"You gain a lot of followers
when you spread a lot
of unpleasant things."
</details>

芬兰教育官员表示，这套课程有助于保护民主——如果我们无法分辨真假，就会受到欺骗，从而变得脆弱。这源于那句广为流传的名言：“你有权拥有自己的观点，但你无权拥有自己的事实。”

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Finnish education officials say
that the curriculum
helps protect democracy,
that if we can't tell
what's true from what isn't,
then we can be deceived
and we're left vulnerable.
It roots back to this popular saying,
“You have the right to your own opinion,
but you do not have the right
to your own facts.”
</details>

数据显示，这种标准化的方法确实有效。自开放社会基金会（Open Society Foundations）的年度媒介素养指数于2017年启动以来，芬兰每年都位居榜首——而2017年正是我们美国人现在所熟知的“橙色方块时代”。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Data shows this standardized
approach works.
Finland has ranked at the top
of Open Society Foundations'
annual Media Literacy Index
every year since it started in 2017,
which we all can now recognize
as the Orange Square Era here in the US.
</details>

## 我们的行动与结语

我不禁又想起了Fyre音乐节，心想如果当年那些社交媒体团队的人，能问出芬兰小学生每天都在问的那些问题，事情会有什么不同。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I can't help thinking a little bit more
about Fyre Festival
and wondering how things
could have been different
if the folks on those social media teams
were asking the same questions
that Finnish elementary
schoolers ask every day.
</details>

而这正是我们能发挥作用的地方。我们有能力要求我们选出的官员制定全国性的媒介素养教育标准。我们可以在日常生活中练习用新闻人的思维方式思考，并在看到虚假信息时大声指出来——无论是反驳朋友说过的某句话，还是在一条不准确的社交媒体帖子下贴上经过核实的新闻报道链接。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
And that's where we come in.
We have the power
to ask our elected officials
to enact national media
literacy education standards.
We can practice thinking
journalistically in our day-to-day
and call out misinformation
when we see it --
whether that’s pushing back
on something that our friend said
or posting the link
to a verified news article
on a social media post that is inaccurate.
</details>

我们可以订阅本地报纸；当我们确实从社交媒体获取信息时，可以确保这些信息来自受过新闻专业流程训练的创作者。我们也可以把这套工具分享给我们的孩子以及生活中的其他人。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
We can subscribe to our local newspapers,
and when we do get our information
from social media,
we can make sure it's coming from creators
who are trained in a journalistic process.
We can share this toolkit
with our children
and the others in our lives.
</details>

所以，今天离开这里时，让我们记住要多问问题——哪怕面对的是我们通常赞同的人。让我们保持好奇心，核查我们的信源，并在困难的时候依然为真相发声。因为独立思考的能力，或许正是我们仅存的最重要的公民技能。谢谢大家。（掌声）

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
So when we leave here today,
let’s remember to ask questions --
even with people we tend to agree with.
Let's stay curious, check our sources
and speak up in the name of truth,
even when it's hard.
Because that ability
to think for ourselves
might just be the most important
civic skill we have left.
Thank you.
(Applause)
</details>
