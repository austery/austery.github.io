### 西班牙高铁追尾事故致至少68人死亡
在西班牙，《纽约时报》采访了当地一起造成至少68人死亡的列车相撞事故的幸存者，该事故成为该国历史上最严重的列车事故之一。
<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
In Spain, the Times has been speaking with survivors of the train crash that killed at least 68 people there, making it one of the deadliest train accidents in the country's history.
</details>

事故发生在昨天上午，一列从马德里开往塞维利亚的高速客运列车追尾了停在同一轨道上的另一列列车。一名坐在行驶列车后方几节车厢的乘客表示，撞击将他震醒，转头看到一块金属飞穿过车厢，导致坐在他右侧的乘客遇难。另一名坐在更后排的乘客描述称，他爬出列车后迅速赶往前端施救。他说人们将部分残骸用作简易担架，轨道旁的杏仁林变成了临时野战医院。这是西班牙今年发生的第二起重大列车相撞事故。今年1月，同一家铁路运营商在同一条线路上发生的碰撞曾导致46人丧生。
<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
The crash occurred yesterday morning when one high-speed passenger train traveling from Madrid to Seville rearended another that was stopped on the same track. A passenger who was sitting a few cars back in the train that was moving said he was jolted awake by the impact and turned to see that a piece of metal had shot through the carriage, killing people seated to his right. Another passenger who was sitting farther back described climbing out of the train and rushing to help those who were near the front. He said people were using parts of the wreckage as makeshift stretchers and that the almond groves alongside the tracks became a kind of field hospital. The accident is the second major train crash this year in Spain. In January, 46 people died in a collision involving the same rail operators on the same line.
</details>

在新闻发布会上，西班牙交通大臣表示，负责控制该区域列车信号的老化安全系统在昨天上午发生故障，迫使多列列车停车。随后，在部长称之为不可思议的决策中，交通调度人员允许一列列车继续前行，并且在该车逐渐逼近前方停靠列车时，仍未告知司机刹车。接连发生的致命事故震动了该国，加深了人们对西班牙高速铁路系统安全的担忧——该系统规模在欧洲最大，世界第二。
<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
In a press conference, Spain's transportation minister said the aging safety system that controls train signals in that area went down yesterday morning, forcing multiple trains to stop. Then in what the minister called an inexplicable decision, the traffic control operators allowed one train to continue its journey and did not tell the driver to break even as it closed in on another train still stopped ahead. The back-to back deadly accidents have rattled the country and deepened concerns about the safety of Spain's high-speed rail system, which is the largest in Europe and second largest in the world. [music]
</details>
