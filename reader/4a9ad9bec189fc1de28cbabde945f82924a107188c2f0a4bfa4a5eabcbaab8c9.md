### 人类生育方式的历史转变

三十万年来，人类历史上的每一位父母都递给孩子一副自己从未见过的基因牌。曾经根本没有办法知道里面有什么，无法提前做准备，也无法选择健康。基因彩票就这样转动，抽到什么就是什么。

every parent in human history
handed their child a genetic hand
they'd never seen.
There was no way to know what was in it.
No way to prepare
and no way to choose health.
The genetic lottery ran,
and you got what you got.
</details>

那个时代已经结束了。我们繁衍后代的方式正在永远改变。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
That era is over.
And the way that we have babies
is changing forever.
</details>

但在深入探讨之前，我想先从你以及你是如何来到这个世界讲起。你始于两个细胞：一颗卵子和一颗精子，各携带 23 条染色体。当卵子与精子相遇时，字面意义上的火花迸发了。一阵锌释放的火花开启了你的生命，而正是这个特定的瞬间，锁定了你一生的基因命运。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>

你的母亲产生了两百万颗卵子，你的父亲产生了超过五千亿颗精子。构成你的这组特定的卵子与精子组合，概率仅为万亿分之一。你能够存在于世的几率，大致相当于从全地球所有海滩的所有沙粒中，挑选出某一颗特定的沙子。你能够存在本身，就是一个极低概率的奇迹。换一个夜晚，换另一天，出生的便不会是你。

A different night, a different day,

### 传统筛查的局限与全基因组测序突破

我们是世界上第一家能够对试管婴儿（IVF）培育的胚胎进行全基因组测序的公司。一个试管周期通常会产生 5 到 15 个胚胎。而在今天，这项极为关键的决策，要么就像选美比赛一样——完全看这些胚胎在显微镜下的外观——要么仅仅依赖实际可用的一小片基因信息来决定。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
We were the first company in the world
to be able to sequence the entire genome
of an embryo created during IVF.
A round of IVF creates
anywhere from five to 15 embryos.
And today, that incredibly
critical decision
is made either by a beauty contest --
what these embryos
look like under a microscope --
or they're made with a tiny slice
of genetic information
that's actually available.
</details>

这就是如今试管婴儿门诊中检测的实际情形：他们放大观察某一条特定的染色体：两条染色体都在不在？如果缺失了，它会通知你；如果两条都在，它会告诉你一切正常。如果多了一条染色体，例如多了一条 21 号染色体，那就是 21-三体综合征，也就是唐氏综合征。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So this is what testing
looks like today in the IVF office.
They zoom in on a specific chromosome:
Are both chromosomes present or not?
If they're missing, it'll let you know.
And if both are present,
it'll tell you you're all good.
And if there's an extra
chromosome, 21, for example,
that's called trisomy 21 or Down syndrome.
</details>

但你应当这样理解：你的基因组里有 30 亿个碱基，它的结构就像一本书。这 30 亿个碱基分布在 23 个不同的章节里。目前所有的染色体检测所能做到的，仅仅是告诉你每一个章节在不在。它根本没有去读任何一个章节里面的任何一个字母。所以，如果第 7 章或者说 7 号染色体里面有一个打字错误、一个拼写错误，它是根本找不出来的。它只会告诉你：“嘿，第 7 章一切正常，我只在章节层级做了检查。”

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
But the way you need to think about this
is that you have three billion
bases in your genome,
and it's organized like a book.
So those three billion bases
are in 23 different chapters.
And all this chromosome
testing is able to do
is tell you if every chapter
is present or not.
It's not actually reading
any of the letters
in any of those chapters.
So if in chapter seven
or chromosome seven, there's a typo,
there's a spelling error,
it's not going to be able to find it.
It's just going to tell you, hey,
you know, "Chapter seven is all good.
I only checked at the chapter level."
</details>

这就是全基因组测序变得极其有价值的地方，因为它能切实去阅读每一个字母——整整 30 亿个。那么这到底是什么样子的呢？如果你放大到某一条特定的染色体，你会看到一堆字母。而在那 30 亿个字母中，可能隐藏着一个错误。不幸的是，仅仅几个字母的差异，就决定了一个孩子是永远无法开口说话，还是能够正常言语。所需的仅仅是：改变了几个字母。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So that's where whole genome sequencing
becomes incredibly valuable
because it’s actually reading
every single letter --
all three billion of them.
So what does that actually look like?
So if you zoom in
to a specific chromosome,
you're going to see a bunch of letters.
And hidden in those three
billion letters is a typo.
And unfortunately, just a few letters
can be the difference
between a child that will never be able
to talk and one that will.
That’s all that it takes:
is a few letters changed.
</details>

这项技术之所以极难做到，而我们能成为全球首创，是因为胚胎所含的 DNA 数量极其微少。胚胎学家只送给我们 5 个细胞，而在这 5 个细胞中，只有 30 皮克的 DNA。一皮克是万亿分之一克。我们处理的材料量极小，因此我们不得不发明一种全新的技术，在不造成损坏的情况下，将这些 DNA 扩增复制数十万倍。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
The reason that it's really
hard to do this,
and we're the first to do it in the world,
is because embryos have extremely
small quantities of DNA.
The embryologist only sends us five cells,
and in those five cells
there [are] 30 picograms of DNA.
A picogram is a trillionth of a gram.
So we're working with extremely
tiny amounts of material.
And we had to invent
an entirely new technology
to amplify or copy that DNA
hundreds of thousands of times
without corrupting it.
</details>

现在我们知道它是行之有效的，因为我们拥有胚胎的数据，也拥有九个月后出生的婴儿数据，我们将两者进行比对，确认它们完全吻合。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now we know that it works
because we have embryos,
and we have the subsequent babies
that were born nine months later,
and we've matched them up
and we know that they match.
</details>

### 风险量化与从单基因到多基因的筛查

那么，这项很酷的技术到底能让父母做些什么呢？令人兴奋的是，在最基础的层面上，我们能够让父母了解并筛查我们前面谈到的那 3,000 种疾病：先天性缺陷、心脏缺陷、骨骼缺陷、神经发育障碍、基因突变导致的自闭症，所有这些都是现有检测完全遗漏的基因疾病。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So, cool technology.
What does it actually let parents do?
So what's really exciting
is that at the most basic level,
what we're able to do
is allow parents to understand
and screen for those 3,000 diseases
that we talked about.
Birth defects, heart defects,
skeletal defects,
neurodevelopmental disorders,
genetic forms of autism,
all genetic diseases that are completely
missed by existing testing.
</details>

但我们还能走得更远。想想精神分裂症、双相情感障碍或心脏病。这些疾病是由数百万个变异共同作用的累积影响所致。有些人具有较高的遗传易感性，而另一些人则没有。如今令人瞩目且切实可行的是，我们拥有数十万个人的数据：我们拥有他们的测序数据，也拥有他们的医疗记录，因此我们能够建立在胚胎层面上量化疾病风险的模型。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
But we can go even further than that.
Think about schizophrenia
or bipolar disorder or heart disease.
These are diseases that are driven
by the cumulative impact
of many millions of variants
working together.
Some people are at high
genetic predisposition
and other people aren't.
So what's amazing and what's available now
is that now we have data
from hundreds of thousands of individuals.
We have their sequenced data,
and we have their medical records
so we can build models that quantify
disease risk at the embryo level.
</details>

因此我们能够告诉父母：这个胚胎有较高的遗传易感性，而那个胚胎有较低的遗传易感性。尽管这些模型内部包含了数百万个变异，但对于一个正在进行试管周期且仅有 5 个胚胎的人来说，仅仅通过这 5 个胚胎，就可以预期实现 30% 到 50% 的风险降低。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So we can tell parents, this embryo
is at a high genetic predisposition,
and this embryo is at a low
genetic predisposition.
And despite the fact that these models
have millions of variants inside them,
someone who's going through an IVF cycle
who has only five embryos
can expect to see anywhere
from a 30 to 50 percent risk reduction
with just those five embryos.
</details>

而在今天，这些模型的表现还是它们有史以来最差的时候，因为我们目前拥有的数据是未来最少的状态。随着每年获得的数据越来越多，这些模型会变得越来越强大，这将转化为父母能够用更少的胚胎降低更多的风险。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
And today, the models are the worst
they're ever going to perform
because we have the least data
that we're ever going to have.
Every year, we're getting
more and more data,
and these models are getting
more and more powerful,
which is going to translate
to parents being able
to reduce more risk with fewer embryos.
</details>

<details>

These babies are actually already here.
They’re being photographed
by “The New York Times,”
and they're making international news
because their parents have done something
that has never been possible before.
We are the first generation,
the first life form,
to be able to read its own source code
and actually choose the next generation.

not just have to hope for it.

### 临床案例与单碱基对的影响

我想让大家认识一个人。这是米娅（Mia）。米娅出生时患有无脑回畸形，一种致命的脑部疾病。大多数患有这种病症的儿童活不过 10 岁。这是一种基因突变，没有治疗方法，也没有任何先兆。米娅的母亲做过了大家听过的所有基因检测：携带者筛查、无创产前基因检测（NIPT）。由于这些检测评估的基因组范围还不到 1%，它们根本无法识别出我们讨论的绝大多数基因疾病。

<details>

<details>

no one else in their family
that has these typos.
They're the first ones to have them.

</details>

### 基因变异的另一面与伦理探讨

所以基因彩票分发的并不只是风险，它有时也会分发某些“超能力”。这是一位芬兰奥运滑雪运动员。他控制红细胞生成的基因中存在一个单碱基对的变化。这意味着他的肌肉能比竞争对手获得多得多的氧气。他拥有极强的耐力，在统治赛场的时期几乎不可战胜。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So the genetic lottery
doesn't only deal risk.
It also deals some superpowers.
This is a Finnish Olympic skier.
He has a single base-pair change
in a gene that controls
red blood cell production.
So that means that his muscles get
way more oxygen than his competitors'.
He has way more endurance
and was practically unbeatable
during his reign.
</details>

在美国中西部有一个家庭，其骨密度是有记录的人类的 8 倍，他们的骨骼几乎是不可折断的。你和我只有三种颜色感受器，但已经发现有女性拥有四种颜色感受器。因此，当你和我只能分辨 100 万种颜色时，她们可以看见 1 亿种颜色。她们生活在一个我们字面意义上根本无法想象的世界里。超强的耐力、不可折断的骨骼、1 亿种色彩——这些都不是科幻小说，它们已经真实存在，只不过目前是由基因彩票随机分发的。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
There's a family in the Midwest
