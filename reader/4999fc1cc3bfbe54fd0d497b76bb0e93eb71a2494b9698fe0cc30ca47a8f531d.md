### 死亡互联网成真：机器流量反超与内容农场的工业化泛滥

在技术前沿领域，具身智能的发展速度令人瞩目——在北京举行的百米短跑比赛中，人形机器人已能跑赢人类历史上的任何短跑选手。然而在现实网络世界中，关于人工智能的公共讨论却几乎被一个词所垄断：**垃圾内容**（Slop: 泛指由AI批量生产的低质量、无实质价值的数字废料）。2026年6月，互联网迎来了一个历史性拐点：人类自身构建的网络中，人类流量首次沦为少数派。根据服务数百万网站的 **Cloudflare** 报告，机器人流量已占全部网页请求的 **57.5%**，这一交叉点比其 CEO 预期的2027年底提前了一年多。曾经被视作阴谋论的**死网理论**（Dead Internet Theory: 认为大部分网络活动并非真实人类产生，而是由算法与机器人主导的假说）如今已成为既成事实，网络安全机构 **Thales** 甚至认为这一反超早在2023年就已发生。

更显著的变化在于，新型机器人不仅在抓取数据，能够自主点击链接、填写表单并执行操作的 **AI智能体**（AI Agent）流量同比暴增了 **7,851%**。在内容平台端，这种自动化流水线正在侵蚀原有的生态系统。以 **YouTube** 为例，官方在2026年1月一次性永久封禁了16个频道，这些频道累计拥有3500万订阅者和47亿次播放量。部分运营者单日即可批量上线115个新频道，通过抓取爆款视频、利用AI改写文案并生成画面，单个频道每天上传6到7条视频，宛如一条全压运转的自动化数字排污管。一项来自 **Kapwing** 的研究指出，278个纯AI内容频道累计获得了630亿次播放量，年收入估计高达1.17亿美元。在 YouTube Shorts 上，新账号每刷两条推荐视频就有一条是AI生成或被官方内部定义为“脑腐”（brain rot）的内容；在职业社交平台 **LinkedIn** 上，超过41%的长帖被标记为全AI生成，一个月内有上百万人点击了“疑似AI垃圾”的反馈按钮。

### 重新定义Slop：工具放大效应与激励机制的错位

面对铺天盖地的抵触情绪，**韦氏词典**（Merriam-Webster）将 **Slop** 评选为年度词汇，并将其定义为“通过人工智能大量生产的低质量数字内容”。这一词汇在历史上经历了语义演变：18世纪指代软泥，19世纪指代喂猪的厨余残渣，如今则演变为推送到屏幕上的不受欢迎的合成算法混合物。然而，现行定义将“低质量”与“由人工智能生产”粗暴绑定，造成了一种逻辑误区，即工具本身决定了产物的价值。

事实上，生成式AI并没有发明垃圾内容。早在大语言模型问世之前，内容农场、点击诱饵（Clickbait）、虚假评论和模版化垃圾网页就已经充斥网络。AI所改变的根本不是低质内容的产生逻辑，而是其**经济学成本**——它将制造垃圾的边际成本降到了接近于零。如果将问题单方面归咎于AI技术，推导出的解决路径往往局限于一刀切式的全面封禁、机械式标签化或研发检测系统；但如果认识到问题症结在于“由广告收入与用户参与度算法所激励的低成本垃圾生产”，解决方案就必须转向重构平台的分成与流量激励结构。

从底层本质来看，生成式AI更像是一个**功率放大器**（Amplifier）。放大器本身没有特定的声音属性，输入美妙的旋律，输出的就是交响音乐会；输入杂音噪点，输出的则是一场巨大的噪音头痛。赋予资深工程师无限的AI算力，他们凭借深厚的工程经验与架构判断力能够写出更优秀的系统代码；若将其交由缺乏辨别力的新手，产出的则是膨胀而脆弱的技术债务。技术历史上从未因为一项工具具有滥用潜力就将其全盘废弃——正如人类不会因为有人制作滑稽的电动发声玩具就禁止通电一样，应对之道在于建立防护边界而非抛弃生产力工具。正如 YouTube 信任与安全负责人 **Matt Halprin** 所言，同一项技术既能极大释放个体创造力、产出优质内容，也能被利用来无脑批量生产千篇一律的内容农场制品。

But here's what I find interesting here. Everyone calls this stuff slop. A million people click that button. Merriam-Webster named it their word of the year. Countries are banning AI content from their charts. And yet almost nobody has stopped to just ask a fairly important question, what does slop actually mean? Merriam-Webster's official definition as of 2025 is digital content of low quality that is produced, usually in quantity, by means of artificial intelligence. Word of the year, very official stuff. The term itself started as slang for soft mud in the 1700s, became food waste in the 1800s, and now describes whatever AI puts on your screen that you didn't ask for. Essentially, we've come a full circle. Now, from feeding leftover scraps to pigs in 1840 to feeding synthetic algorithm slurry to humans on TikTok in 2026.

But let's glance at what that means really. We're nothing if not nuanced on this channel. Low quality and by means of artificial intelligence, those two things have been welded together as though they're the same condition, as though the AI part is what makes it low quality, as though the tool used to produce something determines its value. Generative AI did not invent junk content. Content farms existed long before ChatGPT. Clickbait existed long before ChatGPT. Fake reviews, scraped pages, template videos made for advertising sites, all of this existed before a single large language model wrote its very first sentence. What AI changed was the economics. It made producing garbage virtually free.

This is a distinction that matters a huge deal because when you say the problem is AI, the solution looks like banning AI or labeling everything AI touches or building detection systems. But when you say the problem is cheap mass production of garbage incentivized by ad revenue and engagement algorithms, the solution looks completely different. It looks like fixing the incentive structures that made the garbage profitable in the first place.

Think of generative AI as an amplifier. An amplifier doesn't have a sound of its own. It takes whatever signal you feed it and makes it a lot louder. Feed it a beautiful melody and you get a concert. Feed it a static and you get a very loud headache. If you give an experienced senior developer access to an AI coding assistant with unlimited tokens, they produce better code because they have the judgment to direct the tool, to catch its mistakes, to know what good architecture looks like before the first line is written. But give the same tool to somebody with less experience and they produce more code faster, but sloppier and harder to maintain. The tool didn't change, the human input did. And this applies to everything: writing, research, advertising, music. The AI amplifies what you bring to it. If what you bring to it is a bot farm designed to launch 150 YouTube channels per day, you get slop. If what you bring is a decade of expertise and a genuine argument, you get something entirely different.

There are two sides to every coin, except I suppose in quantum mechanics where the coin can be both sides simultaneously until you observe it, at which point it becomes whichever side annoys you more. But the point stands anyway. Nuclear energy, for example, can power a city or contaminate one. Code can hack or it can provide functionality. The capacity for misuse has never in the entire history of human technology been a sufficient argument for abandoning a tool. It's an argument for building safeguards around it. We didn't ban electricity just because somebody used it to make a singing animatronic bass mounted on a living room wall. We just learned to live with the regret.

Now, I have to be clear about something. I am not defending the bot farms in any way. I am a YouTube creator myself. I understand the game that they're playing. I understand the economics. And my advice to anyone running that operation would be exactly the same. Stop. Not because it isn't profitable. Clearly, $170 million a year suggests that it is. But because it's building something corrosive. It is eroding the very ecosystem that makes the profit possible. A bit like a termite eating its own house.

YouTube's own head of trust and safety, Matt Halprin, tried to draw the line. He said, "AI can actually allow people to make a lot of videos. Sometimes those videos are great and it really enhances creativity and you can create a higher volume of high-quality content that we want to encourage and have in YPP. But that exact same new tool can allow you to make lots of videos really quickly that are very similar. They're very generic and don't really have a narrative arc and don't really show your creativity. So, the same technology really enables great stuff, but it also enables stuff that's kind of content farming." And that distinction, in my opinion, is exactly right. But the difficulty of enforcing it tells us something important about why slop as a blanket term is so appealing. It's much easier to condemn a category than to evaluate individual cases.

But the fact that some people misuse AI to produce industrial quantities of garbage should not, cannot, mean that everyone who touches AI is producing garbage. An expert in any field using AI to refine an argument, synthesize research, or make a representative thumbnail is not the same activity as a bot farm uploading seven videos a day to 150 channels. The Merriam-Webster definition doesn't distinguish between these two things, and that is a problem because right now the word slop is being used as a weapon against both.

### 市场反冲与信任坍塌：当反AI成为新时代的营销卖点

泛滥的低质内容正在引发明确且巨大的经济惩罚。在知名3D资产交易平台 **CGTrader** 上，AI生成的3D模型已占总上传量的六分之一，但在销售收入中仅占 **1/90**，绝大多数专业买家坚决拒绝为此类模型付费。在数字音乐领域，一首利用AI生成的麦当娜《Like a Prayer》变体曲目在澳大利亚单曲榜前20名停留了16周、最高冲至第2位，引发了行业的强烈反弹。**澳大利亚唱片业协会**（ARIA）随即出台全面禁令，宣布完全由AI生成的音轨将被永久剔除出榜单和奖项评选，只有人类主导创作的作品才有资格入围。

商业广告领域的反冲同样剧烈。**可口可乐**（Coca-Cola）连续两年推出由生成式AI制作的节日广告大片，因画面诡异且缺乏人文温度而遭到全网猛烈抨击，被《怪诞小镇》主创 **Alex Hirsch** 讽刺为“用失业艺术家的鲜血调制红漆”；**麦当劳**（McDonald's）也在公众舆论压力下撤下了AI生成的广告片。公众在没有专业评论指导的情况下，自发使用“空洞、诡异、数字垃圾”等词汇对品牌进行抵制。与此同时，反AI立场迅速演变为新的商业护城河：**多芬**（Dove）承诺绝不用AI替代真人模特，**Aerie** 凭借“拒绝AI修图与生成”的品牌定位实现了 **23%** 的销售额增长，“纯手工制作”（Made by Humans）正在像“有机”或“公平贸易”标签一样成为高溢价卖点。

这种恐慌最终导致了无差别的**信任蒸发**。**Raptive** 在2025年的一项调查显示，只要受众“感知”到内容包含AI成分，无论其真实情况如何，对该内容的信任度都会暴跌约 **50%**；59%的选民认为其接触的日常信息深受AI算法操控。这种普遍的猎巫心态导致真实的人类创作同样遭到误伤：真实的摄影作品被指责为合成图，精炼的人类文案被怀疑为机器自动生成。这很大程度上源于AI科技公司早期激进的融资宣传——为了推高估值，他们向资本市场大肆鼓吹“AI将全面替代基础岗位、实现极致降本增效”，却在公众产生生存焦虑并反弹抵制时感到困惑。这种将劳动力贬值作为“产品特性”的宣传策略，最终反噬了技术本身的公众接受度。

### 作为表达转译器的AI：超越二元对立与网络生态的未来

跳出非黑即白的对抗思维，行业监管与评估体系需要更精细化的分层。例如在音乐领域，与其采取一刀切式的封禁，不如借鉴体育竞技中的分组机制：设立纯人类创作榜单、AI辅助榜单以及混合类别榜单，将知情权与选择权归还给受众。更重要的是，在“反垃圾内容”的声浪中，人们往往忽略了AI作为**表达均衡器**与**转译器**的核心价值。在金融、科研与技术工程领域，存在大量具备顶尖专业洞察却不擅文字修辞的专家——能够从债券收益率曲线中精准捕捉市场拐点的分析师可能无法写出通顺的研究备忘录，洞察前沿机理的研究员可能受困于论文表述，具备极高情绪感知力的个体在直面沟通时可能语塞受阻。在此类场景中，AI并未取代核心智力资产，而是充当了从“认知信号”到“自然语言”的翻译桥梁，帮助专业知识跨越表达壁垒。

将人与AI的关系简化为“支持AI vs 反对AI”或“人类 vs 机器”的二元对立在分析层面是毫无建设性的。技术发展的核心命题始终在于人的主观意图、专业判断力以及人类在全流程中投入的实质贡献。构建健康的内容生态需要各方协同承担责任：AI企业必须停止将大规模失业作为卖点进行推销；平台方必须重构算法规则，切断低质垃圾内容的盈利链条；网络安全与内容鉴真机制需加速迭代。

更深层的危机在于，支撑开放互联网运行了三十年的底层商业假设正在瓦解——原有的商业模式建立在“网络流量均由人类产生、人类注意力可售卖给广告主”的基础之上。当近六成流量沦为机器交互，网络正在退化为“两座由自动化服务器相互推送定向广告、少数真实人类在夹缝中寻找真实内容”的荒诞场域。未来的数字空间必然向更小型化、高度策展化、注重内容溯源（Content Provenance）与认证标记的垂直社区演进。盲目使用“Slop”这个标签进行无差别攻击，既无法遏制工业级垃圾的蔓延，更会抹杀技术赋能个体表达的真正潜能。
