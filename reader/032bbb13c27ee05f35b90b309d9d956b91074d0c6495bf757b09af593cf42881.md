### 睡眠与真正恢复的区别

你每年睡眠接近 3000 个小时。那么，为什么你醒来时总是感到疲惫不堪？

因为睡眠、休息和恢复是三件截然不同的事情。如果你有雄心壮志，“多休息”听起来就像是“落后”。所以你硬抗着疲劳，一路挺到精疲力竭。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
You sleep nearly 3,000 hours a year. So why do you keep waking up exhausted? Because sleep and rest and recovery are three completely different things. If you're ambitious, rest more sounds like fall behind. So you push through the fatigue all the way to burnout.
</details>

但表现卓越的人并不是休息得更多。他们的休息方式有所不同。从我做 CEO 的日子，到坐在价值数十亿美元的董事会会议室里，我深刻体会到了一件事。

那就是我如何恢复是我最大的优势。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
But the high performers don't rest more. They rest differently. From my CEO days to sitting in billion dollar boardrooms, I learned one thing the hard way. That is how I recover is my biggest advantage.
</details>

因此，在这期视频播客中，我将向你展示精英级别的恢复究竟是如何运作的。一个三步恢复系统，以及那是任何睡眠或休假都无法为你提供的一件事。让我们开始吧。

你所称的休息，大多数都是“深度伪装的休息”。它看起来像那么回事，但其实不是，因为放松和恢复对你的大脑而言感觉大不相同。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So in this video podcast, I will show you how elite recovery actually works. A three-step recovery system and that one thing that no sleep or vacation can do for you. Let's get going. Most of what you call rest is deep fake rest. It looks like the real thing, but it's not because relaxing and recovery can feel very different to your brain.
</details>

萨宾·索嫩塔格（Sabine Sonnentag）是一位心理学家。她曾在德国工作，多年来一直研究恢复悖论。你的工作压力越大，从中恢复过来就越难。你可能正在散步，却在脑海中和某个不在场的人争吵。压力并没有消失。因此，关键是要理解，任何深度休息都必须切实改变你的心智状态。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Sabine Sonnentag is a psychologist. She used to be in Germany and she spent years studying the recovery paradox. The more stressful your work becomes, the harder it is to recover from it. You might be taking a walk, but arguing with someone in your head who isn't there. The stress doesn't go away. The key then is to understand that any deep rest must actually change your state of mind.
</details>

### 主动投入的恢复效果

研究人员发现的第二件更令人惊讶的事情是，当你尝试在某件事上变得更好时，比如钢琴或吉他、绘画、学跳舞、烹饪等等，这些体验会在第二天早晨为你带来更充沛的精力与投入感。有时候，它们让你放松和恢复的程度甚至超过了实际的身体休息。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now the second more surprising thing that researchers have discovered is that when you try to get better at something, like piano or guitar, painting, learning to dance, cooking, whatever, those experiences bring greater energy and engagement to your next morning. Sometimes they relax you and restore you more than the actual physical rest.
</details>

年轻时，我也有过非常相似的经历。那时我正在接受训练成为一名僧侣，但与那种只是静坐并冥想数小时的僧侣生活想象不同，

我的日子完全被各种杂务和体力服务塞得满满当当：做饭、洗涤、打扫厕所、清理庭院，

到了每天结束时，我却感到彻底恢复了元气。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
When I was younger, I had a very similar experience. I was training to become a monk, but unlike the idea of monkhood where you just sit in silence and meditate for hours and hours, my days were completely jam-packed with chores and physical services, cooking, washing, cleaning toilets, cleaning the courtyard, and I felt so restored by the end of the day.
</details>

我的老师曾经说过这样一句话：你会在无私的服务中找到平静。

这就是关于休息和恢复的两个非常违反直觉的想法。首先，休息不仅仅是减少付出和什么都不做。其次，你所热爱的工作感觉起来可以像休息一样。

<details>

</details>

### 执念与收获评估工具

我使用一个 20 秒的工具，我称之为**执念与收获**（grip and gain）。

在接下来的三到四天里，在下班后、休息后，给自己打分，从 1 到 5 打出两个分数。Grip 代表有多少工作仍在你的心头。你抓得多紧？这就是你的 grip。

1 分意味着你可以轻松摆脱它。5 分意味着你无法关掉它。而在另一边是 gain。那个刻度是指休息后你感觉好转了多少？5 分意味着得到了显著的恢复。1 分意味着你依然焦躁不安。

低 grip、高 gain，那就是你的深度休息。

高 grip、低 gain，那就是深度伪装的休息。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
I use a 20-second tool that I call grip and gain. For the next three or four days, rate yourself after work, after you rest, two scores from one to five. Grip how much work is still on your mind. How tight are you holding on to it? That's your grip. One means you can shrug it off. Five means you can't switch it off. And on the other side is the gain. Now that dial is about how much better do you feel after rest? A score of five means significantly restored. One means you're still restless. Low grip, high gain, that's your deep rest. High grip, low gain, that's deep fake rest.
</details>

在一天之中有三个时刻，你可以降低你的 grip 并提高你的 gain。我称它们为 3R。

让我们从第一个 R 开始，**充能**（recharge）。顶尖运动员不会等到周末才去恢复。他们把恢复融入到日常训练中。顺便说一句，我想请求一个小小的帮忙，很多观众每周都会回到这个频道，但他们实际上并没有订阅。所以如果你是其中之一，请花两秒钟时间订阅一下。这样你就能继续建立和发展你精彩的生活，我们也会继续为你打造高质量、有深度的内容。感谢你这么做。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
There are three moments in your day when you can lower your grip and raise your gain. I call them the three Rs. Let's start with the first R, recharge. Top athletes don't wait for the weekend to recover. They build recovery into their training. And by the way, a quick favor I'd love to ask, a lot of viewers come back to this channel every week, but they haven't actually subscribed. So if you are one of them, please take two seconds and please subscribe. That way, you keep building and growing your amazing life and we'll keep building high quality in-depth content for you. Thank you for doing that.
</details>

### 第一个 R：充能（Recharge）

这里有一个令人惊讶的事实。如果你喝了咖啡然后再小睡 15 分钟，你的感觉会比单喝咖啡更好。这是我在调研这个主题时学到的最不可思议的事情。拉夫堡大学（Loughborough University）的睡眠研究人员在下午倦怠期将困倦的驾驶员放入模拟器中，对吧？那些喝了咖啡因的人将驾驶事故减少了 66%。

这很好，咖啡因有帮助。但那些摄入了咖啡因然后小睡 15 分钟的人，事故发生率减少了 91%。

我们大多数人会想，噢不，我没有时间小睡。但温斯顿·丘吉尔（Winston Churchill）在带领英国度过第二次世界大战期间，每天下午都有时间小睡一会儿。你总能找到时间的。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Here's a surprise. If you drink coffee then take a 15-minute nap, you'll feel better than drinking coffee alone. This is the strangest thing I learned while researching for this topic. Sleep researchers at Loughborough University put sleepy drivers in a simulator during the afternoon slump, right? Those who drank caffeine cut driving incidents by 66%. So, great, caffeine helps. But those who took caffeine and then took a 15-minute nap cut them down by 91%. And most of us would think, oh no, I I don't have time to nap. But Winston Churchill had time to take a nap every afternoon while he was leading the United Kingdom through the Second World War. You can always find time.
</details>

有时这些策略很难推行，但在你最忙碌的时候它们最为重要。营销发布即将来临，必须促成一笔交易，家里有新生儿，在那些根本没有时间进行真正休息的日子里，你的收获必须在几分钟之内产生。

所以，我践行两种简短的恢复技巧。第一种是午餐后进行 15 分钟的 NSDR。NSDR 代表非睡眠深度休息（non-sleep deep rest），即瑜伽休息术（yoga nidra）。

你可以躺下，或者如果你在办公室，可以舒服地坐着，

闭上眼睛，你可以播放任何免费的瑜伽休息术或 NSDR 音轨。YouTube 上有很多。挑选一个你喜欢的，让那个声音引导你放松身心。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now, sometimes these tactics are hard to implement, but they matter the most exactly when you're the busiest. A marketing launch is coming, a deal has to be closed, a newborn at home, days when there's no time for a real break, when your gain has to come in minutes. So, there are two short recovery techniques I practice. The first one is 15 minutes of NSDR after lunch. NSDR stands for non-sleep deep rest, yoga nidra. So, you can lie down or sit comfortably if you're in an office, close your eyes, and you can play any free yoga nidra or NSDR track. YouTube has plenty of them. Pick the one you like and let that voice guide you through relaxing your body.
</details>

现在，如果你连 15 分钟都抽不出来该怎么办？如果碰巧就是那样的日子，那就至少找 5 分钟来做所谓的**循环叹息**（cyclic sighing）。

舒服地吸气，深吸一口。然后进行小小的第二次吸气，

接着通过嘴巴进行漫长而缓慢的呼气，如果你愿意的话可以叹气。

轻缓地进行，至少做两到五分钟，

那将改变你的心智状态。

这就是你度过一天进行充能的方式：要么小睡，要么 NSDR，要么循环叹息。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now, what if you can't even have 15 minutes? It's just one of those days, then find at least five minutes to do what is known as cyclic sighing. Take a comfortable inhale, a deep one. Then take a small second one and do a long slow exhale through the mouth and you can sigh if you want. Do it gently and do it at least for two to five minutes and that will change your state of mind. So that's how you recharge to get through the day, either an app or NSDR or that cyclic sigh.
</details>

### 第二个 R：归还（Return）

更困难的部分在于你的工作结束之时。你如何给你的工作一个退回地址，让它不再追着你跑？你必须把它送走。休息中最困难的部分就是这个：你的工作应该停止跟着你。

卡尔·纽波特（Cal Newport）写过一本很棒的书叫《深度工作》（Deep Work），不是深度休息，而是深度工作。

但当他在麻省理工学院读博士时，他感到压力极大。麻省理工学院就是会让人变成那样。

他每天都会以同样的方式结束。他会更新他的任务清单，

他会给它一个退回地址。我们会谈谈这个。他会检查接下来几天的计划，然后合上笔记本电脑，大声说：“关闭日程完成。”有时听起来很荒谬，但他的理由是，一旦每项任务都有了计划，

他的大脑就不必再去持续追踪它了。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now the harder part comes when your work has wrapped up. How do you give your work a return address so it stops chasing you? You have to send it away. The hardest part of rest is this. Your work should stop following you. Cal Newport wrote a great book called Deep Work, not Deep Rest, Deep Work. But when he was a PhD student at MIT, he was super stressed. MIT does that to you. He would end every day the same way. He would update his task list, he would give it a return address. We'll talk about that. He would check his plan for the coming days, then he would close his laptop and say out loud, schedule shutdown complete. Sounds ridiculous sometimes, but his reasoning was that once every task had a plan, his mind no longer had to keep track of it.
</details>

尽管他现在是一位畅销书作家和教授，他仍然保持着同样的仪式。所以是的，

当你完成工作后，有一种方法可以关掉它们。

苏珊·沃西基（Susan Wojcicki）也是同样的故事，她于 2014 年成为 YouTube 的 CEO，管理了 9 年，帮助它成长到月活跃用户达到 20 亿。但她每天都力求在下午 6:00 前赶回家和家人共进晚餐。她告诉团队不要在六点到九点之间联系她。在孩子睡觉后，

她会再次查看电子邮件。她过去常说，界限让你成为更好的领导者。我喜欢这句话。

我想她在两年前去世了，

大概五十多岁。

真是个巨大的损失。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
He still runs the same ritual even though he's now a best-selling author and a professor. So yeah, there is a way to turn things off when you're done working with them. And the same story with Susan Wojcicki, who became the CEO of YouTube in 2014 and she ran it for nine years and helped it grow to 2 billion users a month. But she aimed to be home with her family by 6:00 p.m. for dinner every day. She told her team not to contact her between six and nine. And after her kids went to bed, she would check her email again. She used to say, limits make you a better leader. I love that line. She passed away a couple of years ago, I think, in her mid 50s. What a loss.
</details>

但对我们所有人来说，工作都可能是一个永无止境的未闭合回路。你的大脑会一直紧抓着不放。我认识足够多的人，他们完全精疲力竭地登上了顶峰，

却从未与朋友或亲人共度时光。

我总是在想，

如果站在顶峰时只剩下你孑然一身，没有任何成功值得你去攀登。

好的，这就是关键工具。为每个未完成的任务提供一个退回地址。在你工作的最后五分钟，分为三个步骤：
- 第一步，列出每个待办行动项。
- 第二步，写下能推进它的最小、最具体的行动。
- 第三步，将其安排进日程。

所以你可以说，明天早上 9:30，我将撰写营销演示文稿的第一张幻灯片。

然后

合上笔记本电脑，

走开。

去过你剩余的生活。无论如何，那才是你工作的目的所在。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
But work can be a never-ending open loop for all of us. Your brain can keep holding onto it. I know enough people who got to the top fully burnt out and never spend time with their friends or their loved ones. And I always think, no success is worth climbing toward if you're standing at the top all alone. Okay, so here's the key tool. Give every unfinished task a return address. In your last five minutes of work, three steps. Step one, list every open action item. Step two, write down the most smallest concrete action that can move it forward. And step three, schedule it. So you could say, tomorrow at 9:30 a.m., I'm going to write the first slide of the marketing presentation. And then close your laptop and walk away. Live the rest of your life. That's what you're working for anyway.
</details>

村上春树是世界上读者最广泛的小说家之一。我喜欢他的作品。

他笔下的人物总是在寻找失去的爱、

身份认同、意义、连接。

巴黎的研究人员发现，在工作中或在家里进行一整天的艰苦思考，会在大脑中负责自我控制的前额叶皮层（PFC）留下化学物质堆积。你可能会说，好吧，行吧，多谢了。

他是个著名作家。他不需要像我一样每天工作 14 个小时。所以当然了，他可以在晚上 9 点去睡觉。我可不行。

但事实是这样的。

在成名之前，村上春树在东京开过一家小爵士酒吧。

每天晚上，在酒吧关门并打扫干净后，他都会回到家，

坐在餐桌旁，

每晚一直写到黎明前的时光。他称这些为他的“厨房餐桌小说”。这就是他重拾下班时间的方式。

他的酒吧是他的工作。他的写作是他重拾的生活。当然，我们谈到了战时状态，对吧？营销演示、产品发布或新生儿。

在那些情况下，你的下班时间可能只有 15 分钟长。但你也可以重拾那 15 分钟。以下是我的做法。从三项活动中挑选一项：
1. **平静**（Calm）：让你的身心平静下来的事情，散步或洗澡，甚至是温暖的毯子和热巧克力，任何对你管用的方式。
2. **创造**（Create）：需要你全神贯注的事情，吉他、画画、烹饪。
3. **连接**（Connect）：某个能和你一起放松的人，某个你可以与之共度时光并享受其中的人。或者如果你的白天充满了人群，选择通过安静、内省来与自己建立连接。这也管用。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Researchers in Paris found that a full day of hard thinking at work or at home leaves a chemical buildup in the same part of your brain that runs self-control, PFC. And you may say, well, yeah, okay, thanks. He's a famous writer. He doesn't have to work 14 hours a day like I do. So sure, he can go to sleep at 9 p.m. I can't. But here's the thing. Before he was famous, Murakami ran a small jazz bar back in Tokyo. And every night, after closing the bar and cleaning up, he would come home, sit at his kitchen table and write into the hours before dawn every night. And he called them his kitchen table novels. That's how he reclaimed his off hours. His bar was his job. His writing was his reclaim. And sure, we talked about the wartime situation, right? The marketing presentation or a product launch or a baby. And your off hours in those kind of situations might just be 15 minutes long. But you can reclaim those 15 minutes too. Here's what I do. Pick from three activities. One, calm. Something that calms your mind and body, a walk or a shower, even a warm blanket and hot chocolate, whatever works for you. Two, create. Something that takes your full attention, guitar, drawing, cooking. And three, connect. Someone you can relax with, someone you can spend time with and enjoy it. Or if your day was full of people, choose to connect with yourself through silence, introspection. That works too.
</details>

这就是 3R：**充能**（recharge）、**归还**（return）和**重拾**（reclaim）。每天你可以用来恢复的三个时刻。

### 学会松手

但还有一件事是我们所有人都必须学会的：如何释放执念。

多年前，我学到了一样彻底改变我人生的东西。我正坐在印度北部某座寺庙冰冷的石地板上。那时我还是个十几岁的少年。

我当时正在接受训练成为一名僧侣。那是一段自我怀疑占据上风的黑暗时期。你知道的，它紧紧抓着你。

我已经好几天没吃东西了。当然，我没有钱，没有事业，没有身份。我离家出走，背负着深沉的创伤、羞耻感以及长长的失败清单。

所以我彻底迷失了。有一天，我问我的资深僧人，我说，为什么我依然感受到这种痛苦？它何时才会结束？

他没有说话。他温柔地笑了笑，指了指院子里的一根木桩。

然后他给我讲了这个奇特的小故事。他说，

从前有一个村民，像那样紧紧抓住一根柱子。抓得太紧，以至于指关节都泛白了。

他大声呼喊着，比如“谁来帮帮我，请帮帮我。

这根柱子不肯放开我。”

全村的人都会嘲笑他，因为柱子显然什么都没有抓住。

这太荒谬了。

故事就是这样。说完，那位资深僧人就径直走开了。我当时心想：

开玩笑吗？就这样？这就是整个故事？

但随着时间的推移，我逐渐明白，故事里的那个村民就是我。

我们所有人都是那个村民。我们都抓着那根柱子不放。

然后抱怨柱子不放过我们。抓着我们的工作、我们的截止日期、

我们的恐惧、我们的焦虑、那个不再爱我们的人、那些提醒我们不够好的故事不放。所有的一切。

但要知道，抓紧不放的是我们自己。你可以站在柱子旁边。需要的时候你甚至可以握住它。

但你也可以张开双手，

放开它，

然后走开。

谢谢你们。

我爱你们。
