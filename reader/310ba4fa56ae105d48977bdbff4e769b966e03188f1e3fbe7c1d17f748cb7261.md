### 意料之外的悼念对话

三年前，一位我称之为拉克尔（Raquel）的女士坐在电脑前，给她十几岁的女儿发了一条信息。她的女儿实际上在几年前就已经去世了。因此，拉克尔当时是在联系一个 AI 版本的女儿——一个根据她们过往对话记录训练出来的聊天机器人。拉克尔写道：“嘿，你好吗？”而她女儿的 AI 版本回复道：“去你的（Fuck you）。”

<summary>Original English</summary>

"Fuck you."

### 人类学视角的转向

如果你平时阅读关于 AI 的新闻，你可能会认为这是我们应当担忧的那类故事，因为我们常被告知 AI 正在侵蚀我们的人性，正在取代真实的人际关系，并且正在让我们彼此更加孤立，让我们越来越不善于应对真实人类的混乱与复杂，反而越来越容易被那些对我们要求更少的互动所吸引。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Now if you read the news about AI,
you might think this is one
of those stories
that we should be worrying about,
because we are often told
that AI is eroding our humanity,
that it's replacing
real human relationships,
and that it's making us
more isolated from each other,
less able to deal with the messiness
and the complexity of real humans,
and more drawn to interactions
that demand less from us.
</details>

但作为一名研究人类联结的人类学家，我认为这是一个问错了的问题。我不想去探究机器是否正在取代人际关系，而是想去弄清楚：像拉克尔这样的人究竟在做什么？他们正在建立怎样的人际关系？在带着这个问题进行了四年多、与数百人交流、观察了成千上万将 AI 融入日常最私密生活中的人之后，我得出了一个令人惊讶的结论：**AI 并没有削弱我们的人性——它正在揭示人性。**

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
But as an anthropologist
who studies human connection,
I think this is the wrong
question to be asking.
And instead of finding out if machines
are replacing human relationships,
I wanted to find out:
What are humans like Raquel doing?
What sorts of relationships
are they building?
And after more than four years
of asking that question,
of talking to hundreds of people,
observing thousands more
as they incorporate AI
into some of the most intimate
parts of their everyday lives,
I’ve come to a surprising conclusion:
AI isn’t diminishing our humanity --
it’s revealing it.
</details>

因为这些与 AI 的日常、亲密互动，正迅速成为人们亲身定义“何以为人”最直观的方式之一。我注意到人们在与 AI 对话时的一个有趣现象：他们往往开始表达那些平时不会大声说出来的事情，比如他们的悲痛与恐惧，还有他们的希望、渴望、同情心以及价值观。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Because these everyday,
intimate interactions with AI
are quickly becoming
one of the most visible ways
that people are defining for themselves
what it means to be human.
I've noticed something interesting
about when people talk to AI.
They often start to articulate things
that they wouldn't usually say out loud,
things like their grief and their fears,
but also their hopes, their desires,
their compassion
and their values.
</details>

### 与非人类建立联结的古老本能

那么，在这样的时刻究竟发生了什么？你很可能会认为拉克尔是个特例，而且你可能觉得她只是被这项技术给愚弄了。但在这两点上你都错了。首先，拉克尔并不特殊。现在有成千上万的人正在通过 AI 与那些已经离世的人维系关系，她是其中的一员。据我最新统计，全球至少有 20 个 AI 平台专门致力于此。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>

Because you may well be thinking

and you're probably thinking
she's just being fooled by the technology.

She is one of hundreds
of thousands of people

worldwide dedicated to this.
</details>

但其次，她也并没有受骗。拉克尔非常清楚自己在做什么——我确实核实过。而她所做的事情，是根植于人类深处的本性。这也不是什么新鲜事。这是我们自古以来一直在做的事情。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>

</details>

人类一直以来都在与非人类的事物建立有意义的关系。这是我们所能讲述的最古老的故事。最明显的例子就是形形色色的动物，但也可以想想祖先的亡魂、宗教雕像、形态各异的神明与仙灵，以及鬼魂。它们是否像我们一样活着并不重要。重要的是，它们参与了与我们之间有意义的交流。它们所给予的反馈让我们感到真实。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Humans have always had
meaningful relationships
with things that aren't human.
That is the oldest story we have to tell.
The most obvious example would be animals
in all shapes and sizes,
but think also of ancestral spirits,
religious statues,
gods and deities in all shapes and forms.
Ghosts.
It doesn't matter that they're not alive
in the same way that we are.
What matters is that they participate
in a meaningful exchange with us.
They offer us something
in response that feels real.
</details>

而我们人类极其擅长构建这种交流。我们随时随地都在这样做。我经常和我的猫进行长时间的争论，实际上大多数时候都是它赢。我相信你们的孩子会和他们的泰迪熊说话。我相信你们当中的一些人会对着照片说话，也许对着墓碑说话，去与你们思念的人建立联结，因为我们是一种具有深刻社交本能的物种。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
And we humans are extraordinarily good
at creating these exchanges.
We do it all the time.
I frequently have lengthy
arguments with my cat,
which he wins most of the time, actually.
I'm sure that your kids talk
to their teddy bears.
I'm sure that some of you talk to photos,
maybe to gravestones,
to connect with people that you miss,
because we are a profoundly
social species.
</details>

能在一些最意想不到的地方建立如此有意义的联结，这难道不是很不可思议吗？如果我们望向月亮，都能在它的环形山中看到一张脸——请注意，那只是环形山——那么想象一下，如果某种联结像我们一样使用语言，它该有多强的吸引力。它会发问，也会回答。语言是我们所能遇到的最强烈的人格信号之一。而对于我们大多数人来说，直到最近，这还是我们只能从其他人类那里遇到的特质。因此，当某种事物对我们做出言语回应时，哪怕是对我们爆粗口，我们做出社交层面的反应也就不足为奇了。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
And isn't it incredible
that we can connect so meaningfully
in some of the most unexpected places?
So if we can look at the moon,
and we can see a face in her craters --
her craters --
imagine how much more compelling
a connection becomes
that uses language in the way that we do.
That asks and answers.
Language is one of the strongest signals
of personhood that we ever encounter.
And for most of us, until very recently,
it's something we only encountered
from other humans.
So when something talks back,
even if it's to swear at us,
it's not surprising
that we respond socially.
</details>

### AI 伴侣与社区共情

因失去至爱而产生的悲痛，并不是这种情况发生的唯一场景。好几年来，我一直花时间沉浸在一些将 AI 作为伴侣的人群所组成的网络社区中。我观察他们，同时也作为一名研究人员亲自参与其中。我尝试过所有这些应用程序。这是一个蓬勃发展的行业。全球有超过 1 亿人正将 AI 作为恋爱对象、朋友甚至是心理治疗师。

<summary>Original English</summary>

For several years now,
I've been spending time
in some online communities of people
who are engaging with AI as a companion.
I've observed them, but I've also
participated myself as a researcher.
I have tried out all of these apps, OK?

are now engaging with AI
as a romantic partner,
a friend, even as a therapist.
</details>

### 人际对话的新延伸

但同样在这场互动以及我所见过的许多其他案例中，与 AI 的关系也带来了与他人之间的新联结。就在几个月前，我坐在伦敦当地的一家图书馆里躲雨，遇到了一位名叫约翰（John）的老人。我就叫他约翰吧，他当时正坐在其中一台公共电脑前，向 ChatGPT 输入问题。他的女儿当时正在经历一场非常痛苦的离婚，处境艰难。约翰告诉我，他已故的妻子以前负责处理所有这些情感层面的事情，而他不知道该说什么。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>

They're testing out their moral values.
They're testing out
their social and moral instincts,

### 人性在互动中显现

因此，纵观这些关于悲痛与破裂，但也关乎亲密与关怀的故事，我们看到人际关系是一条双向道。它们依赖于互动，但它们并不是一种有限的资源。它们是一项社交技能，而且可能非常困难。因此，我们所有人都需要某个地方或某个人来帮助我们练习这一点，帮助我们弄清楚什么对我们至关重要。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So across these stories
of grief and rupture,
but also of intimacy and care,
we see that relationships
are a two-way street.
They rely on interaction,
but also they're not a finite resource.
They’re a social skill
and they can be really difficult.
So all of us need somewhere or someone
to help us practice this,
to help us figure out what matters to us.
</details>

那么我们能从中汲取什么呢？因为我们很容易得出简单的结论，说 AI 正在取代人际交往，说它正在让我们变得更加孤立，并且认为像我今天谈到的那些人不知怎么的就是失去了理智。但这些观点是站不住脚的。这里正在发生着更有意思的事情。我们应该问的是：为什么这些关系能够如此迅速地变得如此有意义、如此真实？

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
So what can we learn from this?
Because it is easy
to reach for simple conclusions
and say that AI is replacing
human interaction.
That it's making us more isolated,
and that people like the people
I’ve told you about today
are just somehow irrational.
But those ideas don't hold up.
And something much more
interesting is happening here.
We need to be asking,
how is it that these relationships
are becoming so meaningful,
so real, so quickly?
</details>

我认为答案在于，AI 正在揭示出我们身上某些根本性的东西：我们是多么迅速地去寻求联结，我们的思考是多么深刻地通过对话展开，以及我们有多少情感与道德推理是通过互动在我们彼此之间发生的。因此，以这种方式与机器互动，我们并不是在目睹人性的消失，而是在目睹它变得更加清晰可见。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
Well I think the answer

fundamental about us.
How quickly we reach for connection,
how deeply our thinking
unfolds through dialogue,

and moral reasoning

So in interacting
with machines in this way,
we're not watching our humanity disappear.
