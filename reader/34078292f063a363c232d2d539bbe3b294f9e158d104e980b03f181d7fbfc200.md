### 开场与核心分歧：时间线与“仅靠软件的奇点”

今天和我一起参与节目的是 Ege Erdil 和 Daniel Kokotajlo。Ege 是 Google DeepMind 的研究员，Daniel 是 AI Futures Project 的作者之一，该机构发表了《AI 2027》和《AI 2040》。我不会把这完全称为一场辩论，因为我希望这更像是一场讨论。但你们之间存在一系列关键分歧，这导致你们中的一位——Daniel——认为 AI 很可能在未来几年内消灭我们所有人，而 Ege，你认为这不会发生。我觉得这次对话的一个核心分歧就是时间线。你们预计极端超级智能（extreme superintelligence）多久会出现？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Today, I’m joined by Ege Erdil and Daniel Kokotajlo. Ege is a researcher at Google DeepMind. Daniel is one of the authors at the AI Futures Project, which published AI 2027 and AI 2040. I wouldn’t call this exactly a debate, because I hope it’ll be more of a discussion. But there is a set of key disagreements you guys have, which leads one of you, Daniel, to believe that AI is likely to kill us all within the next few years, and Ege, you to believe that will not happen. I feel like a big crux in this conversation is just timelines. How soon do you expect extreme superintelligence?
</details>

我已经阐述过，Paul 在上一期节目中也阐述过，为什么极端超级智能可能很快就会出现。Ege，为什么你认为这不太可能？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I’ve articulated, and Paul has articulated in the last episode, why there could be extreme superintelligence soon. Ege, why do you think that’s unlikely?
</details>

我和 Daniel 之间，以及我和 Paul 之间的主要区别，就在于你们有多相信“仅靠软件的奇点”（software-only singularity）。这个想法是：你有一个固定的算力池和大致固定数量的数据（至少是非合成数据）。然后发生的事情是，你得到了更好的 AI。这些 AI 能够更高效地利用这笔资源池来推动 AI 研究的进展，于是你得到了一个反馈循环，这个循环在熄火之前至少会持续很多个数量级。然后这种改进如此巨大，以至于你在六个月或类似的时间里就获得了——我不知道——10 年的进展，诸如此类。我认为我们现有的证据不足以让我对此赋予很高的置信度。也许未来三年内发生这种情况的概率是 10% 到 20%。这可能是最大的分歧，而 Daniel 认为这有超过 50% 的可能性。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
The main difference between me and Daniel, and also between me and Paul, is just how much you believe in the software-only singularity. This is the idea that you have a fixed pool of compute and a roughly fixed amount of data that is at least not synthetic. Then what happens is that you get better AIs. Those AIs are able to use that pool of resources way more efficiently to make progress on AI research, and you get this feedback loop that at least goes for many orders of magnitude before it fizzles out. Then that improvement is so big that you get, I don’t know, 10 years of progress in six months or whatever, something like this. I think the evidence we have is not sufficient to give high credence to that. Maybe that’s 10-20% likely to happen over the next three years. That’s probably the biggest disagreement, where Daniel thinks that’s over 50% likely.
</details>

为什么你认为未来几年内出现极端超级智能的概率超过 50%，而 Ege 认为概率是 10% 到 20%？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Why do you think it’s more than 50% likely that there will be extreme superintelligence within the next few years, whereas Ege thinks that’s 10-20% likely?
</details>

首先，我并不认为我们可能在几年内就全都会死。我想说的是，除非我们大幅放慢 AI 公司的速度，否则我们很可能会失去控制，几年内就会有未对齐的 AI 主导局面。看起来我们正接近完全自动化 AI 研究过程。这就是核心论点。有多接近？看起来这将在 2027 年发生。也许不会，也许在 2028 年，也许在 2029 年，但我会说在 2027 年发生的可能性略大于不发生。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
First of all, I don’t think that we will probably all be dead in a couple years. The thing I would say is, unless we substantially slow down the AI companies, then probably we will lose control and there will be misaligned AIs running the show in a couple years. It seems like we’re getting close to fully automating the AI research process. That would be the main claim. How close? It seems like it’s going to happen in 2027. Maybe not, maybe 2028, maybe 2029, but I would say it’s slightly more likely than not in 2027.
</details>

一旦你完全自动化了 AI 研究过程，事情很可能会加速。正如 Ege 所描述的，它们很可能会大幅加速，尽管我确实认为这方面存在一些不确定性。然后我认为，当前 AI 系统的许多局限性——比如数据效率低下之类的——随着进展急剧加速，会相对迅速地消融。最终你会得到能够从各种经验中快速、灵活学习的 AI。你或许可以称它们为“压倒顶尖专家的 AI”（top-expert-dominating AIs）。就我而言，压倒顶尖专家的 AI 实际上就是超级智能。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Once you have fully automated the AI research process, things will probably accelerate. More likely than not, they will accelerate a lot, as Ege was describing, although I do think there’s some uncertainty there. Then I think that a lot of the limitations of current AI systems, such as the data inefficiency or whatever, will melt away relatively quickly as progress accelerates dramatically. You’ll end up with AIs that are able to flexibly learn quickly from all sorts of experience. You might call them top-expert-dominating AIs, perhaps. Top-expert-dominating AIs are effectively superintelligence as far as I’m concerned.
</details>

这里有一个天真的问题。我们回到五年前，那时的 AI 系统甚至连对话都无法维持。现在我们有了能够解决千禧年大奖难题（Millennium Prize Problems）的系统。它们是很不错的程序员，等等。如果我们按这个速度再前进五年，你认为仅靠这一点就有可能给我们带来与人类专家不相上下的 AI 吗？如果我们展望未来五年，我们甚至还需要那么多的递归自我改进（RSI）吗？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Here’s a naive question. We go back five years and we have AI systems that can’t even hold a conversation. Now we have systems that are solving Millennium Prize Problems. They’re pretty good coders, et cetera. If we just go another five years of progress at this pace, do you think it’s likely that that alone gives us AIs that are competitive with human experts? If we’re looking forward five years, do we even need that much RSI?
</details>

我们可能不需要，但我认为这还不清楚。如果我们没有任何 RSI，我预计一切都实现自动化、人类无事可做（人类被完全自动化）的中位时间线大概是距今 15 到 20 年。这是一个中位时间线。所以在未来五年内，你仍然得给它 20% 左右的可能，认为它可能会发生。我认为这是一种非常现实的可能性。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
We might not, but I think it’s unclear. If we don’t get any RSI, my median timeline to everything being automated and humans having nothing to do — they’ve been completely automated — is something like 15-20 years from now. That’s a median timeline. So in the next five years, you still have to give it 20% or something that it could happen. I think that’s a very live possibility.
</details>

虽然在仅靠软件的奇点和类似问题上存在分歧，但在某种意义上，这些分歧并没有那么重要。我只是说，Daniel 认为某件事在未来三年内发生的概率是 50% 到 60%，而我说是 20%。这个问题可能很有趣。但即使只有 20% 的可能性，如果情况非常糟糕，你仍然应该采取行动。所以，尽管在数字上这可能是一个较大的分歧，但我不认为这在定性上是一个重大的分歧。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
While there are disagreements about software-only singularity and similar things, in some sense those disagreements don’t matter that much. All I’m saying is, Daniel is saying something is 50-60% likely to happen over the next three years, and I’m saying it’s 20% likely. That question might be interesting. But even if it’s only 20% likely, if the situation is very bad, you should still do something about it. So even though it’s maybe a larger disagreement numerically, I don’t think it’s a significant disagreement qualitatively.
</details>

### 暂停研发、真实世界的代价与渐进式突破

那么，你在 Google 工作而不是在 Anthropic 门外抗议的原因——这是我们行为上的一个实际差异——与其说是对不同情景的概率判断不同，不如说是……嗯，到底是什么？我很好奇。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
So the reason why you’re working at Google instead of protesting outside of Anthropic, which is a practical difference between our behavior, is less to do with the probabilities on the different scenarios and more to do with… Well, what actually? I’m curious.
</details>

有几件事。在这个具体问题上——暂停（研发）是不是一个好主意——我担心任何涉及我们在现实世界中看到某种显著版本的代价之前就针对风险采取行动的计划。现在，几个月来人们在这些讨论中总是引用的例子是 Hugging Face 事件。但我个人认为 Hugging Face 事件并没有造成多大损失，从大局来看，那是一个相当小的事件。就造成的损失而言，它比一次飞机失事要小得多。我确实觉得这类似于：如果你有一项新技术，比如核反应堆或飞机之类的，你实际上还没有看到任何重大的事故发生，但你有一大堆关于事情为什么可能会出错的理论论证，然后你就据此进行干预……我总体上对这种做法持怀疑态度。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Several things. In this particular question of whether a pause is a good idea or not, I’m worried about any plan that involves us acting on risks before we have seen some significant version of the costs in the real world. Right now, the thing people always cite in these discussions, since some number of months ago, is the Hugging Face incident. But I personally think the Hugging Face incident didn’t cost that much and was a pretty small incident in the grand scheme of things. It was much less significant than a plane crash in terms of the cost imposed. I do see it as analogous to if you have a new technology, like nuclear reactors or aircraft or whatever, and you haven’t actually seen anything significant go wrong, but you have all these theoretical arguments for why things might go wrong, and you’re intervening on the basis of that… I’m just suspicious about that approach in general.
</details>

我说这话时质疑的是这样一种观点：获得对齐 AI 的方式是在对齐领域取得某种理论突破。机器学习里没有任何事情是那样运作的。机器学习不是一个会有重大理论突破的领域。过去 10 年里没有过重大的理论突破。所有的突破都是渐进式的。你把这个算力乘数叠加在那个算力乘数之上，然后获得这种效率提升。它就是非常渐进的。你同时优化很多维度。这就是你让它们变得更强大的方式。所以我不明白为什么你会认为我们让它们更对齐的方式会有什么不同。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
The thing I’m challenging when I say that is the view that the way you get aligned AIs is to have some theoretical breakthrough in alignment. Nothing in machine learning has worked like that. Machine learning is not a field that has large theoretical breakthroughs. There have been no large theoretical breakthroughs over the past 10 years. All of them have been incremental. You stack this compute multiplier on top of this compute multiplier, and you get this efficiency gain. It’s just very incremental. There are a lot of dimensions that you optimize at once. That’s the way you make them more capable. So I don’t see why you would assume anything different about the way we would make them more aligned.
</details>

这也许是你们之间的一个关键分歧。我们是否有可行的干预措施可以采取？具体来说，如果我们有更多时间，那些有可能提升这些 AI 系统所做之事的价值以及它们对待我们方式的干预措施，是否会变得更可行？AI Futures Project 刚刚提出了一项放缓方案（pacing scheme），你们说这会将智力爆炸推迟一年——你们原本预计它在 2027 年发生。所以你们认为这会把它放慢到 2028 年？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
This is maybe a key disagreement between you guys. Are there tractable interventions that we could make? Specifically, are those interventions more tractable if we get more time to make them, interventions that are likely to increase the value of what these AI systems do and how they treat us? The AI Futures Project has just put out a pacing scheme that you say will delay the intelligence explosion by a year, which you are expecting in 2027. So you think this would slow it down to 2028?
</details>

差不多吧。我们有不确定性，但大概如此。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Something like that. We have uncertainty, but yeah.
</details>

如果智力爆炸发生在 2028 年而不是 2027 年，会有什么改变？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
What changes if we get the intelligence explosion in 2028 rather than 2027?
</details>

有几件事。首先，我认为在 AI 对齐以及理解 AI 如何运作的科学等各种事情上，进展正在取得。虽然进展不够快，但确实在取得。我们每多争取一年时间，就会有额外的进展，从而削减坏结果发生的概率质量。我想说的第二件事就是普遍的认知，你可以称之为社会和政府的“AGI 觉醒”（AGI-pilling）。这实际上可能是最重要的事情。大多数人根本没有认真对待这些事情，也不真正理解正在发生什么或即将发生什么。在我看来，这总体上是件坏事。如果总体上有更多人理解即将发生什么，我们的处境可能会更好。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
A couple things. First of all, I think that progress is being made on AI alignment and the science of understanding how AI works and all sorts of things like this. It’s not going fast enough, but it is being made. Each additional year of time that we buy, there’s additional progress, which cuts away at that probability mass of the bad outcomes happening. The second thing I would say is just general awareness, what you might call AGI-pilling of society and of government. This might actually be the most important thing. Most people just don’t really take this stuff seriously, don’t really understand what’s happening or what’s coming. That seems broadly bad to me. We’d probably be in a better situation if more people in general understood what was coming.
</details>

我认为这个过程正在发生。它甚至可能正在呈指数级发生。每过去一年，就有更多的人了解更多，更多的人开始关注，更多的人开始参与进来。只是，如果这项技术在世界上没有做出实实在在的事情，就很难唤醒人们对它潜力的认识。例如，如果我们暂停在当前的能力水平，假设我们这么做了。是的，人们觉醒的这个过程会继续缓慢进行。但看看金融市场对这项技术可行性的怀疑吧。存在巨大的怀疑。这种怀疑可能也在公众中广泛存在。公众并不真正喜欢 AI。他们也不太喜欢数据中心之类的东西。但这并不是因为他们意识到了将会发生什么。他们只是对它有一种短视得多的厌恶。如果我们现在暂停，那么人们仍然不会理解利害关系是什么。关于这件事的公共讨论仍然会非常糟糕，因为它将建立在错误的前提之上。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I think that process is happening. It might even be happening exponentially. Every year that goes by, more people learn more, more people pay attention, more people start getting involved. It’s just very difficult without tangible things that the technology does in the world to wake people up to its potential. For example, if we pause at the current level of capabilities, let’s assume we do that. Yes, this process of people waking up continues slowly. But just look at the skepticism in the financial markets about the viability of this technology. There’s huge skepticism. That’s also probably just shared broadly throughout the public. The public doesn’t really like AI. They don’t really like data centers and stuff. But that’s not because they realize what’s going to happen. They just have a much more myopic distaste for it. If we pause now, then people are still not going to understand what’s at stake. The public conversation about this will still be very poor because it will be operating from bad premises.
</details>

把能力推进到一个让每个人都显而易见的地步是有好处的——即如果从那以后继续前进，世界将被彻底改变。因为通过普通的说服方式做到这一点极其困难。你也许能在政治上促成某种暂停，但它会由那些实际上并不认同你世界观的派系来支持。他们会出于其他原因支持它。他们不喜欢大型科技公司。他们不喜欢所有这些公司都在建造当地社区不支持的数据中心。那会是出于其他原因，而不是你认为最重要的原因。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
There’s an advantage to getting the capabilities to a point where it’s evident to everyone that if we keep going from there on, the world will be totally transformed. Because doing this through ordinary persuasion is extremely difficult. You might get some kind of pause politically, but it would be supported by factions who don’t actually agree with your worldview. They would support it for some other reason. They don’t like big tech companies. They don’t like the fact that all these companies are constructing all these data centers that the local communities they’re in don’t support. It would be for some other reason and not for the reason that you think is most important.
</details>

我技术上同意你的看法，但在精神上不同意。也许一种建模方式是这样的：每当一种新的 AI 能力出现时，人们只要得知该能力存在，就会立即做出一些快速的认知更新。但随后，随着该能力更多地部署到世界各地等等，还会有大量额外的认知更新发生。现在能力进展得足够快，以至于仅仅通过更广泛地部署当前能力所能带来的认知提升，我们还远远没有耗尽。如果我们暂停下来，继续部署当前的能力但不让它们变得更聪明，我们会在几年内继续看到认知的显著提升。然后在此之后，这种提升才会逐渐减弱。我确实认为现在事情进展得足够快，作为一级近似，觉醒是连续时间的函数，而不是能力的函数。尽管它确实在一定程度上也是能力的函数。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I technically agree with you, but in spirit disagree. Maybe one way of modeling it is something like: for each new AI capability that appears, there are some rapid updates that people make immediately just upon learning that that capability exists. But then there’s a whole bunch of additional updates that get made as that capability gets more deployed into the world and so forth. Right now capability progress is moving fast enough that we still haven’t nearly exhausted the gains in awareness that would come from just deploying the current capabilities more. If we had a pause where we continue to deploy the current capabilities but not make them smarter, we would continue to see significant gains in awareness over the course of several years. Then it would taper off after more than that. I do think that things are going fast enough now that to a first approximation, the wake-up is a function of serial time rather than a function of the capabilities. Although there’s some function of the capabilities.
</details>

我还想说的另一件事是，很多持反 AI 观点的人并不认同我的世界观，他们反 AI 的原因也和我不一样。但这种情况也在很大程度上发生着变化。我们已经看到多位参议员和众议员站出来说“AI 可能会杀死我们所有人”以及“AI 可能会失控”之类的话。这主要就发生在过去一年里。所以这也正朝着我认为是正确的方向发展，人们正在醒来，更加理解局势的严重性。我不认为它朝正确方向发展的速度够快。我不认为它会在 12 个月内达到需要的程度，但也许在 24 个月内，或者在 36 个月内。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Another thing I would say too is that a lot of the people who have anti-AI opinions don’t share my worldview and don’t have anti-AI opinions for the same reasons as me. But that’s also changing to a larger extent. We’ve seen multiple senators and congresspeople come out and say, "AI could kill us all," and, "The AIs could go rogue," and things like that. That’s mostly happened just in the last year. So that too is trending in what I would consider the right direction, where people are waking up and understanding the magnitude of the situation more. I don’t think it’s trending fast enough in the right direction. I don’t think that it’s going to be where it needs to be in 12 months, but in 24 months maybe, or in 36 months maybe.
</details>

我同意这个观点，即如果你现在暂停，你在未来几年内仍然会看到认知的提升。但考虑到我们现在所处的位置与我认为人们需要达到的位置之间的距离，鉴于技术的影响，我认为如果你暂停在当前的能力水平，那是不会发生的。这也许就够了。你可能会认为当前的支持水平和当前的政治联盟之类的已经足够促成暂停。然后这就会带来你在意的好处，即它放慢了事情的速度，即使人们这样做时并没有完全理解将要发生什么，而且是出于不同的动机。这是一个站得住脚的立场。我只是从更长远来看担心，如果我们作为一个文明就是坚持在这个话题上保持非理性，那么我们就会做出很多糟糕的选择，很多没什么道理的选择。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I agree with the point that if you pause now, you will still get an increase in awareness for a couple more years. But given the distance between where we are now and where I think people need to get, given the implications of technology, I think that’s just not going to happen if you pause at the current level of capabilities. It might be enough. You might have the view that the current level of support and current political coalitions or whatever are sufficient to get a pause to happen. Then that has the benefits that you care about, that it slows things down, even though it’s not done with a complete understanding of what’s going to happen and it’s done for different motivations. It’s a defensible position.
