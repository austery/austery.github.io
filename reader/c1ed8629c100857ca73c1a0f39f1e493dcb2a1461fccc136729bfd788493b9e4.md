### 阶层烙印：生理恐慌与阶层流动的神话

即使在个人获得巨大经济与社会成功之后，贫困带来的心理印记也依然牢不可破。大约一年半前，当我试图去买一杯咖啡时，收银台的刷卡机显示我的银行账户被掏空了。在那一瞬间，我的理性彻底瓦解，胸口发紧、下颌紧绷、皮质醇激增，完全忘记了包里还有其他信用卡，只能不断道歉后夺门而出并痛哭失声。这种看似过激的反应，与我如今作为**《纽约时报》畅销书作家**、**普利策奖入围者**或房主的身份无关，而完全源于我过去的出身——一个来自南布鲁克林、在资源匮乏中长大的工人阶层女孩。

尽管美国一直标榜自己是“机遇之地”，但现实中的**社会阶层向上流动**（Upward Social Mobility: 个人在社会经济阶梯上的晋升）异常艰难，像我这样从经济底层起步的人中，只有不到 10% 能够最终到达顶层。那天付不起咖啡钱只是一个临时的资金调配问题，但在我身体里泛起的那种恐慌，早在 2009 年大衰退艰难维系小企业时、在 1998 年半工半读大学而无其他账户或亲属可求援时，便已经深深铭刻。那种像千千万万美国人一样在**月光族**（Paycheck to Paycheck: 靠工资维持生计无储蓄）状态中挣扎的身体记忆，从未真正消失。

I'm here to tell you a story about getting a cup of coffee. Or I should say attempting to get a cup of coffee because when I went to pay for it, I couldn't. This is about a year and a half ago and there was nothing wrong with my card's chip, the store's reader was fine. The problem was with my bank account, it was empty.

Now, a rational person would have just taken out another card, but in that moment there was nothing rational about me. My chest tightened, my jaw clenched, my cortisol spiked. I lost all awareness of a purse with a wallet and other pieces of plastic inside of it and instead I just apologized profusely, walked out the door and burst into tears.

Dramatic? A tad. But my reaction had very little to do with who I am in this moment, a New York Times bestselling author, a Pulitzer Prize finalist, a homeowner, and it had everything to do with who I once was. A working-class girl from South Brooklyn who didn't grow up with much.

Despite our nation's reputation as a land of opportunity, the truth is upward social mobility in America is actually quite arduous and less than 10% of people that start out like me on the bottom rungs of the economic ladder ever manage to make it to the top. And somehow I did. So while it was a temporary problem paying for the coffee that day, you know, I moved things around, other accounts, that panic in my body was known from before, from 2009 when I was struggling to get my small business through the Great Recession, from 1998 when I was working my way through college and there was no other account to pull from or parent or relative to call, all the other times that I was living like so many of my fellow Americans from paycheck to paycheck.

### 匮乏与安全感：阶层塑造世界观与文化原型

我们身处自**镀金时代**（Gilded Age: 19世纪末美国财富极端集中、贫富差距急剧扩大的时期）以来最严重的财富不平等时代，但公众往往带着事不关己的冷漠态度讨论这种不平等，却忽视了它对民主构成的系统性威胁。即使在一个资本主义社会，人们也极度避讳公开谈论金钱。我的 **Oura 智能指环**在咖啡事件那天提示我承受了异常压力，原因给出了工作或睡眠不良，却根本没有金钱焦虑的选项。然而在现实中，经济焦虑直接与高血压、抑郁症甚至预期寿命缩短挂钩。更深层次的问题在于：财富的多寡如何塑造我们的认知、行为以及对政治和权力的看法？

科学研究表明，一个人的**原始出身阶层**（Social Class of Origin: 个体成长期的家庭经济与社会地位）在塑造身份认同方面的作用，几乎与种族、性别或性取向一样重大。年仅七岁的孩子仅凭一张照片就能极高准确度地识别出他人的社会阶层。物质充裕与匮乏塑造了截然不同的心智模式：
* **资源充裕的环境**：孩子在安全感中成长，基础需求得到满足后，追求的是脱颖而出与**个体能动性**（Agency: 个体自主选择并影响自身生活与环境的能力）。其课外活动、大学、职业乃至党派选择，都是展示自我个性的身份声明。其文化原型如同《黑客帝国》中的 **Neo**，英雄之旅始于在红药丸与蓝药丸之间做出的自主选择。
* **资源匮乏的环境**：近 40% 的美国儿童生活在这种动荡中，外部世界不可预测，房东的一张通知或一张意外医疗账单就能将生活摧毁。因此，安全感必须在群体中寻找，服从集体规范比张扬个性更重要，在逆境中坚守责任胜于自我表达。其文化原型如同《虎胆龙威》中的警察 **John McClane**，不为寻求自我救赎，只为捍卫誓言、法律与所爱之人而战。

<summary>Original English Source</summary>

It's no secret that we are living in a time of wealth inequality not seen since the Gilded Age, but too often it's talked about with the clinical distance of other people's problems. An inconvenience that we wish somebody would do something about. And far less discussed is the shared urgent threat that this poses to our democracy. And if we don't start talking about income inequality like the civic issue it needs to be, we will surely slide further towards autocracy than we already have. Maybe irrevocably so.

Part of the problem is even though we're a capitalistic society, we fascinatingly hate talking about money. Like a lot of you, I have an Aura Ring. And the day of the coffee incident, it told me I'd experienced an unusual amount of stress that day. And it gave me a few reasons, work, bad sleep. And maybe it's because Aura is from Finland, but money wasn't an option, which is unfortunate because in America, financial anxiety is linked to things like hypertension, depression, and even reduced lifespan.

But it all got me thinking, well, if money can impact our health, how does having money or not having money impact our behavior and our thoughts? How does our socioeconomic situation impact the way we see politics and the way we vote? And for the last year, I have been on a deep dive into the sociology and psychology of social class. And what I've discovered may surprise you.

We're accustomed to thinking about our social class as little more than a matter of what's in our bank account, if you own a home or if you rent. But scientists have found that your social class of origin plays nearly as large a role in shaping your identity as your race, gender, or sexuality. And that children become aware of their own social class standing just a little later than they become aware of their race and gender. In fact, kids as young as seven can, with outrageous accuracy, correctly identify somebody else's social class just by looking at a photo.

Your social class doesn't just impact who you are. It impacts the way you think. It's one of the least discussed topics in America and yet it's silently at play in so many aspects of our society. Growing up with resources or growing up in scarcity affects the way you see the world, your place in it, how you make decisions, and how you see big abstract things like the role of government or the very idea of democracy itself.

See, the well-resourced child is born into a place of security. And with their basic needs having been met, their parents can set out to getting them to thrive. And in America, that often means distinguishing oneself as an individual. Exhibiting agency, the choices one makes about extracurriculars, what college you go to, your career, and eventually your political party affiliation, these aren't just matters of selection, they're statements of identity. And essentially, the way you present yourself to the world. So, if you want to think about a typical middle-class hero, it would be Neo from The Matrix. He's just toiling away in that office job, and then his hero's journey begins when he makes a choice. Will it be the red pill or the blue pill?

Now, for the child born into scarcity, which is almost 40% of children in America today, things are far more precarious. Security is not guaranteed. The world feels beyond your control because at any moment, something could present itself, a landlord, an unexpected medical bill, that throws everything into a tailspin. And so, security is found in groups, and conformity is encouraged, and expressing your own sense of agency is less important than staying true to who you are in the face of constant adversity. Think about the cop, John McClane, in the blockbuster hit Die Hard. He doesn't show up to that Christmas party looking for trouble, but he's going to fight for who he loves, his wife, and what he took a vow to defend, the law.

### 阶层隔离与制度脱节：舒适阶级对政治权力的垄断

这种认知维度的撕裂，由于生活物理空间的完全分离而进一步固化。富裕群体与底层群体各自生活、工作、礼拜，社交圈仅限于相同经济背景的人，使得各自的阶级文化逐渐固化并失去交集。传统上充当缓冲地带的庞大中产阶层，随着排名前 1% 的家庭财富超过底层 90% 的总和，如同骨骼间被磨损殆尽的软骨一样消失了。

取而代之的是由富裕阶层后代构成的**舒适阶级**（Comfort Class: 虽个人资产未达巨富，但依靠家族安全网生活并占据高声望话语权职位的群体）。他们占据着中等收入但高声望的媒体、咨询和文化岗位，深刻塑造着美国的公共政策与社会叙事。其结果演变成一种事实上的**阶级隔离**（Class Apartheid: 基于经济实力形成的深度社会与政治隔离状态）：
* **政治与捐赠阶层的盲区**：许多身为百万富翁的政客、幕僚和咨询顾问从未体验过底层生存的生理恐慌，自以为能掌控政治与选民意志。捐赠阶层倾向于围绕文化和价值议题投票，寻求反映自身理念的候选人。
* **日常生存经验的认知鸿沟**：在大学时，我曾省吃俭用去高档意大利餐馆点了一份帕尔玛茄子打包作为次日口粮，第二天却被家境优渥的室友当作醉酒后的零食吃掉。在她眼里这只是一次微不足道的不体贴，而对我来说却是夺走了已经规划好的下一顿饭，迫使我面对经济断顿的危机。

当有产者完全无需担忧温饱时，他们很容易居高临下地指责底层选民“仅仅因为鸡蛋涨价就改变选票”。但对于在贫困线上挣扎的人而言，民主等抽象概念显得极为遥远且无关紧要，这直接导致低收入群体在民主选举中大量弃权。

<details>
<summary>Original English Source</summary>

These two groups don't just think differently, they rarely converge, living and working and worshipping separate from each other. Essentially, only socializing with people of very similar circumstances. And so, individual worldviews convene and hardened into class cultures. Cultures with very different ideas of what good governance and leadership even looks like.

Now, for generations, America's humble middle class existed between these two groups, like cartilage between bone. But, as the top 1% of households has accrued the same amount of wealth as the bottom 90% combined, that cartilage has grown thin. And essentially, our middle class has been replaced by a comfort class. The children of the upper class who on paper may not be rich, but who live with the security of a familial safety net. Often working the kind of mid-pay, high-prestige jobs that influence a lot of American culture and policy.

And the result is almost a class apartheid, where a small group of people with generational wealth control a society whose culture they don't fully understand. Our politicians, many of which are millionaires, their staffers and consultants, all come up from a place where money has afforded them a sense of agency and control. Such that they have a tendency to overestimate their influence on politics and the power of their own supporters' votes. The donor class, unfamiliar with that physiological panic that need can induce, tend to vote on social issues and look to support candidates who mirror their values. But, that need is still very, very real.

An anecdote. In college, I was paired with a lovely roommate from an upper middle class background. And one night, I decided to splurge and go and join her for an Italian meal out, carefully packing up my leftovers. But, the next day when I went to the fridge, my eggplant parm was gone. The victim of my roommate's late-night drunchie. I flew into a rage, and my poor bewildered roommate could not understand. But, to her it was an inconsiderate snack, and to me it was my next meal that I thought I'd already paid for. And now, I was left to stretch my dollar another day.

It's easy to judge people for voting based on the price of eggs when you are certain where your next meal is coming from. But, for the financially struggling in America, politics, let alone democracy, can seem a secondary concern, and sometimes just an irrelevant one, which is why you see so many lower-income Americans not participating in democracy in the first place.
</details>

### 民主的心脏病：威权诱惑与弥合鸿沟的路径

更为严峻的后果在于，当承受经济脆弱性的群体参与政治时，他们往往倾向于支持具有威权色彩的政治强人。从匈牙利、土耳其、塞内加尔到菲律宾，跨国研究均证实了**经济不平等、民主倒退与政治强人崛起**之间的强正相关性。这些强人承诺解决底层疾苦，却常常通过煽动分裂、树立外敌替罪羊来巩固权力；而不安全感本身正是种族主义和排外情绪的温床。

与此同时，极端财富不平等催生了另一群威权受益者。在镀金时代他们被称为**强盗大亨**（Robber Barons: 垄断核心工业并操纵政治的巨商），在后苏联时代的俄罗斯被称为**寡头**（Oligarchs: 攫取国家经济命脉干预政权的资本寡头），今天则被称为亿万富翁。他们出于与底层完全相反的动因拥抱威权主义——通过政治献金换取商业垄断、政府采购并凌驾于法律之上，从体制瓦解中牟取暴利。

经济不平等如同**民主制度的心脏病**，常常在爆发到不可挽回之前被漫不经心地忽视。要扭转滑向威权的轨迹，社会必须打破冷漠与隔离：不仅需要在政策上选出真正巩固工薪阶层与中产阶级的领袖，更需要跨越阶层壁垒重建同理心。在网络上攻击陌生人轻而易举，但当我们真正去倾听邻里与朋友的真实困境时，经济与尊严的裂痕才有可能被愈合。

<details>
<summary>Original English Source</summary>

And equally concerning is that often when they do, they tend to support authoritarian-leaning figures. From Hungary to Turkey, Senegal to the Philippines, researchers have found a consistent correlation between economic inequality, the decline of democracy, and the rise of strongman leaders. The kinds of people that promise to fix working people's problems, often by sowing divisions and blaming an other, which is a very reliable tactic because precarity also correlates to increases of racism and xenophobia.

There's still yet another group that tends to emerge in these historic moments of deep economic disparity, and they choose to great benefit in autocracy and patronage and all sorts of other undemocratic practices. In the Gilded Age, we called them robber barons. In post-Soviet Russia, we called them oligarchs. Today, we call them billionaires, able to live above the law, exchanging donations for business monopolies and government contracts. For very different reasons than the financially struggling, they see great benefit in autocracy.

Economic inequality is like the heart disease of democracy, so often ignored until it's too late, a threat to our very way of life. To fix it, we've got to stop distancing from each other and from the issue. We need to support candidates that will buttress the working and middle class, but we also need to know each other, to have empathy outside of our backyard, to cultivate compassion beyond our socioeconomic bracket, because it's so easy to rail online at strangers and much harder to ignore the problems of our friends and neighbors. Thank you.
</details>
