### 蚊子的魅力与疾病传播

我觉得很多蚊子其实挺漂亮的。如果你把蚊子放在体视显微镜下，然后放大观察，你首先会看到它的整个身体。接着再放大一些——你会看到它的眼睛——上面有许许多多小眼面，它们的排列方式真的非常美。然后，你还可以把口器放得更大，就能看到所有的鳞片和伸出来的毛发。我觉得那看起来简直太棒了。你知道，它是一种纤细而美丽的昆虫。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
I think a lot of mosquitoes are actually quite pretty. If you put a mosquito under the stereoscope and you get to, like, zoom in, you first see the whole body. And then you zoom in more -- you get to the eyes -- they have all these little facets, and it's just really beautiful how those are arranged. And then, you can zoom in even more on the mouthparts, and you see all the scales and the hair sticking out. I think that just looks amazing. You know, it's a slender, beautiful insect.
</details>

我叫 Felix Hol，是一名蚊子生物技术专家。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>

### 蚊子如何寻找并叮咬人类

蚊子大约有 3,500 种，但只有一小部分，大约 90 种，会以人为食。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
There's about 3,500 species, but only a small subset, around 90, feed on humans.
</details>

所以基本上，我坐在这里，呼出大量二氧化碳，而蚊子真的能从很远的地方闻到，比如几十米外。这会激活它们。它们开始飞行，然后它们会迎着二氧化碳羽流冲刺并盘旋搜索。当它们离我更近时，它们会转向更多地去闻体味，所以基本上就是我身上汗液的味道。当[它们]靠得更近时，我身体散发出的体温就会成为一个重要得多的线索，当然还有视觉线索。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
So basically, I'm sitting here, breathing out a bunch of CO2, and mosquitoes can really smell that from far away, like, tens of meters. That activates them. They start flying, and then, they kind of surge and cast through a CO2 plume. When they get closer to me, they switch to more, like, smelling body odors, so basically just my sweaty smell. When [they] get even closer, the body heat that my body radiates, that becomes a much more important cue, and of course also visual cues.
</details>

它们的脚上也有味觉和嗅觉感受器，所以当它们在你皮肤上行走时，它们可以尝到你皮肤表面的味道。蚊子会用喙轻触皮肤，然后它会把食物管和唾液管同时刺入。接着，食物管其实就会在你的皮肤里寻找血管，然后开始吸食。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
They also have taste and smell receptors on their feet, so when they’re walking on your skin, they can taste the surface of your skin. The mosquito will tap the skin with the proboscis, and then it will slide in both a food canal and a salivary duct. And the food canal will then, actually, just, in your skin, search for a blood vessel, and it will start drinking.
</details>

蚊子吸食人血的时间大约是，最快的可能 20 秒左右，也许 30 秒，而其他种类可能会花更长一点的时间，比如一分钟或一分半钟左右。它们有动机把这件事[做]得很快，因为我们可能会注意到它们，一巴掌拍下去，一切就结束了。那样对蚊子来说就太惨了。所以它们非常擅长非常隐蔽、非常迅速地完成这件事。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Mosquitoes feed off of humans for about, the quickest ones would be 20 seconds or so, maybe 30, and then other species may take a bit longer, like a minute or a minute and a half or so. They have an interest [in doing] this quickly, because we may just notice them, slap them and then it’s over. So that'd be sad for the mosquito. So they're really good at doing this very stealthily and very quickly.
</details>

### 杀虫剂抗药性与意外的行为改变

无论我们做什么原本打算阻止它们叮咬我们的事情，它们大概都会找到绕过的办法，对吧？蚊子正在非常迅速地适应我们人类施加给它们的压力。我想，当我们试图减少蚊媒疾病的负担时，我们真的必须记住这一点。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Whatever we do that was intended to prevent them from biting us, they will probably find ways around this, right? Mosquitoes are adapting really rapidly to the pressures that we as humans put on them. I think we really have to keep that in mind when we're trying to, you know, reduce the burden of mosquito-borne diseases.
</details>

杀虫剂当然是设计用来杀死蚊子的。但鉴于它们在环境中存在了这么多，蚊子现在对它们[正变得]有抗药性了。我们最近其实做了一些研究，得到了一些初步数据，表明蚊子在没有死于其中一些杀虫剂时，这会让它们变得过度活跃。于是它们实际上开始飞得更多了。所以我们还不知道这是否也会转化为蚊子叮咬更多的人，但你可以想象，当它们过度活跃时，它们可能会飞得更多、叮咬更多的人。所以像那样，你可以看到，有时候，一项原本意图做某件事的干预措施，甚至可能会在像蚊子这样的昆虫身上诱发另一种实际上适得其反的行为。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
Insecticides, of course, are designed to kill mosquitoes. But given that they've been in the environment so much, mosquitoes are now [becoming] resistant to them. And we've actually done some recent work where we have preliminary data that suggests that mosquitoes, when they don't die of some of these insecticides, it makes them hyperactive. So they start actually flying more. So we don't know yet if that also translates into the mosquitoes biting more people, but you can imagine that when they're hyperactive, they may fly more and bite more people. So like that, you can see that, sometimes, an intervention that is intended to do one thing may even actually induce another type of behavior in an insect like a mosquito that is actually counterproductive.
</details>

### biteOscope 与野外研究

我们的实验室位于荷兰奈梅亨，在拉德堡德大学医学中心。我们正在尝试构建新技术，让蚊子未知的生物学特性变得可见。我们制作的一个有趣的东西叫做 biteOscope。它有点像一个成像装置，在里面我们制作了一个我们喜欢称之为“皮肤模拟物”的东西。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
The lab is located in Nijmegen, in the Netherlands, at Radboud University Medical Center. We are trying to build new technology to make, like, unknown biology of mosquitoes visible. One of the interesting things we built is the biteOscope. It is a kind of like an imaging setup in which we built a thing we like to think of as a skin mimic.
</details>

所以对你我来说，它看起来就像一块塑料，但对蚊子来说，它其实正好具备蚊子认为它像真实皮肤所需的全部关键要素。于是它们可以停在上面，可以在上面走来走去，可以叮咬它，然后当它们叮咬它时，它们实际上会把口器刺入这种人造血液中。我们可以在它们做这件事的时候拍摄它们。我们可以获得非常高分辨率的影像。然后，我们就可以非常准确地研究它们的行为。例如，它们是如何叮咬的？它们多久叮咬一次？这是否取决于它们是否被感染？这是否取决于我们能涂在这种假皮肤表面的特定化合物？

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
So to you and me, it will just look like a piece of plastic, but for a mosquito, it actually just has all the key components that the mosquito needs to think that it's like real skin. So they can land on this, they can walk around, they can bite it, and then when they bite it, they actually slide their mouthparts into this artificial blood meal. And we can film them while they're doing this. And we can get really high-resolution footage. And then, we can really accurately study their behavior. For instance, how do they bite? How often do they bite? Does that depend on whether they're infected? Does that depend on particular compounds that we can put on this fake skin surface?
</details>

在实验室里，那些蚊子非常标准化。我们可以得到很多蚊子，而且我们确切地知道它们基本上是在什么条件下饲养的。但当然，它们和我们在真实自然界中发现的蚊子还是有点差距的。我们真的希望在尽可能多的流行病地区解决这些问题。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
In the lab, those mosquitoes are very standardized. We can get lots of them, and we know exactly under what conditions they were, basically, raised. But of course, they're a bit far from what we find in real nature. We really want to address these questions in as many endemic settings as possible.
</details>

所以当我们做野外工作时，我们直接从野外采集蚊子。我们通常会做的是拜访人们的房子，[然后]我们询问是否允许在他们家里采集蚊子。你就在清晨走进一所房子。蚊子还处于有点像停在墙上休息的状态。我拿着我的小吸尘器进去，我们把蚊子从墙上吸下来，然后我们把它们带到野外实验室。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
So when we do fieldwork, we collect mosquitoes straight from the field. So what we typically do is visit people's houses, [and] we ask if we’re allowed, to collect mosquitoes at their houses. So you just go into a house early in the morning. Mosquitoes are still kind of like resting on the wall. I come in with my little vacuum, we vacuum the mosquitoes off the wall and then we take them to the field lab.
</details>

我们以开放科学的方式开展所有工作。所有的代码，我们都全部公开、开源，而且我们主要只使用尽可能便宜的组件。如果其他人直接开始使用我们的工具，效果真的最好。所以开源和便宜，这就是把工具交到人们手中的主要方式。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
We practice everything in an open-science fashion. All the code, we make all of that available, open source, and we just mainly use as-cheap-as-possible components. It really works best if others just start using our tools. So open source and cheap, that's the main way to get it into people's hands.
</details>

### 展望未来

（音乐）
每年大约有 50 万人死于蚊媒疾病，而且这个数字实际上还在上升。由于它们对比如欧洲或北美的人们影响没那么大，它们得到的关注似乎比本应得到的少得多。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
(Music)
About half a million people die of a mosquito-borne disease each year, and the number is actually rising. Since they don't affect people in, for instance, Europe or North America that much, they seem to be getting much less attention than they maybe should.
</details>

我对未来的希望是，我们将能够利用关于蚊子实际如何运作的见解，来减轻蚊媒疾病的负担。所以虽然我觉得蚊子非常迷人、非常有趣，但我确实希望它们停止——比如——真的让人生病。所以这不只是推出下一代杀虫剂，过几年它们又会对它产生抗药性。我们可以利用我们对它们所了解的一切，想出能让我们真正、可持续地遏制传播的策略。在过去的几十年甚至更长时间里，我们已经学到了很多。但我们每学到一件事，就会引出另外 70 个问题。所以从这个意义上说，是的，这是一个永无止境的故事。

<details><summary>Original English</summary>
My hope for the future is that we'll be able to use insights about how mosquitoes actually work to reduce the burden of mosquito-borne diseases. So while I find mosquitoes very fascinating and intriguing, I do want them to stop, like, actually making people sick. So it's not just putting out the next insecticide that, in a couple of years, they'll be resistant to again. We can use everything that we've learned about them to come up with strategies that allow us to really, sustainably, curb the spread. We've learned a lot over the past, like, decades or even longer. But with every single thing we learn, that will pull open another 70 questions. So in that sense, yeah, this is a never-ending story.
</details>
