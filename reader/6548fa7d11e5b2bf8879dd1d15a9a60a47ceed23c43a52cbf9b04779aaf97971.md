### 玻璃摩天大楼与气候变暖的建筑反思

多年来，在世界各地的城市中，现代天际线的标志性形象一直都是高耸的玻璃摩天大楼。但在一个全球温度越来越热的变暖世界里，关于这些设计是否真正合理存在着越来越多的争论。落地窗显然带来了更多的光线，并提供了迷人而广阔的视野。但与实体墙相比，它们吸收的环境热量最多可达10倍。它们真正只能依靠大量的空调才能适合居住，而这正在对能源资源造成越来越大的消耗。

特别是一些阳光充沛国家的高楼大厦正在尝试适应。例如科威特的一栋建筑仅在南向墙面上开小窗。那里的另一栋建筑则用提供遮阳的垂直翼板隔断玻璃墙。新加坡的一家玻璃酒店则拥有缠绕着植物的钢网骨架。使用多层玻璃或采用超薄热反射涂层进行处理也能起到帮助作用。建筑师表示，我们可以从回顾历史中吸取经验。过去的高层建筑主要由沉重的砖石砌成，只有很小的窗户，这在防热方面起到了很大的作用。

最近的一项研究对比了纽约的九栋高楼，其中包括该市最古老的摩天大楼之一——建于1929年的著名克莱斯勒大厦（Chrysler Building），结果发现这座老式建筑的能源效率遥遥领先。

<details>
<summary>Original English</summary>
For years in cities around the world, the defining image of the modern skyline has been soaring glass skyscrapers. But in a warming world where temperatures everywhere are getting hotter and hotter, there's a growing debate about whether those designs really make sense. The floor to ceiling windows obviously let in more light and allow for glamorous sweeping views. But compared to a solid wall, they also let in as much as 10 times more ambient heat. They're truly only habitable with a lot of air conditioning, which is becoming a bigger and bigger drain on energy resources. Towers in some countries that get a lot of sun in particular are trying to adapt. One building in Kuwait, for example, put only small windows on the southern walls. Another one there breaks up walls of glass with vertical fins that provide shade. And a glass hotel in Singapore has a steel mesh skeleton strung with plants. Using multiple layers of glass or treating it with ultra thin heat reflective coatings can also help. And architects say we could learn from looking to the past. Tall buildings used to be mainly built out of heavy masonry with only little windows, which made a difference heatwise.

One recent study compared nine highrises in New York, including one of the city's oldest skyscrapers, the famous Chrysler building from 1929, and found the vintage building was by far the most energy efficient.
</details>
