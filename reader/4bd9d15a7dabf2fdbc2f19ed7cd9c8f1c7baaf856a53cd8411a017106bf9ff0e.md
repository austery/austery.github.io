### 大型工程建设的困境与突破

在我们进一步深入探讨约翰逊之前，我想先从摩西谈起。在过去一二十年里，人们一直在抱怨现在搞建设变得太难、阻碍太多，尤其是政府搞建设。如果看看第二大道地铁，他们已经修了大约50年。开通了一英里半。花费了超过40亿美元。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

Before we get more into Johnson, I want to first start with Moses. In the last decade or two, people have been complaining that it’s become too hard and too difficult to build things, especially for the government to build things. If you look at the Second Avenue subway, they’ve been building it for around 50 years. They’ve opened up a mile and a half. It cost more than $4 billion.
</details>

人们一直在说：“《权力经纪人》出版了。这是一本影响巨大的书。我们曾经担心权力过度集中并下放给一个人去搞建设。我们想要防止这种情况，所以我们制定了环境审查、土地利用审查、社区委员会和诉讼程序。”人们说：“我们矫枉过正了。我们走向了另一个极端。”你怎么看？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

People have been saying, "The Power Broker came out. It was an immensely influential book. We were concerned about so much power being centralized and delegated to one person so they could build. We wanted to prevent that, and so we made environmental review and land use review and community boards and litigation." People say, "We’ve overcorrected. We’ve gone too far in the other direction." What do you think of that?
</details>

他们还说，由于所有这些保障机制，罗伯特·摩西在今天不可能以同样的方式行事。但事实是，当罗伯特·摩西刚起步时，同样存在各种防范机制。当时人们也同样在说：“你什么都建不成。”那是在20世纪20年代。人们当时说：“政府什么都建不成。障碍太多了。什么都建不起来。我们需要公路。这是汽车时代。人人都在买汽车，但我们只有陈旧的老路。没人能把路修起来，因为业主不希望公路穿过他们的土地。”罗伯特·摩西出现了，突然之间那些障碍都消失了，因为不管有怎样的保障机制和其他一切，他只管去建。在写《权力经纪人》时，看到他如何绕过一切阻碍，对我来说实在太令人着迷了。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

They also say that because of all these safeguards, Robert Moses could not operate in the same way today. But the fact is, when Robert Moses started, there were also safeguards. People were also saying, "You can’t build anything." This was in the 1920s. People were saying, "Government can’t build anything. There are too many blocks. Nothing’s getting built. We need highways. It’s the age of cars. Everyone’s getting automobiles, but we have the same old roads. Nobody can build them because the property owners don’t want the roads built through their land." Robert Moses comes along, and all of a sudden those things vanish because, despite all the safeguards and everything else, he just builds. Doing The Power Broker, it was just fascinating to me to see how he gets around everything.
</details>

不管遇到什么。他要在琼斯海滩建一个海滩。有一天他看到了那里。他说：“这是我见过的最棒的海滩。离纽约市只有27英里。如果我能修一条通往这里的路，我就能为纽约市民提供这个绝佳的海滩。”但是修那条路似乎不可能，因为业主们不希望路穿过他们的农场和地产。他基本上就是说：“我偏要建。”看他一步步去做，克服每一个障碍——障碍是如此之多——然后突然间它开放了，琼斯海滩建成了，纽约人可以前往了。这是一种政治天才的体现，也是意志力的体现：“我一定要做成。”了解这些非常引人入胜。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

It doesn’t matter what it is. He’s going to build a beach at Jones Beach. He sees it one day. He says, "This is the greatest beach I’ve ever seen. It’s only 27 miles from New York City. If I can build a road out here, I will give the people of New York City this great beach." But it seems impossible to build that road because the property owners don’t want the road going through their farms and property. He basically says, "I’m going to build it." To watch him do it, to overcome each obstacle, there are so many, and then all of a sudden it’s open and you have Jones Beach and you have it accessible to New York. It’s an act of political genius, and it’s also an act of will: "I’m going to do it." That’s fascinating to learn about.
</details>

</details>

对此的回答是，当摩西当年搞建设时，他们也在说同样的话。“谁也建不成任何东西。我们需要给汽车修路，但这些路必须穿过几十个农场、大量地产。绝不会有人能做到的。”但他横空出世，并且做成了。所以今天也会有人出现并把它做成。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

The answer to that is, when Moses was building, they were saying the same thing. "Nobody’s ever going to build. We need roads for the automobiles, but they’d have to go through dozens of farms, scores of properties. No one’s ever going to be able to do it." But he comes along and does it. So today someone would come along and do it.
</details>

### 民主制度下的建设与两难

我认为这对民主政体来说是一个深刻的问题：我们如何才能既不走向五六十年代摩西那种极端，又不落入我们现在的处境？在我居住的加州，我们在过去十年里花费了——我不知道——150亿美元。而这150亿美元的建设投入，却没有给我们换来一条新线路或一英里新的铁路。而在另一种不同的体制下……你可以说今天的中国就像四五十年代的纽约市。他们有一种非常类似摩西的态度，在过去10到20年里修建了数万英里的铁路。当然，代价是类似规模的人口搬迁和鲁莽行事。那么，我们怎样才能在两个极端之间找到一个好的平衡点呢？
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

This, I think, is a profound question for democracies: how do we end up in a place which is not the extremes of ’50s or ’60s Moses, but also not where we are now? Where I live in California, we’ve spent, I don’t know, $15 billion over the last decade. We haven’t gotten a single new line or single new mile of railway that those $15 billion have been spent to build. Whereas in a different system… You could say China today is like New York City in the ’40s and ’50s. They have a very Moses-type attitude and they put up tens of thousands of miles of railways over the last 10-20 years. But of course, at the cost of similar amounts of displacement of people and recklessness. So how do we land in a good spot between either extreme?
</details>

我不确定是否存在这样一个好的平衡点。你刚才说的就是现实情况。要进行任何大规模建设，你都会面临几十个、大量甚至成百上千个不希望把项目建在那里的业主。所以问题是，任何工程到底该怎么建起来？然后就会有某个人出现，并且能够把它做成。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

I’m not sure there is a good spot. What you just said is the situation. To build anything on a large scale, you’re confronted with dozens or scores or perhaps hundreds of property owners who don’t want it built there. So the question is, how does anything get built? Then someone comes along and can do it.
</details>

当罗伯特·摩西起步时，向长岛修路似乎是不可能的，确实是不可能的，因为道路必须穿过大量地产，而大多数业主不希望道路穿过去。他们当时有权力阻止，因为摩西当时没有土地征用权。但摩西基本上就是说：“我一定要建。”他总能找到办法克服每一个困难。我想表达的是什么呢？我想说的是，每隔一段时间，就会出现一个在为公众建造重大工程方面堪称天才（如果这个词恰当的话）的人物。可能过去几十年都没有这样一个人。你会说：“一切都停滞了。再也不会有任何重大工程建成了。”然后突然有人出现，顷刻之间工程就建成了。这就是政治天才的本质。我碰巧在写的两个人，林登·约翰逊和罗伯特·摩西，约翰逊面对的是民权，摩西面对的是公共工程，当时人们都说“这不可能做到”。但他们做到了。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

When Robert Moses started, it seemed impossible, actually impossible, to build roads out into Long Island because the road would have to cross scores of properties, most of whose owners didn’t want the roads coming through. They had the power to stop them then because he didn’t have the power of eminent domain. But Moses said basically, "I’m going to build it." He would find a way to overcome each difficulty. What am I trying to say by that? I’m trying to say that every once in a while someone comes along who’s a genius, if that’s the right word, at getting big things built for the public. Decades may pass when there’s no one like that. You say, "Everything’s stalled. Nothing big is ever going to be built again." Then someone suddenly comes along and all of a sudden things get built. That’s the nature of political genius. I happen to be writing about two guys, Lyndon Johnson and Robert Moses, Johnson with civil rights and Moses with public works, where people were saying, "You can’t do this." But they did it.
</details>

如果我说得不对请纠正我，但如果容我对你说的话尝试做一种解读，听起来你似乎认为我们今天需要一点摩西。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

Correct me if I’m putting words in your mouth, but if I may attempt an interpretation of what you said, it sounds like you think we need a little Moses today.
</details>

很有见地。我有时也会琢磨你刚才说的那句话。回答是，我当然不希望有像罗伯特·摩西那样无情且无视公众舆论的人。但与此同时，你需要有人能找到办法把工程建起来。尽管有那么多人不想让它建，他还是必须找到办法。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

A great insight. I sometimes wonder about just what you said. The answer is, I certainly don’t want someone who’s as ruthless and disregarding public opinion as a Robert Moses. But at the same time, you need someone who’s going to find a way to get things built. He’s going to have to find a way despite the fact that so many people don’t want it built.
</details>

### 理想主义者的转变与代价

很多没读过这本书的人有这样一种印象，觉得你认为摩西所做的一切都是糟糕的，我们必须确保再也不出现摩西这样的人。所以听到你说我们今天又需要一点摩西，挺有意思的。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

A lot of people who haven’t read the book have this impression that you think everything Moses did was bad, and that we need to make sure that we can never have a Moses again. So it’s interesting to hear you say that we need a little Moses again.
</details>

当人们对我说他所做的一切都很糟糕时，我的回答是：“你没读过这本书。”因为毫无疑问，书的前半部分，好几百页，写的都是一个年轻的理想主义者，他看到城市居民对休闲娱乐、对长岛海滩的巨大需求，并且想方设法让城市居民能够去到那里。他做事的方式展现出了非凡的天才与意志力，那是非常了不起的。
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

When people say to me everything was bad that he did, what I say is, "You didn’t read the book." Because certainly the first parts of the book, hundreds of pages, are about a young idealist who sees this great need for recreation, for beaches on Long Island for the city people, and is going to find a way to make them accessible to people in the city. It’s an act of genius and willpower the way he does it, and that’s great.
</details>

那么，这是否演变成了摩西谁的话都不听、唯一重要的就是他自己想建什么的状态？确实演变成了那样。他确实变了。但早期的摩西，那个理想主义者，在搞建设之前，是文官体制细节方面的专家。他把所有的精力都投入到试图改革文官体制上，以便让人们凭借功绩而非政治关系获得晋升。他在这方面付出了巨大努力。你能看到一种真正的理想主义。在早期的工程中你能看到一种真正的理想主义，当时他说：“我想让纽约人能够去到这些海滩。”
<details><summary>Original English</summary>

Now, does it evolve into where Moses listens to nobody, and the only thing that matters is what he wants to get built? It does evolve into that. He does change. But the young Moses, the idealist, before he’s building, is an expert in the technicalities of civil service. He devotes all his energies to trying to reform the civil service so that people get promoted on merit instead of political pull. He works so hard at that. You see a real idealism.
