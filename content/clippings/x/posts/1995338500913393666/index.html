You are a fan, not an investor. If the only thing you hear from that Musk interview is “I don’t buy stocks, I only build”. The real tell was when he quietly told you who gets to be AI king today and who is being groomed to run the empire next. Today Nvidia is king. Tomorrow, the most valuable operator over the next decade is Google. Why? Most people heard “Nvidia is strong, Google is also good.” That’s not how decision-makers hear it. Any CEO with a few billion in AI capex on the line heard something very different: What keeps them up at night is not “will my product sell,” it’s “will my supply chain hold.” Right now 80–90% of global AI acceleration sits on one vendor. That’s not a moat, that’s a single point of failure. That’s why you see the so-called contradiction that isn’t a contradiction at all: everyone is still scrambling for Nvidia’s parts, while every major buyer quietly builds a second leg – in-house ASICs, TPUs, custom inference chips. Even 5–10% penetration is not about saving money this quarter; it’s buying a survivable alternative. The moment a “backup” becomes a production-grade line, the gameboard changes. Valuation follows that physics. For Nvidia $NVDA, the last two years have been priced on almost perfect assumptions: share north of 80%, gross margins in the 75%, ten years of cash flows discounted as if nothing structural intervenes. But if multi-sourcing is a physical necessity, that curve cannot extend linearly. Volume can grow, but share and margins will be mechanically squeezed; the imagination premium in the DCF bleeds back toward normal. Flip the frame and Google $GOOG becomes the underpriced side of the same story: it builds the models, designs the accelerators, owns the cloud and the data-center economics. That is exactly the profile the market is still valuing like “an ad business with AI optionality.” That gap is the real upside. I’m not interested in the emotional “can Nvidia be killed” debate. The real question is more primitive and more predictive: who can rewrite the cost of intelligence. Once you put power, packaging, optical interconnect, topology and utilization into the same equation, this stops being a chip duel and turns into a long chain about how system-level TCO (Total Cost of Ownership)is going to be redistributed between two architectures. I’ve built a full cost-of-intelligence framework that pulls Nvidia and Google onto the same coordinate system. The full IC memo is where I open that chain end to end and show exactly how the curve bends. Link in comments.
