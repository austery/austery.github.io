柯达如何意外揭露核试验：一场辐射侦测与历史教训 veritasium 2020-09-16

领域: geopolitics

分类: geopolitics

主讲人: veritasium

来源: 查看

发布时间: 2020-09-16

摘要:

本文揭示了美国政府在早期核试验中的保密工作如何被柯达公司意外揭露。通过分析受污染的X光胶片，柯达科学家朱利安·韦伯推断出核裂变的存在。文章探讨了核尘埃的传播、对公众健康的长期影响（特别是碘-131和锶-90），以及政府与柯达之间达成的秘密协议。最后，文章还介绍了核试验遗留的放射性物质在现代法医学和文物鉴定中的应用。

三位一体核试验：秘密的开端

在新墨西哥州的沙漠中，有一个地方每年只向公众开放两天，但我却有幸与一个小型的摄制组独自造访。

View/Hide Original English

There's a place in the New Mexico desert open to the public for just two days out of the year, but I got to visit by myself with only a small film crew.

这里就是三位一体核试验场（Trinity bomb test site），1945年7月16日凌晨5点29分45秒，第一颗核弹在这里爆炸。

View/Hide Original English

This is the Trinity bomb test site, where on July 16th, 1945, at exactly 5:29 and 45 seconds in the morning, the first nuclear bomb was exploded.

这次试验被称为“三位一体”，现场曾有一座约30米高的铁塔。

View/Hide Original English

The test was called Trinity; there was actually a tower here that stood 100 feet or about 30 meters.

见证此次试验的人员名单，堪称20世纪物理学界的“名人录”，其中包括费米、费曼、奥本海默和冯·诺依曼。

View/Hide Original English

The list of people who witnessed the test reads like a who's who of 20th-century physics: Fermi, Feynman, Oppenheimer, von Neumann.

然而，这次试验是最高机密，普通民众不应知晓。

View/Hide Original English

But the test was top secret; the general public weren't to know.

那么，如何隐藏一场巨大的核爆炸呢？

View/Hide Original English

But how do you hide a huge nuclear explosion?

当时，《阿尔伯克基论坛报》发布了一份声明，声称那是一次大型但意外的常规爆炸。

View/Hide Original English

Well, the Albuquerque Tribune published a statement claiming it was a large but accidental conventional explosion.

不仅是普通民众被蒙在鼓里，甚至新墨西哥州州长在广岛原子弹爆炸后，才得知“三位一体”是一次核试验。

View/Hide Original English

It wasn't just the general public who were kept in the dark; the governor of New Mexico was not even aware that Trinity was a nuclear test until after the bombing of Hiroshima.

即便如此，关于爆炸的细节仍然寥寥无几。

View/Hide Original English

Even then, details about the blast were scarce.

柯达的意外发现

然而，位于美国另一端纽约州的胶片公司柯达，却发现了正在发生的事情。

View/Hide Original English

But Kodak, the film company based on the other side of the country in New York state, well, they figured out what was happening.

这一切都始于一些有缺陷的X光胶片。

View/Hide Original English

It all started with some defective x-ray film.

柯达发现，当他们冲洗未使用的胶片时，胶片上出现了几十甚至上百个小黑点；这些胶片即使从未开封，也已经暴露在辐射中。

View/Hide Original English

Kodak found when they developed unused film it showed tens or even a hundred little dark spots; the film had been exposed to radiation even though it had never been taken out of its packaging.

这实际上并不是一个新问题。

View/Hide Original English

This was actually not a new problem.

在第二次世界大战期间，镭（Radium: 一种放射性元素）被广泛用于制造夜光表盘、飞行仪表和手表。

View/Hide Original English

During World War II, radium was used extensively to make glow-in-the-dark dials, flight instruments, and watches.

镭之所以发光，是因为它具有放射性，会释放阿尔法粒子（alpha particles: 一种由两个质子和两个中子组成的粒子）。

View/Hide Original English

Radium glows because it is radioactive; it gives off alpha particles.

当时，一些镭最终进入了这些工厂的纸张和纸板中，由于物资稀缺，剩余的纸制品被回收利用，导致少量镭进入了普通的纸张供应。

View/Hide Original English

Now, some of this radium ended up on paper and cardboard at these factories, and because supplies were scarce, leftover paper products were salvaged and recycled, leading small amounts of radium to end up in the general paper supply.

当这些纸张被用于包装柯达胶片时，镭发出的辐射会在胶片使用前就使其曝光。

View/Hide Original English

And when this paper was used to package up Kodak film, radiation from radium would expose the film before it was ever used.

这个问题如此严重，以至于柯达改变了其供应链，精心挑选了几家造纸厂，以便严格控制原材料。

View/Hide Original English

And it was such a problem that Kodak altered their supply chain, carefully selecting just a few paper mills where they could strictly control the raw materials.

其中一家造纸厂位于印第安纳州文森斯市的沃巴什河畔。

View/Hide Original English

One of the mills was in Vincennes, Indiana, on the Wabash River.

这家工厂生产草板纸，用于插入X光胶片之间。

View/Hide Original English

It made strawboard, which was inserted between sheets of x-ray film.

几个月来，这种草板纸一直工作正常，直到1945年8月6日生产的一批产品开始导致X光胶片上出现斑点。

View/Hide Original English

Now, for months, this strawboard was working perfectly until a batch manufactured on August 6, 1945, started to cause spots to appear on the x-ray film.

韦伯的科学侦测

因此，柯达的科学家朱利安·韦伯被指派解决这个问题。

View/Hide Original English

So Julian Webb, a scientist at Kodak, was tasked with solving the problem.

他从胶片曝光区域背后的草板纸上打出小孔，然后测量了从这些材料中发出的阿尔法粒子。

View/Hide Original English

So he punched out little holes of strawboard behind where the film had been exposed, and then he measured the alpha particles coming off that material.

然而，其水平并没有明显高于背景辐射，这排除了镭以及其他天然存在的铀、钍和锕的同位素，它们都是阿尔法粒子发射体。

View/Hide Original English

But the level was not appreciably higher than background, and this ruled out radium and the other naturally occurring isotopes of uranium, thorium, and actinium, all alpha emitters.

但当他测量贝塔辐射（beta radiation: 一种由高速电子或正电子组成的电离辐射）时，他发现了显著的放射性活动；这与他在胶片上看到的情况相符。

View/Hide Original English

But when he measured beta radiation, he found significant activity; this matched what he saw on the film.

这种辐射穿透了几层材料，这是贝塔粒子能做到而阿尔法粒子不能做到的。

View/Hide Original English

The radiation penetrated several layers, something beta particles can do but not alpha.

在几个月的时间里，他测量了这种放射性物质的半衰期（half-life: 放射性物质衰变一半所需的时间），发现只有大约30天。

View/Hide Original English

And over a period of months, he measured the half-life of the radioactive substance, finding it was only about 30 days.

这个半衰期，结合贝塔粒子的能量，使韦伯得出结论：放射性污染物很可能是铈-141（Cerium-141: 一种只能来自核裂变爆炸的同位素）。

View/Hide Original English

This half-life, combined with the energy of the beta particles, led Webb to conclude that the radioactive contaminant was most likely Cerium-141, an isotope that could only have come from a nuclear fission explosion.

更重要的是，同样的污染物也出现在柯达另一家位于爱荷华州塔马市的工厂的纸张中，这家工厂距离数百公里。

View/Hide Original English

What's more, the same contaminant appeared in paper from another of Kodak's mills, this one hundreds of kilometers away in Tama, Iowa.

核尘埃的传播与柯达的秘密

那么，铈-141是如何进入那些草板纸中的呢？

View/Hide Original English

So how did Cerium-141 end up in that strawboard?

当第一颗原子弹在新墨西哥沙漠中爆炸时，钚核心发生了裂变链式反应（fission chain reaction: 核裂变反应持续进行的过程）。

View/Hide Original English

Well, when that first atomic bomb exploded in the New Mexico desert, the plutonium core underwent a fission chain reaction.

钚原子核分裂成两部分，释放能量并产生更多中子以维持链式反应。

View/Hide Original English

Plutonium nuclei ripped in two, releasing energy and more neutrons to continue the chain reaction.

但裂变是一个混乱的过程，它并不总是将原子核分裂成相同的产物。

View/Hide Original English

But fission is a messy process; it doesn't always split the nucleus into the same products.

相反，会产生数百种不同的原子核，几乎所有都具有放射性，它们随蘑菇云被带入平流层（stratosphere: 大气层中位于对流层之上的一层），并随风向气流扩散。

View/Hide Original English

Instead, hundreds of different nuclei are produced, virtually all of them radioactive, and they are carried up into the stratosphere with the mushroom cloud and dispersed on air currents whichever way the wind blows.

在这种情况下，它们传播了一千多公里，最终到达爱荷华州和印第安纳州上空。

View/Hide Original English

In this case, they traveled over a thousand kilometers, ending up over Iowa and Indiana.

在那里，雨水捕获了其中一些放射性粒子，导致它们从天而降，这就是放射性尘埃（radioactive fallout: 核爆炸后散布在环境中的放射性物质）一词的由来。

View/Hide Original English

Here, rain captured some of these radioactive particles, causing them to fall out of the sky, hence the term radioactive fallout.

这些放射性物质最终进入了流经柯达两家造纸厂的河流中，而这些造纸厂正是利用河水来造纸。

View/Hide Original English

This radioactive material ended up in rivers like the ones that run beside the two Kodak paper mills, which used that river water to make their paper.

这就是铈-141最终导致柯达X光胶片模糊，并揭露美国核秘密的原因。

View/Hide Original English

So that's how Cerium-141 ended up fogging Kodak's x-ray film and exposing the U.S. nuclear secret.

柯达的侦测消息传回了洛斯阿拉莫斯的科学家耳中，他们对此很感兴趣，并想了解更多。

View/Hide Original English

Now, word of Kodak's detection made it back to scientists at Los Alamos, who were intrigued and wanted to know more.

一位顾问致信韦伯，询问他每平方英里检测到的放射性强度、降落粒子的尺寸以及放射性物质的半衰期。

View/Hide Original English

A consultant wrote to Webb to find out how much radioactivity per square mile he detected, and the size of the particles which fell, and the half-life of the activity.

尽管韦伯在1945年底就完成了实验，但他直到1949年才在《物理评论》上发表，我相信这是因为他认识到自己的发现是多么敏感。

View/Hide Original English

Although Webb completed his experiments by the end of 1945, he only published them in Physical Review in 1949, and that's because I'm sure he recognized just how sensitive his findings were.

毕竟，他本人曾在伯克利和橡树岭参与曼哈顿计划（Manhattan Project: 二战期间美国研发原子弹的秘密计划），利用电磁技术分离铀同位素。

View/Hide Original English

After all, he himself had worked on the Manhattan Project at Berkeley and Oak Ridge, using electromagnetic techniques to separate uranium isotopes.

政府的回应与公众健康隐忧

柯达清楚地认识到他们需要采取措施保护胶片，因此他们在建筑物进气口安装了空气采样器，以监测空气中的核尘埃。

View/Hide Original English

What was clear to Kodak was that they needed to take steps to protect their film, and so they installed air samplers to monitor for fallout in the air intakes of their buildings.

核尘埃是一个极其重要的问题；核弹正在被测试，这在一定程度上污染了大气。

View/Hide Original English

Fallout is a vitally important subject; bombs are being tested which to some extent contaminate the atmosphere.

当时，美国政府意识到他们需要仔细考虑在哪里进行核弹试验。

View/Hide Original English

Now, the U.S. government was aware they needed to carefully consider where to test nuclear bombs.

“我们需要一个更靠近我们国内实验室且有空中支援的地点，在美国大陆的某个地方，但具体在哪里呢？”

View/Hide Original English

What we need is a site closer to our stateside laboratories and available air support, somewhere in the continental United States, but where?

“三位一体”试验后，曼哈顿计划的放射安全负责人斯塔福德·沃伦建议，试验地点至少应距离平民居住区150英里。

View/Hide Original English

After Trinity, the Manhattan Project's chief of radiological safety, Stafford Warren, recommended tests be conducted at least 150 miles from civilian populations.

1948年，空军气象学家霍尔兹曼上校告诉选址委员会，东海岸的地点是最佳选择，因为否则美国盛行的西风会将核尘埃扩散到全国各地。

View/Hide Original English

In 1948, an Air Force meteorologist, Colonel Holzman, told the site selection committee that a location on the East Coast would be best because otherwise the prevailing westerly winds across the U.S. would spread the fallout across the country.

他们选择的地点是内华达州，几乎是美国最西边的地方，距离拉斯维加斯仅100英里。

View/Hide Original English

The site they chose was Nevada, almost as far west as you can go and just 100 miles from Las Vegas.

理由是这个地点更靠近武器实验室，可以加速其开发。

View/Hide Original English

The rationale was this site was closer to weapons labs so it would accelerate their development.

1951年首次核弹试验后，柯达在纽约罗切斯特的总部检测到了核尘埃。

View/Hide Original English

After the first bomb test there in 1951, Kodak detected fallout at their headquarters in Rochester, New York.

一场暴风雪后，盖革计数器读数是正常背景辐射的25倍。

View/Hide Original English

After a snowstorm, Geiger counters read 25 times the normal background.

因此，柯达威胁要起诉美国政府，要求赔偿“因内华达试验或任何进一步的原子能试验对我们产品造成的巨大损害”。

View/Hide Original English

So Kodak threatened to sue the U.S. government for the considerable amount of damage to our products resulting from the Nevada tests or from any further atomic energy tests.

但双方并未诉诸法律，而是达成了一项协议：原子能委员会（Atomic Energy Commission: 负责美国核能开发和管理的政府机构）将提前向柯达乃至整个摄影行业发出关于即将进行的试验以及根据气象模型预测的核尘埃可能扩散范围的警告。

View/Hide Original English

But instead of a lawsuit, the two sides came to an agreement: the Atomic Energy Commission would give Kodak, and indeed the entire photographic industry, advanced warning about upcoming tests and where the fallout was likely to end up given meteorological models.

作为回报，柯达公司同意对放射性尘埃保持沉默。

View/Hide Original English

In return, the company agreed to keep quiet about radioactive fallout.

从1951年到1963年，美国在内华达州进行了100次地面核试验；正如预测的那样，由此产生的放射性原子核混合物被吹散到全国大部分地区。

View/Hide Original English

And from 1951 until 1963, the U.S. conducted a hundred above-ground nuclear tests in Nevada; the resulting mixture of radioactive nuclei was blown, as predicted, across most of the country.

当这些原子落在农田上时，它们被牲畜吃掉或被农作物吸收，并进入了食物链。

View/Hide Original English

When these atoms fell on agricultural land, they were eaten by livestock or taken up in crops, and they became part of the food supply.

被奶牛食用的放射性碘-131（Iodine-131: 一种放射性碘同位素）通过牛奶传递给人类，这尤其成问题，因为碘在人体内会集中在甲状腺中。

View/Hide Original English

Radioactive Iodine-131 eaten by cows is passed on to humans through their milk, and this is particularly problematic because iodine is concentrated by the body in the thyroid gland.

而甲状腺正在发育的儿童饮用牛奶最多；据估计，核试验导致了数万例额外的甲状腺癌病例。

View/Hide Original English

And children with small, developing thyroids drink the most milk; it's estimated that the nuclear tests led to tens of thousands of extra cases of thyroid cancer.

原子能委员会对碘-131并不太担心，因为它只有八天的放射性半衰期，衰变很快。

View/Hide Original English

The Atomic Energy Commission wasn't too worried about Iodine-131 because it has a radioactive half-life of only eight days; it decays quickly.

他们更担心锶-90（Strontium-90: 一种放射性锶同位素），其半衰期接近30年。

View/Hide Original English

They were more concerned about Strontium-90, which has a half-life of almost 30 years.

锶-90由于其独特的性质组合，可能代表着最大的危险。

View/Hide Original English

Strontium-90, because of a unique combination of properties, probably represents the greatest danger.

锶-90在生物化学上与钙相似，因此一旦摄入，它会进入我们的牙齿和骨骼。

View/Hide Original English

Strontium-90 behaves biochemically like calcium, so when ingested, it ends up in our teeth and bones.

当它发出电离贝塔辐射（ionizing beta radiation: 能够电离物质的贝塔粒子辐射）时，可能导致骨癌、周围组织癌或白血病。

View/Hide Original English

And as it emits ionizing beta radiation, it can cause bone cancer, cancer in the surrounding tissues, or leukemia.

在被称为“乳牙调查”的项目中，圣路易斯的科学家在1950年至1970年间收集了32万颗乳牙。

View/Hide Original English

In what's become known as the baby tooth survey, scientists in St. Louis collected up 320,000 teeth between 1950 and 1970.

他们发现，1963年出生的孩子乳牙中的锶含量是1950年出生的孩子的50倍。

View/Hide Original English

They found that kids born in 1963 had 50 times more strontium in their baby teeth than those born in 1950.

“目前存在的证据表明，核尘埃仅导致背景辐射小幅增加，相应地，突变也仅微量增加。”

View/Hide Original English

Presently existing evidence indicates that fallout has caused only a small increase in background radiation and correspondingly only a minute increase in mutations.

然而，实际上，碘-131和锶-90，以及核尘埃中的其他放射性同位素，几乎肯定增加了美国人口的癌症和其他疾病发病率。

View/Hide Original English

In reality, both Iodine-131 and Strontium-90, plus other radioactive isotopes in the fallout, almost certainly increased rates of cancer and other diseases in the U.S. population.

这一点得到了研究的证实，这些研究显示疾病发病率与放射性同位素摄入量之间存在关联。

View/Hide Original English

This is borne out by studies showing correlations between rates of disease and radioactive isotope uptake.

图表显示，在1963年《部分核禁试条约》（Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty: 1963年禁止在大气层、外层空间和水下进行核武器试验的国际条约）签署后不久，1964年达到了峰值；该条约禁止在大气层、水下和外层空间进行核试验，只留下地下试验。

View/Hide Original English

The graph shows a peak in 1964, just after the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty was signed in 1963; it banned nuclear tests in the atmosphere, underwater, and in outer space, leaving only underground tests.

该条约的核心目标是解决公众对核试验规模及其产生的放射性尘埃日益增长的担忧。

View/Hide Original English

A central aim of the treaty was to address growing public concern over the scale of nuclear tests and the resulting radioactive fallout.

近50年后，1997年美国参议院最终就此事举行听证会时，与柯达达成的协议受到了严厉批评，特别是参议员汤姆·哈金。

View/Hide Original English

When the U.S. Senate finally held a hearing on the matter almost 50 years later in 1997, the agreement with Kodak was sharply criticized, especially by Senator Tom Harkin.

他说：“政府警告了整个摄影行业，并提供了潜在污染的地图和预测。我问，那些给奶农的地图在哪里？给这些地区儿童父母的警告在哪里？”

View/Hide Original English

He said the government warned the entire photographic industry and provided maps and forecasts of potential contamination. Where, I asked, were the maps for dairy farmers? Where were the warnings to parents of children in these areas?

“政府保护了胶卷，却没有保护我们孩子的生命；这张图景有问题。”

View/Hide Original English

The government protected rolls of film but not the lives of our kids; there's something wrong with this picture.

我认为主要问题在于，在40年代和50年代，人们对放射性尘埃的生物效应知之甚少。

View/Hide Original English

And I think the main problem was that in the 40s and 50s, not enough was known about the biological effects of radioactive fallout.

基因会时不时地发生改变或突变，从而改变生物体的特征。

View/Hide Original English

Every so often, a gene changes or mutates, and a characteristic of the organism is altered.

我们不知道突变发生的具体过程。

View/Hide Original English

We do not know the exact process by which mutations occur.

癌症需要数年甚至数十年才能显现，而核尘埃模型预测的污染物扩散更为均匀，因此浓度较低。

View/Hide Original English

Cancers took years or even decades to appear, and fallout models predicted a more uniform and therefore less concentrated spread of contaminants.

当纽约奥尔巴尼的学生在一次大雨后测量到千倍于背景辐射的水平时，原子能委员会关于此事件的报告称其为“一个非常强烈的局部核尘埃区域的有趣例子”。

View/Hide Original English
