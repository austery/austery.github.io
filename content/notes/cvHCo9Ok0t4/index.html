洛杉矶山火后的余生：重建之路与社区的力量 New York Times Podcasts 2026-01-07

领域: life-family

分类: personal-growth

来源: 查看

摘要:

本集节目聚焦 2025 年洛杉矶山火的幸存者故事。Wirecutter 编辑 Mike Cohen 和 Gregory Han 分享了他们在阿尔塔迪纳（Altadena）失去家园或被迫撤离的惨痛经历。通过他们的视角，探讨了气候变化下自然灾害的不可预测性，并提出了核心建议：在灾难中，官方指令可能滞后，唯有提前投资邻里关系和保持自救意识，才是生存的关键。

灾难中的 Wirecutter 精神

We'll be talking about some specific pieces of advice in the context of emergency preparedness, but we're also going to hear the human side of this story. We'll talk with Mike and another colleague of ours, Gregory Han, who lived through the eaten fire, about the things they've learned over the past year, about the unpredictability of natural disasters and just how long, arduous, emotional, and expensive the road to recovery can be.

Original English

At Wirecutter, we offer advice and recommend gear to prepare for natural disasters. Unfortunately, climate change is making these weather events more frequent, more intense, and more unpredictable.

Christine Cyr Clisset: Mike 和 Gregory 经历了这场灾难。因为 Wirecutter 的精神根深蒂固，他们全程都在像真正的 Wirecutter 作者一样思考：关注细节，记录下可以与他人分享的教训。他们总结出的指导建议适用于任何自然灾害，比如你需要了解的房屋保险知识，或者你现在就可以对房屋和社区进行的投资，这些投资在紧急情况下带来的回报可能远超你的想象。

Original English

Christine Cyr Clisset: Mike and Gregory lived through a disaster. And because the wire cutter ethos runs deep, they were thinking the whole time like true wire cutter writers. People who consider the details, who take notes on the lessons they could share with others. They came through it with guidance that could apply to any natural disaster, like what you need to understand about home insurance in case you ever really need to use it, or the ways you can invest in your home and community now, wherever you live, that may pay off in an emergency a lot more than you think.

圣安娜风与撤离时刻

Christine Cyr Clisset: 虽然很多人认为火灾是核心问题，但极端强风对这场灾难的影响同样巨大。2025 年 1 月初，**圣安娜风（Santa Ana winds）**在洛杉矶达到顶峰。这些极端的风和干燥的条件导致原本可能被控制的小火迅速蔓延。

Original English

Christine Cyr Clisset: Although many people might associate fire as the central problem of the LA wildfires, extreme wind had just as much to do with the disaster. In early January 2025, the Santa Ana winds peaked in Los Angeles. These extreme winds and dry conditions caused small fires that may have otherwise been contained to spread quickly.

Gregory Han: 在风暴到来之前，我确实非常不安。我意识到天气状况可能会很严重，因为有一位年轻的当地气象学家 Edgar McGregor，他提前大约一周就发出了警告。

Original English

Gregory Han: I was definitely on edge prior to the wind arriving and I was aware of the severity of that possible weather situation because of a local young meteorologist who gave microclimate reports of just Altadena and he warned people maybe a week in advance. His name is Edgar McGregor.

Gregory Han: 1 月 6 日下午，我和妻子 Emily 带着两只小猫离开了阿尔塔迪纳。我们只带了一晚上的换洗衣物、一台笔记本电脑和一些猫粮。我们以为只会离开一两天。但随后，火势完全改变了方向，我意识到我们回不去了。

Original English

Gregory Han: On the afternoon of January 6th, Gregory, Emily, and their two kittens left Altadena with enough clothes for one overnight. He took a laptop and some cat food, and we thought we'd be gone for a day, maybe 2 days. But then, as the day progressed, the fire completely changed its direction, and I became very aware that we were not going to be returning home.

核心教训：自救与社区投资

Christine Cyr Clisset: 如果你要给那些从未经历过失去家园的人一些建议，你会说什么？

Original English

Christine Cyr Clisset: Is there any advice you would give someone?

Michael Bradley Cohen: 有两个答案。从个人层面来说，这种灾难会迅速告诉你什么是最重要的，什么是值得装进包里的——那通常只是一个小包。从实际操作层面来说，当自然灾害发生时，如果你在寻找那些“知道该怎么做”的人，他们可能并不在场。所以，你必须成为那个能救自己的人。

Original English

Michael Bradley Cohen: Two answers. On like a personal side of things, it would be when you're going through a disaster like this, it tells you really quickly what is most important and what is worth packing in a bag. From a practical standpoint, when a natural disaster is happening, if you're looking around for the people who know what to do, they might not be there. So, you have to be that person for yourself.

Michael Bradley Cohen: 灾难发生得太快，可能没有穿着制服的工作人员来给你有组织的指示。你必须掌控自己的生存。但过去一年里，最深刻的一个教训是：我非常庆幸自己认识我的邻居。社区里的不同人都在互相支持。这真的是不幸中的万幸。

Original English

Michael Bradley Cohen: In a disaster, it's probably happening so fast that there aren't uniformed crew members with organized instructions. So, you have to be in charge of your own survival. But he says one big lesson has stood out to him over the last year. He is so grateful that he knows his neighbors. We look out for each other or we're trying because nobody else is going to do it for us.

Gregory Han: 你需要对自己的直属社区进行一定程度的投资，这在灾难中会立即变得非常有价值。不要指望别人为你做准备，你要准备好为别人提供帮助。如果你只是通过打个招呼、与邻居建立联系来投资这种资源，它在生死关头可能会带来巨大的回报。

Original English

Gregory Han: There's a level investment that you need to make within your own immediate community that will become very very valuable almost immediately. I think it's important to prepare to be there for other people, too. So that is a resource you can't buy, but it's something you can invest in by just saying hello and speaking to your neighbors and connecting in a way that may pay off in life situation.

Christine Cyr Clisset: 这是 Gregory 和 Mike 的第一个重要教训：投资你的社区。一个好的第一步是建立一个简单的沟通方式，比如 WhatsApp 群组。下一集，我们将讨论“恢复”：当眼前的灾难结束，你究竟需要考虑和做些什么，才能开始拼凑回你的生活？

Original English

Christine Cyr Clisset: This is Gregory and Mike's first big lesson. Invest in your community. A good first step is to create an easy way to communicate with your neighbors. This could be a text chain or a group chat through something like WhatsApp. The next lesson Gregory and Mike learned is about recovery. What do you actually need to think about and do once the immediate disaster is over in order to begin piecing your life back together?
