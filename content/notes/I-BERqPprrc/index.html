无法律背景、无编程经验：Max Junestrand 如何打造 55 亿美元的 AI 法律平台 Legora

关键字 legal-tech startup-scaling competitive-strategy artificial-intelligence leadership-development

摘要

Legora 联合创始人兼 CEO Max Junestrand 分享了公司从 3 人团队快速扩张至 300 人的历程。他探讨了如何将 Dota 2 的竞技精神融入创业，通过 AI 打造“10倍速律师”。内容涵盖了在顶级律所孵化、通过 YC 进军美国市场、以及在快速扩张中保持人才密度的核心策略。

指数级增长与未来愿景

Max: 在过去的两年里，我们从斯德哥尔摩的 3 名工程师、零客户，成长到现在的 750 多家客户、300 名员工，每个季度的规模都在翻倍以上。

英文原文

Max: Over the past 2 years, we've grown from three engineers in Stockholm, zero clients, and now over 750 clients, 300 people, more than doubled every single quarter.

主持人: 就在你刚刚完成上一轮融资的几个月后，你打算如何利用这笔资金？

英文原文

Interviewer: This is only a few months after you just raised your previous series. What are you going to be doing this using this money for?

Max: 我认为现实情况是，这个市场目前只完成了从 0 到 1 的过程。现在我们正处于从 1 到 10、从 10 到 100 的征程中，还有太多的东西需要构建。所以当我思考我们所处的领域时，我们仍然站在起跑线上。我们不能做快速跟随者，我们必须成为创新者。

我是 Max，Legora 的 CEO 兼联合创始人。Legora 是一个法律工作的发生平台。我们致力于通过 AI 解决法律任务，使命是打造“10倍速律师”（10x lawyers）。

英文原文

Max: I think the reality is that this market has only done 0 to 1. Now we are on the journey of 1 to 10 and 10 to 100 and there's so much left to build. So when I think about our space, we're still on the starting line. We can't be fast followers. We got to be the innovators. Hi, I'm Max. I'm the CEO and co-founder of Lora. Lora is the platform where legal work happens. We solve legal tasks with the help of AI in the mission of creating 10x lawyers.

从 Dota 2 到 CEO：竞技游戏带来的领导力

Max: 对我来说，没有竞争就没有兴奋感。我不会因为处于竞争激烈的环境中而感到压力，反而能从中获得能量。我成长于瑞典的群岛地区，那是一个小社区，竞争不够激烈。所以，我对体育和电子游戏都非常感兴趣。我曾经参加过 Dota 2 的比赛。

经历过那种极具竞争力的环境后，我想把其中的一些特质带到大学里。当年的大量游戏经历教会我：如果你想变得卓越，你就必须“硬磕”（grind）。仅仅投入更多时间是不够的，你必须玩得更聪明，学习得更多。你必须带来比任何人都更高的强度。在 Dota 中，一个小队有五名选手，这五个人必须无缝协作才能共同实现目标。

我认为我的领导风格可能受到早期游戏经历的影响，也就是与那些在很多方面我觉得比我更聪明的人一起工作。我认为 CEO 的核心工作就是能够吸引各领域的优秀人才，他们拥有你所不具备的技能组，然后你得让这个“乐队”和谐演奏。当他们开始协作时，新的可能就会被解锁。我们在产品层面和公司层面都在玩我所说的“无限游戏”。所以今天我们要关注对大家最重要的事情，那就是让你们的团队更轻松、更有效地使用 AI 工作。

英文原文

Max: So I think for me without competition there is no excitement. I don't get stressed being in a competitive environment. I get energy from it. I grew up in Sweden in the Swedish archipelago. It was a small community where I didn't get enough competition. So, I got very interested both in sports and in video games. I used to compete in this video game called Dota 2. Having gone through that very competitive environment, I wanted to take some of that with me to college. Gaming a lot for me back in those days taught me that if you want to be great, you have to grind. It's not enough to just play more hours. You got to play smarter and you got to learn more. You have to bring a level of intensity better than anybody else. In Dota, you're five players on one team. That group of five people have to collaborate seamlessly to reach the right goals together. I think some of my leadership style is probably influenced by some of the early gaming working with individuals who in many aspects I find much smarter than myself. I think that is a a core job of of a CEO to be able to attract people within all different types of fields who bring skill sets that you don't have and then you got to make the band play together and then when they start to work together something new unlocks and we're playing what I like to call the the infinite game both on a product level but also on a company level. So today we're going to focus on what matters most to all of you, which is making it easier and more effective for your teams to work with AI.

在顶级律所孵化：见证“掉下巴”的时刻

Max: 就像我说的，尽管公司已经从零成长为独角兽，但在我看来，我们真的还处于起跑线上。我们完成了 0 到 1，剩下的还有 1 到 10 和 10 到 100 的路要走。法律行业对我们非常有吸引力，原因之一是那里有很多有意义的问题等待解决。

我认为对 Legora 来说，从一开始就与客户共同成长至关重要。我们在瑞典最大的律师事务所 Mannheimer Swartling 进行了孵化。他们非常慷慨，在早期提供了时间和空间，我们甚至在他们的办公室里有间会议室。

当我做产品演示时，我会请用户拿一份他们的旧备忘录，或者他们处理过的案例的一部分，上传到系统中，然后让 Legora 生成一份初稿。当他们看到结果时，那真的是一种“身体后仰、惊掉下巴”的时刻。我认为这种体验会产生动力，并给你带来巨大的能量，让你跑得更快。

英文原文

Max: So when I think about our space, as I said, it really feels like we're still on the starting line despite having grown the company from zero to unicorn. We've done 0 to one and now there's journey of 1 to 10 and then 10 to 100 left to do. So one of the things that made the legal industry very attractive to us was that there were so many problems, meaningful problems to go and solve. I think incredibly important for Luora since the very beginning has been to grow together with our customers. We were incubated in the biggest law firm in Sweden, Mannheimer Swartling. They were generous enough with their early time and space. We actually got conference room in their office. When I was doing a demo, I would ask the users to take, you know, maybe one of their old memos or or part of of a case that they had worked on, upload it into the system, and then ask Legua to do a first draft of it. When they saw the results, I mean, it was kind of a, you know, leaning back, drop the chin moment. I think those types of experiences breeds momentum and gives you a lot of energy to keep moving forward even faster.

进军美国市场：YC 经历与时差挑战

Max: 当时我们有几个不同的选择。我们可以从欧洲的 VC 那里融资，已经拿到了几个投资意向书；或者我们可以接受 Y Combinator 的邀请去旧金山。我们必须参加 YC 吗？我不知道，因为我们在欧洲已经有很多业务在增长了。

但去旧金山吸引我们的一点是，瑞典是一个很小的生态系统，如果我们想在大联盟里打球，就必须去美国。既然要做这件事，就做到最好。我不想在工作中产生自满情绪，我想挑战真正困难的事情。我们希望在美国市场占有一席之地。

在 YC 期间，我们用一个半月左右的时间将 ARR（年度经常性收入）从零做到了近 100 万美元。很大一部分原因是我大部分时间仍花在欧洲业务上。我试图在旧金山处理这些工作，我买了一个补光灯，在凌晨 1 点到上午 10 点之间接听世界另一端的电话。

对于 Legora 来说，出现在客户所在的地方至关重要。无论是为了实地服务客户，还是根据他们的需求调整产品，这都是必选项。所以当我们进入美国市场时，我们真的想确保我们在美国的第一批客户是顶尖的律师和律所，并且在市场上有举足轻重的地位。我们非常幸运地联系到了 Cleary Gottlieb 以及 Goodwin Procter。我认为这两家律所对 AI 想要实现的目标以及 Legora 如何帮助他们实现目标都有高度的共识。达成共识后，就是出发的时刻了。在那之后，我们很快在曼哈顿开设了办公室。

英文原文

Max: We had a couple of different options. We could have raised money from European visas. We had a couple of term sheets. Or we could accept the offer to join Y Combinator and go to San Francisco. Did we have to do YC? I don't know. Because we had so much business already growing in Europe. But what attracted us of going to SF was Sweden is a small ecosystem and so if we want to play in the big leagues, we need to be in the States. If we are going to do this thing, let's be the best at it. I didn't want to bring sort of complacency into my into my job. I want to do things and I want to be the best at it and I want to do really hard things. We wanted a foot in the door towards the the American market. I mean we went from zero to almost a million ARR in like a month and a half during Y Combinator and a big reason for that was because I was spending a majority of my time still in Europe. I tried to make it work from San Francisco. I bought this ring light shine on my face when I took calls between 1:00 a.m. and 10:00 a.m. over the night towards the other side of the world. So I think for LEGO, it's incredibly important to be where our customers are. And so when we think about both serving them on the ground, but also tuning the product to their needs, it's just a mustave. So when we entered the US market, we really wanted to make sure that who we work with in the US as our first set of clients are excellent lawyers, excellent law firms, but also carry a lot of weight in the market and we were very lucky in contact with Cleary Gottlieb and also Goodwin and Proctor. I think with both those two firms there was a lot of alignment in what they want to achieve with AI and how Legora can help them get there. We had alignment and then it was, you know, time to go. After that, we quickly opened an office here in Manhattan.

规模化扩张：招聘标准与 CEO 角色的演变

Max: 早期我们待的地方比现在简陋得多。前 30 家律所和内部法务团队进入平台时的所有入职培训（onboarding）都是我亲自处理的。从那些早期阶段我们学到的是：你只有一次机会。律师们太忙了，你没有第二次机会让他们熟悉产品。所以产品必须奏效，不能崩溃，用例必须行得通并解决问题。

你必须做好预期管理，说明产品今天能做什么，以及一周后、一个季度后、一年后能做什么。他们明白这项技术不仅会留下来，而且将成为未来的工作方式。他们想上这辆车，想让自己的声音被听到，想对未来的样子拥有发言权。这太棒了，我们不会把这视为理所当然。

到目前为止，我最喜欢的成就是阅读我们客户喜爱的 Slack 频道，那里汇集了反馈和使用情况，能看到产品对我们服务的人群产生的真实影响。没有什么比听到客户说我们让他们的一天变得更好，或者帮助了他们的业务更让我感到满足了。比如他们能提前完成工作，回家陪孩子睡觉。我认为专注于为用户提供最大的价值，周围的其他问题似乎都会迎刃而解。

英文原文

Max: We're at a much much scrappier place than what we're in today. I handled I think the first 30 law firms and in-house teams coming onto the platform with all the onboardings. The learnings that we took from those early days is that you only get one shot. Lawyers are so busy that you don't get a second chance onboarding them on the product. So it has to work has to be it can't break and the use cases need to work and it need to solve and you need to do a good job of setting the expectations of what the product can do today versus what the product will be able to do a week from now, a quarter from now, a year from now. They understand that not only is this tech sort of here to stay, it is the way that work will be done. They want to get on the train, they want their voices to be heard and they want to have a say in what that future looks like. And that is awesome. That is not something that we take for granted. My favorite accomplishment so far is frankly just reading through our customer love Slack channel where we collect feedback and aggregated usage of the real impact that the product is having for the people that we serve. There's nothing as rewarding, at least for me, as when people that we serve let us know we've made their day better or we've helped their business in some way. they were able to finish a piece of work and get home to their families to see their kids before they went to bed. I think the focus on let's deliver the most value for our users. Other problems sort of around that seems to solve itself.

Max: 如你所见，我们搬到了纽约的新办公室，我身后还有不少空桌子。我们在各个团队都在积极招人，我想在今年夏天之前几乎就能坐满。团队现在大约有 300 人，分布在丹佛、纽约、伦敦、斯德哥尔摩、印度和悉尼的办公室。

对我来说，将公司从 3 人带到 300 人最难的部分是，你的角色变化是最快的。你不能执着于过去做事的方式或你正在做的事情。你必须每天醒来思考：我今天如何为业务做最优决策？以及一个月、一个季度、一年后的决策。你必须做自己的好老板，必须保持自律，不要把精力花在容易的事情上，而是去处理那些真正困难的事情。我认为作为创始人兼 CEO，保持公司的专注是你的责任。我们不能做快速跟随者，我们必须成为创新者。

更重要的是，如何规模化并继续保持文化？我对此有一些想法。我仍然非常积极地参与招聘，每个入职的人我都会面试。我在每个雇员身上寻找的一点是：这个人的成长潜力有多大？我会请他们讲述职业生涯中处理过的最难的问题。那个问题是什么？你直接贡献了什么解决方案？如果今天让你重新处理那个问题，你会做得有什么不同？因为我认为从第一次解决问题到第二次解决问题的学习能力极其重要，这是你需要具备的最核心技能。此外，我总是寻找一种野心。我们寻找那些想对公司的结果和我们在市场上交付的价值负责的人。这种价值观可能是帮助我们走到今天的最重要因素。

英文原文

Max: Well, so as you can see, we're we're in our new space here in New York and there's quite a few empty desks here behind me. We're growing super aggressively across, you know, all different teams. I think we're gonna have filled almost all of this before summer this year. The team is about 300 people today spread across several offices in Denver, New York, London, Stockholm, India, Sydney. So, I think the hardest part for me growing the company from 3 to 300 was that your role probably changes the fastest. You cannot be married to the way that things were done or to what you're doing. You just got to wake up every day and think about how do I optimize for the business today? and maybe a month, a quarter, a year out. And those are the decisions you have to take. You need to be your own best boss. And you have to be very disciplined about not working on the easy things, but working on the really hard things. And I think it's your responsibility as the founder and as the CEO to keep the company focused. We can't be fast followers. We got to be the innovators. Most importantly, it's how do you scale and continue to keep the culture? You know, I have some ideas around that. I I take a very active part in in our recruiting. Still interview everyone who joins the company. One of the things that I look for in in everyone that we hire and I think how much capacity does this person have to grow? Tell me about the hardest problem that you worked on. What was that problem? What did you directly contribute to solving that problem? And and how would you do it differently if you had to go back to that problem today? Because I think the ability to learn from the way that you approach a problem the first time to the time that you have to approach a problem the second time is super important. The most important skill that you need to bring to the table. And then I always look for a sense of ambition. We're looking for people who want to take responsibility of the outcome of the company. The outcome that we deliver in the market. That value has probably been the most important part of of helping us go from where we were to where we are today.

给创业者的建议：保持对问题的激情

Max: 如果要给刚开始创业的自己一些建议，那就是：你真的需要对你正在做的事情感到兴奋。因为如果进展顺利，你会工作得非常辛苦，并在一段极高强度的时期内全神贯注于这些问题。我每天醒来都在思考如何交付更多价值，如何让 Legora 成为一家更成功的企业。

如果我们选择的是一些我们并不那么兴奋的事情，那会困难得多。我迫不及待想在 2027 年第一季度开全员大会，看看那时的规模和成就。因为如果在 2025 年初告诉我，到 2026 年第一季度我们会坐在这里，我绝对不会相信。所以，有很多值得期待的事情。

英文原文

Max: If I were to give some advice to myself starting out again, you need to really be excited about what you're doing because if it goes well, you're going to be working so hard and you're going to be focused on these problems for such an intense period. I wake up every day and I'm thinking about how do we deliver more value and how do we make Legora a more successful business. I think had we picked, you know, maybe something we weren't as excited about, that would have been much harder. you know, I I can't wait have a town hall in Q1 of 2027, see the size and and see what we've accomplished by then because if you would have told me that we would, you know, be sitting here Q1 of 2026 back at the start of 2025, I would have never believed you. So, yeah, I think there's uh there's a lot to be excited about.
