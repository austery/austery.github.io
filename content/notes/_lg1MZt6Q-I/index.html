《纽约时报》播客《The Daily》深度剖析近期引发广泛关注的美债市场动荡。首席经济记者本·卡塞尔曼详解债券机制、10年期美债收益率飙升的多重成因（强劲增长预期、通胀反弹担忧、财政赤字与债务供给压力），以及高利率对房贷、企业融资与日常生活的深远影响。

全球最重要的金融市场

Ben Casselman: 但从很多维度来看，债券市场其实比股票市场（stock market）还要重要得多。甚至可以说，它是地球上最重要的金融市场。无论你是否意识到，你的财务生活都与债券市场紧密绑定在一起。

Original English

Ben Casselman: But in many ways, it is more important than the stock market. It's arguably the most important market on Earth. And your financial life is tied to the bond market whether or not you know it.

Rachel Abrams: 即使人们根本没有意识到这一点。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Even if you don't even realize it.

Ben Casselman: 没错，哪怕你从未主动决定去投资债券，它也很可能存在于你的生活中。首先，在你的退休投资组合中很可能就有债券——如果你有 401(k) 或 IRA 个人退休账户，里面大概率配置了一定比例的债券，所以你在退休金账户里的财富状况直接受债券影响。

但更广泛地说，整个经济体系的借贷成本，也就是利率（interest rates）——无论是你的房贷利率（mortgage rate）、汽车贷款利率，还是学生贷款、信用卡利率，甚至大公司的借贷成本——归根结底都由债券市场决定。因此，如果你想要借钱，或者你工作的公司需要借钱来维持运营、发放薪资，债券市场都在扮演着核心角色。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Even if you don't even realize it. Even if you never made a decision to go invest in bonds, it's probably somewhere in your life. For one thing, because you've probably got some bonds somewhere in your retirement portfolio. If you've got a 401K, if you've got an IRA, chances are there are some bonds in there, so your wealth is tied up in it. But more broadly, interest rates, borrowing costs in our economy—your mortgage rate, your car loan, your student loan, credit cards, what big companies pay to borrow money—all of that is set by the bond market. So, if you want to borrow, or if the company you work for wants to borrow money to keep paying you, the bond market is at the center of it.

Rachel Abrams: 你将为我们揭开这层迷雾。但在深入细节之前，我相信许多听众已经了解什么是股票了。为了让大家能以此为参照，能否请你先对比一下股票（stock）与债券（bond）的根本区别？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: You are going to make that less opaque for us. But before we do, I'm sure a lot of our listeners know what a stock is. So, to start us off on familiar ground, can you explain the difference between a stock and a bond?

Ben Casselman: 没问题。当你买入一家公司的股票时，你实际上是在购买这家公司的一小部分所有权（ownership）。如果公司经营得好、利润丰厚，股价就会上涨，你也跟着赚钱；反之，如果公司破产倒闭，你的股票就可能变得一文不值。所以股票投资具有很高的风险，但潜在回报也很大。

而债券则完全不同，债券本质上是一张借据（IOU / loan）。借钱的主体向你承诺：“你借给我 100 美元，我承诺每年支付你一定数额的利息，并在约定的期限（比如 10 年后）把这 100 美元本金全额还给你。”

Original English

Ben Casselman: Yeah. So, when you buy a stock, you're buying a little piece of ownership in a company. And if that company does great, you do great. If the company goes bust, your stock can be worth nothing. It's high risk, high reward. A bond is a loan, it's an IOU. Somebody is saying, "Lend me $100, and I will pay you some amount of interest every year, and at the end of some period of time, 10 years or whatever, I will give you back your $100."

Rachel Abrams: 这就是为什么债券通常被视为比股票更安全的投资工具。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Which is why bonds are generally seen as a safer bet than stocks.

Ben Casselman: 是的，在绝大多数情况下，债券确实比股票稳健得多。因为只要借款人不违约，你就确切地知道自己能拿回多少利息和本金。借款人可以是苹果这样的大公司，也可以是纽约州或加州等地方政府发行市政债券来修建桥梁或学校。

但当我们在新闻里听到关于“债券市场大动荡”的报道时，通常所指的并不是企业债或市政债，而是美国国债（U.S. Treasury bonds）——也就是由美国联邦政府（U.S. Federal Government）发行的借据。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Exactly. Most of the time, yes, because you know what you're going to get. And that borrower could be Apple, it could be the state of New York borrowing money to build a bridge or a school. But when we talk about the bond market in the news, what we're usually talking about is the U.S. government, U.S. Treasuries—loans to the United States federal government.

万亿美元流转的国债市场

Rachel Abrams: 原来如此，我们所说的核心其实是美国政府的借条。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Okay, so these are government IOUs.

Ben Casselman: 没错。每天在市场上被买入和卖出、完成换手的这类政府借据总额大约高达 1 万亿美元。

Original English

Ben Casselman: That's right. And about a trillion dollars worth of those IOUs change hands, get bought and sold in the market every single day.

Rachel Abrams: 每天 1 万亿美元！

Original English

Rachel Abrams: A trillion dollars every single day.

Ben Casselman: 是的，日交易量就达到 1 万亿美元。这是世界上规模最大、流动性最强的金融资产池。其中最受全市场瞩目的焦点，就是10年期美国国债（10-year Treasury bond）。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Every single day. It is the biggest pool of money anywhere in the world. And the absolute center of that universe is the 10-year Treasury bond.

Rachel Abrams: 这正是我频繁在财经头条中看到的名词——10年期美债。为什么大家如此痴迷于这只特定期限的债券？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: That is the thing that I have seen in headlines over and over again, the 10-year Treasury. Why is everyone so obsessed with this particular bond?

Ben Casselman: 因为 10 年是一个非常关键的折中期限。美国政府发行的借据期限范围很广，短的有 1 个月、3 个月，长的有 20 年、30 年。但 10 年期正好处于中间位置，它既不是极短期的资金拆借，也不是几十年后的遥远未来。

因此，10年期美债成为了全球金融体系的基准利率（benchmark）。无论是银行发放 30 年期固定房贷，还是大企业发行长期公司债来投资设厂，它们在设定借款利率时，都是在 10 年期国债收益率的基础之上加上一定的风险溢价。10年期美债的收益率直接决定了全社会的融资基调。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Because 10 years is this great middle ground. The government borrows for as short as a month, and as long as 30 years. But 10 years is kind of in the middle. And so it has become the benchmark. It is the measuring stick that everything else gets measured against. If you want to take out a mortgage, the bank looks at the 10-year Treasury and adds a little bit to it. If a company wants to borrow money, they look at the 10-year Treasury and add a little bit to it.

Rachel Abrams: 原来它是整个金融世界的测量标尺。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: It's the measuring stick.

Ben Casselman: 没错。而近期发生的核心事件就是：10年期美债的收益率（yield）一直在持续攀升。

Original English

Ben Casselman: That's right. And the yield on that 10-year Treasury has been going up.

价格与收益率的跷跷板

Rachel Abrams: “收益率”（yield）这个词在新闻里无处不在。你能用最直白的语言解释一下什么是收益率吗？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: And yield is another word that I see everywhere in headlines. What does yield mean in plain English?

Ben Casselman: 简单来说，收益率就是投资者持有该债券所能获得的有效年化投资回报率。

但这里有一个极其重要且反直觉的机制：债券的价格（price）和收益率（yield）是反向变动的。它们就像跷跷板的两端——当债券价格下跌时，收益率就会上升；当债券价格上涨时，收益率就会下降。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Yeah. So, yield is just the return that you get on your investment. But the crucial thing to understand about bonds is that price and yield move in opposite directions. They're on a seesaw. When prices go down, yields go up. When prices go up, yields go down.

Rachel Abrams: 为什么会反向变动？这听起来有点让人绕不过弯来。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Why do they move in opposite directions?

Ben Casselman: 我们可以用一个具体例子来说明。假设政府发行了一张面值 100 美元的债券，承诺每年支付 5 美元的利息。如果你以 100 美元买下它，你每年的收益率正好是 5%（5/100）。

但是，如果市场上大家都想抛售债券，没有人愿意出 100 美元，你可能只需要花 90 美元就能在二手市场上买到这张债券。然而，政府每年承诺支付的利息金额依然固定是 5 美元，到期时归还的本金也依然是 100 美元！因为你实际只付出了 90 美元的成本，每年却能拿到 5 美元利息，再加上到期还能多赚 10 美元的本金差价，你的实际回报率（收益率）就大幅上升到了 6% 甚至 7% 以上。反过来，如果大家都抢着买，把价格推高到 110 美元，你的收益率就会被压缩得非常低。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Think of it this way. If I have a bond that pays $5 a year, and I bought it for $100, my return is 5%. But if nobody wants that bond and I have to sell it to you for $90, you're still getting that $5 every year, but you only paid $90 for it. So your return, your yield, is higher. You got a discount on the price, which makes your return better.

Rachel Abrams: 我明白了！所以当新闻里说“10年期国债收益率正在飙升”时，实际上意味着投资者在大量抛售美债，导致债券价格下跌，进而推高了收益率？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Ah, okay. So when we hear that yields are going up, that means prices are going down. People are selling bonds.

Ben Casselman: 完全正确！

Original English

Ben Casselman: Exactly right.

Rachel Abrams: 那么，为什么现在大家都在抛售美国国债，导致收益率一路冲高？是不是有什么简单的一句话解释？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: So why are yields going up? Why are people selling bonds? Is there a nice simple one-sentence explanation?

Ben Casselman: 要是真有那么简单就好了。我很希望能给你一个像儿童科普动画《Schoolhouse Rock》那样简单的单句答案，但这次的情况远比那要复杂得多，需要我们一层一层拆解。

Original English

Ben Casselman: If only. I would love to give you the simple one-sentence Schoolhouse Rock version. This is going to take more than a Schoolhouse Rock.

美联储与加息周期的前奏

Rachel Abrams: 那我们就循序渐进地来剖析。第一步是什么？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Let's take it step by step. What's step one?

Ben Casselman: 最基础的第一步，也是这一切的源头，就是美联储（Federal Reserve）。

过去一年半以来，为了对抗 40 年未见的高通胀，美联储以几十年来最快的速度持续大幅提高基准短期利率（benchmark interest rates）。

Original English

Ben Casselman: So, step one, at the barest minimum, is the Federal Reserve. Over the last year and a half, the Fed has been raising interest rates at the fastest pace in decades in order to fight inflation.

Rachel Abrams: 当美联储提高短期利率时，会如何传导到债券市场？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: And when the Fed raises interest rates, how does that affect the bond market?

Ben Casselman: 当美联储把短期利率从接近 0% 一路推高到超过 5% 时，新发行的短期理财、银行存单或者短期国债都能提供高达 5% 的利息。

相比之下，过去那些在低利率时期发行的、每年只付 1% 或 2% 利息的旧债券，瞬间就失去了吸引力。谁愿意拿着每年只给 1.5% 利息的旧国债呢？于是投资者纷纷抛售旧债券，换取更高收益的新资产。这种抛售直接压低了旧债券的价格，促使整个市场的债券收益率整体上扬，向美联储的高利率看齐。

Original English

Ben Casselman: If the Fed is paying you 5% to keep your money in a super safe short-term account, you're not going to be happy holding an old bond that only pays you 1.5% or 2%. You're going to sell that old bond to go get the new higher interest rate. And when everybody sells those old bonds, the prices drop, and the yields go up to match what the Fed is doing.

Rachel Abrams: 这听起来很顺理成章：美联储加息，债券收益率随之上升。但为什么最近几个月大家格外焦虑？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: That makes total sense: the Fed raises rates, bond yields go up. But why has there been so much panic recently?

Ben Casselman: 关键在于：美联储目前的加息周期实际上已经接近尾声了。市场普遍预期美联储可能只会再加息一次，甚至可能已经结束加息了。

然而，令人意想不到的是，就在美联储放慢加息步伐的最近一两个月里，长期国债收益率不但没有企稳，反而在以更迅猛的势头向外狂飙。这说明市场背后正在发生美联储加息之外的、更深层次的剧烈变化。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Because the Fed is mostly done raising rates. Most people think the Fed is either at the end or very close to the end of its rate-hiking campaign. But over the last month or two, yields on long-term bonds have shot up even faster. Something else is driving this beyond just the immediate Fed rate hikes.

收益率飙升的三大推手

Rachel Abrams: 那么这个“更深层的原因”到底是什么？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: So what is that something else?

Ben Casselman: 经济学家和金融市场目前主要将其归结为三大推手，每种推手代表了对经济走向的一种截然不同的解读视角。

第一种是乐观的解释：美国经济展现出了惊人的韧性与强劲活力。

在过去一两年里，几乎所有华尔街专家都在预测美国经济即将在 2023 年陷入严重衰退。然而现实情况是，美国的就业市场依然极为火爆，失业率维持在历史低位，消费者依然在持续消费，企业投资依然稳健。经济不但没有崩溃，反而展现出可能长期维持强劲增长的态势。如果经济长期处于扩张轨道，那么未来的市场基准利率自然会维持在更高水平，投资者就不会甘于接受低回报的长期债券。

Original English

Ben Casselman: There are three main theories, and they reflect very different views of the economy. The first theory, which is the optimistic one, is that the U.S. economy is just way stronger than anybody expected. For the last year or two, every economist was predicting a recession. But instead, hiring has stayed super strong, consumers keep spending, businesses are investing. The economy is booming. And in a booming economy, you expect interest rates to stay higher for longer because there are so many good investment opportunities.

Rachel Abrams: 这听起来确实是个好消息！但第二种推手是什么？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: That sounds like good news! What's the second theory?

Ben Casselman: 第二种是相对悲观的担忧：通胀可能会死灰复燃，美联储将被迫长期维持超高利率。

虽然过去一年整体通胀率有所回落，但近期由于国际油价飙升、劳动力成本坚挺，部分通胀数据再次抬头。如果通胀顽固停留在 3% 到 4%，无法回落到美联储 2% 的目标，美联储就不得不在未来数年内把利率维持在 5% 甚至更高的高位（即所谓的 Higher for Longer）。

对于买入 10 年期债券的投资者来说，通胀是债券收益最大的敌人。如果未来 10 年每年的通胀率是 4%，而你的债券每年只付 3% 利息，你实际上是在持续亏钱。因此投资者要求更高的收益率作为补偿，否则绝不接盘。

Original English

Ben Casselman: The second theory is the worrisome one: inflation is not truly dead, and the Fed is going to have to keep interest rates high for a very long time. We saw recent data showing inflation picking back up, largely driven by oil and energy prices. If inflation stays stuck at 3% or 4%, the Fed can't cut rates. And inflation is poison for bond investors. If you lock your money up for 10 years at 3% interest, but inflation is 4%, you're losing purchasing power every single year. So investors demand higher yields to protect against that inflation risk.

Rachel Abrams: 那么第三种推手呢？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: And what's the third theory?

Ben Casselman: 第三种推手则是关于供求关系的纯粹现实：美国政府正在发行海量的国债，但买家正在减少。

由于庞大的联邦财政赤字（budget deficit），美国财政部为了筹集资金弥补缺口，正在以前所未有的规模向市场抛售数万亿美元的新国债。然而与此同时，以往的大买家——比如美联储自身正在缩表不再购买国债，海外央行也在放缓购买步伐。

当供给天量增加而需求跟不上时，根据基本的经济学规律，债券价格必然下跌，从而迫使收益率飙升，只有提供更高的利息才能吸引买家把这些天量借据买下来。

Original English

Ben Casselman: The third theory is simple supply and demand: the U.S. government is borrowing an astronomical amount of money, and there aren't enough buyers. The federal deficit is huge. The Treasury is issuing trillions of dollars in new debt to pay for government spending. At the same time, major buyers like the Federal Reserve are stepping back, and foreign buyers are slowing down. When you have a massive flood of new bonds hitting the market and not enough buyers, the price has to fall, and yields have to go up to entice investors to buy all that debt.

从宏观市场到个人钱包的传导

Rachel Abrams: 这三大因素叠加在一起，形成了我们今天看到的局面。那么这对于坐在家里的普通人、普通家庭来说，究竟意味着什么？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: When all three of those things come together, what does that actually mean for the ordinary person sitting at home?

Ben Casselman: 这对普通人的生活会产生立竿见影且极其沉重的影响，最典型的体现就在房地产市场。

因为 30 年期固定房贷利率直接锚定 10 年期美债收益率，随着美债收益率突破逼近 5%，目前的房贷利率已经飙升到了近 8%——这是 23 年以来的最高水平！

想象一下，两年前房贷利率只有 3% 时，每月还款额可能只需要 1500 美元；但在 8% 的利率下，同样金额的贷款，每个月的还款额可能直接暴涨到 2800 甚至 3000 美元以上。这直接将成千上万原本打算买房的年轻家庭彻底挡在了购房大门之外。

Original English

Ben Casselman: It has immediate, painful consequences, and nowhere is that clearer than the housing market. Mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury yield very closely. As Treasury yields have surged toward 5%, 30-year fixed mortgage rates have shot up to near 8%—the highest in 23 years. A couple of years ago, when mortgage rates were 3%, a monthly payment on a median-priced home might have been $1,500. At 8%, that same house with the same loan costs $2,800 or $3,000 a month in mortgage payments. That crushes affordability for regular people.

Rachel Abrams: 这确实让买房变得几乎难以承受。不仅如此，现有的房主也不愿意卖房，因为他们一旦卖掉房子，就不得不放弃自己手里持有的 3% 超低房贷利率，去接受 8% 的新利率，结果导致市面上的二手房库存几乎完全被冻结了。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: It locks everyone out. Existing homeowners don't want to sell because they don't want to give up their 3% mortgage to take on an 8% mortgage, which freezes the whole market.

Ben Casselman: 没错，这就是所谓的“金手铐”效应（lock-in effect）。

不仅是房贷，高收益率还意味着信用卡欠款利率、汽车贷款利率、小企业经营贷款利率全线大幅攀升。对于背负债务的企业来说，再融资成本大幅增加，许多本来打算招人或扩产的企业不得不削减开支甚至裁员。最终，高利率会像减速机一样，逐步让整个经济活动冷却下来。

Original English

Ben Casselman: Exactly, the lock-in effect. And it extends beyond housing: credit card debt is more expensive, auto loans are more expensive, small business loans are more expensive. Companies that need to roll over existing debt now have to pay much higher interest, leaving less money for hiring and expansion. Over time, that slows the entire economy down.

低利率时代的终结与新常态

Rachel Abrams: Ben，回顾过去十几年，从 2008 年金融危机到新冠疫情期间，我们似乎习惯了接近于零的超低利率时代。我们现在看到的是短暂的周期波动，还是整个时代的历史性转折？

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Ben, looking back over the last decade and a half—from the 2008 financial crisis through the pandemic—we grew used to essentially near-zero interest rates. Are we looking at a temporary blip, or is this a fundamental turning point?

Ben Casselman: 越来越多的经济学家和市场参与者开始意识到：过去 15 年的“超低利率时代”可能才是历史上反常的特例，而现在我们正在回归历史常态。

如果你把视野拉长到 20 世纪 70 年代、80 年代甚至 90 年代，5% 到 7% 的国债收益率和正常的利率水平才是历史惯例。那个由全球化红利、科技通缩和危机时期极度宽松货币政策造就的“免费资金时代”（era of free money）可能已经彻底落幕了。

Original English

Ben Casselman: More and more people are realizing that the post-2008 era of rock-bottom, near-zero interest rates was the historical anomaly, not the norm. If you look back to the 1970s, 80s, and 90s, 5% yields and higher interest rates were completely standard. The era of free money—fueled by hyper-globalization and emergency post-crisis policies—is likely over, and we are settling into a new normal.

Rachel Abrams: 这意味着无论是政府、企业还是个人，都必须重新学会如何在资金有成本的真实世界中生存和规划财务。

Original English

Rachel Abrams: Which means governments, businesses, and regular households all have to adjust to a world where money actually costs something to borrow.

Ben Casselman: 确实如此。借钱不再是免费的，每一笔开支、每一笔赤字和每一笔投资都必须接受更高成本的严格审视。

Original English

Ben Casselman: That's exactly right. Borrowing isn't free anymore, and every deficit, every budget, and every investment has to be weighed against real costs.
