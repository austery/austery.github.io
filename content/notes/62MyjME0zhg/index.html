摘要:

本期《纽约时报》书评节目包含两场重磅深度访谈：资深书评人德怀特·加纳详述为何给予马龙·詹姆斯新作《消失者》职业生涯罕见的极高赞誉；畅销作家约翰·格林则深度分享了从青少年文学跨界成人小说创作、应对成名焦虑以及重构自我价值的心路历程。

节目开场与访谈导视

MJ·富兰克林: 我是 MJ·富兰克林（MJ Franklin），这里是《纽约时报》（The New York Times）书评播客。今天我们为大家准备了两场精彩访谈。我最近采访了畅销书作家约翰·格林（John Green）。他以青少年小说闻名，但如今推出了自己的首部成人虚构小说。我非常期待能与大家分享我们这场广泛深入的对话，内容涉及写作、成名以及年轻时对他产生深刻影响的书籍。稍后就会播出。

首先，德怀特·加纳（Dwight Garner）是《纽约时报》的资深专职书评人。最近，他为马龙·詹姆斯（Marlon James）的新小说《消失者》（The Disappears）撰写了书评。剧透一下：他非常喜欢这部作品。他甚至评价道：“这是一部杰作，是近十年来、甚至可能是本世纪迄今为止最优秀的小说。”对于一位阅历深厚的评论家来说，这无疑是极高的赞誉。因此今天，我想把德怀特请到演播室，带大家一窥幕后。我想和他聊聊这是一本怎样的书、它为何如此出色，以及更广泛地探讨一下在当今时代撰写书评是怎样的一种体验。

MJ Franklin: I'm MJ Franklin and this is the book review from the New York Times. Today we have two interviews for you. I recently sat down with the bestselling author John Green. He's known for his young adult novels, but now he is out with his first work of fiction for adults. I'm excited to share our wide-ranging conversation about writing fame and the books that made an impression on him when he was young. That's coming up in a bit. First, Dwight Garner is a longtime staff book critic at the New York Times. Recently, he reviewed Marlon James' new novel, The Disappears, and spoiler alert, he loved it. So, today, I wanted to bring Dwight into the studio and peak behind the curtain a little bit. I wanted to chat with him about what this book is, what makes it so good, and more broadly, what's it like to write criticism today.

世纪之作的赞誉与书评的影响力

Original English

New York magazine has this great books newsletter called Book Gossip which pointed out that this review seems to have concretely moved the needle in terms of book sales and cultural impact in ways that few reviews do anymore. So, it's superlative in that way. You say the book is a banger, but so is this review. How are you feeling?

Original English

Dwight Garner: Well, thank you MJ. I appreciate that. You know, it's the kind of review that a critic, at least me, only gets to write once in a career. I mean, I've never written a review like that. But then again, I haven't been able to review a book like this, you know, and so you have all these pent up feelings that sort of emerged. I mean, it's a long book and I went back and reread the previous book. So, you know, that's about 1,200 pages right there. And um this is just uh this is one of the rare reading experiences of my life. And I just felt like I had to bring, you know, both guns to bear on it and not just review it, but sort of review my feelings about it.

Original English

MJ Franklin: Yeah. You said you only get to write one of these types of reviews in your career. What do you mean by that? Is that about the audience's tolerance for that kind of unadulterated praise or just the scarcity of work that moves you in that way?

德怀特·加纳: 嗯，我想一个人也许能写出两三次吧。保琳·凯尔（Pauline Kael）可能就写过好几篇。但我只是觉得，作为一名评论家，你在日常工作中大部分时间都在进行微调——给这部作品评个B-加，给那部评个B加，或者给一本令人失望的大家之作评个C。你是在进行细致的打磨和微调。而当你突然遇到一部让你彻底震撼、让你觉得“天哪，这完全改变了我对当代小说可能性的认知”的作品时，你就会像按捺不住一样，不仅要吹响号角，还要击响锣鼓。

You're sort of shaving and calibrating. And to get a book that just blows you off the sofa and you just think, holy moly, this changes the whole way I'm thinking about contemporary fiction, you just can't, you know, you can't just blow the horn. You've got to bang the gong.

解读马龙·詹姆斯与《消失者》

MJ·富兰克林: 你能给我们做个核心概述吗？《消失者》讲了什么？这本书是关于什么的？这位作家又是谁？

Original English

MJ Franklin: Can you give us a topline? What is the disappearance? What is this book about? And who is this author?

德怀特·加纳: 好的。马龙·詹姆斯是一位现居美国的牙买加作家。他写过四五部小说，其中最著名的是荣获布克奖的《七杀简史》（A Brief History of Seven Killings）。那是一部围绕针对鲍勃·马利（Bob Marley）的暗杀未遂事件展开的、充满迷幻色彩的犯罪小说史诗，非常杰出。随后他开启了一个三部曲系列，被称为“非洲版《权力的游戏》”或“非洲版《指环王》”，分别是《黑豹，红狼》（Black Leopard, Red Wolf）和《月光女巫，蜘蛛王》（Moon Witch, Spider King）。我虽然读了，但并没有完全被吸引，对我来说有点过于类型化和冗长。

但这部新书《消失者》，虽然被标记为他“暗星三部曲”中的第三部，实际上完全是一个独立的作品。它是一部发生在当代加勒比海虚构小岛上的警匪犯罪小说。主角是一名被派去调查一系列失踪案的私家侦探，这些失踪者包括白人游客、同性恋男子、穷人以及无权无势的边缘群体，当地警方根本漠不关心。整本书是一场纯粹、充满诗意且极度残酷的黑色电影式冒险，语言极其华丽。

Original English

Dwight Garner: Yeah. Well, Marlon James is a Jamaican writer who now lives in America. He's written four or five novels, most famously A Brief History of Seven Killings, which won the Booker Prize. And that was a sort of psychedelic crime novel epic about the attempted assassination of Bob Marley. It was fantastic. And then he did a couple of fantasy novels in a trilogy, which were sort of billed as African Game of Thrones or African Lord of the Rings: Black Leopard, Red Wolf, and Moon Witch, Spider King. I read those. I wasn't quite as taken by them. It was a little bit too genre for me and a little sprawling. But this new book, The Disappears, while it's sort of billed as the third book in the Dark Star trilogy, it really stands completely alone. It is a contemporary crime novel set on a fictional Caribbean island, and it's about a private eye who's hired to investigate these disappearances, disappearances of white tourists, disappearances of gay men, disappearances of poor people, people the police don't care about. And it is just a pure poetic, hard-boiled noir adventure with unbelievable language.

MJ·富兰克林: 那么，为什么这样一篇书评会引起如此巨大的反响？现在很多业内人士都在讨论你的评论所带来的轰动效应。

Original English

MJ Franklin: That's a good question. You know, um, you know, his last novel or his LA I mean the Seven Killings was big, but why did this review in particular create such a splash?

德怀特·加纳: 这是一个好问题。老实说，这种反响让我觉得有点不可思议，甚至有点怪异。

Original English

Dwight Garner: That's a good question. You know, um, you know, his last novel or his LA I mean the Seven Killings was big, but it's weird.

MJ·富兰克林: 怪异？怎么说？

Original English

MJ Franklin: Weird. How so?

德怀特·加纳: 我也不知道。对我而言，这只是一篇书评而已。但看到人们对它的反应——人们在推特、Instagram上转发，在书店里讨论——我意识到，在当今这个充斥着平庸算法推荐和温吞宣传的时代，读者其实极其渴望一种毫无保留、发自肺腑的真正批评热情。当一个你信任的评论家站出来说“你必须停下手中的一切去读这本书”时，那种力量依然是不可替代的。

Original English

Dwight Garner: I don't know. Just this this sense of you know to me it's just it's it's a book review, you know, but to see people react to it on Twitter, on Instagram, talking about it in bookstores—I realized that today, in an era of bland algorithmic recommendations and lukewarm PR, readers are hungry for genuine, uninhibited critical passion. When a critic you trust comes along and says "drop everything and read this," that power still exists.

当代文学批评的困境与信念

MJ·富兰克林: 这引出了一个更大的话题：在当下的文化环境中撰写书评究竟是怎样的体验？如今严肃的书评阵地越来越少，很多地方的书评都变成了简单的剧情介绍或公关通稿。你如何看待书评人的角色？

Original English

MJ Franklin: That brings us to a larger question: what is it like to write criticism today? There are fewer and fewer venues for serious book reviewing, and much of what passes for reviews has become plot summary or PR fluff. How do you see the role of the book critic right now?

德怀特·加纳: 批评是一门孤独的技艺。现在的文化氛围往往倾向于“盲目积极”，好像如果你不喜欢一本书，你就是在攻击作者本人一样。但批评家的职责不是去当啦啦队长，也不是去当杀手。我们的职责是作为忠实而敏锐的第一批读者，带着你所有的智识、阅读经验、审美偏见和诚实去面对文本。

你需要有一套自己的标尺。当你遇到坏作品时，要敢于指出它的平庸；而当你遇到真正伟大的杰作时，你也必须拥有识别它的审美勇气，并且不吝赞美。你不能害怕使用强烈的词汇。如果一本书配得上被称为杰作，你就应该大声说出来。

Original English

Dwight Garner: Criticism is a lonely craft. The culture today tends toward unearned positivity, as if disliking a book is an attack on the writer as a human being. But the critic's job isn't to be a cheerleader, nor is it to be an assassin. Our job is to be a faithful, discriminating first reader, bringing all your intellect, reading history, aesthetic prejudices, and honesty to the text. You have to have a standard. When you see bad work, you call out the mediocrity. But when you see real greatness, you have to have the aesthetic courage to recognize it and not hold back your praise. You can't be afraid of big words. If a book is a masterpiece, say so.

MJ·富兰克林: 德怀特，非常感谢你能来和我们分享，这太棒了。

Original English

MJ Franklin: Dwight, thank you so much. This has been great.

德怀特·加纳: 谢谢，MJ，非常愉快。

Original English

Dwight Garner: Thanks, MJ. It was fun.

对话约翰·格林：转向成人虚构小说

MJ·富兰克林: 接下来是与畅销书作家约翰·格林的对话。约翰·格林以其极具轰动效应的青年小说而闻名，包括《无比美妙的痛苦》（The Fault in Our Stars，又译《星运里的错》）和《寻找阿拉斯加》（Looking for Alaska）。距离约翰上一次写虚构小说已经过去了大约十年。在这期间，他出版了备受好评的非虚构散文集《人类纪评鉴》（The Anthropocene Reviewed），并涉足了许多其他项目。值得注意的是，这是约翰第一部面向成年读者的虚构小说。约翰，非常感谢你能来。

Original English

MJ Franklin: Coming up next is my conversation with bestselling author John Green. John Green is known for his incredibly popular young adult novels, including The Fault in Our Stars and Looking for Alaska. It's been about 10 years since John has written fiction. In the past few years, he published a critically acclaimed collection of essays, The Anthropocene Reviewed, and pursued many other projects. And notably, it's John's first book for grown-ups. John, thank you for being here.

约翰·格林: 噢，太感谢你能邀请我了，MJ。能来这里真的很荣幸。

Original English

John Green: Oh, thank you so much for having me, MJ. It's really an honor to be here.

MJ·富兰克林: 我想先从一个读者感受说起。我和许多人一样，从小是读着你的书长大的。你的作品已经成为整整一代人青春记忆的一部分。这次读到你写给成年人的新小说，感觉既熟悉又有一种截然不同的成熟与深沉。是什么让你决定在这个时候写一部成人小说？

Original English

MJ Franklin: I want to start with a reader perspective. Like so many people, I grew up reading your books; they've become a part of the fabric of growing up for an entire generation. Reading this new novel for adults felt both familiar and distinctly more mature and profound. What made you decide that now was the time to write a book for adults?

约翰·格林: 我也希望如此，MJ。[笑] 我很长时间没有写虚构作品了，部分原因是我在写完上一本小说后，感觉自己陷入了一种创作枯竭。而且，当《星运里的错》获得那么巨大的成功之后，那种关注度其实给我带来了很大的心理负担。我发现自己很难再回到那种纯粹、安静的写作状态中。

当我开始构思这个新故事时，我意识到主角们所面临的困境——那种中年时期的迷茫、与慢性疾病的共处、对失去的恐惧，以及对自身有限性的认知——这些主题不再适合装进一个青少年的框架里。它们需要一个成年人的视角来承载。我想探讨的是当青春的狂热褪去之后，生活依然在继续时，我们该如何寻找意义。

Original English

John Green: I hope so too, MJ. [laughter] I hadn't written fiction for a long time, partly because after my last novel, I felt creative exhaustion. Also, after the massive success of The Fault in Our Stars, that level of attention was psychologically overwhelming. I found it very difficult to return to a quiet, pure state of writing. When I began conceiving this story, I realized the dilemmas the characters face—middle-age disorientation, living with chronic illness, the terror of loss, and the recognition of our own finitude—could no longer be shoehorned into a YA framework. They demanded an adult lens. I wanted to explore how we find meaning when the frenzy of youth fades and ordinary life continues.

成名、焦虑与重新审视自我

MJ·富兰克林: 谈到成名，你曾公开谈论过成名对你的心理健康造成的影响。在《星运里的错》爆火之后，你成为了一个文化符号，拥有数以百万计的追随者。那种关注度对你来说究竟意味着什么？

Original English

MJ Franklin: Speaking of fame, you've spoken openly about the toll fame took on your mental health. After The Fault in Our Stars exploded, you became a cultural phenomenon with millions of followers. What was that level of scrutiny really like for you?

约翰·格林: 我不知道你怎么看，MJ，但我的二十多岁过得相当灾难，充满焦虑和自我怀疑。然后在三十多岁时突然遭遇了这种巨大的公众关注。老实说，我并没有真正享受那段极度出名的时期。

名气就像一种奇怪的毒药。它会扭曲你与现实的关系，让你误以为自己的声音比别人的更重要，或者让你陷入一种持续的偏执，害怕自己说错一句话就会失去一切。更糟糕的是，它会侵蚀你的内部评价系统。你开始根据外界的反应来衡量自己的价值，而不是根据你是否忠于自己的艺术和生活。我花了很长时间、通过心理治疗和远离社交网络，才慢慢找回自己的重心。

Original English

John Green: I don't know about you, MJ, but my 20s were pretty catastrophic, filled with anxiety and self-doubt. And then in my 30s, this massive public attention suddenly hit. To be completely honest, MJ, I did not particularly relish that period of intense fame. Fame is a strange kind of poison. It distorts your relationship with reality, tricking you into thinking your voice matters more than anyone else's, or trapping you in constant paranoia that saying one wrong thing will ruin everything. Worse, it erodes your internal metric of worth. You start measuring value by external feedback rather than staying true to your art and life. It took a long time, extensive therapy, and stepping away from social media to regain my grounding.

MJ·富兰克林: 在那段时期，你是如何保持创作的真实性的？尤其是当你写下的每一个字都会被数百万人剖析的时候。

Original English

MJ Franklin: During that period, how did you maintain artistic authenticity, especially when every word you wrote was being dissected by millions?

约翰·格林: 答案是：我当时根本无法保持，所以我停止了写小说。我转去写了《人类纪评鉴》中的散文。散文对我来说是一根救命稻草，因为在散文中我不需要躲在虚构角色的面具后面，我可以直接审视自己对世界的真实感受——那种脆弱、不确定和对微小事物的感激。

写非虚构散文重新教会了我如何去观察周围的世界，而不是沉溺于自我关注。只有当我学会了放下“约翰·格林这个畅销作家品牌”的包袱，我才能重新成为一个单纯的故事讲述者。

Original English

John Green: The truth is, I couldn't at the time, which is why I stopped writing novels altogether. I pivoted to writing the essays in The Anthropocene Reviewed. Nonfiction essays were a lifeline for me because I didn't have to hide behind fictional characters; I could directly confront my honest feelings about the world—vulnerability, uncertainty, and gratitude for small things. Writing essays retaught me how to look at the world around me rather than being obsessed with myself. Only when I learned to drop the baggage of "John Green the bestselling author brand" could I return to simply being a storyteller.

从青少年文学到成人文学的蜕变

MJ·富兰克林: 中场休息后，我们将继续探讨约翰·格林职业生涯的这一转折点。

回顾这一路走来，你如何看待青年文学与成人文学之间的界限？很多人对青少年小说存在偏见，认为它不如成人文学严肃。

Original English

MJ Franklin: After the break, John Green on this moment in his career. Looking back at this journey, how do you see the boundary between YA literature and adult fiction? Many people hold a prejudice against YA, treating it as less serious than adult literature.

约翰·格林: 我永远感激青少年文学社区。青少年是世界上最棒的读者，因为他们阅读时倾注了全心全意。对于青少年来说，阅读往往关乎生死存亡——他们是在通过故事寻找自己是谁、世界如何运转的答案。

但成人文学给作者提供了不同的空间。在青少年小说中，你往往在描写“初次经历”的激烈冲击——初恋、第一次面对死亡、第一次感到幻灭。而在成人小说中，你面对的是那些冲击之后的漫长余波。你必须处理妥协、处理生活的不可逆性、处理那些没有戏剧性高潮但依然沉重的日常现实。

Original English

John Green: I will always be deeply grateful to the YA community. Teenagers are the best readers in the world because they read with their whole hearts. For young adults, reading is often existential—they are looking for answers about who they are and how the world works. But adult literature offers a different narrative canvas. In YA, you focus on the intense shock of "firsts"—first love, first confrontation with death, first disillusionment. In adult fiction, you reckon with the long aftermath of those shocks. You have to deal with compromise, the irreversibility of life, and the quiet weight of ordinary realities that lack dramatic climaxes.

MJ·富兰克林: 这部新小说的核心冲突是什么？是什么让你觉得这个故事非写不可？

Original English

MJ Franklin: What is the core conflict of this new novel? What made this story feel absolutely undeniable to you?

约翰·格林: 这本书关于两个在生活中感到迷失的人，他们试图在彼此身上找到某种救赎，但很快发现没有人能成为另一个人的救世主。我一直对人类试图建立深层联结却又不可避免地面临孤独的悖论着迷。

将自己如此私密、脆弱的一面展现出来是有风险的，MJ。因为一旦你说出了某些真相，它就永远留在了公共领域。但这就是小说的魔力：你把自己最隐秘的恐惧和痛苦写下来，然后读者读到它并说：“我也曾有过这种感觉。”那一刻，孤独就被打破了。

Original English

John Green: The book centers on two people feeling lost in their lives, attempting to find redemption in one another, only to realize that no human being can be someone else's savior. I've always been fascinated by the paradox of our longing for deep connection alongside our inescapable isolation. Putting something so intensely personal out there is risky, MJ, because once you say something, it's out in the world forever. But that's the magic of fiction: you write down your most private fears and heartbreaks, and a reader reads it and says, "I have felt that too." In that singular moment, loneliness breaks.
