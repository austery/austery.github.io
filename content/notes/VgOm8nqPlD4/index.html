想让别人改变？换个提问方式

关键字 behavioral-change organizational-change interpersonal-communication problem-solving

摘要

推动改变的困境

几年前，我曾与一位感到沮丧的企业管理者交谈。他总是向团队提出新想法，探讨如何能更好、更快地协同工作。但他的团队似乎并不经常采纳这些想法。在谈话结束时，他说了一句让我印象深刻的话：“我不明白他们为什么就是不肯改变。”

英文原文 A few years ago, I was speaking with a business leader who was frustrated. He was always pitching new ideas to his team on how they could work together better and faster. But his team didn't seem to take up those ideas very often. At the end of our conversation, he said something that stayed with me. "I don't understand why they won't just change."

在我作为顾问的工作中，我见过人们以成千上万种不同的方式要求同事做出改变。比如“我希望你改变写报告的方式”，或者“我希望你在需求预测中使用这些新输入项”。有时则是“我希望你更频繁地与主管沟通”。这些要求在细节上各不相同，但本质是一样的：都是要求他人以不同的方式做事。有时这行得通，有时则不行。但这是为什么呢？为什么有些要求能成功，而另一些却失败了？促使身边共事者发生改变的秘诀究竟是什么？

英文原文 In my work as a consultant, I've watched people ask their colleagues to change in a million different ways. It's "I want you to change the way you write that report," or "I want you to use these new inputs in your demand forecast." Or sometimes, "I want you to meet with your manager more often." They're different in detail, but they're the same in spirit. It's people asking other people to do things differently. Sometimes it works, and sometimes it doesn’t. But why? Why do some asks succeed when other asks fail? What's the secret to getting the people you work with to change?

梦想家与侦探

在与企业领袖、行为科学家以及数千名员工互动多年后，我发现了这一点：希望同事做出改变的人分为两类，我将他们称为“梦想家”和“侦探”。

英文原文 After years of interacting with business leaders, behavioral scientists, thousands of employees, here's what I found. There are two types of people who want their coworkers to change, and I call them the dreamers and the detectives.

梦想家通常只问一个问题：“大家为什么不去做呢？”梦想家坚信新的做事方式比旧方式更聪明、更快捷、更公平。他们认为好处是不言而喻的，并且很难理解为什么会有人持有不同看法。但梦想家往往无法如愿以偿。人们没有改变，是因为路上存在太多阻碍。

英文原文 The dreamers mostly ask one question: Why wouldn’t people do it? Dreamers have a passionate belief that the new way of doing things is smarter, faster, fairer than the old way. They think the benefits are self-evident, and they struggle to comprehend that anyone else would see it differently. But often, the dreamers don't get their wish. People don't change because there are too many barriers in their way.

侦探则提出一个不同的问题。不是“大家为什么不去做呢？”，而是“大家为什么不肯做？”侦探不会沉溺于自己对于新方式为何优越或重要的感受。相反，他们会去调查阻碍人们改变的摩擦力与抑制因素。而始终取得更好结果的，正是这些侦探。

英文原文 The detectives ask a different question. Not “Why wouldn’t people do it?” but instead, “Why won’t people do it?” Detectives don't get caught up in their own feelings about why the new way of doing things is good or important. Instead, they investigate the frictions, the disincentives that will prevent people from changing. And consistently, it's the detectives who get better results.

这里有一个例子。一家连锁酒店希望其员工使用一种更现代、更精密的工具来设定客房价格，这种工具更加依赖自动化和机器学习。但通过深入调查，管理层了解到员工对此持怀疑态度。他们不相信新工具能比自己的定价直觉更出色。因此，他们召集了该连锁酒店中最熟练、最有经验的员工，让他们加入该新工具的早期用户组，以此向整个组织传递信号：它确实很有价值。这起到了效果。不久之后，绝大多数拥有该工具使用权限的员工每周都在使用它。

英文原文 Here's one example. A hotel chain wanted its staff members to use a more modern, sophisticated tool to set room prices, one that relied more on automation and machine learning. But through good investigation, leaders learned that staff members were skeptical. They weren't convinced that the new tool could improve on their own pricing instincts. So they pulled together the chain's most skilled, experienced staff members and put them into an early user group for the new tool to signal to the organization it really was valuable. And it worked. Before long, the vast majority of staff members who had access to the tool were using it every week.

探寻阻碍的三种问法

在您自己的生活中，我相信也存在希望他人以不同方式做事的情况。作为一名侦探，您可以通过几种不同的方式来询问“他们为什么不肯做？”。

英文原文 In your own life, I'm sure there are things that you want other people to do differently. As a detective, you can ask, “Why won’t they do it?” in a few different ways.

这里有一个简单的问法：人们缺乏哪些知识与技能？ 这个问题能找出人们对变革所掌握的信息差距，以及在履行自身职责时的能力差距。如果这是阻碍所在，您就需要为人们提供所需的信息和工具。

英文原文 Here's a simple one. What knowledge and skills are people missing? This question roots out gaps in the information people have about the change and gaps in their own ability to play their part. If this is the barrier, you need to give people the information and tools that they need.

这是另一个变体：会有哪些摩擦阻碍人们的前进？ 有时人们觉得没有时间或资源去以不同方式做事。有时其他人正在悄悄鼓励他们根本不要做出改变。在这些情况下，您必须消除阻碍摩擦，提供时间，提供资源，并与那些有影响力的其他人进行深入艰难的对话。

英文原文 Here's another variant. What frictions will get in people's way? Sometimes people feel they don't have the time or resources to do things differently. Sometimes other people are quietly encouraging them not to change at all. In these cases, you have to remove the frictions, provide time, provide resources, have hard conversations with those other influential people.

还有一个供您考虑的问法：人们为什么会不想改变？ 当人们觉得改变无法获得任何好处，或者更糟的是，觉得改变会让自己有所损失时，他们完全不做出改变就不应令人感到意外。当这种情况发生时，您必须帮助人们以不同视角看待事情，消除他们毫无根据的恐惧，或者展示您将如何亲自协助防止最坏情况发生。

英文原文 One more for you to consider. Why won't people want to change? When people feel they don't gain anything by changing, or worse, when they feel that they have something to lose, we shouldn't be surprised when they don't change at all. When this happens, you have to help people see things differently, alleviate their unfounded fears or show how you will personally help prevent the worst of them.

当你提出这些问题时，你会了解到很多情况。你会发现，被你要求去运行新风险评估流程的人，恰恰就是设计旧流程的人；对他们而言，这种改变代表着地位的丧失。你会了解到，你的运营主管非常喜欢调度货车配送；对他们而言，转用自动化系统意味着失去了一项珍视的创造性活动。一旦你了解了情况，就可以采取行动。

英文原文 When you ask these questions, you learn a lot. You learn that the person you're asking to run the new risk assessment is the very same person who designed the old one. To them, the change represents a loss of status. You learn that your operations manager loves scheduling truck deliveries. To them, the change to an automated system represents the loss of a treasured creative activity. And once you've learned, you can act.

行动建议与原则

在开始付诸实践时，请牢记以下几点建议：

首先，大声问出来。去设想人们为什么不肯改变是好事，但把他们约来开个会或者喝杯咖啡，当面切实询问他们，效果要好得多。

其次，多问几次。通常情况下，只有在解决了像“时间不够”这类简单阻碍之后，你才会发现更复杂的改变阻碍，比如深层的个人恐惧。

最后，绝不上纲上线进行道德评判。当人们的表现不符合你的期望时，很容易指责他们懒惰或抗拒，但道德评判无助于让你达成想要的结果——即成功且持久的改变。

英文原文 As you get started, here are a few tips to keep in mind. First, ask out loud. It's good to imagine why people won't change, but it's much better to sit them down in a meeting or over coffee and actually ask them. Second, ask more than once. Often you won't find the complex barriers to change, like deep personal fears, until you've addressed the simple ones, like not having enough time. Finally, never moralize. When people aren't behaving the way you expect, it's tempting to say that they're lazy or resistant, but moralizing doesn’t help you get the thing that you want: a successful, sustained change.

顺便提一句，在职场之外，你同样可以成为一名侦探。也许你想让姐姐开始使用家庭共享日历，或者希望老朋友多分享一些生活近况，同样的原则都适用。

因此，下次你想让某人以不同的方式做事时，请记住这个关键问题：不要问“大家为什么不去做呢？”，而是要问：“大家为什么不肯做？”你会惊讶地发现，改变提问方式能够改变一切。

谢谢大家。

英文原文 By the way, you can be a detective outside the workplace as well. Maybe you want your sister to start using the family's shared calendar, or an old friend to send more life updates. The same principles apply. So the next time you want someone to do something differently, remember the magic question. Don’t ask, “Why wouldn’t they do it?” Ask instead, “Why won’t they do it?” You'll be surprised how changing the question can change everything. Thank you.
