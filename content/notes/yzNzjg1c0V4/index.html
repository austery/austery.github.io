第一章：面对压垮感时如何克服拖延

When it comes to the question of how to create lasting change in our lives, it turns out that many of us were led astray by some of the most influential philosophers throughout history.

Knowing in and of itself does not guarantee doing. While knowing is an important and necessary first step, it is far from sufficient.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 这里有几个例子。许多人深知积极生活方式的益处，却依然没有去锻炼。在发达国家中，大多数人都清楚哪些食物有益健康，但依旧大量摄入垃圾食品。我们把生活所馈赠的许多事物视作理所当然，并不是因为我们不知道心怀感恩远胜于不知感恩。大体上，我们清楚应该如何对待自己的孩子、伴侣或同事。我们懂得该做什么，但往往未能付诸实践。因此，问题不在于缺乏知识甚至不在于缺乏理解，而在于这些知识或理解并没有转化为实际行动与作为。

Most people in developed countries know which foods are good for them and still consume junk in large quantities. We take much of what life has to offer for granted, not because we don't know that gratitude is better for us than ingratitude. Generally, we know how we ought to treat our child, our partner, or our colleagues. We know what to do, but all too often we fail to do it. The problem therefore is not lack of knowledge or even understanding but rather that this knowledge or understanding is not translated into acts and deeds.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 这种“知行差距”是我在非常个人的层面上切身体验过的。在研读心理学和哲学多年之后，我逐渐意识到，我对美好生活的知识和理解，远远超过了我内心生活的实际质量。如果让我参加一场关于公开演讲焦虑、养育快乐儿童、或者坚定表达自身需求与意愿的重要性的考试，我能拿满分。对于几乎所有与心理学相关的问题，我都能给出令人信服、文采斐然、而且在大多数情况下完全正确的答案。此外，我通常还能引经据典，列举实证研究来支持自己的论点。

If I were taking an exam on public speaking anxiety, raising happy kids, or the importance of assertively expressing one's needs and wants, I would ace that test. I had convincing, eloquent, and in most cases, correct answers to just about any question related to psychology. Moreover, I would usually be able to refer to research to support my claims.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 然而在日常生活中，我却承受着严重的公开演讲恐惧，犯下了许多不必要的育儿错误，而且非常频繁地回避提出对我很重要的问题，因为我害怕随之而来的冲突对峙。虽然无论是作为演讲者还是作为父母，自踏入积极心理学领域以来我已经取得了长足的进步，但我深知自己本可以做得更好。我深刻认识到，无论对我自己还是对他人而言，通往更幸福、更健康、更充实生活的道路，绝不仅仅是用额外的知识铺就的。相反，真正的进步取决于找到更好的方法，将我们现有的知识应用到日常生活之中。

While, as both the speaker and a parent, I'd come a long way since beginning my journey in the field of positive psychology, I knew I could do much better. It became clear to me that for myself and for others, the path to a happier, healthier, and more fulfilled life is not paved solely with additional knowledge. 最小可行干预（MVI）：以微小步骤驱动终身改变

Tal Ben-Shahar: 请思考以下几个问题：究竟是哪种具体的行动对你的幸福贡献最大？哪种工具或技巧给你的生活带来了最显著的积极改变？你引入的哪项改变对你的福祉产生了极为深远的影响？作为一名幸福学的教师与学者，我经常被问到这些问题。我的回答是：正是那些我日复一日践行的小巧幸福习惯——那些琐碎细小的事情、那些简短的干预、那些看似不起眼的改变，最终逐步重塑了我的整个人生。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: Think about the following questions. What action has contributed most to your happiness? Which tool or technique has made the biggest positive difference in your life? Which change have you introduced that significantly impacted your well-being? I'm often asked these questions as both a teacher and a student of happiness. My answer is that it is the bite-sized happy habits that I apply day in and day out. the small stuff, the brief interventions, the seemingly unremarkable changes that gradually transform my life as a whole.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 我把这些幸福习惯称为“最小可行干预”（Minimum Viable Interventions，简称 MVI）。这个概念融合了商业世界中弗兰克·罗宾逊（Frank Robinson）提出的“最小可行产品”（MVP）思想，以及幸福科学核心中的“积极干预”（positive interventions）概念。罗宾逊于 2001 年提出了“最小可行产品”（MVP）这一术语，用来描述一种虽然远未完善、但足以投放市场接受检验的产品。它使创造该产品的公司能够收集数据并在后续逐步迭代改进，同时仍能向早期采用者交付切实的价值。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: I call these happy habits minimum viable interventions or MVIS. A term that brings together Frank Robinson's idea of the minimum viable product or MVP from the business world with the concept of positive interventions which is central to the science of happiness. Robinson introduced the term MVP, minimum viable product in 2001 to describe a product that while far from finished is good enough to be put out there in the market. It allows the company that created the product to gather data and improve it over time while still delivering value to early adopters.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 同理，MVI 也绝非完美的终极干预方案，但它“足够好”，而且最关键的是，它能为践行者提供实实在在的价值。构成 MVI 的第二个要素是“积极干预”，指的是有助于提升个人整体幸福感的广泛实践、工具与技巧。这包括冥想、体育锻炼、写日记、撰写感恩信、发掘自身优势、积极倾听、沉浸于大自然等等。一项行动若要称得上是积极干预，它必须具备循证依据，经过充分的研究验证，并被证明能切实改善人们的生活。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: In a similar way, an MVI is not the perfect intervention, but it is good enough and most importantly, it provides real value to those who practice it. Positive interventions, the second ingredient of MVIS, refer to the wide range of practices, tools, and techniques that contribute to a person's overall well-being. These include uh meditation, physical exercise, journaling, writing gratitude letters, strength spotting, active listening, time in nature, and many, many more. For something to qualify as a positive intervention, it needs to be evidence-based, well researched, and shown to measurably improve people's lives.

Tal Ben-Shahar: MVI 方法的哲学渊源可以追溯到更久远的 14 世纪以及奥卡姆的威廉（William of Ockham）的思想。他最为著名的原理——奥卡姆剃刀（Occam's razor）或称简约原则——指出最简单的解决方案往往就是最优解。当我们把这一理念应用于个人改变时，便能为最小可行干预得出明确的定义：MVI 是尽可能简短、但在促成真实、显著且持久的改变上又是绝对必要的行动。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: The philosophical roots of the MVI approach go back much further to the 14th century and the work of William of Aam. His best known principle known as AAM's razor or the principle of parsimony suggests that the simplest solution is often the best solution. When we apply this idea to personal change, we arrive at a clear definition of minimal viable interventions. MVIS are actions that are as brief as possible and as long as necessary to bring about real significant and lasting change.

微剂量干预与重大干预的对比

Tal Ben-Shahar: 重大干预手段——例如闭关禅修营、密集的自助训练营、严苛的魔鬼训练，甚至一次蜜月旅行——往往只具有暂时的效果，带给我们短暂的幸福感峰值，随后我们便会迅速回落到基准水平。与之形成鲜明对比的是，频繁且规律地进行微剂量的运动、正念呼吸、记录反思、善意之举或表达感恩，却能够带来具有实质意义且经久不衰的转变。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: Major interventions like a meditation retreat, a self-help intensive, a grueling boot camp, or even a honeymoon tend to have only a temporary effect, giving us a short spike in happiness before we return to baseline. In contrast, frequent and consistent micro doses of movement, mindful breathing, journaling, acts of kindness, or expressions of gratitude can lead to meaningful and lasting change.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 就我个人而言，如果必须在每年参加一次为期十天的禅修营与每天坚持三分钟的冥想之间做出抉择，我会毫不犹豫地选择后者。尽管在禅修营中花费的累计时间要庞大得多，但日复一日坚持不懈的微练习却能产生更为深邃、更加持久的影响。同样，如果你必须在周末跑步一小时与每天数次进行 20 到 30 秒的高强度活动之间做出取舍，你很可能从简短的每日活动中获益更多。投入数小时甚至数天的时间试图去创造改变，往往最终收效短暂；相反，分散在岁月长河中的点滴微小瞬间，却能汇聚成深远重大的力量。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: Personally, if I had to choose between, say, a 10-day meditation retreat once a year and 3 minutes of daily meditation, I would choose the latter. Even though the total time spent on a retreat is much greater, the consistent daily practice has a deeper, more lasting impact. Similarly, if you had to choose between running for an hour on the weekend or moving vigorously for 20 to 30 seconds a few times a day, you would likely benefit more from the short daily activity. Investing hours and even days in trying to bring about change often ends up having a momentary effect. In contrast, spread across time, moments add up to a momentous impact.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 顺便声明一下，以免大家产生误解，我并不反对重大干预。恰恰相反，如果我们拥有无限的时间和耐心，我的建议绝对是大事与小事并举。然而现实的问题在于，时间是有限的，绝大多数人根本没有精力和心力去两者兼顾。结果就是，他们最终什么都没做。为什么？因为他们坚信真正的改变必须依赖那些轰轰烈烈的重大干预，而他们根本抽不出那么大块的时间。他们往往没有意识到，微型干预究竟蕴含着多么惊人的能量。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: Now, just so that I'm not misunderstood, I have nothing against major interventions. On the contrary, if we had unlimited time and patience, my recommendation would be absolutely to engage in both the big and the small stuff. The problem is that time is finite and most people don't have the bandwidth for both. As a result, they end up doing nothing. Why? because they believe that real change requires those big interventions and they don't have time for them. What they often don't realize is just how powerful micro interventions can be.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 我完全赞同参加为期 10 天甚至 3 个月的禅修营，只要它不被当作一项孤立孤立存在的单次干预。当它真正改变人生时，几乎无一例外是因为在此之后伴随着日复一日、持之以恒的日常实践。对于为期一周的密集自助课程而言亦是如此。真正的蜕变源于人们将这些仪式感持续带入日常生活之中。此外，有些幸福习惯并不需要占用额外的专属时间或精力，因为它们完全可以依附并叠加在我们已经在做的事情上。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: I'm all for a 10day or even a 3month meditation retreat as long as it is not a standalone intervention. When it is life-changing, it's almost always because it's followed by consistent ongoing daily practice. The same is true for a week-l long self-help intensive. The real transformation comes from the rituals people carry into their everyday lives. Subsequently, some happy habits don't require extra time or effort because they can be tagged onto things we're already doing.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 例如，带着觉知专注倾听同事发言，所花费的时间并不比心不在焉地干坐在会议室里更多。只要稍加练习，给予真诚的赞美就能变得像开口批评一样毫不费力。而选择爬楼梯所需的时间，往往与等电梯差不多，有时甚至比等电梯还快——除非你在摩天大楼里工作。我们的祖先从来不需要专门抽出固定的时间去健身房撸铁，运动本身就自然而然地编织在日常生活的肌理之中，成为散落在一整天里的短暂、天然的穿插活动。只需极小的投入，这些活动便能带来巨大的差异。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: For example, mindfully listening to our colleagues takes no more time than mindlessly sitting in a meeting. With a bit of practice, offering genuine compliments can become just as effortless as criticizing. And taking the stairs can take as much time or sometimes even less than waiting for the elevator unless you work in a skyscraper. Our ancestors didn't carve out time for formal gym sessions. Movement was simply woven into the fabric of daily life in brief natural inserts throughout the day. With little investment, these activities can make a big difference.

支撑微习惯的科学实证研究

Tal Ben-Shahar: 在我建议的每一项 MVI 背后，都有着大量的实证科学研究作为支撑。这里列举几个范例：日本的吉田教授（Professor Riku Yoshida）曾让实验参与者锻炼肱二头肌，每天仅仅做一组、每组仅有 6 次弯举。他们每周坚持 5 天，持续 4 周。实验结果显示，他们的肌肉力量获得了实实在在的增长。与此形成对照的是，另一组参与者每周仅在一天内集中完成 5 组每组 6 次的弯举，而不是将运动分散在不同日子里，他们在相同周期内力量没有任何增长。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: There is a great deal of research behind each of the MVIS I suggest. Here are a few examples. Japanese professor RKU Yoshida had participants exercise their biceps by doing just one set a day of six hand curls. They repeated the single set 5 days a week over four weeks. The results showed an actual increase in muscle strength. By contrast, another group that did five sets of six curls on just one day a week rather than spreading the effort out experienced no increase in strength over the same period.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 与此类似，《国家地理》（National Geographic）的研究员丹·布特纳（Dan Buettner）对全球“蓝色地带”（Blue Zones）——也就是世界上最长寿居民集中的地区——进行了深入研究。他建议，为了延年益寿，我们应当定期“给自己找点麻烦”。我非常喜欢“给自己找点麻烦”这个说法：比如选择走楼梯而不是乘电梯，自己手提生活用品杂货而不是叫外卖送货上门，或者亲自站起身去电视机前换台而不是按遥控器。通过参与这些微运动，有助于抵消久坐不动生活方式所带来的危害。这些轻微的“不便”，切实地促成了更健康、更长寿、更优质的生活。

Original English

Tal Ben-Shahar: Along similar lines, Dan Buettner, a researcher with National Geographic, who studied the blue zones, these are regions where people live the longest, recommends that for a longer life, we should regularly inconvenience ourselves. I love that term, inconvenience ourselves by choosing the stairs over the elevator, carrying groceries instead of having them delivered, or getting up to change the channel rather than using a remote. We engage in micro movements that help counter the harms of a sedentary lifestyle. These slight inconveniences contribute to a healthier, longer, and better life.

Tal Ben-Shahar: 从演化生物学的角度来看，这种机制完全合情合理。我们的身体和心智天生就更能适应短促而频繁的刺激干预，而不是漫长而罕见的剧烈刺激。来自斯坦福大学、梅奥诊所、比萨大学以及其他研究机构的研究表明，即便仅仅进行 1 分钟深长缓慢的鼻腔呼吸——尤其是呼气时间长于吸气时间时——就能激活副交感神经系统，抑制交感神经系统的过度兴奋。换言之，它能让我们从“战斗或逃跑”的应激状态平稳切换到“休息与消化”的放松状态。久而久之，这种简短的练习能够帮助儿童和成年人为整个生活引入更多的平和与宁静。
