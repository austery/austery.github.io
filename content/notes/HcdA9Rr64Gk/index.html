AI狂潮与氦气危机交织：RAM暴涨如何重塑全球科技硬件版图

你是否知道，在未来两年内，由于导致如今 iPhone 售价不断飙升的相同原因，市面上所有售价低于 500 美元的平价笔记本电脑可能会彻底消失？以我在 2021 年花费 1200 美元购买的 iPhone 13 Pro Max 为例，这在当时是市场上最昂贵的智能手机。然而，仅仅几年后，每个人都在讨论 内存危机（Memory Crisis: 存储芯片供需失衡导致的行业性短缺），各大科技厂商也纷纷开启了罕见的价格上调。笔记本电脑等硬件变得越来越贵。虽然面对普遍的通货膨胀，人们对涨价已有心理准备，购买 1200 美元手机的消费者或许不会因为多花 200 美元而放弃购买最新的 iPhone，但苹果公司的提价只是宏观大局中的冰山一角。这场内存危机有着更广泛且深远的影响，最严峻的后果在于它可能会彻底摧毁 500 美元以下的低端设备 market。这一价格区间承载着广大学生、学校以及首次购买电脑群体的基础刚需。因此，本期内容不仅关乎单纯的 RAM 危机，更关乎它给科技市场及全球经济带来的深层锁链效应。我们将深入剖析这场危机的根源、它是如何爆发并演变至今的，以及随着局势恶化未来会发生什么。

在过去很长一段时间里，随机存取内存（RAM: Random Access Memory，用于临时存储处理器工作数据的易失性存储介质）本质上是一种大宗商品。它呈现出极强的周期性、低利润率的特点，供需关系非常直接。RAM 行业的三大主导厂商——三星（Samsung）、SK海力士（SK Hynix）和美光（Micron）都曾在产能过剩的泥潭中承受过巨额亏损。它们在 2019 年的行业低谷中留下了共同的创伤：当时三家企业生产了过多的芯片，却面临买方绝迹的窘境。到了 2022 年，谁都不愿意再次向市场倾销多余的产能。这带有某种讽刺意图，因为在我的产品经理求职面试中，如何精准预测产品产量以满足全年供给同时让年底库存归零，是一个非常经典的面试题。不可思议的是，像 RAM 如此重要的支柱产品，其供需预测竟然偏离得如此严重。这种周期循环已经重复了三十年：RAM 价格上涨刺激制造商扩大投资，导致供给过剩，随后价格暴跌，投资戛然而止；两年后供给再度收紧，价格回升，然后循环往复。

2017 年和 2018 年是该行业的黄金年代。来自智能手机、数据中心、PC 以及游戏主机的需求产生共振，将 RAM 价格推向巅峰。当时，三星半导体部门贡献了公司总利润的 50% 以上，美光也创下了历史性的收益新高。在行业现金充沛的刺激下，三大巨头一如既往地将资金激进地投入到新产能建设中。然而到了 2019 年，市场风向陡然逆转。

For the longest time, RAM memory was a commodity. It was cyclical, it was low margin, everyone who needed it had it. It was boring, predictable, and very straightforward. The three main producers of RAM, Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron, had all burned money in oversupply. All three had a shared trauma in 2019 bust what all three produced way too much, and they simply ran out of people to sell to. So, by 2022, none of them were itching to flood the market again. There's somewhat of an irony with this because when I interviewed for product management jobs in my career, this was a very common question in an interview. It is interesting how such a monumental product, such as RAM, had their supply demand predictions quite off. But, I digress. This cycle repeated for three decades. The prices on RAM would rise, manufacturers invest, the supply goes up, then the prices collapse, investment stops, and two years after the investment stops, supply tightens again, and prices recover. Then, the whole thing repeats. The memory business is predictably cyclical, which makes what happened next all the more extraordinary. 2017 and 2018 were bonanza years. Demand from smartphones, data centers, PCs, gaming consoles converged, and RAM prices went through the roof. Samsung's semiconductor division, which produces RAM, was generating 50% of the company's total profit. Micron had its record earnings at the time. The entire industry was flush, and in that flush state, all three companies did what they always do when the money's flowing. They spend aggressively on new capacity. Then, in 2019, the market reversed.

供需错配：加密货币崩盘与需求退潮的余震

在这种周期性力量的作用下，2019 年以来的两起关键性市场降温，进一步加深了存储芯片制造巨头对产能过剩的戒备心理。首先是作为非典型内存需求源泉的加密货币挖矿（Cryptocurrency Mining: 利用显卡等硬件的计算能力解决复杂数学难题以换取虚拟货币的过程）市场彻底崩盘。随着挖矿利润化为乌有，该市场在 2019 年走向瓦解。在 2017 年 12 月，比特币价格曾达到 20,000 美元的顶峰，但次年便跌至 6,000 美元。使用单张 GPU 显卡开采以太坊的收益从 2017 年的每月 150 美元直接归零。当挖矿失去盈利空间，矿工们不仅停止采购新芯片，还开始在二手市场上抛售手中的硬件。这给内存市场带来了极大的二次冲击，造成了大规模的过剩。

一个典型的跨国连锁反应发生在两年后的哈萨克斯坦。当中国在 2021 年取缔加密货币挖矿后，成百上千的中国矿工迁移至哈萨克斯坦。这使其迅速成为全球第二大比特币挖矿国。由于挖矿设备的大量涌入，其消耗的电量甚至达到了哈萨克斯坦全国发电量的 8%。这直接导致该国国家电网崩溃，政府随即下达了挖矿禁令。失去立足之地的矿工们被迫关停业务，并大量倾销手中的芯片。在过去能卖到 700 美元的高端芯片，其价格在二手市场上迅速跌落至 300 美元。

**RAM 行业也经历了同样的阵痛：多年的产能过度扩张在需求骤停的时刻迎来了反噬。**导致市场反转的第二个原因在于智能手机市场触及了天花板。作为全球最大的智能手机市场，中国在 2018 年的手机销量甚至低于 2013 年，因为几乎每个潜在买家都已经拥有一部手机。与此同时，在黄金繁荣期超额订购了大量 RAM 的数据中心客户也停止了新采购，因为他们的仓库里堆满了从未启封的库存。

当供给严重压倒需求，存储市场遭受了多重打击。结果是 RAM 价格暴跌 42%，整体市场规模缩水了近 25%。仅在 2019 年一年，三星的利润就暴跌了 60%，SK 海力士的产品价格也下跌了 32%，服务器 RAM 更是预计在年底前大跌 50%。这迫使三星、SK 海力士和美光三大巨头紧急拉下刹车：三星叫停了新工厂的建设，SK 海力士将投资降至生存底线，美光也大幅削减了资本预算，从 30 亿美元削减至能向其投资者交代的任何数字。

Cryptocurrency mining, which had been an anomalous source of memory demand, collapsed. Why did it collapse? Because it became simply unprofitable. Bitcoin peaked at $20,000 in December 2017 and then fell to $6,000 next year. Mining Ethereum with one GPU chip went from making $150 a month in 2017 to zero next year. And when mining becomes unprofitable, miners stop buying the chips. And when they exit the mining business and no longer need the chips, they start selling them. And when they start selling them, they flood the second-hand market with the chips that they had. And that creates a massive oversupply of memory. It's fascinating how a collapse in one market or one industry can drive such a drastic change in another one. A good example is what actually happened in Kazakhstan 2 years later. When China banned crypto mining in 2021, hundreds of Chinese miners packed their bags and relocated to Kazakhstan. Kazakhstan became the number two Bitcoin mining country in the world, and the country was so flooded with mining hardware that it consumed 8% of the country's entire electricity generation capacity. This crashed the national power grid, and so the government said, "No more mining." So, miners had to shut down and leave. And the ones who decided to exit the mining business started selling their chips. And so, high-end chips that had been sold for $700 in the past dropped to $300.

RAM went through its own version of that cycle. Years of overbuilding, then demand suddenly stops, and all of the extra supply hits the market that doesn't want it. The second reason why the market reversed is the change in the smartphone demand, which also went down because the market simply ran out of new buyers. China, which is the largest smartphone market on the planet, had already sold a phone to almost every single buyer they could have sold to. Just think about it. In 2018, China was selling fewer phones than in 2013. Data center customers that pre-ordered a ton of RAM in the boom years stopped purchasing new chips because they had warehouses full of the chips that they never touched. And so, the supply overpowered the demand. And the memory market was hit by multiple factors at once. And as a result, the prices fell 42%. The total memory market shrank by almost 25%. Samsung profits plummeted 60% in 2019 alone. SK Hynix prices dropped 32%. Server RAM was projected to fall 50% by end of year. Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron hit the brakes hard. Samsung stopped building its new factory. SK Hynix cut RAM investment to bare minimum. And Micron slashed capital budget from $3 billion down to whatever number they could justify to their investors.

AI 军备竞赛：HBM 的利润引力与产能截流

这种短暂的供需平衡在 2022 年末被彻底打破，AI 时代的降临对芯片产能进行了新一轮的大规模重构。当时疫情暴发刺激了 PC 和笔记本电脑的需求，众人居家办公，学校采购新 Chromebook，需求飙升短暂回填了存储市场，但到 2022 年即告终结。随后迎来了 ChatGPT 的发布，标志着 AI 时代的开启。GPT-4 推出时拥有 8,000 token 的上下文窗口，而到了次年，谷歌的 Gemini 已经能支持 200 万 token。紧随其后的 Claude 等前沿大语言模型也加入了这场军备竞赛。所有的前沿 AI 实验室都在拼命构建能够承载更大上下文记忆的模型，而上下文窗口中增加的每一个 token，都直接转化为对海量存储器的刚性需求。

问题不仅在于 AI 需要庞大的内存容量，更在于它所需要的特定内存类型。AI 运行所依赖的高带宽内存（High Bandwidth Memory (HBM): 通过垂直堆叠多个 DRAM 芯片实现高吞吐量和低功耗的特制超高速内存），其生产周期是传统 RAM 的四倍。因此，市场需求开始全面转向这一溢价极高的特殊内存类型。模型训练的竞赛触发了数据中心的大战，进而带来了对 AI 芯片的指数级需求。为了确保英伟达（Nvidia）、AMD 以及谷歌 TPU 等核心 AI 芯片的供应，各大主流芯片厂商开始提前数年为 HBM 的产能配额进行预付款锁定。

**三巨头做出这一重大调整的底层动机是 HBM 拥有远远超过传统 RAM 的利润率：传统 RAM 的利润率在 80% 左右，而 HBM 则高达 87%。**在半导体制造这种重资产行业中，即便看似只有 7% 的微小差距，也能在扣除庞大的折旧成本后，成倍放大企业盈利。1% 的价格或毛利改善，往往能转化为 20% 的营业利润增长。若按每千兆字节（GB）拆分，HBM 的售价约为 10 美元，而标准 RAM 仅为 3 美元。

除此之外，高带宽内存的需求由超大规模云服务商（Hyperscaler）承载。这类大型 B2B 合同极具黏性，客户在 AI 霸权的竞赛中完全依赖这三家内存巨头，对价格极其不敏感。对于三星、SK 海力士和美光而言，即使这意味着要颠覆现有的业务模式和市场定位，忽略如此诱人的暴利市场也是极不理智的。到了 2025 年，当这些商业决策的数据公之于众时，SK 海力士和三星都在开足马力生产 HBM 并缩减 RAM 的产线，美光甚至关闭了部分消费级业务以全力切换至 HBM。这导致 HBM 与 RAM 的产能比例达到了惊人的 3:1。普通消费者所需的笔记本、台式机和手机的内存，只能用超算和云巨头挑剩的残余产能来维系。

Then COVID hits and the PC and laptop demand goes up again. Everyone working from home, schools buying new Chromebooks, and this spike in demand refilled the memory market. But briefly. And it did so until 2022. The launch of ChatGPT and the beginning of AI era. OpenAI's GPT-4 launched with an 8,000 token context window. By next year, Google's Gemini came out with 2 million. And then Claude and every other frontier LLM. Every major AI lab was in a race to build models that could hold more memory. And every additional token in a context window translates directly into the need for more memory. The problem isn't just that AI needs a lot of memory. It's the type of memory that it needs. The type of memory that it needs takes four times as long to make as regular RAM. And therefore, the demand shifted towards high bandwidth memory, also known as HBM. HBM is essentially a premium form of RAM. The model race caused the data center race. The data center race led to the exponential demand in AI chips. And AI chips caused the demand in high bandwidth memory. And so, all the major AI chip manufacturers who needed the memory for AI chips, such as Nvidia, AMD, and Google's TPUs, started pre-paying for multi-year HBM allocation. And where there is demand, there is supply.

In 2024, the same three names, Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron, shift production from consumer RAM to HBM. They did not kill RAM production entirely, but it was significantly chopped. Now, why did they do it? Because the margins on HBM versus standard RAM are substantially higher. RAM sits at about 80% margin, and HBM at 87%. In isolation, the 7% difference doesn't sound huge, but in manufacturing, such as semiconductors, small changes in price or margin translate into large changes in profit. A 1% improvement in price can drive 20% operating margin. And if you slice it per gigabyte, HBM sells for roughly $10 per one versus $3 for standard RAM. So, there's a very solid premium and a very solid reason as to why you might want to shift the production from RAM to HBM. Plus, the demand for high bandwidth memory is signed and pre-contracted for years in advance. Your main consumer is a hyperscaler, which is a gigantic B2B contract that is sticky by nature. They're not going to go anywhere. Your consumer is not price sensitive, and their own race for AI supremacy depends on you. So, when you are Samsung, SK Hynix, or Micron, it would not be reasonable not to go for this market, even if you have to change your entire business model or market positioning. And the sequence of these decisions brings us to 2025, when the numbers became public. SK Hynix started pumping out HBM. Samsung cut RAM production and also started pumping out HBM. Micron closed consumer business to switch to HBM. And the ratio of HBM production to RAM became 3:1, and this is what led to the prices. The consumers who need laptops, desktops, or phones were being served by whatever was left after the hyperscalers took their HBM.

资源瓶颈：卡塔尔氦气危机对传统 RAM 的致命一击

在产能本就极其脆弱的背景下，一场地缘政治冲突突然爆发，给已经处于边缘的传统 RAM 供给泼上了最后一桶汽油。2026 年 3 月，发生了一起看似毫无关联却影响深远的黑天鹅事件：一架伊朗无人机袭击了卡塔尔的拉斯拉凡工业城（Ras Laffan Industrial City）。这里是全球最大的氦气生产设施所在地。

氦气在半导体制造的光刻**（Lithography: 利用紫外线等光源在硅片上蚀刻电路图案的精密制造工艺）以及半导体检测的泄漏检测（Leak Detection）中扮演着不可替代的角色**，且对纯度的要求必须无限接近 100%。由于化学物理特性的限制，人们无法在全球其他地方凭空增产这种稀缺气体。三大内存巨头的晶圆厂完全依赖来自卡塔尔的氦气供给。袭击发生后，全球氦气价格在短短几周内翻了一番。在气体供应面临严重短缺的情况下，三家巨头的晶圆厂不得不实施紧急配给机制，将有限的氦气资源全部倾斜给利润率最高的 HBM 生产线。如果还有任何多余的氦气残余，传统 RAM 才能分得一杯羹；如果没有，传统 RAM 的产线便只能被迫停工。这一材料层面的致命卡脖子危机，将我们彻底带入了如今内存危机的最高峰。

Then, in March 2026, a completely unrelated event poured fuel on the fire. Iranian drone hits Ras Laffan Industrial City in Qatar. Ras Laffan is a home to the world's largest helium production facility. Now, why is helium important and what does it have to do with this problem? Because helium is an essential component in semiconductor manufacturing. It is used in lithography, it is used in leak detection, and you need close to 100% purity. So, you can't magically start producing more of it anywhere else in the world. All three giants need helium for memory manufacturing, and all three sourced helium from Qatar. The strikes in Qatar doubled prices on helium within weeks, and with helium being scarce, the same three fabs had to prioritize gas allocation and give all of it to HBM. If there is anything left for RAM, consumers are lucky. If there is nothing, nothing it is, which brings us to today. The pinnacle of the RAM crisis.

硬件重定价：低端市场的消亡与地缘扩产博弈

这种原料短缺与产能截流所引发的生存危机，正以不可阻挡之势在下游消费电子市场引发剧烈的多米诺骨牌效应。下游硬件设备制造商的成本吸收能力已达极限。当核心组件的价格暴涨时，厂商的毛利被彻底蚕食，导致整个低端硬件业务不再具有商业可行性。在过去，很多发达国家的家庭或学校也许从未关注过 500 美元以下的设备，但这个低价市场对三大群体有着至关重要的社会经济意义：首先是极度依赖 Chromebook 的中小学教育系统；其次是依赖平价硬件的南亚、亚太甚至日本的学校和公共教育项目；最后是发达国家中依靠固定收入支撑的低收入家庭和初次购买电脑的学生。当这个低价位区间被彻底摧毁时，数以百万计的人群将被阻隔在现代教育和数字化工作所需的门槛之外。

多米诺骨牌的第二张是整体 PC 市场的全线涨价。联想（Lenovo）、戴尔（Dell）、惠普（HP）等 PC 巨头均已发出预警，受内存价格飙升影响，自 2026 年下半年起，设备价格将普遍上调 15% 至 20%，涨幅达数百美元。同样的危机也在预算有限的安卓手机市场蔓延。内存芯片成本约占整机生产成本的 10% 至 20%，对于利润极薄的小米（Xiaomi）、OPPO、vivo 等品牌而言，他们根本没有利润空间来缓冲这种成本激增，被迫进行剧烈提价。而一直维持高利润的苹果公司同样面临组件价格暴涨，根据 TechInsights 的拆解测算，在之前的 iPhone 17 Pro 中，苹果在 RAM 和闪存上的成本分别只有 39 美元和 13 美元；而在当前的内存暴涨环境下，这两项成本分别飙升至 145 美元和 51 美元。为了捍卫其高达 50% 的毛利率底线，苹果将被迫将未来的 iPhone 18 Pro 起售价上调至接近 1,400 美元。娱乐终端同样未能幸免，索尼调高了 PlayStation 5 的售价后，其销量瞬间崩溃了近 60%；任天堂将下一代游戏机 Switch 2 价格上调 20% 后，其股价单日暴跌 10%。

摆脱这场全球性内存危机的唯一路径是新增产能的释放。目前各大厂商已在加速扩建晶圆厂。SK 海力士计划将整体产能翻倍；三星已经重启了其庞大的平泽 P5 晶圆厂的建设，目标是在 2027 年底投产。不过，英特尔（Intel）直言不讳地指出，整个市场在 2028 年之前都不会迎来实质性的缓解。在此期间，一些台湾的小型晶圆厂正在利用老旧的技术和低性能、低成本的芯片，艰难支撑着低端 PC 和笔记本电脑的市场空缺。在这场供给自救战中，中国的长鑫存储（CXMT: 中国主要的 DRAM 制造商）扮演着关键的变量。这家仅成立十年的年轻企业在非 HBM 市场具有极强的扩产潜力。长鑫存储没有选择进军利润丰厚的 HBM 赛道，并非因为缺乏意愿，而是因为受到技术限制，无法获取用于生产高性能 HBM 芯片的极紫外光刻机（Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) Lithography: 利用波长极短的极紫外光蚀刻纳米级芯片电路的顶尖设备）。如果长鑫存储的产能能按预期在 2027 年下半年集中释放，并恰逢 AI 基础设施投资的阶段性降温，RAM 价格甚至可能会迅速崩盘。然而，美国实施的出口管制政策已对长鑫存储的设备采购设定了硬性天天花板。如果中国能在 2027 年前成功实现关键半导体设备的本土化，扩产就能继续推行；如果失败，全球科技供应链将面临更漫长而痛苦的阴霾。

在这场全球半导体的地缘变局中，三星、SK 海力士和美光无疑为股东的利益做出了最符合资本逻辑的最优选择。而在这场博弈中被夺走廉价设备的普通消费者，只能默默承受科技通胀的代价。

What did this crisis lead to? The domino effect. Perhaps the most noticeable one from the business standpoint is that the entire sub $500 laptop market is actively disappearing. See, the thing is, vendors and manufacturers have the ability to absorb costs, but when there is a sharp increase in component cost, a manufacturer gets to the point where they cannot absorb those costs anymore. It starts eating into the margins, and collapse in margins make the business non-viable. Now, on one hand, you may think, "Okay, when was the last time I bought any hardware device such as a laptop under $500?" On the other hand, a sub $500 laptop market has a massive implication on three groups of consumers. One, primary and secondary education that lives off of Chromebooks. Two, emerging markets. South Asia, Asia-Pacific, and even Japan school projects are heavy users of sub $500 hardware. And three, lower-income consumers in developed markets. Students, first-time PC buyers, households on fixed incomes, and so on. When that entire price tier disappears, it locks millions of people and schools out of the basic computing they need to participate in modern education and work.

Two, the entire PC market reprices. Lenovo, Dell, HP, and others all have warned about 15 to 20% price hikes starting H2 2026 by several hundred dollars. The same thing is happening with budget phones. Memory is about 10 to 20% of the phone production and low margin Android manufacturers, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, no longer have a buffer. Now, it is important to note that these brands are not as widespread in North America, but this is very important in the global context. Budget smartphones in emerging markets or in the markets where Chinese exports are prevalent, go through this deepest price increase of any device category. Number four, Apple raising prices. They haven't raised their prices in years and this is the first time that they have publicly surrendered to a crisis in the component market. iPhone price increase on the new phone has not kicked in yet, but TechInsights ran the math and Apple previously spent $39 on RAM in a single device and $13 on flash per iPhone 17 Pro. At current memory prices, those figures go up to $145 and $51 respectively. Apple cannot absorb over $100 increase in RAM costs per device without either raising prices or accepting margin compression. So, to protect its gross margin of about 50%, they have to increase their prices and price iPhones 18 Pro at almost $1,400.

Number five, gaming. Sony raised prices on the PlayStation 5 and the sales crashed by almost 60%. Nintendo raised Switch and Switch 2 prices by up to 20% and their shares fell 10% in a single day. Xbox is expected to follow. The wider implications of gaming market getting hit from this is when hardware sales slow down, game sales will follow.

So, what will happen next and what should we do about this? The only thing that can get us out of this crisis is the increase in supply, which in our case means the expansion of capacity. The industry needs more production and more fabrication plants with a solid incentive to invest in RAM and not switch to HBM. These plants are indeed being built. SK Hynix plans to nearly double their capacity. Samsung resumed Pyeongtaek P5 construction at beast semiconductor factory and targets late 2027. Intel said without much sugarcoating that there won't be a relief until 2028. In the meantime, smaller Taiwanese factories are stepping in to serve regular consumers. Their chips are older and cheaper. They don't perform as well as the newest ones, but this is the reason why they can still afford to sell to budget laptops and PCs.

So, we spoke about Korean, American, and Taiwanese manufacturers, but where would we all be without China? China is rolling up its sleeves with CXMT, China's main RAM producer. CXMT is the youngest of them all. The company is only 10 years old and analysts say that it can provide the supply faster than anticipated. You may ask, "Why be a good Samaritan if everybody else pivoted to HBM and rightfully so?" The main reason is that CXMT does not have extreme ultraviolet machines to produce the HBM. So, they're not doing it not because they don't want to be doing it, but because they can't. Now, there is a chance that we might be seeing somewhat of a whipsaw. If CXMT aggressively expands capacity and the supply arrives in the second half 2027 and the supply surge coincides with any softening in AI spending, prices could collapse as fast as they rose. However, there is a significant constraint. US export controls have effectively capped CXMT's expansion. The whipsaw scenario is possible, but it has a big dependency. If China succeeds in localizing equipment by 2027, the expansion resumes. If not, we're screwed.

So, where does this leave us? Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron made the right call for their shareholders and shifted to produce memory for AI. The consumers who got hurt on the way up will probably buy into the market on the way down. The companies that produce both will profit from both. This is the reality of the capital allocation in tech these days, and we're just watching how the scenario unfolds. But at least now you know who made this decision, how it was made, and exactly what it costs to everybody else. We hope this was helpful, and we'll see you in the next episode. Bye.
