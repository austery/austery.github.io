Original English

Bo Seo: Chapter one, how good arguments work. I felt the need to write good arguments because I think our public conversations are in a state of crisis and that is the more visible crisis that we see where every disagreement out in media or in the public square is a source of division and pain. But it's also the hidden crisis of all the people who are driven away from participating in those conversations at all because they fear that it's going to be pointless or it's going to be painful and it's not going to be worth their time.

Bo Seo：良好的辩论不仅是民主制度运转所必须履行的职责，更是民主社会在最佳状态下所渴望达成的境界：让持有不同意见的人们——哪怕有时面对的是不可调和的根本分歧——能够坐在一起对话，寻找共识的交集，找出彼此需要跨越分歧共同协作的节点，从而达成更优的解决方案。而我们当下的争论之所以如此充满分裂、痛苦与尖锐对立，原因之一就在于我们任由“如何妥善处理分歧”这项技能退化萎缩了。我们不再将辩论视为一种需要后天磨炼和持续练习的技能；相反，我们将其看作一种出于本能冲动或防卫心理而盲目卷入的冲突。由此产生的恶性辩论反过来又削弱了我们对分歧所能带来的正面价值的信心。当我们丧失了对辩论效用的信念时，我们就会以更加防御性的姿态去面对分歧。这种缺乏信念与缺乏自信的状态会陷入恶性循环并不断自我强化，由此引发的连锁反应最终导致整个对话的质量进一步急剧恶化。

Original English

Bo Seo: Good arguments is not only what democracies have to do. It's what they on their best days aspire to be, which is a conversation between people who disagree sometimes over irreconcilable things to find areas of agreement to spot points where they need to work across their differences to reach a better solution. One of the reasons why our arguments are so divisive and painful and present is that we've allowed the skills of disagreeing well to atrophy. We no longer view argument as a skill and as something to be worked at. Rather, we see it as something we jump into out of instinct or defensiveness. The bad arguments that result in turn decrease our confidence in what disagreements can do for us. And when we lack that faith in what arguments can do, we tend to approach them more defensively. We tend to go into it with a more defensive posture. And as a result, the lack of faith and lack of confidence tends to be self-reinforcing. And the resulting cascade means that the quality of the conversation further degrades.

辩论的悠久历史与公民参与传统

Bo Seo：我所倡导的良好辩论传统有着极其深厚的历史渊源。这可以一路追溯到古代时期：在古希腊，清晰且具说服力地陈述自己观点、与其他公民展开讨论与辩论的能力，被视为公民资格的基本要求之一——这正是公民参与自我治理的核心真谛所在。随着历史的发展，这一传统在伦敦的茶馆、酒馆和咖啡馆中继续演进：当议会内部正在激烈辩论时，普通市民也会自发聚集在一起，就相同的议题展开辩论，仿佛他们也是议会议员一样，在亲身践行自我治理。这种传统后来在美国得以延续，许多开国先勋在大学里创立辩论社团，他们将培养并向整个国家注入这种辩论精神，视为自身作为国家领袖和奠基者的核心使命之一。尽管在现代日常生活中，这种传统已经变得越来越难以寻觅，但在美国历史、民主发展史乃至世界历史上，充满活力的公开辩论曾几何时正是普通人日常生活不可或缺的一部分。

Original English

Bo Seo: The tradition of good argument that I'm trying to advocate for is very much rooted in history. It goes back all the way to antiquity where in ancient Greece the ability to make your point persuasively to engage other citizens in discussion and debate was seen as a kind of a requirement of citizenship. that it's what it meant for us to govern ourselves. And that developed through tea houses and pubs and coffee houses in London where while the debates of Parliament were going on, citizens would gather and have the same debates between themselves and to kind of play out as though they were parliamentarians, as though they were governing themselves. And that tradition kind of carries forth in the United States with a lot of the founding fathers starting debate clubs and colleges viewing part of their role as leaders and as founders of a new country as instilling in the nation that spirit of debate. And though that tradition has become harder and harder to discern in our everyday lives, there have been periods in the history of the US and the history of democracies and the history of the world where those debates were a feature of people's day-to-day lives.

詹姆斯·法默与马尔科姆·X的辩论：三大核心启示

Bo Seo：说到这里，我脑海中浮现出的一场极具代表性的高规格辩论，就是民权运动领袖詹姆斯·法默（James Farmer）与马尔科姆·X（Malcolm X）之间的一系列历史性辩论。他们两人对于种族解放的实现路径以及应对种族主义的正确策略持有截然不同的观点。这是一系列在表面上同属一个阵营、总体目标在某种意义上相互结盟的两人之间展开的分歧讨论；然而，他们丝毫不避讳在公众视野中以极其坦诚、有力、毫无保留的方式公开表达分歧。他们之所以敢于这样做，是因为他们彼此清楚对方会给予尊重的回应，清楚双方都能坦率地直面不同观点，而且坚信通过这样的深入对话，他们能够抵达凭借个人单打独斗所无法达到的思想高度。

Original English

Bo Seo: So, one high-profile debate that comes to mind is the series of debates between the civil rights leader James Farmer and Malcolm X, who had a different view of what racial liberation looked like, what the appropriate response to racism was. And these were a series of disagreements between people who were ostensibly on the same side, whose objectives were in some sense allied, but they were not shy about voicing their disagreements in really candid, strong, forthroated ways in the view of the public. knowing that the other side would respond respectfully, that they would be candid about the disagreements, and through that conversation that they would be able to get somewhere they couldn't on their own.

Bo Seo：从法默与马尔科姆·X的辩论中，我总结出了三条深刻的经验教训。第一条是系统性训练的重要性。法默师从历史上最伟大的辩论教练之一梅尔文·托尔森（Melvin Tolson）——丹泽尔·华盛顿在电影《伟大辩手》（The Great Debaters）中正是塑造了他的形象；而马尔科姆·X也是在监狱的辩论项目中找到了自己的发声方式，该项目允许服刑人员与当地大学和学校展开辩论，为他们通过研读文本和彼此交流所形成的思想提供了表达的出口。因此，第一点启示在于：为了让我们能够再次举办那种能够拓宽人类认知边界的公共辩论，我们必须开始进行系统性训练，必须着手训练年轻一代，同时也必须训练我们自己，使我们具备参与这类深度对话的能力。

Original English

Bo Seo: There are three lessons that I take away from the farmer Malcolm X debates. The first is the importance of training. farmer came up under one of the great debate coaches who ever lived, Melvin Tolson, who was memorialized actually by Denzel Washington in that movie, The Great Debaters. And Malcolm X too found his voice through a debate program in prison that allowed inmates to debate against local colleges and local schools and to find an outlet for the kind of thinking that they had done through engagement with texts and with each other. So the first is in order for us to be able to host the kinds of public debates again that enlarges our understanding of what's possible, we have to start training and we have to start training our young people. We have to train ourselves to be able to engage in those conversations.

Bo Seo：第二条教训是辩论形式与规则的重要性。在这两位人物的辩论中——有些发生在大学校园，有些在电视上播出，有些则通过广播电台播放——令人震惊的是他们被赋予了多么充裕的时间来阐明自己的观点。那绝非现代电视上常见的一两分钟发言紧接着就互相打断的模式。通常情况下，他们会获得非常充裕的时间来完整铺陈自己的论点，因为双方心里都清楚：当一个人讲完后，另一个人会完整发言，之后自己还会获得下一轮发言机会。因此，他们能够——有时甚至是必须——静下心来耐心倾听，心里明白属于自己的反驳机会并不是在这一秒插话，而是在随后的轮次中到来。这充分证明了：我们让两人针锋相对时所设立的对话形式与结构具有不可忽视的重要性。

Original English

Bo Seo: The second is the importance of format. So in the debates between these two men and some of them were in colleges, some of them were on television, some of them were in radio. It's striking how long they were given to make their points. It wasn't one to two minutes then interruptions. Often they would be given quite a long stretch of time to present their arguments with the knowledge that once they had spoken the other person would speak and they would get another turn. So they could and they were sometimes forced to sit back and listen knowing that their chance was not now, it was coming later. So there's an importance of the kind of format in which we pit two people against one another.

Bo Seo：你在那些辩论中看到的第三点，则是与持有异见的人建立一种超越分歧本身的深层人际关系的重要性。在这些万众瞩目的公开辩论舞台背后，法默与马尔科姆·X在私下里一直在深入了解彼此。他们会登门拜访对方的家庭。正是通过建立这种超越具体观点分歧的人际纽带——引荐彼此的家人、见识对方生活中的其他侧面、共同参与生活中的其他活动——你往往能够极大拓宽辩论场域内的沟通空间。在辩论中，你因而可以更加坦诚相待，可以毫无保留、直抒胸臆地据理力争，以最真实的态度面对对手，因为你知道双方之间有一段坚实的人际关系在托底，无论争论多么激烈都不会彻底崩塌。

Original English

Bo Seo: The third thing that you see in those debates is the importance of having a relationship with the person that you're disagreeing with that's greater than just the disagreement itself. So behind the scenes of these big public encounters, Farmer and Malcolm X were getting to know one another. They were going to one another's houses. 从边缘者的沉默到学会倾听

Bo Seo：我最初开始接触辩论，是因为我在小学三年级时从韩国移民到了澳大利亚，当时我几乎完全不会说英语。我很快发现，跨越语言与文化隔阂最艰难的部分，其实是适应现实生活中的真实人际对话——在对话中，人们可能会在说到一半时毫无预兆地转向完全不同的话题，人们的面部表情和肢体动作往往与他们嘴里说出的话并不一致。而且我发现，所有这些理解上的困难在发生争论时都会被成倍放大：在争论中，情绪往往更加激动，人们更容易做出破坏性举动，频繁互相打断，并且会在意想不到的时刻突然加快或放慢语速。面对这一切困境，我当时的应对策略是在学校里表现得极为顺从迎合，把自己的所有想法和观点都深藏在心底。而彻底改变这一切、将我从这种沉默闭塞状态中解救出来的契机，是我加入了学校的辩论队——这一切都源于我小学五年级老师所给出的一个承诺：“在辩论中，当一个人讲话时，其他任何人都不得发言。”对于一个早就习惯了在日常交谈中不断被打断、被边缘化并被排挤出对话圈子的人来说，这句承诺听起来简直就像是一种救赎。

Original English

Bo Seo: So I started debating because I moved in the third grade from South Korea to Australia without really speaking English. And I quickly found the hardest part of crossing language lines of cultural lines was adjusting to real life conversation where people can mid-sentence pivot into talking about something else where what their faces say and what their gestures say don't always match the words that are coming out of their mouths. And I found that all of those difficulties tended to compound in disagreements where passions tend to run and people tend to be more disruptive, to interrupt, to speed up and slow down at unexpected moments. And in response to all that, I resolved to be very agreeable in the way in which I presented at school and to keep my thoughts to myself. And the thing that changed that and shook me out of that was I joined the debate team off the strength of one promise from my fifth grade elementary school teacher which was that in debate when one person speaks no one else does. And to someone who had been used to being interrupted and spun out of conversation that sounded to me like a kind of salvation.

Bo Seo：作为一个真正处于边缘、语言不通、在操场和教室里都显得格格不入的局外人，我所学到的最重要事情之一，就是学会倾听。我学会了察言观色、解读整个房间的氛围；在自己开口发声之前，我学会了先摸清周围环境的温度与情绪。我之所以学会这样做，首先是为了保护自己免受伤害，但更是因为这是让我自己的声音最终能被他人听到的唯一途径。事实证明，许多最顶尖、最成功的辩手往往都来自这种微边缘的背景，拥有身处外围者的独特视角。我认为其中的原因在于：虽然人们往往更容易把注意力集中在站在讲台前发表辩论演讲的那个人身上，但辩论的大部分实际博弈以及在辩论中脱颖而出的核心策略，实际上都依赖于极其专注的倾听——不仅要聚精会神地听取对手所说的每一个字，还要站在听众与评委的视角去倾听，从而精准预判他们接下来最需要听到什么样的论证。因此，对我而言，最初作为一种劣势和必须克服的障碍起步的经历，最终却转化为了巨大的核心竞争优势。因为我认为辩论最伟大的启示之一就是：为了让自己的声音被听见，你必须首先学会倾听。

Original English

Bo Seo: One of the things that I learned as a person really on the margins who didn't really speak the language, was visibly out of place on the playground and in the and in the classroom was I learned to listen. I learned to read a room. I learned to take the temperature of a place before I ever raised my voice. And I learned to do that not to get hurt first of all, but also because it was the only way in which I would ever be heard. And it so happens that many of the most successful debaters tend to come from these slightly marginal backgrounds to have the perspective of someone on the periphery. And I think the reason for that is because though it's easier to focus on the person at the front of the room delivering a debate speech, much of the action of debate and much of the strategy of getting ahead in debate involves listening really intently not only to what one's opponent is saying, but also to listen from the perspective of an audience so that you might be able to anticipate what they might need to hear next. And so what started for me as a kind of a liability or uh a disadvantage that I had to overcome ended up being a tremendous strength because I think one of the great lessons of debate is in order to be heard, you have to first listen.

信息食谱的多样性与跨文化适应

Bo Seo：一场辩论的质量上限，完全取决于辩论者带入其中的信息储备、知识底蕴与思维技巧。而我们当下所看到的最令人担忧的现象之一，就是大众的“信息食谱”严重缺乏多样性与丰富度，根本无法支撑起我们所期望拥有的那种高质量对话。首先，我们绝不能允许有线电视上充斥的所谓辩论秀，成为我们本应在日常生活中亲自展开的真实分歧探讨的替代品。我们的政治领袖或我们最喜爱的媒体名嘴，绝不能变成我们外包独立思考与自主对话的化身代理人。因此，第一原则就是：不要将大众媒体视为主体思考的替代品，而应将其视为获取思维武器和知识养分的来源，进而将这些知识迁移运用到我们自己日常参与的对话当中。

Original English

Bo Seo: Debates are only as good as the information and the knowledge and the skills that debaters bring to it. And one of the more concerning things that we see at the moment is people's information diets not being sufficiently varied, not being sufficiently rich to sustain the kinds of conversations that we want to have. The first thing is we cannot allow the debates that we see on cable television to be a kind of a replacement for the disagreements that we should be having in our day-to-day lives. So our political leaders or our favorite media personalities can't be like avatars to whom we outsource the work of thinking for ourselves and having these conversations for ourselves. So the first is viewing media not as a substitution but as a source of equipment and knowledge that we can then transfer into the kinds of conversations that we have.

Bo Seo：第二点在于，我们在获取信息时必须保持信息来源的丰富性与多元化。党派化媒体和信息茧房所赖以滋生的心理冲动之一，正是我们渴望获得自我认同的心理本能——我们希望新闻媒体能够如实回弹并印证我们对世界既有的既定信念。然而，正如辩论能够激励我们主动探寻不同观点、将差异视为富有建设性、发人深省且开阔眼界的契机一样，我们也应该用同样的视角来审视我们所接收的各种不同信号、信息与多维视角；因为唯有更丰富、更多元的信息食谱，才能让我们看清更多维度的现实，并持之以恒地挑战和反思自身的固有思维。

Original English

Bo Seo: The second thing is having a a variety and a diversity of sources that we turn to to get our information. And one of the impulses that partisan media, siloed information sources feed off is our desire to be validated, to have news reflect back to us what we already believe to be true about the world.
