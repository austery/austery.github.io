保密协议渗透富豪日常生活

Will Jarvis: 几十年来，在全美范围内，保密协议（Non-Disclosure Agreements，简称 NDA）主要流行于商业领域，目的是防止员工泄露公司机密。但如今，《纽约时报》发现，保密协议已成为富豪日常生活中不可或缺的必备工具。

现在，人们要求管家、私人厨师、家庭教师、景观设计师、健身教练——任何进入他们私人宅邸的人——签署保密协议。康涅狄格州一家律师事务所的执行合伙人表示，在过去的十年里，他收到的关于保密协议的咨询量轻松增加了上百倍（increased a hundredfold）。

他说，推动这一趋势的一个因素并不令人意外：如今随手拍张照片上传到网上变得前所未有的容易。互联网让保守秘密变得困难重重。为了应对这种情况，保密协议文件本身也变得越来越复杂。一家专门提供保姆和其他家庭工作人员的家政机构创始人表示，过去只有简短一个段落的合同，现在甚至可以长达 15 页。

有趣的是，富人并不是唯一热衷于保密协议的群体。特朗普政府表示，希望让现有的联邦雇员和新入职员工都签署保密协议，以防止向新闻媒体泄密。而早在几年前，最高法院（Supreme Court）也开始要求法官助理及其他雇员签署保密协议。

英文原文

Will Jarvis: For decades across the US, NDAs, or non-disclosure agreements, were mostly a thing in the business world to try and keep employees from spilling company secrets. But now, The Times has been looking at how they become a go-to tool in the everyday lives of the wealthy. People are asking their housekeepers, private chefs, tutors, landscape designers, fitness trainers, anyone who comes onto their property to sign an NDA. A managing partner at a Connecticut law firm said over the last 10 years, the number of inquiries he gets about NDAs has, quote, easily increased a hundredfold. He said one factor driving it, which isn't that surprising, is that it's never been easier to snap a photo and post something online. The internet makes it hard to keep secrets. To account for that, the documents themselves are also getting more complicated. The founder of a staffing agency that specializes in providing nannies and other household workers said contracts that used to be a simple paragraph can now run 15 pages. Interestingly, the wealthy aren't the only ones leaning into NDAs. The Trump administration has said it wants to make current federal employees and new hires sign them to prevent leaks to news outlets. And as of a few years ago, the Supreme Court started pushing clerks and other employees to agree to NDAs, too.
