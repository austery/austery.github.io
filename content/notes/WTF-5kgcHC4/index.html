三大趋势的根源：战争、建房限制与鲍莫尔成本病

针对这三个各自独立的趋势制作了数个视频之后，我们认为它们是由三种根本不同的因素驱动的，因此需要政客们采取三种截然不同的解决方案。那么，驱动这三个趋势的各自是什么呢？

英文原文 Now, having done several videos on these three individual trends, we believe that they are driven by three fundamentally different things and therefore need three very different solutions from politicians. So what is driving each of these three trends?

让我们从最近的一个开始：飞涨的食品和汽油价格。这两者固然截然不同，但我们把它们归为一类，因为我们认为它们基本上是由相同的两个事件驱动的：首先是乌克兰战争，其次是伊朗战争。

英文原文 Let's start with the most recent one. Skyrocketing food and gas prices. These are of course two very different things, but we put them in one category because we believe that they have been fundamentally driven by the same two events. First, the war in Ukraine and then the war in Iran.

诚然，在疫情期间食品和天然气价格都曾经历过初期的小幅上涨，但真正的爆发是在全球最大的能源、粮食和化肥生产国之一入侵了欧洲的粮仓之后。正如你在这里看到的，食品价格从未真正恢复。幸运的是能源价格曾略有好转，直到伊朗在遭到美国和以色列袭击后关闭了霍尔木兹海峡。由于这场战争再次大幅减少了全球能源供给，天然气价格自然暴涨。而且由于天然气也是化肥生产的关键原料，伊朗战争不太可能让食品价格在短期内回落。这就是为什么我们认为食品和天然气价格上涨的主要驱动力是外部战争。

英文原文 Sure, both food and gas prices got an initial small bump during Covid, but they really took off after one of the world's biggest energy, food and fertilizer producers invaded the grain basket of Europe. As you can see here, food prices never truly recovered. Luckily, energy prices did get a bit better until Iran closest traded for moves after it was attacked by the U.S. and Israel. 相比之下，尽管全球房价都有所上涨，但当我们为之前的视频梳理关于其真实成因的三大主要理论时，发现这主要是当地未能建造足够房屋的问题，至少在人们真正想居住的地方没有建足够的房。这与2007年大不相同。那时房子同样让人难以负担，但那主要是由于投机泡沫。而如今在全球各大城市空置房比例处于历史低位这一事实，在我们看来，指向的是全球范围内大规模的建设失败，而非投机问题。

英文原文 In contrast, while house prices have gone up across the globe, when we for a previous video went over the three major theories about what's actually causing it, we found that it's mostly a local problem of not building enough, at least not where people actually want to live. And that's very different from 2007 back then. Houses were unaffordable also, but that was mostly due to a speculative bubble. And the fact that in global cities now there's a historically low fraction of empty homes. Well, in our opinion, that points to a massive global construction failure, not a speculation problem.

这种情况往往是因为当地人有极强的动机去阻止新建住房，这种现象被称为邻避主义（NIMBYism）。也就是说，对大多数选民而言建造更多住房是一件好事，但“别建在我家后院”。因此在如今的大多数城市建房极其困难。分区规则、高度限制、车位配比要求以及漫无休止的许可审批，使建设量远远落后于日益增长的城市人口需求。当更多的人争夺固定数量的房屋时，房东就会做出任何人都会做的事：挑选最稳妥的租客或出价最高的人。

Zoning rules, height limits, parking requirements, and endless permitting have kept construction far below what growing urban populations need. When more people compete for a fixed number of homes. Then landlords do what anyone would do. Pick the safest tenants or the highest bidder.

诚然，日益加剧的不平等可能会重新排定队伍，你可以说把没有大学学位的打工人进一步推到了队伍的后面，这确实是个问题。但这并没有改变根本的大问题：在人们真正想住的地方没有建造足够的房屋。因此如你所见，尽管食品加天然气危机和住房危机都是可负担性危机的一部分，但它们可能需要完全不同的应对措施，因为它们的起因截然不同。

英文原文 Sure, rising inequality can reshuffle the line, you could say, and push workers without a college degree. Further to the back of that line. And that's a problem. But this doesn't change. The underlying big problem is that not enough homes are being built where people actually want to live. So as you can see, while both the food plus gas and housing crisis are part of the affordability crisis, they will probably need a very different response because they are caused by very different things.

但在探讨这些对策之前，我们还必须讨论驱动可负担性危机的第三个也是最后一个因素：托育、教育和医疗保健等基础服务长达数十年的价格上涨。正如我们在最近的视频中所解释的那样，原因可能很反常识——恰恰是因为电视和衣服变得如此便宜，基础服务才变得昂贵得多。能够解释这一现象的理论被称为鲍莫尔成本病（Baumol's cost disease）。

英文原文 But before getting to these solutions, we still have to talk about the third and final factor driving the affordability crisis. The decade long price increases of essential services like childcare, education and health care. As we explained in a recent video. The reason may paradoxically be that precisely because TVs and clothing have become so much cheaper, essential services have gotten much more expensive. The theory that can explain this is called Baumol's cost disease.

为了理解它的含义，我们可以把托育行业的发展与汽车工业做个对比。今天的幼儿园保育员并不比一个世纪前的保育员效率更高，毕竟一个人能照顾的幼儿数量是有限的。相反，汽车工厂现在每个工人生产的汽车数量远远超过100年前。但托儿所仍然必须向其员工支付跟上整个经济水平的工资，否则这些员工就会跳槽去薪酬更高的工作岗位。

英文原文 And to understand what it means, let's compare the evolution of childcare to the car industry. A nursery worker today is not more productive than one a century ago. After all, he or she can only look after so many toddlers. A car factory, by contrast, produces vastly more cars per worker than it did 100 years ago. But the nursery still has to pay its staff wages that keep up with the rest of the economy, or these workers will leave to better paid jobs.

因此，托育的成本年复一年上涨，即使或者说确切地是因为照料幼儿所付出的努力几乎没有任何改变。大学教育和医疗保健的运作方式大致相同：它们都是劳动密集型且难以自动化的行业。如果你仔细观察通胀统计数据，就能清楚看到这些服务是如何全都变得更加昂贵的，而玩具、电视和微波炉等量产商品的价格却大幅缩水。

英文原文 So the cost of childcare rises year after year, even though or actually precisely because the effort it takes to look after a toddler has barely changed college education and health care work largely the same way. They are labor intensive and hard to automate. And if you look closely at inflation statistics, you can see exactly how these services have all become much more expensive. While the price of mass produced goods like toys, TVs and microwaves has collapsed.

现在你可能会问：等一下，你不是在欧洲吗？欧洲的医疗保健实际上仍然非常便宜承受得起，这难道不只是美国的问题吗？是的，部分如此。确实，在美国做一次剖腹产或核磁共振检查的花费可能是西班牙或德国的数倍。但作为一个背负极高税单的欧洲人，我有个坏消息要告诉你：由政府为医疗保健买单并没有治愈成本病，它只是把这些成本转嫁给了政府。

英文原文 Now you may say, but wait a minute. Aren't you in Europe, where health care is actually still very much affordable? Isn't that really just a US problem? Yes, partially. Sure, a C-section or an MRI can cost several times more in the US than in Spain or Germany. But as a European with a sky high tax bill, I have bad news for you. The government paying for health care does not cure cost disease. It just transfers these costs to the government.

也就是说，虽然我的医疗账单一直保持在很低的可承受水平，教育支出也是如此，但这里的政府现在却陷入了深重危机，因为在实施哪种紧缩政策上无法达成一致。这很大程度上是因为政府在医疗保健方面的支出一直在持续攀升。总而言之，尽管食品和天然气、住房以及基础服务都需要变得更能让人承受以解决可负担性危机，但它们的驱动因素截然不同。因此我们在本视频中认为，它们需要三种完全不同的解决方案。

英文原文 That is while my medical bills have remained very affordable and the same for education bills. The government here is now in a deep crisis because it cannot agree what austerity to implement. And for a big part, that's because government healthcare spending just keeps going up. So in summary, while food and gas, housing and essential services all needs to become more affordable to solve the affordability crisis, they are driven by very different things. And therefore we argue in this video that they require three very different solutions.

降价的理论路径与价格管制的陷阱

为了解决生活成本危机，我们提出了一个三部分构成的解决方案，从根源上应对上述每个成因。不过，为了充分理解该方案，我们仍需简要解释一下现实中如何才能降低价格。

英文原文 So to solve the affordability crisis, we actually propose a three part solution plan that tackles each of these causes at the root. However, to understand the plan fully, we still briefly have to explain how you can actually reduce prices.

从本质上讲，经济学中有三种降低价格的途径。前两种可以通过著名的供求相互作用来理解：如果人口增长，对住房的需求增加，其价格就会上涨；但如果随后建造了更多住房，价格就会下降。理论其实很简单：如果我们希望必需品变得更便宜，要么减少对它们的需求，要么增加它们的供给。

英文原文 Essentially, in economics, there are three ways to reduce prices. The first two can be understood through the interplay of the famous supply and demand. If the population grows, more houses are demanded and their price will go up. But if more houses are than being built, the price will go down. The theory is really simple. If we want the essentials to become more affordable, we need to either reduce demand for them or increase their supply.

然而，因为我们讨论的是必需品，其核心特征就是你无法真正削减对食品、教育、医疗和住房的需求，因此在大多数（而非全部）情况下，我们倾向的解决方案毫无意外是必须找到增加住房、能源、食品和服务供给的方法。

英文原文 However, because we are talking about essentials here, where the entire idea is that you cannot really reduce the demand for food, education, medical care and homes, it should come as no surprise that our preferred solution in most of the three cases, not all, is that we need to find a way to increase the supply of homes, energy, food and services.

但有趣的是，许多公开辩论的重点并不在此。例如，当《华尔街日报》询问选民如何解决可负担性危机时，最受欢迎的一些回答包括：限制处方药价格、限制房租上涨、限制大型投资者购房、为劳动家庭设立托育费用上限。你看，限制价格、管制租金，这把我们引向了工具箱中的第三种潜在工具——一种在初级经济学中很少学到的工具。事实上，有时跟经济学家谈论这个会惹来很多非议。也就是说：如果价格上涨太快，政府难道不能直接要求供应商别涨价吗？

英文原文 But interestingly, that's not what a lot of the debate has been about. For example, when the Wall Street Journal asked voters how to fix the affordability crisis, some of the most popular answers were capping the price of prescription drugs, limit rent increases, restricting large investors from buying homes, capping childcare costs for working families. You see, capping prices, controlling the rent. This brings us to our third potential tool in the toolbox, a tool that you rarely learn much about in economics 101. In fact, you can get in a lot of trouble sometimes talking about it with economists. That is, if prices are rising too fast, can't the government just tell suppliers not to do that?

是的，在理论上，价格管制有时确实是正确的解决方案——也就是在缺乏充分竞争的情况下。在某些情况下，价格上涨可能不仅是因为供给天生不足，而是垄断者选择限制供应，以便索取更高价格来获取更高利润。

英文原文 Yes, actually, in theory, price controls can be the right solution sometimes. That is, if there's not enough competition. You see, in some cases, prices may not just go up because there's not enough supply inherently, but rather because monopolists choose to restrict supply so they can charge a higher price for higher profits.

然而，尽管我们认为价格管制有时能带来暂时的缓解，但我们并不认为它能真正解决可负担性危机，因为我们讨论的所有市场实际上都竞争相当充分。这里有许许多多的农民，其中很多人赚不到多少利润；有众多的石油供应商、众多的房东，还有许许多多的学校和医院。在这些情况下，价格管制可能会带来许多非预期的后果，比如短缺或黑市。

英文原文 However, while we think price controls can sometimes bring temporary relief, we do not actually think that it can truly solve the affordability crisis because all of the markets that we are talking about are actually pretty competitive. There are many farmers and many of them do not earn much of a profit. There are many oil suppliers, there are many landlords, and there are many, many schools and hospitals. In these cases, price controls can have many unintended consequences like shortages or black markets.

为了明白其中的原因，设想一下，如果我们试图通过直接告诉养鸡农户不能再索取高得离谱的价格，以此将鸡肉价格拉回到乌克兰战争前的水平。如果全美只有一家庞大的养鸡企业，这种方法在缺乏竞争的情况下可能奏效：庞大的养鸡垄断企业可以随心所欲定高价，至少远高于养殖成本，公司赚取暴利而许多家庭无法每周吃得起鸡肉。此时政府介入限制价格，该公司无法再通过提价多赚钱，增加利润的唯一途径就是养殖并销售更多的鸡。于是它扩大养殖规模，最终以更低的价格提供了更多的鸡肉，皆大欢喜。

英文原文 And to see why, imagine that we try to bring back the price of chickens to their level before the Ukraine war by simply telling chicken farmers they cannot charge such ridiculously high prices anymore. If there was just one giant chicken company in the whole country, this approach could actually work with no competition. The giant chicken monopoly can charge whatever it wants for chicken, at least far above what it costs to raise these birds. The company will make huge profits and many families cannot afford chickens every week. Now the government comes in caps the price. The company can no longer earn more by charging more. So the only way left to grow its profits is to sell and raise more chickens. So it raises these chickens, and you end up with more chickens at a lower price. Everybody's happy.

但现在设想鸡肉市场并非垄断，而是成千上万的小农户在相互竞争。顾客喜欢最低的价格，如果某种鸡肉变贵了，他们就会换买便宜的。价格通常已经非常接近养鸡的成本了。如果把价格压到成本线以下，对于这些小农户来说，养鸡就会迅速变得无利可图。
