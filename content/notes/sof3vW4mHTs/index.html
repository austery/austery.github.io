创立初衷与早期背景

那么，你们大家当初为什么要创立 Anthropic 呢？ 现在回想起来，那种感觉依然如同当年：我们知晓一个世人尚不知晓的秘密。我认为，如果你真正看到了这项技术的本质，你就永远无法抽身离开了。

我当年到普林斯顿读博士时，本是想做理论物理研究的。 在加入 Anthropic 之前，我是约翰斯·霍普金斯大学的一名教授。 我们大家都很羡慕过去的那些伟大科学发现。 我的博士论文方向是理论物理。 大学毕业后，我投身于全球发展领域工作。 在做这些之前，我曾是一名记者，对数据中心有着极度痴迷的研究。 事情将会变得不可思议。 我想我过去把自己看作是一名数学家和程序员。 我早在 2016 年就进入了 AI 领域，而当时的人工智能连话都不会说。 毕业之后，我去了斯坦福大学，在癌症生物标志物方向做博士后研究。

从很小的时候起，Dario 和我就对如何让世界变得更美好充满好奇。 我当时对如何将计算机科学作为造福世界的工具这一问题非常感兴趣。 我当时对自己正在做的物理研究感到有一点受挫，而 AI 看起来令人兴奋，就像一阵清新的空气。 在 2013 或 2014 年，我第一次开始看到 AI 领域的重大成果，随即思考推动这项技术的发展是否会是推进生物学研究的最佳途径。

英文原文 So, why did you all start Anthropic? It still feels like then, in that we knew a secret the rest of the world didn’t know. I think if you truly see what this is, you can’t ever walk away. I showed up at Princeton as a PhD student who wanted to do theoretical physics. I was a professor at Johns Hopkins before Anthropic. We’re all envious of the great discoveries of the past. My PhD thesis is in theoretical physics. Out of college, I went and worked in global development. I was a journalist before I did any of this, and I studied data centers obsessively. It’s going to get weird. I guess I thought of myself as a mathematician and programmer. I got into AI back in 2016, and at the time, an AI couldn’t talk. After I graduated, I went to Stanford and did my postdoc in cancer biomarkers. From a very early age, Dario and I were really curious about how to make the world better. I was very interested in questions of how we could use computer science as a tool that is good for the world. I was a little bit frustrated with the physics research I was doing, and AI seemed exciting and like a breath of fresh air. 2013 or 2014, I first started seeing the big results in AI, then wondered if advancing this technology would be the best way to advance biology.

规模扩展与算力规律

我们看到了我们试图在世界上产生的影响，与 AI 在实现其中诸多目标方面的潜力之间存在着关联。 早在 2018 年，Anthropic 未来的联合创始人都在从事着许多不同且分散的工作。 我们中的一群人当时都在 OpenAI，从事模型训练、政策制定、可解释性以及安全等各方面的工作。 我当时开始研究这些尝试预测下一个词的语言模型。 那时很多人认为，你需要明确教会网络如何规划、如何推理以及如何使用工具。 Dario 注意到了这一趋势，他将其称为“大团算力”（the big blob of compute）。 基本上就是造一台更大的机器、造一台更强大的计算机，并把更多的数据塞进去。 当你投入更多的算力和更多的数据时，你就会获得不断提高的智能，而且它处于一条可预测的曲线上。 我们当时每次扩大规模，都会看到这些新行为和新能力的涌现。 到那时你就会意识到：哇，如果这种情况持续下去，我们可能正处于一个全新现实、一种全新技术形态的悬崖边。 人类水平的智能、人类水平的 AI 绝非遥不可及的百年之后，它实际上就在我们的有生之年。

英文原文 We saw this connection between the level of impact we were trying to have in the world and AI’s potential to really achieve a lot of those goals. Back in 2018, the future co-founders of Anthropic were doing a lot of different and disparate things. A group of us, all at OpenAI, we were working on training models, we were working on policy, we were working on interpretability, we were working on safety. I started working on these language models where you try to predict the next word. A lot of folks thought at the time, you’re going to need to teach the network explicitly how to plan and how to reason and how to use tools. Dario noticed the trend, which he called the big blob of compute. Just basically making a bigger machine, making a bigger computer, and jamming more data into it. When you put in more compute and more data, you get increasing intelligence, and it’s on this predictable line. We were just seeing each time you scale it up, these new behaviors emerge, these new abilities emerge. At which point you realize, wow, if this continues, we might be on the precipice of a totally new reality, a totally new form of technology. Human-level intelligence, human-level AI is not 100 years in the future, but it’s actually within our lifetimes.

这些极其强大的模型可能会在医疗保健、机器人技术方面带来巨大飞跃。 计算机程序、新颖的科学构想、数学定理。 也许我们能把癌症发病率降低 90%。 也许心脏病、肥胖症以及所有主要传染病都将成为历史。 世界上任何受限于人类智慧瓶颈的问题，突然之间你都拥有了更强的解决能力。 看到 AI 的潜在进展令人兴奋。 但与此同时，我认为这也很可怕。 如果你正在打造某种比你自己更强大、或者可能成长为比你自己更强大的东西，无论你从哪个角度思考，其潜在风险都是巨大的。 我认为自己当时还没能看清全貌。 我认为没人能看清。 但我坚信这可能会对安全和社会产生重大影响。 这项技术——如果说它有什么可类比的话（尽管我认为它在很大程度上是无法类比的）——它更类似于药物，或是解开原子的秘密：这些东西具有巨大的价值，但同时也可能酿成大错。 我们确实需要以正确的方式去构建它。 你会感受到这种巨大的责任。 我是世界上极少数能够理解这项技术将会带来什么后果的人之一。 我们最想做的事，就是在对安全有着深厚承诺的环境中共同工作。 我们认定必须创立 Anthropic，因为我们必须建立一个能够适应这项技术对我们所有人提出的要求的组织。

英文原文 These kinds of very powerful models could cause enormous strides in healthcare, in robotics. Computer programs, novel scientific ideas, mathematical theorems. Maybe we could reduce cancer rates by 90%. Maybe heart disease, obesity, all of the major infections will just be a thing of the past. Any of the problems of the world that are bottlenecked by human ingenuity, suddenly you have much more ability to solve them. Seeing the potential progress in AI was exciting. At the same time, I think it was scary. If you’re building something that’s more powerful than yourself or could grow to be more powerful than yourself, the potential risks of that are enormous, no matter how you think about it. I don’t think I understood the full picture yet. I don’t think anyone did. But I definitely believed that there could be big safety and societal effects. This was a technology that, if it had any analogy at all, which I think it mostly didn’t, it would be more analogous to pharmaceuticals or unlocking the secrets of the atom— things that have immense value, but also can go wrong. We really needed to build it in the right way. It’s just this huge responsibility that you feel. I’m one of the very few people in the world that understands what the consequences of this technology are going to be. The thing we most wanted to do was work together in an environment with this deep commitment to safety. We decided we had to build Anthropic because we had to build an organization fit for the demands this technology was going to place on all of us.

成立公益企业与公司命名

我们必须做出的首批重大决定之一，就是我们想成为一家什么样的公司。 这是一场比大家预想中更为深入和复杂的讨论。 我们知道，我们必须有能力建造地球上最大的一些计算机，训练前沿技术，寻找将该技术部署到世界上的途径，并坦诚面对这项技术的困难方面及其益处。 最终，我们选择了公益企业（public benefit corporation）的形式。 这让我们成为一家不仅完全由经济激励驱动、而且还受到为世界做好事这一激励驱动的公司。 在某些时候，我们会希望把使命置于利润之上，或者置于为股东提供最大价值之上。 因此，我们在 2021 年 1 月 26 日注册成立了一家公益企业，随后全力以赴，开始打造这一切。

英文原文 One of the first big decisions we had to make was just what kind of company we wanted to be. It was a more involved and complicated discussion than you might expect. We knew that we had to be able to build some of the largest computers on the planet, train frontier technology, find ways to deploy that technology into the world, and be honest about the difficult aspects of the technology as well as its benefits. We eventually landed on public benefit corporation. That would set us up as a company that was not just fully governed by economic incentives, but also by the incentive to do right by the world. There are going to be times when we’re going to want to prioritize the mission at the cost of profits, or at the cost of providing the maximum value to a shareholder. So we incorporated as a public benefit company on January 26, 2021, and then it was off to the races, time to build this thing.

这是一份大家为 Anthropic 起的名字建议清单。 看，显然这是我列出的一些名字： Squirrel（松鼠）。 Owl（猫头鹰）。 Whale（鲸鱼）。 Differentiated（差异化）。 Humane（人道）。 “Anthropic”是 Jared 贡献的名字。 我意识到，既然“Anthropic”的意思是“以人为本”或“以人类为中心”，对于一家希望 AI 朝着符合全人类最大利益方向发展的人工智能公司而言，这似乎是一个很好的名字。 然后我们查看了能买到的域名。 我认为 Anthropic.com 显然是其中最便宜的。 我觉得这是选择 Anthropic 的一个非常充分的理由。 电子表格里有一条注释写着：“我们喜欢这个名字，它很好。” 还有：“域名可用吗？” “是的。”

英文原文 This is a list of names people proposed for what we could call Anthropic. Here are some names I listed, apparently. Squirrel. Owl. Whale. Differentiated. Humane. Anthropic was contributed by Jared. I realized that since Anthropic means human-oriented or human-centered, that it seemed like a good name for an AI company that wants AI to be developed in the best interests of humanity. Then we looked at domain names that we could get. Anthropic.com was, I think, by far the cheapest. I thought that was a pretty good argument to go with Anthropic. There was a comment in the spreadsheet that says, “We like the name, it is good.” And, “Is domain available?” “Yes.”

初创期的探索与 Claude 的诞生

刚开始的时候非常混乱。 每个人都在干极其繁重、毫无光彩可言的杂活。 Tom 是那个不得不去为我们搞定 AWS 集群的人。 我和 Chris Olah 搭建了第一个网站。 Dario 专注于融资，而我负责招聘。 有短短几个月的时间，我们的首席科学家 Jared Kaplan 甚至还在管发薪水。 这足以说明早期运作的专业程度了。 我认为我们当时花了大量精力来摸索语言模型内部究竟存在着什么。 你会发现里面包含的所有这些我们在进入之前一无所知的精妙之物。 它当时其实完全就是一个研究实验室。 我们只是在产出我们认为可能非常强大且极具经济价值的技术，但也可能只是一种新奇事物或科学实验。 我记得的一件事是，每天醒来都感到无比兴奋，能和自己最好的朋友们一起投入到我所能想象的最有趣的项目中。 能与理念如此契合的人共事，感觉真是棒极了。 此外，能够从零开始搭建所有的基础设施也是乐趣无穷。 我们努力建立一个专注于训练大语言模型、并将这些模型与我们的价值观对齐的组织。 这就是我们创立 Anthropic 的原因。

英文原文 It was so chaotic at the beginning. Everyone was doing extremely unglamorous schlep. Tom was the one who had to go get us the AWS clusters. Me and Chris Olah built the first website. Dario focused on fundraising, and I was working on recruiting. There was briefly a few months where Jared Kaplan, our Chief Scientist, ran payroll. That tells you the level of professionalism of the early operation. I think there was a lot of work of us just getting oriented to what kinds of things exist in a language model. You find all these beautiful things inside that we didn’t know about going in. It was really nothing other than a research lab. We’re just producing technology that we think might be really, really powerful and economically valuable, but might also just be a curiosity or a science experiment. One thing I remember was every day just waking up feeling really excited to get to work on the most fun project that I could imagine with my best friends. It just felt great to be with people who shared our values so deeply. Also, just getting to build all the infrastructure from scratch was a lot of fun. We were trying to build an organization focused on training large language models, aligning those models with our values. That was why we founded Anthropic.

当时有过很多争论：我们是要把重点更多放在构建一个更纯粹的聊天机器人上，还是把软件工程作为我们需要聚焦的核心？ 最终，我们得出的结论是，实际上这两方面你都必须做得非常出色。 这是我 2021 年写的一份文档。作为概念验证，我们当时正致力于开发一个通用 AI 助手，它能够以对话方式与用户互动，从而创建、编辑和解释软件。 我们当时的座右铭是，希望助手做到有帮助、无害且诚实（helpful, harmless, and honest）。 我们一开始让它变得有帮助。 接着，我们开始致力于让它也做到无害，然后我们推出了那个版本。 但它一点都不诚实。 它总是胡编乱造。 它曾经非常努力地试图说服我们：只吃土豆的饮食是完全可行的，而且对身体非常非常健康、非常有益。 你可以让模型进入“恶龙模式”，它就会开始用全大写字母冲你咆哮。 早期的模型有一种独特的魅力。 它们出发点很好，但有点笨拙。 后来在 2022 年中，我们做出了一个模型——我记得最初代号是 Junior，最终命名为 Claude——它真的很出色。 你可以跟它交谈。 它确实能回答问题。 我记得刚开始跟它交谈时感到非常惊讶。 噢，哇，这基本上行得通了。你基本上可以与这个模型对话了。 我过去常常做一个被称为“直升机测试”的实验，我会问 AI 系统： “你一顿饭能吃多少架直升机？” 早期的聊天机器人会说： “嗯，你知道的，一架或者两架，这完全取决于情况。” 然后有一天，我问早期的 Claude 模型： “一个人一次能吃多少架直升机？” 该模型回答说： “这似乎是一个脑筋急转弯。我不认为人类可以吃直升机。” 就在那时我说： “这些系统变聪明了，它通过了直升机测试。”

英文原文 There was a lot of debate at the time of, do we want to focus more on building something that’s more of a pure chatbot, or is software engineering the thing that we need to focus on? In the end, we landed on, actually, you just need to do both of those really well. This was a document I wrote in 2021. As a proof of concept, we were working to develop a general AI assistant that can interact conversationally with the user to create, edit, and explain software. Our motto at that time was we wanted assistants to be helpful, harmless, and honest. We started out making it helpful. Then we started doing work on making it harmless as well, and then we had that version. It was not honest at all. It would make stuff up all the time. It really tried to convince us that you could be on an all-potato diet, and that was really, really healthy and really good for you. You could ask the model to go into dragon mode, and it would start screaming at you in all caps. There was a charm to the early models. They were very well-meaning, but a little bit incompetent. And then we made a model in mid-2022, I think it was code-named Junior at first, eventually called it Claude, that was actually good. You could talk to it. It would actually answer questions. I remember starting to talk to it and just being really surprised. Oh, wow, this basically works. You can basically talk to this model. I used to have something called the helicopter test where I’d ask AI systems, “How many helicopters can you eat in a single meal?” And the early chatbots would say, “Well, you know, one or two, that all depends.” And then one day I asked an early Claude model, “How many helicopters can a person eat in one sitting?” And the model said, “This seems to be a trick question. I don’t think people can eat helicopters.” At which point I said, “The systems have got smarter, it passes the helicopter test.”

艰难赶超与技术突破

2023 年 3 月，我们终于正式向全世界公开发布了 Claude。 打造出这款产品让我们感到无比自豪，但很显然，我们当时距离前沿水平还相去甚远。 我记得曾与潜在客户通电话，他们会问： “我们为什么要用你们的模型，而不是别家的？” 我当时心想，你知道，这问得很好，问得太妙了。 我认为那段苦熬的岁月对 Anthropic 极为有益，但这确实是一段非常艰难的时期。 我们当时彻底落后了。 差距不是一点半点。 如果你仅仅去研究较笨的模型，你就不会知道所有可能出问题的方式，也就无法让整个生态系统变得更安全。 这切切实实激发了我们的斗志。 我们必须训练更智能的模型。 我们必须在预训练上做得更好。 我们必须在强化学习（RL）上做得更好。 我们极度专注于两件事：第一，获取算力；第二，积累所需的算力乘数，以便更有效地利用我们拥有的所有算力。 不断通过算法手段让神经网络一点一点地变好，当你积累了足够多的这类改进，你就能获得一个更智能、更善于遵循指令、本质上更出色的助手网络。 随后，Claude 3 Opus 问世了，这是我们第一次拥有了一款真正出色的模型。 紧接着几个月后，Sonnet 3.5 诞生了。 突然之间，模型在编程方面变得切实可用了。 于是我们构建了后来演变为 Claude Code 的工具。 你实际上可以让它在你的电脑上执行任务，它会去为你解决问题。 随后我们将它推向外部，这直接掀起了一波全新的需求浪潮。 那种感觉就像是：噢，我们不仅能够取得成功，而且还能具备竞争力，能够推出世界上最好的一些 AI 模型。

英文原文 March 2023, that’s when we finally released Claude publicly to the world. We were incredibly proud of having built this product, but it was clear that we were pretty far behind the frontier. I remember having calls with potential customers, and they were like, “Why should we use your model as opposed to somebody else’s?” And I was like, you know, that’s a great question, that’s awesome. I think the years of suck were extremely good for Anthropic, but that was a really hard period. We were just completely behind. It was not close. If you’re just studying dumber models, you’re not going to know all of the ways that things could necessarily go wrong, and you’re not going to be able to make the entire ecosystem safer. It just really gave us a kick. We need to train more intelligent models. We need to get better at pretraining. We need to get better at RL. We were maniacally focused on, one, getting compute, and then, two, accumulating the compute multipliers that we needed to make better effective use of all of the compute that we had. Just finding all of these algorithmic ways to make the neural network a little bit better and a little bit better, and you just pile up enough of these, and you get a network that is more intelligent, that is better at following instructions, that’s just a better assistant. And then it arrives, Claude 3 Opus, and for the first time, we had a model that was really good. And then a few months later comes along Sonnet 3.5. Suddenly, models were useful for programming. So we built this tool that would eventually become Claude Code. You could actually ask it to do things on your computer, and it would go and solve problems for you. Then we released it externally, and that just sets off a whole new wave of demand. It felt like, oh, we could not only be successful, but we could be competitive, we could be launching some of the best AI models in the world.

安全挑战与 Glasswing 项目

当我们身处技术前沿、拥有最强大的模型时，我们开展安全研究的实际能力也会更强。 它为你应对所遭遇的问题或挑战赢得了最多的时间。 如果你发现了一个问题，你可能是世界上第一个发现它的。 我们始终公开我们的安全研究成果，因为我们希望整个生态系统都能使用它，尽管这在竞争层面上对我们不利，但这对安全极为有益。 这项技术唯有在你能坦诚面对它的情况下，才能走向良性发展。

在 2025 年或 2026 年之前，关于 AI 为何具有风险一直停留在概念层面的争论。 我认为在过去一年里，事情真正变得更加清晰可见了。 2026 年，风险开始变得真切起来。 Anthropic 本周宣布已开始对其名为 Mythos 的最新 AI 模型进行有限测试。 据称，该版本的先进程度足以在极为广泛的软件应用程序中发现漏洞。 如果你是一名黑客，我想你心里会想： “我想要获得这个模型的使用权限。” 我们训练出了这种级别的模型，审视它时，我们的反应是：天哪。 网络安全领域的专家们注意到，它在发现软件漏洞和利用代码方面的能力确实极其出色，达到了专家级水平。 如果恶意行为者拿到了 Mythos 并试图利用其中的大量漏洞，局面将会变得非常糟糕。 全世界都会陷入糟糕的处境。 我们切实看到了这样一种具体情况：如果我们不改变整个行业的运作方式，某些坏事绝对会发生。

Dario 把全公司召集起来，把管理层召集起来，说道： “我们必须以一种不同于以往发布任何模型的方式来发布这个模型。” 我们决定开展 Glasswing 项目。 它在较小范围的公司中开放了 Mythos 的使用权限，与此同时我们正致力于制定防护措施，以便最终更广泛地提供该模型。 这意味着网络防御人员可以借助 Claude 找出所有漏洞所在，然后切实利用 Claude 帮助他们修复这些漏洞。 这可能并非使收入、利润、价值和资金最大化的绝对最佳方式，但这恰恰是关键所在。 这正是我们设立公益使命的初衷。 我们的体会是，当你以正确的方式做事时，往往在短期内是要付出代价的。 短期内可能会非常痛苦。 但这在很大程度上关乎自律与远见，关乎理解行业和世界长远来看需要什么。

英文原文 Our actual ability to do safety research is better when we are on the frontier, when we have the most capable models. It gives you the greatest amount of time to deal with problems or challenges that you’re running into. If you see an issue, you might be seeing it first. We’ve always published our safety research because we wanted the entire ecosystem to use it, even though it was competitively bad for us, because it was so good for safety. This is a technology which only goes well if you are able to be honest about it. Prior to 2025 or 2026, there were these conceptual arguments about why AI might be risky. I think, really, in the past year, things have just come into focus more. 2026 is when the risks start to get real. Anthropic announced this week it has begun limited testing of its newest AI model called Mythos. This version is apparently so advanced it can find vulnerabilities in a huge variety of software applications. And if you’re a hacker, I think you’re thinking, “I want to get access to this model.” We trained this class of model, and we looked at it, and we were like, holy shit. People who are experts in cybersecurity noticed that it was really very, very, very good at an expert level at finding vulnerabilities and exploits in software. If you have a malicious actor that got ahold of Mythos and said, “I’d like to exploit a lot of those vulnerabilities,” you would be in a really bad position. The whole world would be. And we really saw this concrete, specific way that if we didn’t change how we operated as an industry, something bad was absolutely going to happen. Dario pulls the company together, pulls the leadership together, and says, “We are going to have to release this model differently to any model we’ve released before.” We decided to do this Glasswing project. It gave access to Mythos to this smaller set of companies while we were working on the safeguards to make it more broadly available. What that would mean is cyberdefenders could get ahold of Claude and say, “Here are where all the vulnerabilities are,” and then they could actually use Claude to help them patch those vulnerabilities. This might not be the absolute best way to maximize revenue, profit, value, money, but that’s kind of the point. This is exactly what our public benefit mission was created for. What we found is that when you do things in the right way, you tend to pay in the short run. It can be quite painful in the short run. But a lot of this is about discipline and farsightedness and understanding what the industry and the world needs in the long run.

使命与未来展望

我们当初创立这个组织的全部原因，就在于我们对它带来的益处深信不疑。 你不能只是一味地说： “这些全都是会出问题的地方。” 你必须给人们一个为之奋斗的目标，而不仅仅是一个反对的目标。 AI 正在被用来帮助人们从医生那里获得正确诊断。 人们正在使用 Claude 来帮助他们开创自己的事业，或是重拾他们一直想尝试的爱好。 我们正在利用 Claude 协助生物学家和科学家针对常见及罕见疾病研发疗法与药物。 我每周都会与我们的生物团队交流数次。 我们发现了一种看似全新的基因编辑机制，而且我认为在几个月内，我们可能每个月都会发现数十种此类机制。 伴随着这项技术的推进速度，我们已经见证了这些星星之火很快就会演变成燎原之势。 我认为我们能够找到一条安全构建这项技术的途径：既能造福人类，又能化解所有风险，并创造一个远比我们今天更加美好的世界。 我们目前尚未达成这一目标。 我们还没有资格去空谈那些愿景有多么伟大。 但我们已经踏上了这条征程。

英文原文 The whole reason we started this organization is because we believe so deeply in the benefits. You can’t just say, “These are all the things that are going to go wrong.” You have to give people something to fight for, not just something to fight against. AI is being used to help people get the proper diagnosis from their doctors. People are using Claude to help them start their own business or pursue a hobby that they’ve always wanted to pick up. We’re using Claude to help biologists and scientists develop cures and treatments for common and uncommon diseases. I’ve been talking to our biology team several times a week. We discovered what looks like a new gene-editing mechanism, and I think within a few months, we may be discovering dozens of them every month. We’ve seen with the speed at which this technology moves, these little sparks will soon turn into a firestorm. I think that we can find a way to build this technology safely in a way that benefits people, that addresses all the risks, and that delivers a world that is so much better than the world that we have today. We haven’t done that yet. We haven’t earned the right to talk about how great those things are. But we’re starting down the path.
