访谈卡洛与权力的机制

我最近采访了罗伯特·卡洛。他花了过去 50 年的时间撰写一部四卷本的林登·约翰逊传记，在此之前，他又花了 7 年时间撰写纽约建设巨头罗伯特·摩西的传记。他的著作被公认为对政治权力最深刻的探索之作，恰好也是我个人最喜欢的书籍。

每当我结识新朋友时，他们经常问我的第一个问题往往是：“在尚未采访的人中，你最想采访谁？”在这 6 年里，我的回答始终如一：罗伯特·卡洛。

为了促成这次采访，我付出了大量努力。我联系过他的经纪人，致电过他的出版商，还联系过存放其档案的博物馆，甚至给他寄过亲笔信。最终我终于得以与他促膝长谈，对采访的效果也非常满意。

访谈内容将于明天发布。与此同时，在为这次采访做准备并重读这些传记的过程中，我梳理了书中关于如何积累和运用权力的种种经验法则，而这部视频短片正是这些思考的成果。

英文原文 I recently interviewed Robert Caro. He's spent the last 50 years writing   a 4-volume biography of Lyndon Johnson,  and he spent another 7 years before that   writing the biography of Robert Moses,  who was the master builder of New York.  His books are widely regarded as the best  explorations of political power that have   ever been written, and they also happen  to be literally my favorite books ever.  Whenever I meet somebody new, one of the  first questions that they often ask me is,   "Who's the person you most want to  interview that you haven't yet?"  And for 6 years, I've answered that  question in the same way: Robert Caro.  I've spent a lot of time trying  to make this interview happen.  I've chased down his agent, I've called  his publisher, I've contacted the   museums which are hosting his archives. I've even sent him handwritten letters.  I finally got to sit down with him, and  I'm very happy with how it turned out.  It'll be out tomorrow. In the meantime, as I was  preparing for this interview and rereading all   of these biographies, I was writing down all the  lessons that they laid out about how to accumulate   and use power, and this video essay is the result.

罗伯特·摩西：未获选举的城市主宰

罗伯特·摩西是纽约的城市建设主导者。我们尊贵的嘉宾正是全美首屈一指的城市规划师罗伯特·摩西先生。他在实体城市建设方面的体量超过了前人与后人。

他主持修建了三区大桥（Triborough Bridge）和韦拉扎诺海峡大桥（Verrazzano Bridge）、跨布朗克斯高速公路（Cross Bronx Expressway）、琼斯海滩（Jones Beach）、林肯中心（Lincoln Center）、谢亚球场（Shea Stadium），以及纽约上州的 2 座巨型发电大坝，此外还有数百处公园和游泳池。截至 1964 年纽约州建成的 899 英里高速公路中，有 672 英里出自摩西之手。按今天的货币价值计算，他的工程项目总成本达数千亿美元。

在超过 30 年的时间里，罗伯特·摩西始终是纽约最具权势的人物，其影响力超过了任何民选的市长或州长，尽管他本人从未担任过任何民选职务。正如过去三十多年里无数次听到的那样，人们总说：“你根本不是选出来的。”而我对他们惯常的回应则是：“你们也没这个必要。”

那么，他是如何获取如此庞大的权力，又是如何维持它的？

英文原文 Robert Moses was the master builder of New York.  Our distinguished guest was Mr. Robert  Moses, the nation's foremost city planner.  He built more of the physical  city than anybody before or since.  He built the Triborough and Verrazzano bridges,  the Cross Bronx Expressway, Jones Beach, Lincoln   Center, Shea Stadium, 2 giant power dams upstate,  in addition to hundreds of parks and pools.  Of the 899 miles of highways completed in New  York State by 1964, Moses had built 672 of them.  His projects collectively cost hundreds  of billions of dollars in today's money.  For more than 30 years, Robert Moses was the  most powerful person in New York, more powerful   than any elected mayor or governor, despite  the fact that he was not elected to anything.  As I've heard it, ad nauseam, for the last 30-odd  years, they say, "You don't have to be elected."  My stock reply to them is,  "You don't have to be, either."  How did he get that much  power, and how did he keep it? 

公共机构与债券法案的制度漏洞

部分原因在于，摩西亲自起草了用来约束他自身的法律。他曾被称为奥尔巴尼（Albany，纽约州府）最出色的法案起草人，并利用这一专长，为自己写入了立法者在表决通过法案时根本未曾意识到的各项权力。

罗伯特·摩西的政治智慧或许在他对公共机构（public authorities）的运用中体现得最为淋漓尽致。这些是由政府设立、负责兴建城市基础设施的法人实体。公共机构通过发售债券为特定工程（比如一座新桥梁）筹措资金。一旦大桥建成，该机构便向通行车辆收取过桥费，直至筹集到足以偿清债券的款项。按照最初设想，随后该机构将拆除收费站，将大桥移交市政，机构本身随之解散。

至少最初的设想是这样。这也是奥尔巴尼在纽约州议会通过《1933年三区大桥管理局法案》（Triborough Bridge Authority Act of 1933）时的预期。然而，在该法案最终版本那连篇累牍的法律条文深处，埋藏着几处由罗伯特·摩西亲自起草、看似微不足道的修改。

该法案第 1 条明确规定，债券一旦清偿完毕，该管理局即告解散。既然债券期限最长为 40 年，而大桥的收费收入偿还初始成本的速度要快得多，管理局似乎有着非常明确的存续期限。

然而，在第 9 条的一个分款中，摩西添加了一句表述：管理局可以“不时”通过发行新债券来清偿旧债券；并且这些新债券可以用于“任何其他法人机构宗旨”（for any other corporate purpose）。

如此一来，在原债券即将到期之前，管理局便获得了发行一笔全新 40 年期债券的权利，且资金可用于完全不同的其他用途。

英文原文 Part of the answer is that Moses  wrote the laws that governed him.  He was called the best bill drafter in  Albany, and he used that skill to write   himself powers that the legislators voting  on his bills didn't know they were granting.  The political genius of Robert Moses is perhaps  best illustrated by his use of public authorities.  These are government-created corporations  that build city infrastructure.  Public authorities sell bonds to pay for  a specific project, say, a new bridge.  Once the bridge is built, the authority would  charge drivers a toll until it had collected   enough money to pay off its bonds. Then the authority would tear down   the toll booths, hand the bridge over  to the city, and go out of existence.  At least, that was the idea. That's what Albany assumed would   happen when the New York State Congress approved  the Triborough Bridge Authority Act of 1933,   whose final version included, buried within pages  and pages of legalese, a few seemingly minor   alterations that were drafted by Robert Moses. Section 1 of this act clearly stated that   the authority would dissolve  once the bonds were paid off.  Since the bonds could run at most 40 years, and  the revenues from the bridge would pay back the   initial cost far sooner, it seemed like the  authority had a clear built-in expiry date.  However, in a subsection of Section 9, Moses  added a single sentence that said that the   authority could, "from time to time," pay  off its old bonds by issuing new ones.  And those new ones could be "for  any other corporate purpose."  So just before the original bonds were  about to expire, the authority now had   the right to issue a totally fresh 40-year  note for a potentially totally different use. 

无限扩张的工程权限与资金池

那么，管理局可以用这笔巨资建造什么？

最初的法案只允许其修建 2 座大桥及其引道。三区大桥管理局主席罗伯特·摩西称这项工程远不止是一座单纯的桥梁。他说：“它将成为位于城市心脏地带的一条庞大交通主干道。”

第一辆车辆从岛上穿过收费站驶出。车上的乘客是身着租来无尾礼服的年轻男子，他们在此停靠等待了一整周，只为争夺首辆通行的荣誉。

摩西的修正案还赋予了该管理局建设连接道路以及“与项目用途不相抵触的设施”的权利。如果按照摩西预想的创造性方式去解读，这些权利足以允许摩西改建纽约的大部分道路、公园和住宅。因为如果某条路连接着通往另一条路的道路，而后者又连接着通往大桥的道路；或者某个公园恰好位于通往大桥的某条连接路沿线，那么三区大桥管理局便理所当然有权对其进行改造和改建。

所有这些工程都可以依托大桥产生的巨额通行费收入，以及三区大桥管理局据此进行举债融资的权利来支持。

对比之下可以看出悬殊：1946 年，整个纽约市政府在触及法定举债上限前最多只能借入 1.5 亿美元；而就在同一年，三区大桥管理局能够借入 3.6 亿美元。也就是说，摩西名下的大桥管理局所能动用的资金，是全美最大城市可支配额度的两倍以上。

英文原文 And what could the authority  build with all this money?  The original act let it build  2 bridges and their approaches.  Triborough's chairman, Robert Moses, called  this project more than a mere bridge. "It   will be," he said, "an immense artery of  travel in the very heart of the city."  From the island, through the toll  gate, rides the first vehicle.  Young men in rented tuxedos, the passengers. They parked here all week for   the honor of being first to cross. Moses' amendments also gave this authority the   right to build connecting roads and "facilities  not inconsistent with the use of the project."  These rights, when interpreted as creatively  as Moses intended, would allow Moses to alter   most of the roads, parks, and housing in New York. For if a road connected to a road which connected   to a road which connected to the bridge, or  if a park lay along the road that connected   to the road that connected to the road that  connected to the bridge, surely the Triborough   Authority was entitled to improve and change it. All this construction could be financed by the   bridges' enormous toll revenues and the right of  the Triborough Authority to borrow against them.  To give some perspective, in 1946, the entire  New York City government could only borrow   $150 million before hitting its legal debt limit. That very same year, Triborough   could borrow $360 million. So Moses' bridge authority   had access to more than double the amount  of money as the largest city in America. 

宪法契约保护与权力的不可撤销性

如果未来的某位州长或市长最终察觉到下放给该管理局的庞大权力和收入，并决定收回这些权力，又会如何？

摩西对此同样早有预谋，他的解决办法极其高明，奥尔巴尼的任何律师或立法者事前均未察觉。

美国宪法规定，各州不得通过任何“损害契约义务的法律”，而政府实体订立的契约亦受其保护。公共机构发行的债券正属于此类契约。摩西在法案中悄然加入了一项条款，允许他在这些债券契约中补充“任何具有类似或不同性质、以任何方式影响债券安全或保障的事项”。

这些事项包括：三区大桥管理局可以按其意愿自行决定收取多少过桥费，收取任意长时间，并可以留存由此产生的盈余资金。

这意味着，摩西只需将各项权力写入自己发行的债券合同中，便能将其彻底固化。从债券售出的那一刻起，无论是州立法机构、州长还是市长，事实上除了召开制宪会议之外的任何手段，都无法剥夺这些权力。

摩西所拥有的权力在债券的整个存续期内都获得了保障，而这一存续期又可以随时通过再融资无限期延长。

仅凭几处细微的修改，摩西就将一项原本只为修建 2 座大桥的法案，演变成了一个能在纽约任何地方建造任何项目的机构根基，且该机构行使权力的合法性还受到美国宪法的保护。

英文原文 And what if a future governor or mayor  eventually learned about all this power   and revenue that had been delegated to this  authority, and they decided to claw it back?  Moses had planned for this, too, and  his solution was so clever that no   lawyer or lawmaker in Albany saw it coming. The United States Constitution says that no   state shall make "any law impairing the obligation  of contracts" entered into by a government entity.  A bond issued by a public  authority is such a contract.  Moses snuck a clause into the act which  allowed him to add "any other matters   of like or different character, which in  any way affect the security or protection   of the bonds" into these bond contracts. These matters included that Triborough could   charge whatever tolls it wanted  and for however long it wanted,   and it could keep the surplus thus generated. That is to say, Moses could now entrench his   powers simply by writing them  into the bonds that he issued.  From the moment those bonds were  sold, those powers could no longer   be revoked by the legislature or by the  governor or by the mayor, by anything,   in fact, short of a constitutional convention. The powers that Moses now had were guaranteed   for the bonds' entire lifetime, a lifetime which  could always be extended simply by refinancing.  So with just a few minor tweaks, Moses had turned  an act meant to allow for the construction of   2 bridges into the basis of an institution that  could build basically anything it wanted anywhere   in New York, and whose authority to do so was  protected by the United States Constitution. 

头衔叠加与行政不可撼动性

这些债券契约将三区大桥管理局的权力铸成了坚不可摧的磐石，但其中没有任何条款保证罗伯特·摩西本人能始终担任其主席。

那么，摩西是如何将这一职位稳坐长达 30 年之久的？

部分答案依然在于法律。摩西在起草《三区大桥法案》时明确规定，其委员在任期内只有在“经过正式听证并提出指控”后才能被解职。然而，他与市长或州长之间的分歧，更多在于他决定建什么以及在建设过程中拆迁了谁，而非其个人操守问题。正如卡洛本人所写：“罗伯特·摩西本人并不腐败。事实上，正如任何在世的公职人员一样，他对为自己谋取私利毫无兴趣。用政客们的话说，他在金钱上是一清二白。”因此，根本没有任何正式指控可以提出。

但这只能解释他为何没有在任期中途被免职。为何他能每隔 6 年都获得连任？

在鼎盛时期，摩西犹如一头拥有十余个头颅的九头蛇。他同时兼任 12 个不同的政府职位：纽约市建设协调员、州公园委员会主席、州电力管理局主席等等。这些职位中，有的由市长任命，有的由州长任命，没有任何一个人能够同时将他的头衔全部砍下。而罢免他的任何一个职位，都意味着你将彻底得罪这位依然掌握着纽约最高实权的人物。

英文原文 These bond covenants had turned the  Triborough Authority's powers into granite,   but nothing in them guaranteed that  Robert Moses would remain its chairman.  So how did Moses retain  this position for 30 years?  Part of the answer is, again, the law. Moses drafted the Triborough Act so that   its commissioners could be removed midterm only  on "stated charges after a formal hearing."  But any differences he had with the mayor or  governor were more about what he decided to   build and whom he displaced to build  it rather than his personal conduct.  As Caro himself writes, "Robert  Moses was not himself corrupt.  He was, in fact, as uninterested  in obtaining payoffs for himself   as any public servant who ever lived. In the politicians' phrase, he was money honest."  So there were never any formal charges to bring. But this only explains why he wasn't   fired in the middle of his term. Why was he reappointed every 6 years?  At his peak, Moses was a hydra with a dozen heads. He held 12 different government posts at the   same time: city construction coordinator,  chairman of the State Council of Parks,   chairman of the State Power Authority, and so on. Some of these positions were appointed by the   mayor, some of them by the  governor, and so no single   person could cut off the heads simultaneously. And removing him from any one position meant that   you were making an enemy of the man who would  still be the most powerful person in New York. 

民意基础与政绩速度

更重要的是，没有哪位市长或州长真想解雇他。

今天我们对罗伯特·摩西的记忆，主要基于 20 世纪 70 年代固化的公众印象，当时《权力的经纪人》（The Power Broker）刚刚出版，而纽约正经历着犯罪率激增、去工业化和白人群体外迁的黑暗年代。摩西为此承担了大部分指责，毕竟《权力的经纪人》副标题就是“罗伯特·摩西与纽约的衰落”。但依我之见，那场危机更多与更宏观的结构性因素有关，与摩西本人关系不大；像底特律和圣路易斯这些与他毫无瓜葛的城市，经历的衰败反而剧烈得多。

在他职业生涯的绝大部分时间里，摩西享有极高的声望。在 20 世纪 30 年代和 40 年代，纽约的高中生评选摩西为最令人钦佩的纽约人。这是因为他接连不断地交付了数百万纽约市民可以切实使用和享受的公共设施：海滩、公园、游泳池。他们过去只能从码头跳进浑浊的河水里，而现在却能在宏伟现代的高档露天泳池里嬉戏。耗资整整一百万美元，孩子们都说物有所值。

而且他的建设速度和规模是前所未见的。在担任该市公园专员的头 4 个月里，他让施工队伍日夜轮班，完成了 1,700 处公园的翻修，而这一切期间没有爆出丝毫个人丑闻。正如卡洛在《权力的经纪人》中所写：“在公园和游乐场开幕式上，经常能看到孩子们在家长的引导下齐声欢呼：‘2，4，6，8，我们感谢谁？摩西先生！摩西先生！摩西先生！’”

关键在于，摩西能够在一个选举周期的时限内交付这些庞大且深得民心的公共工程。如果市长授权罗伯特·摩西建造某个项目，他完全可以预期在自己争取连任之前，就能站在剪彩仪式现场。罗伯特·摩西、瓦格纳市长和斯佩尔曼枢机主教等显要人物一同出席了大桥布鲁克林一侧的剪彩仪式。

在今天，政府如果能在短短一两年内建起大型工程，几乎是不可想象的。例如，纽约第二大道地铁于 1972 年动工，第一期路段直到 2017 年才正式通车；仅这最初的 2 英里就耗费了纳税人 45 亿美元。

策略二：利用“一致同意协议”掌控议程

第二个重要策略是制定议程。

一项通过的法案具体表述如何，取决于最终交付表决的是哪个版本以及进行了哪些最终修改。谁能制定这一议程，谁就能控制最终结果。因此，由谁将自己推上这一位置便不言而喻了。

约翰逊控制议程的主要工具是“一致同意协议”（unanimous consent agreement）。

默认情况下，参议院的辩论是没有时间限制的。一名参议员可以就一项法案发表任意长时间的演说；而在发言过程中，他还可以提出与该法案毫无关系的修正案，每一项修正案都可能进一步拖延法案的审议。而一致同意协议针对单一法案暂停了这些程序自由，它明确限定了辩论的分配时间，并限制了提出修正案的机会。

早期的领袖很少使用此类协议；即便偶尔使用，也往往是在某项法案经过数周辩论、各方已筋疲力尽之后才动用。约翰逊则将此类协议的运用变成了参议院的常规做法。在达成此类协议之前，他甚至不允许法案进入大会辩论程序。

原因在于，约翰逊希望完全掌控哪些法案能够交付审议，并防止大会辩论改变任何人的既定主张。他希望每一次投票都在私下预先达成协议，而在私下的交易斡旋中，他的筹码能够发挥最大功效。他最不愿看到的，就是因公开漫长的法案辩论在最后一刻改变某位参议员的主意，或是某项突如其来的修正案破坏了他耗费数周艰苦达成的妥协方案。正如保罗·道格拉斯（Paul Douglas）参议员所言，在约翰逊治下，所有好戏都在大幕拉开前的后台上演完毕。

英文原文 The second important tactic   was setting the agenda. What is said by a bill that   passes depends on which version actually comes  up for a vote and with what final modifications.  Whoever gets to set this  agenda controls the outcome.  So you won't be surprised by who  put himself in this position.  Johnson's main tool for controlling the  agenda was the unanimous consent agreement.  By default, Senate debate is unlimited. A senator can talk about a bill for as long   as he likes, and as he does so, he can also offer  amendments that have nothing to do with that bill,   each of which can stall the bill even further. A unanimous consent agreement sets those   freedoms aside for a single bill. It fixes the allotted time for debate   and constrains the opportunity to amend it. Earlier leaders had rarely used such agreements,   and even when they had, they'd only used  them after weeks of debate on a bill that   had already been exhausted in its deliberations. Johnson made their use the norm in the Senate.  He wouldn't even allow a bill to reach the  floor until such an agreement was in place.  The reason was that Johnson wanted to  control which bills reached the floor   and to keep the floor from changing anybody's  mind, because he wanted every vote settled   in private beforehand through the wheeling  and dealing where his leverage worked best.  The last thing he wanted was for a long public  debate about the merits of some bill to change a   senator's mind at the very last minute, or for a  surprise amendment to unravel a deal that he had   spent weeks painstakingly brokering. As Senator Paul Douglas put it,   under Johnson, all the action takes  place offstage before the play begins. 

策略三：信息垄断与《1957年民权法案》的利益撮合

约翰逊运用的第三个策略是促成所有对自己有利的交易。

约翰逊确保参议院的内部信息尽可能全部汇总到他一人手中：

  • 他让每个委员会指派一名工作人员，向他汇报法案起草的实时进展；
  • 他的助理紧盯每一位参议员的一举一动；
  • 他自己整天泡在休息室和走廊里，对同僚施以哄劝并倾听诉求。

因此，他确切掌握每一位参议员的真实意图，以及为了达成目标他们愿意和不愿意放弃什么。

在任何一届会期中，参议院都会面对数百个潜在法案和修正案。某位参议员在一项法案上的投票，可以用来交换另一位参议员在另一项法案上的选票；而任何一项法案都可以通过十几种不同的修改方式来争取支持。因此，可供达成的交易组合几乎无穷无尽。约翰逊确保自己成为大多数交易的发起人和撮合者；如此一来，他便能确保每一次交易的最终结果都能让自己占据上风。

一个典型的例子是他如何推动《1957年民权法案》在参议院获得通过。

约翰逊极度渴望确保当年能通过哪怕一部民权法案，即便内容十分微弱。这是因为他志在参选总统，但在 1960 年，一个一贯投票反对民权的南方人绝不可能赢得民主党总统候选人提名。

问题在于，似乎根本不存在这样一部法案：既足够强硬以赢得北方自由派的支持，又足够温和使南方保守派不至于动用冗长辩论阻挠通过。其中的症结在于，由谁来主审南方选举官员被控压制黑人选民投票权的司法案件。南方参议员坚持必须由陪审团审判；但北方参议员对此感到出离愤怒，因为在他们看来，这无异于掏空整个法案的核心。

该法律的全部目的就是打击将黑人公民排除在选民名册之外的行为。但南方各州规定陪审员必须是注册选民，全白人组成的南方陪审团绝不可能认定拒绝对黑人登记的白人登记官有罪。因此，任何一方能够接受的版本，另一方都会断然拒绝。

缺乏创造力的立法者到此往往会退缩。但在长达 7 个月的时间里，约翰逊巧妙构筑了一场涵盖各方不相干阵营的精妙妥协，其附属产物便包括这部民权法律的通过：

  • 西部参议员如愿获得了一项他们争取多年、位于爱达荷州与俄勒冈州之间联邦大坝建设的表决权；
  • 选区内拥有强大铁路工会的中西部共和党人，获得了将陪审团审判权扩大至罢工工人的条款；
  • 温和派获得了一项保障黑人公民能够进入联邦陪审团的条款，从而可以向选民宣称他们创立了一项新的公民权利；
  • 与此同时，保障南方选举官员享有陪审团审判权的条款得以保留，这使得法案保持在足够微弱的程度，南方派系因而同意不对其进行冗长辩论阻挠。

爱达荷州的水坝、对工会工人的保护以及联邦陪审团规则，原本与南方的投票权风马牛不相及——至少对于缺乏林登·约翰逊那种内幕洞察与政治创造力的人来说是如此。

英文原文 The third tactic that Johnson employed was  brokering all the deals to his advantage.  Johnson made sure that as much of the Senate's  information as possible ran through him.  He had every committee assign a staffer  to tell him what was being drafted.  His aides tracked what every senator was up to. And he spent his own days in the cloakroom and   in the hallways cajoling and  listening to his colleagues.  So he knew exactly what each senator really wanted  and what he would and wouldn't give up to get it.  In any session, the Senate faces hundreds  of possible bills and amendments.  A senator's vote on one bill can be traded  for another senator's vote on a different one.  And any single bill can be amended a  dozen different ways to win somebody over.  So there's almost an endless combination  of possible deals to be made.  Johnson made sure that he was the one  instigating and brokering most of these deals.  As a result, he would be making sure that  any deal left Johnson coming out ahead.  A good example is how he got the Civil  Rights Act of 1957 passed through the Senate.  Johnson was desperate to make sure  that at least some civil rights   bill passed that year, even if it was weak. That's because he wanted to become president,   but no Southerner with his record of consistently  voting against civil rights could hope to win the   Democratic presidential nomination in 1960. The problem was there seemed to be no bill   strong enough to win the Northern  liberals, yet weak enough that   the Southerners wouldn't filibuster it. The sticking point was who would preside over   the court cases where Southern electoral officials  were accused of suppressing Black voters.  Southern senators insisted on jury trials,  but Northern senators were livid because,   in their view, this would gut the whole bill. The entire point of the law was to combat the   exclusion of Black citizens from the voting rolls. But Southern states required jurors to be   registered voters, and an all-white Southern  jury would never convict a white registrar   for turning away Black voters. So any version that one side   could accept, the other would reject. A less creative legislator would have backed out.  But over the course of 7 months, Johnson  built a masterful deal that brought together   wholly unrelated factions and whose byproduct  included the passage of this civil rights law.  Western senators got a vote on a  federal dam that they had wanted   for years between Idaho and Oregon. Midwestern Republicans who had powerful   railroad unions in their states got the right  to a jury trial extended to striking workers.  And moderates got a clause guaranteeing that  Black citizens could serve on federal juries,   so that they could tell voters that  they had created a new civil right.  Meanwhile, the jury trial guarantee for  Southern election officials stayed in,   which kept the bill weak enough that  Southerners agreed not to filibuster it.  A dam in Idaho and protections for union  workers and federal jury rules had nothing   to do with voting rights in the South, at least  not to anyone who lacked the inside knowledge   and political creativity of Lyndon Johnson.

结语与访谈预告

卡洛的著作是对政治权力运作机制最出色的记录。但这些书写得太引人入胜了，人物比小说更生动，故事比小说更扣人心弦，以至于读者很容易沉醉其中不断翻页，却从未停下来问自己：这些故事揭示了哪些关于权力的经验教训？

我之所以制作这部视频短片，既是为了系统梳理这些经验教训究竟是什么，也是为了让大家具备必要的背景认知，以便在观看我对卡洛的专访时能够像我一样收获良多。
