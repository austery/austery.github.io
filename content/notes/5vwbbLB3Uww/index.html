从出狱到重生：用安全庇护打破女性监禁的恶性循环 TED 2026-09-07

领域: society-thinking

分类: politics-society

主讲人: TED

来源: 查看

发布时间: 2026-09-07

摘要:

苏珊·伯顿（Susan Burton）在经历丧子之痛与多次入狱后，通过戒酒互助会获得倾听与新生。她创办了女性过渡安置所和SAFE联盟，通过提供安全住所、生活技能培训与心理支持，帮助数千名出狱女性重返社会、与家人团聚，从根本上降低再犯率并修复社区。

坠入深渊：丧子之痛与司法系统的冷漠循环

1997年10月4日之前，我的人生陷入了无尽的黑暗。25岁那年，我生下了儿子K.K.，然而在他年仅五岁时，一场突如其来的车祸夺走了他的生命。失去了儿子的笑声、爱与活力，我的生活彻底崩溃，陷入了下行螺旋。我开始借酒消愁，酗酒进一步演变成了物质滥用（Substance Use: 依赖性药物或酒精的成瘾性使用）。因为物质滥用，我被一次又一次送进监狱。

每次刑满释放，我都发誓绝不再回去，但却一次次重蹈覆辙。我清楚地记得有一次，我站在法官面前哀求：“请问有任何人能帮帮我吗？”然而法官只是冷漠地再次将我判刑入狱。在那个系统里，没有人真正在乎，也没有人愿意倾听一个成瘾者的求助呼声。

Original English Source

Before that date, my life had not been easy. When I was 25 years old, I gave birth to a son. We called him K.K. The next thing that happened changed my life forever. He was struck by a car. That is the day I lost him. At only five years old, he was gone. His laughter, his love, his energy were all taken from me.

My life became a downward spiral. I began to drink. Drinking led to substance use. For the substance use, I was imprisoned. Every time I left prison, I swore I wouldn't go back. But I did. Over and over and over again.

I remember one day, standing in front of a judge and asking that judge, "Is there any help for me?" And the judge sentenced me to prison one more time. No one cared. No one was listening.

倾听的力量：从互助戒断中获得新生

走入绝境的我，在1997年10月4日迈进了一个前所未见的房间——匿名戒酒互助会（Alcoholics Anonymous: 简称AA，一个致力于帮助成员戒除酒瘾的国际互助组织）。当时我正处于人生的最低谷，彻底走投无路。然而，在这个房间里，人们站起来分享各自的故事，一种释然感瞬间涌遍我的全身。在这里，大家愿意倾听。

我深吸了一口气，站起来说：“看看酒精和毒品把我变成了什么样。”面对这群完全陌生的人，迎面而来的不是评判，而是即时的同情、同理心与爱。这种接纳足以抚平我深藏多年的痛苦、羞耻、内疚与悲伤。我不断倾诉，他们耐心倾听，我把数十年来试图压抑的所有心声全部倾倒出来。在被倾听的过程中，我开始了疗愈。是倾听拯救了我的生命，并为我推开了一扇全新的大门。

在洛杉矶富裕的圣莫尼卡（Santa Monica）戒瘾期间，我观察到一个鲜明的反差：当地的人们并不会因为物质滥用或酗酒而被直接关进监狱。这让我陷入沉思：为什么当年的法官没有递给我一张纸条说“伯顿女士，去参加AA吧”？难道我不值得获得一张写有求助方向的纸条？难道我不配得到一个通往解决方案的指引吗？

Original English Source

On October 4, 1997, I walked into a room unlike any room I'd ever entered before. People were standing up, sharing their stories. A wave of relief washed over me. I got a cup of coffee and just took everything in. At that point, I was at the lowest point in my life. I was out of ideas, and I didn't know how I was going to make it out of the condition I was in.

But when I walked into that room in 1997, I was ready to be heard. In the rooms of Alcoholics Anonymous, they listened. I took a deep breath and stood up, and said, "Look what drugs and alcohol have done to me." The immediate compassion, empathy and love that rolled off these complete strangers was enough to put a sizable dent in my pain, my shame, my guilt and all that sorrow.

I kept talking, and they kept listening. I shared all the thoughts I had spent decades trying to silence. As they listened, I began to heal. Listening saved my life, and it opened a door.

I got sober in Santa Monica, an affluent part of Los Angeles. And I noticed something there. People in Santa Monica weren’t going to prison for substance use, for alcoholism. And I thought, why didn't that judge give me a paper and say, "Miss Burton, go to AA.” Was I not worth a piece of paper? Was I not worth direction toward a solution?

从一张床位到十二栋安全屋：女性互助过渡模式的建立

随着康复带来的力量，我开始挂念那些和我经历相似的女性——她们在监狱的高墙内外反复进出，苦苦寻找安全感却一无所获。于是，我找了一份拿最低工资的工作，积攒下13,000美元。我拿出其中的3,000美元作为首付租下了一栋房子，并添置了家具。

每天清晨醒来，我都会前往灰狗巴士站（Greyhound bus station），那里是刚出狱女性被送达下车的地方。我对那个车站再熟悉不过，因为我自己曾无数次在那里下车。看着她们拎着装满随身物品的垃圾袋、怀揣希望走下大巴，我会走上前对她们说：“姑娘，我有一栋房子。如果你愿意来住，那是一个安全的地方，欢迎你住进来。”

不久后，我们房子里就住下了11个女人。我们一起做饭、一起去参加互助会议、共享资源、一起欢笑、一起痛哭，最重要的是，我们一起疗愈。很快，人们看到了我的行动并纷纷加入，我们建立起一支团队。如今，我们在整个洛杉矶已经拥有了12栋庇护屋。

我们建立的是一个“女性帮助女性”的社区家园。每个房屋都配备了专业支持人员，居住者在此支付租金、分担家务、采购生活用品，学习成为对自己和社区负责的成员。我们协助她们寻找工作、培养金融素养（Financial Literacy: 理解并有效运用个人财务管理技能的能力），并解决遗留的法律问题。通过这套机制，数千名女性成功重返社会，数百名母亲得以与孩子骨肉团聚。

Original English Source

So as I got stronger in my recovery, I thought about women just like me, cycling in and out of prison, looking for safety and not finding it. So I got a job. A minimum wage job. And I saved $13,000. And I took $3,000 of it and put down on a house. And I bought some furniture.

Every morning I’d wake up, and I'd go down to the Greyhound bus station where women leaving prison were dropped off. And I knew that station well because I had been dropped off there so many times myself. And as they got off the bus carrying a trash bag of belongings and hope in their hearts, I would walk up to them and I'd say, "Girl, I have a house. And if you want to come live there, it's a safe place to be. You can come live there."

And before long there were 11 of us living in that house. We cooked together. We went to meetings together. We shared resources. We laughed together. We cried together. But most of all, we healed together.

Before long, people saw what I was doing, and they joined me. And we created a team. Today, there [are] 12 houses across Los Angeles. (Applause) Thank you.

What we built was a house of women helping women. Each house is staffed to support its residents who pay rent, do chores, buy groceries and learn to be accountable, responsible members of the community. We help them find jobs, learn financial literacy and help them with any legal issues. As a result, thousands of women have come back to the community and hundreds of mothers have been reunited with their children.

SAFE 联盟与全球扩展：打破跨代监禁的系统工程

2017年，我出版了回忆录《成为伯顿女士：从监禁、康复到引领在押女性权益抗争》（Becoming Ms. Burton: From Prison to Recovery to Leading the Fight for Incarcerated Women）。为了分享我的故事，我在全美各地的监狱高墙内举办了新书分享会。在那里，我倾听了无数在押女性的心声。她们泪流满面，恳求在获释后能有一个容身之所；她们极度渴望重建生活、与孩子团聚、在社区找到工作，彻底摆脱刑事司法系统的束缚。

在美国，每年约有200万名女性从监狱和看守所获释。她们拎着装满物品的垃圾袋走出来，期盼有人倾听，期盼有人伸出援手。当缺乏援助、没有安全落脚点时，太多人只能被迫走回犯罪的老路。

我深知仅凭一己之力无法为所有女性提供新生活的资源，因此我创立了自由与平等姐妹联盟（Sisterhood Alliance for Freedom and Equality: 简称SAFE，旨在复制女性过渡安置房模式的非营利组织），将这一成功模式向外推广。我们的第一期培训只有13名女性，而截至目前，我们已经培训了超过700人去倾听、去建立支持系统、去传递希望。

当出狱女性拥有安全住所时，家庭得以团聚，累犯率（Recidivism: 刑满释放人员再次犯罪并重返监狱的比率）显著下降，整个社区也随之更加稳定。我们从一栋互助房屋起步，如今已建立起覆盖全美20个州，并延伸至乌干达、肯尼亚和尼日利亚的全球女性互助网络。

我们已经将这种模式的复制演进成一套科学体系。这里有坚强的领导力、庞大的社会需求和无比明确的目标：在更多城市建立更多房屋，让更多母亲与孩子重聚，让更多女性打破延续数代的监禁恶性循环。

Original English Source

In 2017, I published my memoir, "Becoming Ms. Burton: From Prison to Recovery to Leading the Fight for Incarcerated Women." In order to tell my story, I held book talks all over the nation, behind prison walls.

There I would listen to women. They were pleading for a place to go after they were released. They were sitting with tears streaming down their faces, wanting to rebuild their lives, be reunited with their children, find a job in their community [and] break free from the criminal justice system.

Every year in this country, around two million women are released from prisons and jails. They walk out with a trash bag of belongings, hoping someone will listen. Hoping someone will help. When there's no help, when there's no safe place to land, too many of them will go back.

I knew I could not provide resources of a new way of life to all those women. So I created SAFE, Sisterhood Alliance for Freedom and Equality, to replicate my model. Our first training was with 13 women. To date, we've trained over 700. (Applause) To listen. To create support. To give hope.

When women like me have safe housing, families reunify, recidivism drops and communities stabilize. I started with one house of women helping women. Today, there is an entire network of women helping women in 20 US states and also in Uganda, Kenya and Nigeria. (Applause)

We have replication down to a science. The leadership is there, the demand is there, and the goal is clear. More houses in more cities. More mothers reunited with their children. More women breaking a cycle that has lasted generations.

公共安全与社区修复：以慈悲倾听重铸生命尊严

我相信，我们每个人都共同承担着修复刑事司法系统所造成创伤的责任。这不是简单的慈善，这关乎公共安全，关乎家庭保全，关乎社区修复。

当有人带着慈悲而非评判倾听我时，我的人生被彻底改变了。自1997年那一天起，我的故事证明了当人们获得支持、希望与决心时，能够创造怎样的奇迹。

我无法让逝去的儿子重新回到身边，但我坚信，每当有一位女性走进我们的大门找到安全归宿，每当有一个孩子与母亲紧紧相拥，每当有一位女性挣脱曾囚禁我的命运枷锁，我儿子的生命遗产便超越了他短暂的岁月。我相信他正在天上微笑着注视我的努力，我也希望他能为自己的母亲感到骄傲。这一切的发生，都始于当年有人带着慈悲而非评判，认真倾听了我。

Original English Source

I believe we all share a responsibility to heal the harms caused by the criminal justice system. This is public safety. This is family preservation. This is community repair.

When someone listened to me with compassion rather than judgment, it changed my life. My story since that day in 1997, shows what is possible when people have support, hope and determination.

I cannot bring my son back. But I know every time a woman walks through the doors and finds safety, every time a child is reunited with his mother, every time a woman breaks free from a cycle that once held me, his legacy goes way beyond his years. I believe he's smiling down on my efforts, and I hope he's proud of his mother.

I am so grateful to be on the TED dot. (Laughter and Cheers) (Applause) And this happened because someone listened with compassion rather than judgment. Thank you so much. (Applause)
