供给冲击与传统央行教条的失效

Joseph Wang: 总体来说，通胀依然相当炽热，因为我们正处于大宗商品的牛市之中。硫磺价格在涨，原油在涨，天然气在涨，农产品也在涨。这些成本必然会传导到实体经济。

Original English

Joseph Wang: Um yeah, I mean just inflation is is quite hot because we have a commodity we we're in a commodity bull market. Sulfur is going up, oil's going up, natural gas, agriculture. It's going to flow through.

Joseph Wang: 传统的央行教条认为，央行应该“穿透（look through）”供给冲击。其背后的理论是：货币政策的传导存在漫长的滞后性，加息无法在短期内凭空增加石油开采量或改善地缘航运。因此面对供给冲击，央行通常选择按兵不动。

然而，在当前的现实环境下，如果通胀已经在高位徘徊了数年，通胀预期随时可能脱锚。如果央行继续教条地选择“穿透”，放任成本推动型通胀肆虐，企业和消费者就会调整定价与薪酬行为，最终演变为全方位的通胀螺旋。

因此沃什选择主动收紧政策，即使面临的是供给端驱动的物价上涨，也要通过抑制总需求来捍卫物价稳定的信誉。

Original English

Joseph Wang: so the traditional central bank view is that you look through supply shocks right so the dogma is that monetary policy acts with long lags. So let's say there's a oil shock, you don't hike because by the time the rate hike takes effect, the oil shock might be over. But if you have inflation that has been high for years, you can't just look through it anymore because inflation expectations become unanchored. People start expecting higher prices, businesses raise prices, workers demand higher wages. So the dogma breaks down. And that's why Warsh is acting. Even though it's supply driven, he has to act to keep credibility.

Joseph Wang: 国债收益率在很大程度上反映了市场对未来货币政策路径的预期。然而，当你拉长到 30年期这样超长的维度时，老实说，没有人能够确切预知 20 年或 30 年后的基准利率路径究竟会怎样。

因此，30年期国债定价中包含着大量的期限溢价（term premium），并且受到纯粹的市场**供求关系（supply and demand）**的剧烈影响。

这就引出了财政部的国债回购计划。财政部的回购操作主要有两个目的：其一是流动性支持（liquidity support），其二是现金管理（cash management）。

他们通过发行流动性极佳的短期国债（T-Bills）筹集现金，然后在二级市场上回购那些流动性较差、交易折价较深的非当期国债（off-the-run Treasuries）。

市场最初对这一机制寄予厚望，甚至有人幻想财政部会像央行 QE 一样大规模吞噬长端供给。但实际推行下来，财政部回购的规模相对克制，而且主要是针对老券的结构性流动性清洗，并不能改变长端国债总发行量庞大的基本面。

Original English

Joseph Wang: So Treasury yields are largely the expected path of policy. However, when you go to the 30-year tenor, you know, nobody really knows what the path of policy will be in 20 or 30 years. So the 30-year bond is heavily driven by term premium and raw supply and demand dynamics. This is where the Treasury buyback program comes into play. The Treasury conducts buybacks for two reasons: liquidity support and cash management. They issue highly liquid Treasury bills and use the proceeds to buy back illiquid, off-the-run Treasury securities that are trading at a discount. Initially, the market had huge expectations that this would act like pseudo-QE. But in reality, the sizes have been modest and targeted, aimed primarily at cleaning up off-the-run frictions rather than artificially suppressing long-term yields across the board.
