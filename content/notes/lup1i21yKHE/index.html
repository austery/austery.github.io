关键字 gun-control public-safety illicit-smuggling community-intervention

摘要

相比美国将持枪视为基本权利，加拿大将持枪定为严格监管的公民责任。然而，尽管加拿大历史上经历了多次严厉的控枪立法与武器禁令，枪支暴力依然存在。本文深入探讨了加拿大控枪法律的演进、回购计划与走私枪支的现实脱节，以及将枪支暴力视为社会不平等下游产物的干预路径。

责任制控枪：加拿大的枪支管理哲学与历史演进

与将持枪权视为不可剥夺权利的美国不同，加拿大将枪支拥有权（Gun Ownership: 法律许可下的特定特权）定位为一项严肃的社会责任。在加拿大，持有枪支不仅需要申请每五年更新一次的执照，申请人还必须完成枪支安全培训课程，并提供背书推荐信，以证明其精神健康状态与持有枪支的社会责任感。与此同时，联邦政府持续收紧合法拥枪的种类范围，并在原有基础上进一步将 179 种攻击性枪支型号列入违禁清单。

加拿大现行的严格枪支管理法案最早可追溯至 19 世纪，但其现代体系主要由两次恶性大规模枪击事件塑造。第一次发生在 1989 年，枪手闯入蒙特利尔工程学院（École Polytechnique）杀害了 14 名女性。惨案发生后，加拿大政府出台了 C-17 法案（Bill C-17），确立了包括购枪等待期、强制安全培训以及对申请人实施背景深度审查等现代枪支管制核心框架。这些举措被普遍认为是加拿大历史上最行之有效的常识性控枪法律，旨在从源头防止枪支落入不当人群手中。

英文原文

Canada has relatively strict gun laws, especially compared with our neighbor to the south. Unlike the United States, where firearm ownership is treated as an inalienable right, in Canada, the laws are set up to make gun ownership a responsibility. Those who want to get a gun have to apply for a license that has to be renewed every 5 years. As part of being a licensee, you have to take a gun safety training course. You have to have people write letters of recommendation and speak on your mental fitness and social responsibility of owning a gun.

In addition to all of that, the federal government has been increasingly limiting the types of firearms that Canadians can legally own. Today, our government is finishing the job, adding a further 179 unique makes and models of assault style weapons to the list of prohibited firearms here in Canada. So, why does gun violence still happen here?

Canada's gun laws go back to the 1800s, but have been shaped largely by two mass shooting events. The first of those happened in 1989 when a gunman entered the École Polytechnique and killed 14 women. In the aftermath, the government passed Bill C-17, which establishes most of the gun control laws that we now know about. Things like a waiting period to get a license, a safety training course, expanded screening for people who are applying to own a gun. All of these things came in the aftermath of that massacre. These represent some of the most effective gun control laws that exist on the books. These are all common sense laws that are there to protect Canadians and also to prevent the wrong people from getting their hands on guns.

C-21 法案的争议：象征性回购与跨境黑枪的现实脱节

2020 年，加拿大新斯科舍省发生了该国现代历史上伤亡最惨重的大规模枪击案，造成 22 人丧生。惨案发生后，联邦政府迅速推进了一系列新的限制举措，即最具争议的 C-21 法案（Bill C-21）。该法案首先实施了针对多款长枪的禁令，随后在 2022 年推行了全国范围的手枪销售冻结，并在 2024 至 2025 年间进一步扩大了违禁长枪的名单。此外，法案还设立了违禁武器的回购计划，但这一措施因参与率极低并遭到部分警务部门的抵制而广受诟病。

政策研究表明，枪支回购计划（Buyback Program: 政府出资回收民用违禁武器的项目）往往更多具有政治象征意义。参与回购计划的民众大多是守法公民，本身极不可能沦为枪支走私犯或犯罪分子；回收的大量长枪也并非加拿大枪击案中的主要涉案武器，这类活动往往沦为政客展示治理成果的拍照机会。以新斯科舍省惨案为例，涉案凶手并非守法持枪者中途失控，其作案枪支中有三支是非法自美国走私入境，另一支则通过遗产非法获取，完全绕过了现有的合法监管。美国作为全球合法及非法枪支的主要输出国，多项枪支溯源案例均证实，加拿大涉案犯罪枪支（尤其是手枪）绝大多数源自美国境内。尽管 C-21 法案加重了走私武器的刑罚并扩大了边境执法权力，但在漫长的美加边境线上，单纯依靠法律条文拦截武器走私依旧面临极大的物理挑战。

And then in 2020, the worst mass shooting in modern Canadian history where a gunman in Nova Scotia killed 22 people. So after the 2020 Nova Scotia massacre, there were a number of different laws that restrict Canadians' access to firearms. The most immediate was a ban on various long guns. After that, in 2022, there was a national handgun freeze and then in 2024-2025, there was an expansion of the long guns that are prohibited in Canada.

These policies came through Bill C-21. The bill also introduced a buyback program for those newly banned models, though several police services said they would not directly participate. The measures from Bill C-21 received criticism because of the low participation rate in the buyback program.

So buyback programs are a very popular policy item for governments. They tend to be very symbolic in nature, and the reason for that is the people who participate in these buyback programs are not likely to become gun traffickers or commit crime. They tend to produce good photo opportunities where lawmakers can show a bunch of firearms that have been submitted into the program, especially when it comes to long guns. Long guns are not typically used in shootings in Canada. Critics also said that not enough was being done to address the real issue, illegal guns, most of which they say come from the US.

The Nova Scotia case is kind of an exemplar in this respect. If you look at how the shooter got his guns, three of them were obtained illegally from the United States. The other was obtained illegally through an estate. This was not a lawful gun owner who decided to go rogue. This was somebody who was skirting the policies and the controls that are already in place. US is a global exporter of both licit and illicit firearms. And even though we don't have long-term systematic data on this phenomenon, the select case studies and instances where people submit guns to traces tend to show that most crime guns, especially handguns, are originating in the United States.

There have been attempts to address this. Bill C-21, that contentious law passed in the aftermath of the Nova Scotia mass shooting, did also include stiffer punishments for smuggling illegal weapons, and it gave border security and law enforcement expanded powers to try to deal with the issue. In reality, this is a very hard thing to do because we share this enormous border with the United States. Attacking this problem through gun control legislation is perhaps missing the bigger picture.

从惩戒到根源治理：枪支暴力的社区干预与社会处方

单纯依靠立法层面的枪支限制，往往忽视了滋生暴力犯罪的深层社会土壤。在加拿大各大城市中，枪击事件具有显著的地域聚集性，高度集中于资源匮乏的社区。这些社区往往聚居着高比例的黑人、原住民及新移民群体，普遍面临服刑人员再融入困难、低就业率以及长期缺乏医疗、育儿支持等严重未满足的基础需求。如果治理方向脱离了这些结构性短板，枪支管制政策便难以触及暴力滋生的真正核心。

更为严峻的是，枪支暴力具有高度的恶性循环特征（Cyclical Violence: 暴力事件引发的报复性连锁反应），受害者极易在报复冲动下转变为新的加害者。枪击案发生后的短期窗口内，发生报复行动的概率极高，这亟需专业的前端干预资源及时介入，在矛盾二次激化前平息事态。若要在加拿大语境下实质性减少枪支暴力，公共政策的重心必须从单纯的条文禁令，转向解决跨代贫困、住房困难与医疗缺失等根本性需求与不平等，因为枪支暴力本身正是这些底层社会系统失灵的下游直接表征。

Gun violence is an issue with difficult and complex factors, but there are things that can be done. I think the biggest issue is that the Canadian government has been focused heavily on gun control restrictions, but not on prevention. If we look at any map of any city in Canada where shootings are happening, you can see that they're clustered. Shootings tend to be concentrated in the most underserved neighborhoods. These are places that have a high percentage of black, indigenous, and recent immigrant groups. These are places that have a high rate of people who are coming in and out of carceral settings, a low rate of employment, a high risk of people not having access to long-term health care, child care, and other kinds of unmet needs. One of the major policy items that could help address gun violence in Canada is to strengthen the communities in which shootings are occurring.

One of the secondary effects of gun violence is that victims can become perpetrators. Gun violence is very cyclical. Whenever somebody gets shot, there's immediate period in the aftermath of a shooting where retaliation is very likely. This is precisely why we need to invest in intervention resources who can go in and cool things down before they bubble up again. If we really want to affect gun violence and reduce gun violence in the Canadian context, policies ought to be addressing disparities and need. And these are things like intergenerational poverty, housing, healthcare. All of these things when they're not met, we see gun violence as a kind of downstream effect of that.
