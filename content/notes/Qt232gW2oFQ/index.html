寻觅地球的双胞胎：系外行星探索与金星生命的微光

摘要

宇宙溯源：跨越代际的“地球双胞胎”寻觅

寻找另一颗地球，即所谓的“B 行星”，是一场跨越数代人的漫长探索。我们渴望发现一颗真正的地球孪生兄弟（Earth twin），它拥有大陆、海洋、阳光，以及含氧的稀薄大气——一个人类可能居住的地方。然而，我们开展这项研究的首要动机并非为人类寻找新家园，而是为了理解我们的地球以及生命的起源。这是当代人回答“我们从哪里来？”和“我们为何在此？”的一种极具天文色彩与科学严谨性的方式。

正如天体物理学家（Astrophysicist）Sara Seager 所言，这种对地外生命的直觉往往源于对星空的初次凝望。在广袤的宇宙中，夜空里的每一颗恒星都是太阳。既然我们的太阳拥有水星、金星、地球、火星等行星，那么其他恒星也理应拥有自己的行星系统。

英文原文

The search for another Earth, the planet B, it's a generations-long search. We want to find a true Earth twin with continents, oceans, and sunlight, and a thin atmosphere with oxygen, a place where we humans could potentially live. The reason we're doing it primarily is not to find a new home for humans, but it's to understand our Earth, our origin of life. It's today's way of answering “Where did we come from?” “Why are we here?” A very astronomical, scientific way to answer that question. I'm Professor Sarah Seager, an astrophysicist and planetary scientist. I work on the search for signs of life beyond Earth. When I first saw the dark sky, it was completely by accident. I was ten years old. We went camping. And I don't know why, but I stepped out of the tent in the middle of the night. I saw the stars, and I just couldn't believe it. And at that moment, I understood at a deep level that there just has to be something else out there. It's such a vast universe. All the stars in the night sky are suns. And if our sun has planets - Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, etc., it makes sense that other stars, you know, other suns should have planets also. And they do.

从边缘到主流：系外行星研究的科学跨越

系外行星（Exoplanet: 太阳系以外绕恒星运行的行星）在三十年前还是一个未知的领域。直到 1990 年代中期，科学家才首次发现绕类日恒星运行的系外行星，这开启了异星生命的可能性。在早期，主流科学界与被视为“疯狂”的研究之间界限分明，当时几乎没人相信这个领域会有所作为，甚至认为投入时间是愚蠢的。

Sara Seager 曾被媒体称为“天体物理学界的印第安纳·琼斯”，因为她愿意在研究中承担风险、尝试新想法，并以一种令大多数人感到不安的方式进行探索。如今，这一领域已彻底进入主流。天文学家已经发现了数千颗行星，还有数千颗候选行星等待确认。这种搜寻工作就像是一个刑事勘测现场，科学家拥有真实的数据，但必须通过大量的推导与解释才能拼凑出完整的真相。

英文原文

An exoplanet is a planet orbiting a star outside of our solar system. 30 years ago, we didn't know about any exoplanets, but when I was in graduate school in the mid 1990s, the first exoplanet around a sun-like star was reported. And it presented the possibility of life on another planet. The line between mainstream research and research that's considered completely crazy, the line is constantly shifting. Back in the early days of exoplanets, it was all considered crazy. No one thought the field would go anywhere. People thought it was silly to spend time on it. But a while back, a journalist called me “the Indiana Jones of astrophysics” because in research I was willing to take risks, try out new ideas, explore in a way that makes most people a bit uncomfortable. And now it's so mainstream. And we astronomers have found thousands of planets, and there's several thousand planet candidates out there just waiting to be confirmed as planets. But the search for planet B or Earth 2.0 is like a forensic crime scene. We have real data. But we have to interpret. We have to make a lot of interpretation to make the picture whole.

金星迷局：浓硫酸云层中的生命悖论

以金星为例，它常被称为地球的“姐妹行星”，因为两者的大小和质量相仿，但金星表面温度极高，完全不适宜生命生存。然而，就像在地球上登山或乘飞机一样，随着海拔升高，气温会逐渐降低。在金星地表上方 50 公里处，温度和压力竟然与地球表面惊人地相似。

早在半个世纪前，卡尔·萨根（Carl Sagan）就曾提议金星云层中可能存在生命。但当时的人们并不完全了解，金星的云层是由高浓度的硫酸（Sulfuric acid）组成的，这会瞬间灼伤皮肤并摧毁已知生命。在这种极端环境下讨论生命——即使是原始细菌——听起来都很荒谬。但在 2020 年秋季，一项关于磷化氢（Phosphine: 在地球上通常由有机物腐败产生的气体）的发现成为了科学探索的转折点。研究团队在金星大气中检测到了微量磷化氢，并提交了长达百页的化学分析报告，证明已知的金星化学过程无法解释其来源。这暗示了金星可能存在生命迹象，迫使我们重新审视宇宙中可能存在的各种行星原型。

英文原文

For example, Venus is sometimes called our sister planet because Venus is about the same size and the same mass as Earth, but the surface of Venus is so hot. It's way too hot for life of any kind. But just like on Earth, if you hike up a mountain or go in an airplane, it gets colder and colder above the surface. And so way above the surface, at 50km above, the temperature is just right for life. In fact, the temperature and pressure in the clouds is just like here on Earth's surface. So over half a century ago, Carl Sagan first proposed that perhaps there's life in the clouds of Venus. But people didn't know at the time what we know now that the clouds are made of concentrated sulfuric acid. Sulfuric acid kills all of our life. It will burn a hole in your clothes and your skin instantly. So this idea of life in the clouds of Venus, even tiny primitive bacteria, it sounds ridiculous. But, you know, throughout life, we hit a crossroads. Just like if you're exploring in the wilderness or you're driving somewhere, do you keep going straight or do you make a right? Well, I came to one of these pivot points because of phosphine on Venus. Could there be life on Venus? Astronomers discovered a gas on the planet that could suggest, “yes.” In the fall of 2020, we made an announcement. We reported the discovery of tiny amounts of phosphine gas in the atmosphere of Venus. Phosphine on Earth occurs natually in decaying organic matter like in dead fish or swamps. And so the researchers were puzzled. How in the world did phosphine get on Venus? And we presented a hundred pages of chemistry. And we explained that all the known chemistry on Venus couldn't produce the amount of phosphine that we reported to detect in the atmosphere of Venus. Thus, it could be a sign of life. So it definitely points us to opening our minds to what is out there. What other types of planets might be out there, what other type of archetypes? There could be way more planet types out there that we just haven't yet conceived of.

终极真相：没有备用行星，唯有守护家园

尽管寻找“真正的地球双胞胎”是终极目标，但迄今为止我们尚未发现完全相同的行星。星际旅行在目前仍然是不切实际的幻想——即便我们可以考虑冬眠或 DNA 远程打印技术，但这些在现阶段都是不可行的。因此，尽管我们热衷于寻找系外行星和地外生命，但必须面对一个核心事实：并没有所谓的 B 行星（There is no planet B）。

这种探索实际上是在告诫我们，地球是多么的奇妙与珍贵。万物在这里共同演化，精巧地契合在一起。正如 Sara Seager 在其回忆录《宇宙中最微弱的光》（The Smallest Lights in the Universe）中所表达的双重含义：为了寻找另一颗地球，我们必须致力于捕捉宇宙中最微弱的光点；而在个人生活陷入困顿或遭遇悲剧时，我们也必须找到并珍惜内心宇宙中那些微小的光芒。我们需要投入资源，竭尽全力照顾好我们唯一的家园。

英文原文

My goal is to find a true Earth twin, and to search for signs of life on it. But so far it's hard to find, and we haven't found any. And when we are ready to find them, whether they'd be just like Earth, is more or less unlikely. The other problem is stars and exoplanets are so far away. We can think of science fiction. We could hibernate. Perhaps we’d send our DNA and get ourselves printed out there on the new planet in the future. But right now it's just infeasible. So, although we love exoplanets, we're trying to find signs of life... Ultimately, there is no planet B. So definitely our search for another Earth informs us just how wonderful our planet is. All the pieces evolve together and fit in an amazing way. And I love our planet. And we really need to spend what resources we can taking care of our planet. Honestly, I'd love to find signs of life anywhere in our solar system or beyond, But it's going to be challenging. It's very hard to find exoplanets. It's very hard to study them. My memoir is titled The Smallest Lights in the Universe, and that has a dual meaning. It means to find the other Earths, we have to really aim to find literally the smallest lights out there. But it also means something here on Earth. It means when times are tough, whether that's the world around us or like a personal tragedy, it means that we have to find those little lights, the smallest lights in our own internal universe, and grab onto those and cherish them.
