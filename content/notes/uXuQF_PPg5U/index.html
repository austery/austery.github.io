赌场老虎机异常与随机性的疑问

在圣路易斯的卢米埃尔广场赌场（Lumière Place Casino），一名男子把手机举在老虎机前，录制屏幕上的转动画面。他玩了几局，有赢有输，随后起身走开。五分钟后，他返回同一台机器前继续下注，但这一次他几乎每转必赢。赌博不到一个小时，他走出赌场时便赚了大约一万美元。

He won some, lost some, and then he got up and walked away. Five minutes later, he was back playing the same machine, but now he was winning on spin after spin. (machine chiming) (coins clattering) After less than an hour of gambling, he walked out of the casino roughly $10,000 richer. (dramatic music)

在密苏里州、伊利诺伊州和加利福尼亚州的至少十家赌场里，同样的事情接连发生。赌徒们会先在老虎机上玩一会儿然后离开，几分钟后返回便开始连续赢钱。调查人员介入调查，但检查老虎机后发现设备似乎都运转正常。

英文原文 At least 10 casinos across Missouri, Illinois, and California saw the same thing. Gamblers would play on a slot machine for a while and then leave, and minutes later, they would return and start winning. Investigators were brought in, but when they examined the slot machines, they all seemed to be working fine.

于是他们把注意力转向监控录像，发现了一些奇怪的现象。当这些玩家返回时，他们的操作方式不同于普通赌徒。他们会把手悬停在旋转按钮上方，等待片刻，然后在极具特异性的时刻按下，仿佛事先知道何时按下才能获胜。

英文原文 So they turned their attention to the security footage, and they noticed something strange. When these players came back, they didn't play like normal gamblers. They'd hover their hand over the spin button, wait, and then press at oddly specific moments as if they knew when to press to win.

这或许合乎逻辑：也许存在某种预测后续结果的方法，因为那些滚轮转动的结果实际上是由机身内部的计算机决定的。赌博固然不可取，但赌场为研究随机性提供了一个理想场所，因为从理论上讲，计算机的每次旋转都应该使用一个随机数。然而，这些数字真的是随机的吗？这个看似简单的问题，竟然将《我的世界》（Minecraft）中寻找钻石、早期核武器的研发以及整个数字世界的安全紧密联系在一起。这是一个关于制造真正的随机性几乎不可能实现的故事。

英文原文 And maybe that makes sense. Perhaps there's some way to predict what was coming next because the outcomes of those spinning wheels are actually decided by a computer inside the machine. Now gambling is a terrible idea, but the casino provides an ideal venue for investigating randomness, because theoretically, that computer uses a random number for every spin. So are those numbers actually random? Well, that seemingly simple question turns out to connect finding diamonds in Minecraft, to the development of the first nuclear weapons, to the security of the entire digital world. 核反应模拟与蒙特卡洛方法的诞生

早在20世纪40年代，物理学家就发现几率在核弹内部发生的过程中扮演着极其重要的角色。其机制大致如下：以一块铀-235核材料为例，当中子撞击U-235原子核时，可能导致原子核裂变，从而释放能量以及两到三个新的中子。如果平均每次裂变能引发超过一次的后续裂变，就会产生失控的链式反应。

英文原文 Back in the 1940s, physicists discovered that chance plays a surprisingly important role in what happens inside a nuclear bomb. It works something like this. Take a core of uranium-235. When a neutron hits a U-235 nucleus, it can cause the nucleus to split, which releases energy and around two or three more neutrons. Now, if on average each split triggers more than one more split, you get a runaway chain reaction. (explosion roars) (ground rumbling)

但这能否发生取决于这些中子穿行的路径。每个中子在撞击原子核前飞行了多远？它是被弹开、被吸收，还是引发了另一次裂变？到1945年，洛斯阿拉莫斯（Los Alamos）的科学家已经测量出了这些事件各自发生的几率，但这些几率会随着中子的速度以及穿行介质的变化而改变。面对数以万亿计的中子，可能性实在太多，根本无法逐一精确计算。

英文原文 But whether that happens depends on the path those neutrons take. How far does each one travel before it hits a nucleus, or does it bounce off, or get absorbed, or does it cause another to split? By 1945, scientists working at Los Alamos had measured the odds of each of these events happening, but those odds changed depending on things like the neutron's speed and what it was passing through. And across trillions and trillions of neutrons, there were far too many possibilities to calculate one by one. (dramatic music)

研究该问题的数学家之一是斯塔尼斯瓦夫·乌拉姆（Stanislaw Ulam）。但在1946年1月的一个早晨，乌拉姆醒来后无法说话。他患上了脑炎，这种脑部炎症差点要了他的命。幸运的是他挺了过来，但康复过程漫长。他在医院度过了数周，期间经常玩纸牌接龙，并开始思考：“赢得一局游戏的几率究竟有多大？”

英文原文 One of the mathematicians working on this problem was Stanislaw Ulam. But then one morning in January 1946, Ulam woke up and couldn't speak. It was encephalitis, an inflammation of the brain that nearly killed him. Luckily, he survived, but his recovery was slow. So he spent weeks in a hospital playing a lot of solitaire, and he started to wonder, "What are the chances of winning a game?" (light curious music)

起初这听起来可能是一个简单的问题，但一副52张的扑克牌有52的阶乘种洗牌方式，也就是大约8乘以10的67次方种不同的排列方式，而且每一种洗牌牌局的发展都会截然不同。乌拉姆花了很多时间试图通过解析方法求出答案，却一无所获。随后他产生了一个简单得多的想法：“我想知道，一种更实用的方法是否是直接摆牌，比如说摆100次，纯粹观察并统计成功通关的次数。”

英文原文 Now, at first, that might sound like a simple question, but a deck of 52 cards can be shuffled in 52 factorial ways, and that's about 8 times 10 to the power of 67 different arrangements. And every one of those shuffles is going to play out differently. Now, Ulam spent a lot of time trying to work out the answer to his question analytically, and he got nowhere. But then he had a much simpler idea. "I wondered whether a more practical method "might not be to lay it out, say, 100 times "and simply observe and count the number "of successful plays."

这能为他提供某种统计上的近似答案。比方说，如果100局中赢了8局，那么整体获胜的概率大概就在8%左右。正是在那一刻，乌拉姆意识到他可以将完全相同的想法应用于中子研究。他不必尝试计算每一种可能的路径，而是可以只从100个中子开始，利用随机数来模拟每个中子将会发生的情况。

英文原文 That would give him some sort of statistical approximation of the answer. So say you win 8 games out of a 100. Well, then your overall chances of winning are probably around 8%. And that's when Ulam realized he could use exactly the same idea, but for neutrons. So instead of trying to calculate every possible path, he could just start with a hundred neutrons and use random numbers to simulate what would happen to each of them.

假设一个中子撞击了一个原子：如果掷出1、2或3，它从原子核弹开；如果掷出4或5，它导致原子核裂变；如果掷出6，它被吸收。接着只需追踪该中子及其产生的任何其他中子的后续状态，并使用更多随机数来决定它们的命运。

英文原文 So say a neutron hits an atom. If I roll a one, a two, or a three, it bounces off the nucleus. If I roll a four or a five, it causes the nucleus to split. And if I roll a six, it gets absorbed. And then I just follow what happens to that neutron and any other neutrons that it produces using more random numbers, and that will decide their fate.

这些只是举例说明的概率数值。在实际模拟中，科学家们使用已经测得的概率来决定哪些随机数对应哪些结果。每次相互作用之后，中子的速度或方向也可能发生改变。因此在下一步中，他们会采用对应其新状态的概率，使用另一个随机数，并继续追踪后续发展。通过重复这一过程，他们可以看到所有相互作用累加起来的效果：如果中子数量持续增加，链式反应就会爆发；但如果损失的中子多于产生的中子，反应就会自行熄灭。

英文原文 Now those are just example odds. In the real simulations, the scientists used probabilities they had already measured to decide which random numbers corresponded to which outcomes. And after each interaction, the neutron's speed or direction could also change. So for the next step, they'd used the probabilities for its new situation. So use another random number, and then they kept following what happened. And by repeating this process, they could see how all those interactions added up. If the number of neutrons keeps growing, the chain reaction takes off. But if more are lost than are produced, it fizzles out.

因此，仅通过模拟所有可能路径中极小的一部分，乌拉姆就能估算出整个核链式反应的演化行为。然而，纯靠人工完成所有这些计算似乎是不可能的。幸运的是，同为数学家兼洛斯阿拉莫斯顾问的约翰·冯·诺伊曼（John von Neumann）当时一直在使用世界上最早的电子计算机之一——埃尼阿克（ENIAC）。在1947年3月，冯·诺伊曼推导出了如何让计算机在短短五个小时内追踪100个中子及其后代经历的数千次碰撞。

英文原文 So by simulating just a tiny fraction of the possible paths, Ulam could estimate how an entire nuclear chain reaction would behave. But doing all of these calculations by hand seemed impossible. Luckily, fellow mathematician and Los Alamos advisor John von Neumann had been working with one of the world's first electronic computers, the ENIAC. In March 1947, he worked out how it could follow 100 neutrons and their descendants through thousands of collisions in just five hours.

平方取中法与伪随机数的陷阱

但是 ENIAC 无法通过掷骰子来决定中子的命运。在那个时代，研究人员需要随机数时，使用的是像这种印满随机数字的书籍。而 ENIAC 根本没有足够的内存来存储它所需要的全部随机数。因此，冯·诺伊曼想出了一种出人意料的简单方法，让 ENIAC 能够自行生成随机数。

英文原文 But the ENIAC couldn't roll a die to decide what would happen to a neutron. At the time, when researchers needed random numbers, they used books of random digits like this one. And the ENIAC didn't have nearly enough memory to store all the random numbers it would need either. So von Neumann came up with a surprisingly simple way for the ENIAC to generate its own random numbers.

假设我想生成一组随机的四位数。我们的做法是先选定一个数字作为“种子”（seed）。比如我们试试 2137。接下来要做的是将其平方：2137 的平方是 4,566,769。现在我要取这个数字中间的四位数，但在此之前必须在前面补一个零，因为初始结果是一个七位数。我不会使用边缘的数字，因为它们实际上更容易预测。末尾的两位数仅取决于种子的末尾，因为 37 的平方以 1369 结尾；因此任何以 37 结尾的数，其平方都会以 69 结尾，这就是为什么我们要去掉边缘的数字。此外，2100多范围内的每个种子平方后都在440万到490万之间，所以它们都以 0 和 4 开头，因此也可以去掉开头的数字。

英文原文 Let's say I want to generate a bunch of random four digit numbers. So the way we're going to do this is I'm actually going to start by choosing one, which is called the seed. So let's try with 2,137. So what I'm going to do next is square it, and 2,137 squared is 4,566,769. Now I'm going to take the four middle digits of this number, but to do that first, I have to add a zero to the front because we initially had a seven digit number. I won't use the digits at the edges because they're actually more predictable. So the last two digits only depend on the end of the seed because 37 squared ends in 1,369. So anything ending in 37, squares to something ending in 69. So that's why we get rid of these guys here. Also, every seed in the 2,100s is going to square to somewhere between 4.4 and 4.9 million. So they all start with a zero and a four, so we can get rid of that too.

但是中间的数字会受到种子中每个数位的影响。因此，序列中的下一个数字将是 5667。如果我们把 5667 平方，会得到 32,114,889。现在去掉两端的两位数，就可以一直这样计算下去。观察这些数字，表面上看不出明显的规律，但看看如果继续算下去会发生什么：从 2137 开始，经过 43 步后得到 4100，接着是 8100、6100、2100，然后再次出现 4100。序列陷入了这个死循环。从这里开始，生成器将无限循环生成这四个相同的数字，并且所有四位数种子中有接近三分之二最终都会落入完全相同的循环中。

英文原文 But the middle digits get a contribution from every digit in the seed. So 5,667 is going to be the next number in the sequence. So if we square that, we get 32,114,889. Now I disregard the two digits at either end, and you can just keep going. (upbeat music) Looking at these numbers, there's no obvious pattern, but watch what happens if I keep going. Starting from 2,137, after 43 steps, I get 4,100, then 8,100, and then 6,100, and then 2,100, and then 4,100 again. The sequence gets stuck in this loop. From here on, the generator will produce these same four numbers over and over forever, and nearly two-thirds of all four digit seeds end up in this exact loop. (dramatic music)

另一些种子最终会归零，而一旦变成零，之后生成的所有数字也全都是零。近五分之一的起始值都会落入这种结局。事实上，如果尝试所有可能的四位起始值，每一个序列最终都会卡在仅有的八个陷阱之一中。冯·诺伊曼深知这些陷阱是一个严重的问题。因此在第一次 ENIAC 模拟中，团队实际上使用的是八位数而不是四位数，这样在发生循环前能够生成长得多的序列。他们还人工对前2000个数字进行了随机性统计检验。随后，为了避免遭遇意外循环的风险，他们让 ENIAC 在计算完这2000个数字后重新从初始种子开始。

英文原文 Others end up at zero, and once they do every number that follows is also zero. Almost one in five starting values end up here. In fact, if you try every possible four digit starting value, every single sequence eventually gets stuck in one of just eight traps. Now, von Neumann knew that these traps were a serious problem. So for the first ENIAC simulation, the team actually used eight digit numbers, not four, so they produced much longer sequences before they looped. They also statistically tested the first 2,000 numbers for randomness by hand. Then rather than risk running into an unexpected loop, they programmed the ENIAC to restart from the original seed after it had got through these 2,000 numbers.

1948年春，ENIAC 终于完成了首次利用计算机对中子行为的模拟。冯·诺伊曼在给乌拉姆的信中写道：“结果非常可喜，该方法显然获得了百分之百的成功。”这种方法后来被称为蒙特卡洛方法（Monte Carlo method），得名于摩纳哥的蒙特卡洛，乌拉姆的叔叔曾喜欢在那里赌博。如今，它已成为统计学与科学计算中最重要的工具之一。

英文原文 And in the spring of 1948, the ENIAC finally ran the first computerized simulations of neutron behavior. Von Neumann wrote to Ulam, "The results are very promising, "and the method is clearly a 100% success." The approach became known as the Monte Carlo method, named after Monte Carlo, Monaco, where Ulam's uncle liked to gamble. Today, it's one of the most important tools in statistics and scientific computing.

然而，这一系列数字并非真正的随机。如果你以相同的种子开始，每次都会得到完全相同的序列。因此它们被称为“伪随机数”（pseudorandom numbers），但这并未妨碍它们发挥巨大作用。随着蒙特卡洛方法传播到洛斯阿拉莫斯之外，对伪随机数的需求也水涨船高。尽管如此，正如冯·诺伊曼戏谑所言：“任何考虑用算术方法生成随机数的人，当然都处于堕落状态。”因为伪随机数带来了三大挑战：第一，序列可能陷入短周期循环；第二，即使看起来随机的序列也可能潜藏模式；第三，如果有人知道了种子，就能推算出其后的每一个数字——它们是可预测的。

英文原文 Now, this sequence of numbers isn't truly random. If you start with the same seed, you get exactly the same sequence every time. So they're called pseudorandom numbers, but that didn't actually stop them from being useful. As Monte Carlo spread beyond Los Alamos, so did this demand for pseudorandom numbers. Still, as von Neumann joked, "Anyone who considers arithmetical methods "of producing random digits is, of course, "in a state of sin." Because pseudorandom numbers came with three major challenges. One, sequences could get stuck in these short loops. Two, even sequences that looked random could hide patterns. And three, if someone knew the seed, they could work out every number that followed. They were predictable.

在我们玩的这个《我的世界》版本中，游戏使用一种算法生成伪随机数，用以决定黏土和钻石等资源出现在何处。其运作原理大致如下：假设我拿一个有七个刻度的转盘，上面的数字从 0 到 6。我们要做的操作是从 1 开始，然后乘以某个数字，比如 3。1 乘以 3 得 3，所以把指针拨到 3。接着我们再把 3 乘以 3 得到 9，但转盘上只有七个位置，所以必须绕圈循环。实际上我们所做的是用 9 除以 7，余数是 2，所以指针绕回到了 2。这种绕圈回旋的模式被称为模运算（modular arithmetic），或者简称为 mod。

英文原文 - In this version of Minecraft we're playing, the game uses an algorithm to generate the pseudorandom numbers that determine where resources like clay and diamonds appear.
