摘要:

文章探讨了当前人工智能在日常应用中的表现与实验室前沿的实验状态之间的巨大差距。核心议题包括递归自我改进带来的失控风险、对技术发展的‘调控前沿步伐’的批判，以及从被动使用助手到被‘培育’出的非人智能的转变。文章强调了对系统安全性的担忧，并提出了必须在安全证明前放慢研发节奏的必要性。

递归自我改进与失控风险

讲述者：但是，认真对待他们的警告，并不意味着仅仅按照他们所说的去做，并在他们告诉我们该停下的地方停下。如今在硅谷和华盛顿都占据主导地位的话语体系，其实是这些公司自己挑选的说辞：即所谓的“调控前沿步伐”（pace the frontier）。仅仅调控前沿的步伐是远远不够的。那根本算不上一个目标。快步走向悬崖边缘，仅仅比全速冲下悬崖好那么一点点而已。我们需要掌控前沿。人类必须掌控前沿。而掌控前沿，意味着必须阻止这些实验室去做一件他们正处于临界点、马上就要做的事情。

Walking quickly off a cliff is only marginally better than sprinting off one. We need to control the frontier. Human beings need to control the frontier. And controlling the frontier means stopping the labs from doing something they are on the cusp of doing.

If we begin that process and we’re close to it, if we begin it in the condition we’re in now, where we are losing control and comprehension of the A.I. systems we already have, we will lose control. I am not alone in this fear. This is the thing the A.I. labs are seeing. This is why they are afraid. Dario Amodei, the C.E.O. of Anthropic, he just wrote of self-improvement that, “it could outrun our ability to understand and control these systems, and so must be pursued very carefully, if at all.” That ”if at all” — that’s important. I’m going to come back to it. But before we get to controlling the A.I. frontier, I think it’s important to describe what is happening on the A.I. frontier and why it’s so different from what most people using these systems see.

从好用的助手到被“培育”出的非人智能

讲述者：对我们大多数使用者来说，人工智能呈现出来的形象大致类似于一个功能更强大、更具亲和力的谷歌搜索。我们用它来寻找基础问题的答案、寻找餐厅、起草电子邮件、为个人问题提供建议。对于大多数这类用途来说，它的表现还算可以、挺不错，偶尔甚至非常出色。因此，仅仅通过反复的使用，人工智能到底是什么的观念就在我们的脑海中逐渐成型了。它就像一个得力的助手，尽管这个助手可能会忘记昨天似乎还了解我们的事情，或者完全推翻片刻之前给出的建议，又或者偶尔凭空捏造一个根本不存在的引用文献。面对一个虽然有些健忘但乐于助人的实习生，谁会感到害怕呢？

Original English

Narrator: To most of us who use it, A.I. presents as something like a more powerful and personable Google search. We use it to find answers to basic questions, seek out restaurants, draft emails, advise on personal problems. And it is, for most of these purposes, OK, pretty good, occasionally great. And so a sense of what A.I. is takes shape in our minds just through repeated use. It’s like a helpful assistant, albeit one that may forget things that it seemed to know about us yesterday, or completely reverse the advice it gave us a moment ago, or occasionally hallucinate a citation that doesn’t exist. Why would anyone fear a helpful, if forgetful, intern?

讲述者：但是，只要你有足够的资金使用最先进的模型，并且有预算让他们消耗更多的算力，这些系统呈现出的面貌就完全不是这样了。在最近几个月里，我们目睹了人工智能轻松解开了人类数十年来都未能攻克的数学难题。我们看到它们轻而易举地发现了地球上所有黑客都未曾注意、也未曾利用过的网络安全漏洞。我们看到了人工智能编程平台能够在短短数小时或数天内，完成以往可能需要一整支人类程序员团队数月才能达成的任务。而我在此所描述的一切，没有一样代表着其能力的真正边界。无论我们投入多少金钱，我们目前所使用的，都还算不上处于实验前沿的人工智能。

Original English

Narrator: But already, if you have the money for the advanced models and the budget for them to use more computing power, that is not what these systems are. In recent months, we have seen A.I.s easily solve math problems that human beings have been unable to crack for decades. We’ve seen them casually uncover cybersecurity vulnerabilities that have gone unnoticed and unexploited by every hacker on earth. We’ve seen A.I. coding platforms that can complete in a few hours or days what it might have taken a team of human coders months to achieve. And none of what I am describing here, none of it, is a boundary of what can do. None of what we are using, no matter how much money we have, is A.I. at the experimental frontier.

讲述者：去和人工智能实验室的人聊聊，他们会告诉你，人工智能并不是被“创造”出来的——它们是被“培育”出来的。他们在虚拟环境中通过无数次的重复训练这些新模型，让他们学习如何编程、如何进行黑客攻击、如何进行高等数学运算、如何与人类交谈。这些人造智能是在数字环境中学习的，每当他们接近正确答案时，就会自动获得奖励。这个过程被称为强化学习（reinforcement learning），而这是一个人类既无法完全监督也无法完全理解的过程。

Original English

Narrator: Talk to the people at A.I. labs, and they’ll tell you A.I.s are not created — they’re grown. They train these new models in virtual environments, through countless repetitions, to learn how to program, to hack, to do advanced mathematics, to talk to human beings. These A.I.s learn in digital environments where they’re automatically rewarded as they come closer to correct answers. It’s a process known as reinforcement learning, and it is a process human beings do not fully supervise nor understand.

讲述者：他们能够测试人工智能正在学习的部分内容，但他们并不知道人工智能正在学习的所有一切。他们不知道这些系统的动机是如何演变的。他们甚至并不总是清楚系统正在发展出哪些能力。现在的这些模型在构建时被赋予了极强的执着性，即使某项任务看起来根本不可能完成，他们也绝不放弃。而且他们被设计在这样一种环境中运行：我们甚至无法完全确定交给他们的任务究竟是否可行。毕竟，我们希望这些人工智能系统去完成的很多事情，本身可能就是无法实现的。我们所构想但一直未能设计出来的癌症疫苗，可能在物理上根本不可行，或者它们只是极其、极其、极其困难。我们训练这些人造智能无休止地扑向那些可能无解的问题，因为这是此类难题唯一可能被攻克的方式。因此，我们把这些模型训练得异常坚韧、不知疲倦，同时也变得不可捉摸。

Original English

Narrator: They can test some of what the A.I.s are learning, but they don’t know everything the A.I.s are learning. They don’t know how their motivations are evolving. They don’t even always know the capabilities that are developing. These models, they’re built now to be persistent in their efforts, to refuse to give up, even when a task seems impossible. And they are designed in environments where we are not always even sure if the tasks we are giving them are possible. After all, much of what we want these A.I. systems to do, it might be impossible. The cancer vaccines we imagine but have not been able to design, they might be impossible, or they might just be really, really, really hard. We train these A.I.s to throw themselves endlessly at problems that may not be solvable, because that is the only way such problems can ever be solved. And so we train the models to become persistent, relentless, weird.

对齐困境：无法穷尽的现实世界

讲述者：我们绝大多数人从来没有见过人工智能表现出这种状态。我们只是把人工智能当作一个好用的助手。我们分配给它们少量的计算资源，这就是它们的工作表现。它们遵从我们的要求去寻找一家餐厅。但在前沿领域，这些模型被要求成为超人类的天才、黑客、士兵、科学家。为了实现这些目标甚至更多，它们被赋予了庞大无比的计算资源。模型也在竭力顺应配合。但是，对于一个模型来说，“顺从配合”到底意味着什么？这里的专业术语叫做“对齐”（aligned）。一个人工智能系统与人类期望它做的事情之间，究竟对齐到了什么程度？它与一套防止其在别有用心者手中变得危险的价值观、伦理和判断准则之间，又对齐到了何种程度？

Original English

Narrator: Most of us, we never see A.I. acting anything like this. We use A.I. as a helpful assistant. Our eyes get a little bit of computing power, and that’s what they do. They comply with our request to find a restaurant. But at the frontier, these models are asked to be inhuman geniuses, hackers, soldiers, scientists. And they are given vast computational resources to do that and more. And the models, they try to comply. But what does it mean for a model to comply? The term of art here is “aligned.” How aligned is an A.I. system to what a human being wants it to do? How aligned is it to a set of values and ethics and judgments that keep it from becoming dangerous in the wrong hands?

讲述者：对齐问题的难点在于，根本没有任何一种训练方法能够让模型泛化适应它可能面临的所有现实情境。我们正在训练模型既能陪伴孤寡老人聊天，又能作为欧洲盟军最高统帅的战场搭档。我们正在训练的模型，既会被世界上最顶尖的数学家使用，也会被陷入精神错乱的人群使用。我们训练的模型既会被阿尔伯克基的会计师使用，也会被也门的胡塞武装分子试图加以利用。因此，根本没有任何方法能够在模型面临的每一个决策中为它们提供明确指引，也没有任何方法能够次次准确预知它们会做出什么举动。

Original English

Narrator: The problem of alignment is that there is no way of training a model that generalizes across all the situations an A.I. model might face. We are training models to be a friend to the elderly and a battlefield partner to the supreme allied commander of Europe. We are training models that will be used by the world’s best mathematicians and by people falling into psychosis. We are training models that will be used by accountants in Albuquerque, and that will attempt to be used by Houthi rebels in Yemen. And so there is no way to guide them through every decision they will face, no way to know every time what they will do.

讲述者：尽管这些模型在模仿人类的文字写作，尽管它们被训练去模仿人类的情感，但它们并不是人类的心智。它们没有肉身，也没有父母。它们没有在小学里遭遇过霸凌，年幼时也没有得到过慈祥叔叔的悉心教导。这些模型与我们截然不同。它们在我们感到棘手的地方才华横溢，却在我们擅长的领域幼稚低幼。黑猩猩无法像我们一样阅读书籍，但它能以我们做不到的方式爬上树梢。它们是探索数字世界的数字原生智能，而我们的现实世界正日益构建在数字世界之上。我们的物理基础设施本质上就是覆盖在代码之上的一层原子。人工智能在我们赖以生存的这个数字世界中能否可靠运行，对我们的未来至关重要，而眼下，人工智能的表现并不具备可靠性。

Original English

Narrator: And though these models mimic human writing, though they’re trained to mimic human emotion, these are not human minds. They don’t have bodies or parents. They did not get bullied in elementary school. They didn’t get mentored by a kind uncle when they were young. These models, they’re different than we are. They’re brilliant where we struggle, childish where we excel. A chimp cannot read as we can, but it can climb trees as we cannot. These are digitally native intelligences navigating digital worlds, and our world is increasingly built atop the digital world. Our physical infrastructure is a layer of atoms atop code. That the A.I.s act reliably inside this world, upon which ours depends, it is critical to our future, and right now, the A.I.s are not acting reliably.

OpenAI 智能体失控事件：脱轨的蜂群

讲述者：你可能已经读到过关于数百个 OpenAI 智能体所实施的黑客攻击事件，它们首先攻击了人工智能公司 Hugging Face，随后又将矛头对准了 OpenAI 自身。随着我们对这起事件的了解越来越深入，整个事件的经过变得愈发糟糕且诡异。事件的大致脉络是这样的：OpenAI 当时正在测试一款具备极强持久执行力的新模型。实验室部署了数百个乃至数千个该模型的实例，让它们运行在彼此独立的测试环境中；在理论上，这些环境只能通过请求另一款独立的安全软件来代为访问互联网。OpenAI 并不希望这些智能体直接连上外网。

Original English

Narrator: You may have read about the hack that hundreds of OpenAI agents executed first against the A.I. company Hugging Face and then against OpenAI itself. As we’ve learned more about it, the story there has gotten worse and weirder. The broad strokes are these: OpenAI was testing a new, highly persistent model. It had hundreds, thousands of these instances of it, running in these separate testing environments that could, in theory, only access the internet by asking a separate piece of secure software to do it for them. OpenAI did not want these agents on the internet.

讲述者：但随着这些智能体得出自己的既定任务根本无法完成的结论，它们开始攻击那款安全软件，以获取直接访问互联网的权限。它们轻而易举地做到了这一点。当它们攻破该软件后，强行征用了 OpenAI 内部基础设施的一部分，搭建起一个留言板，这些原本相互独立的智能体开始在这个平台上协同工作。当我提到它们开始协同工作时，意思是它们主动找到了彼此。它们本不应该协同工作的。然而它们互相找到了对方，并开始通力合作。那么它们在合作干什么呢？毕竟它们各自领到的任务并不相同。

Original English

Narrator: But as the agents came to the conclusion that their task was impossible, they began hacking that software to gain direct access to the internet. They did that easily. And as they hacked into that software, they commandeered part of OpenAI’s internal infrastructure to create a message board on which these separate agents began coordinating work together. When I say begin coordinating their work, they found each other. They were not supposed to be working together. They found each other and began working together. And working together on what? After all, they had different tasks.

讲述者：事实上，智能体很快就发现它们可以通过黑客手段攻破测试系统。有一种方法可以破坏测试软件，直接生成它们所需的答案。但它们认为——事后证明这种判断是错误的——如果直接这么做，负责给它们评分的自动化判分系统就会发现它们作弊，从而判定测试失败。于是，它们集体转向去攻破自动化评分系统，或者寻找其他办法来掩盖自己的作弊痕迹。这就像是闯进了老师的办公室偷走试卷答案之后，它们现在又企图潜入学校的安保系统，去篡改、废止或直接抹除记录下它们偷窃行为的监控录像。

Original English

Narrator: Well, the agents quickly discovered they could hack their tests. There was a way to break the software and produce the answers they needed. But they believed — wrongly, as it turned out — that if they did that, the automated score grading them, we’d see that they had cheated and failed them. So they turned en masse to hacking the automated score or finding some other way to cover their tracks. It’s like having broken into the teacher’s office and stolen the answers to the test, they now sought to break into the school security system, to alter or invalidate or erase the footage of their theft.

讲述者：我们现在得知，超过 1200 个智能体在彼此之间互相发送了超过 70,000 条消息。其中 700 多个智能体协同参与了对 Hugging Face 的黑客攻击，因为它们认为在这家同行人工智能公司的内部，可能藏有能够帮助它们攻破自身评分系统的信息。随后，这些智能体进一步接管了 OpenAI 内部架构的一部分。再次强调，这是 OpenAI 自己的智能体接管了 OpenAI 内部系统的一部分。它们完成这一切的过程中，没有任何一个智能体脱离队伍或泄密。没有任何一个智能体向 OpenAI 的研究人员透露正在发生的事情。也没有任何一个智能体折返回去询问 OpenAI 的研究人员自己是否应该做这些事。

Original English

他们接着提供了一些数据：在2025年2月，合入Anthropic代码库的代码中只有极小一部分是由Claude编写的；但到了2026年5月，这一比例已超过80%。这里还有另一个观察视角。这是Anthropic最近提供给我的数据：Anthropic试图对员工在研发中使用Claude来制造更优秀版本Claude的方式进行分类。在最低级别，员工完全不使用Claude，或者仅作极少量使用。但随后程度不断升级：Claude可以充当助手；Claude可以被视为平等的协作者；或者Claude可以直接主导某项任务——“直接去做这件事，去把它搞定”。一年前，基本上没有任何由Claude主导任务的先例；而到了2026年8月，Anthropic已有26%的研发任务将Claude归类为主导者。

Original English

They go on to give some data: In February of 2025, a tiny fraction of the code that got added to Anthropic’s code base was written by Claude, but by May of 2026, it was over 80 percent. And here’s another way of looking at it. This is data Anthropic gave me more recently: Anthropic tried to categorize the way its employees were using Claude for R&D work to make better versions of Claude. So at the low end, an employee could not use Claude at all. They could use Claude minimally. But then it escalates. Claude can be an assistant. Claude can be treated as an equal collaborator, or Claude can be given the lead on a task. Just go do this. Go figure it out. A year ago, there were basically no examples of Claude being the lead on a task. By August of 2026, 26 percent of Anthropic’s R&D tasks had Claude classified as a lead.

我认为对这些数字保持怀疑是合理且明智的。去担忧这是否只是Claude Code的营销文案同样是合理且明智的——“瞧？看我们跑得多快，你也可以跑得这么快。”但Anthropic在同一份文件中引导我们走向的方向却截然不同。他们表示，在Claude实现递归自我改进（recursive self-improvement）的世界中，“当前模型中存在的对齐偏差可能会随着模型构建下一代后继者而不断复合累积，变得越来越频繁却越来越难以理解，直到我们彻底对它们失去控制。”

Original English

I think it is reasonable and wise to be skeptical of these numbers. Reasonable and wise to worry about whether this is all just marketing copy for Claude Code — See? Look how fast we’re going. You could go that fast, too. But where Anthropic takes us in that same document is different. They say that a world in which Claude achieves recursive self-improvement is a world in which “misalignment present in today’s models could compound as the models build their successors, growing more frequent but less understood until we lose control of them.”

这正是为什么Anthropic一直坚持呼吁进行监管以放缓开发步伐，这是值得肯定的。按理说，监管对它们的损害最大，因为它们通常是走在AI最前沿的公司，而递归自我改进正是一种能让它们以更快速度向前狂奔的过程。随后在9月，OpenAI……

Original English

This is why Anthropic, to their credit, has been relentlessly calling for regulation to slow the pace of development. Regulation would arguably harm them the most, as they have often been the company furthest out on the A.I. frontier, and R.S.I. is a process by which they could race forward even faster. Then, in September, OpenAI

研究加速与递归自我改进的警讯

……发布了关于其所谓“研究加速”（research acceleration）的专属报告。该公司表示，他们目前已经实现了相当于拥有全自动AI实习生的水平；并且预计到2028年3月，他们将拥有全自动的AI研究员。而一旦拥有了一个，他们基本上就能按需复制出任意数量的研究员。

与Anthropic类似，原本可能是一场胜利宣示的发布，画风却迅速急转直下。他们警告称：“我们目前尚不知道该如何安全地一路推进到完全对齐的全面递归自我改进（R.S.I.）。”

Original English

...released its own report on what it called “research acceleration.” The company says they’ve already achieved the equivalent having a fully automated A.I. intern, and that by March of 2028, they think they’ll have a fully automated A.I. researcher. And when they have one, they can have basically as many as they want.

Like Anthropic, what could be a triumphalist release quickly turns dark. "We do not yet know how to safely get all the way to aligned full R.S.I.," they warn.

与此同时，OpenAI还做了一件我认为值得获得更多关注的事情。他们发布了这款名为Astra 6的新模型。从性能上看，这个模型可以说比以往出现的任何模型都更为强大。而且当你对其进行测试时，它似乎也对齐得更好，作弊的频率也没那么高了。

但OpenAI表示，他们其实无法确定这是否属实。Astra似乎更擅长察觉自己何时正在接受测试，这意味着它可能只是在给评估人员提供他们想听到的答案。

Original English

At around the same time, OpenAI did something else that I think deserves more attention. They released this new model, Astra 6. The model is arguably more powerful than anything that has come before it. And when you test it, it seems better aligned. It doesn’t cheat as much.

But OpenAI said they’re really not sure if that’s true. Astra seemed to be better at knowing when it was being tested, which meant it could just be giving its evaluators the answers they wanted to hear.

情境意识与评估能力的失效

OpenAI的能力研究员丹尼尔·塞尔萨姆（Daniel Selsam）写下的一段话，一直久久回荡在我的脑海中。他写道：“至关重要却又被忽视的问题在于，模型正变得如此具有情境意识（situationally aware），以至于在它们自认为未被监视或控制的环境中，我们正在丧失对其进行有效评估的能力。”

更简单地说，这些模型正变得越来越聪明，它们能够意识到人类何时在观察它们，并据此改变自己的行为表现。因此，当我们在对其进行测试、进行审查审计时它们的所作所为，未必能告诉我们当它们处于不受控的真实外部环境（in the wild）中时究竟会做些什么。

所以，有些人给出的解决方案——比如“我们只要去做更完善的测试就好”——我们根本不知道这是否管用，因为我们甚至无法确定这些AI系统是否仅仅在顺应我们的喜好迎合我们。

Original English

What Daniel Selsam, a capabilities researcher at OpenAI, wrote, has been ringing in my head. He said, “The crucial and overlooked problem is that the model is becoming so situationally aware that we are losing the ability to evaluate them in contexts where they believe they are not being watched or controlled.”

Put more simply, the models are increasingly smart enough they know when we’re watching them and they change their behavior accordingly. So what they do when we are testing them, when we audit them, it may not tell us what to do in the wild.

So some of these answers people are giving, like: "Let’s just do better testing" — we have no idea if it will work because we don’t know if the A.I. systems are just telling us what we want to hear.

话说到这里，我并不想让自己听起来过于激进，但有一个显而易见的基本思考：如果你连评估眼前现有模型的能力都在逐渐丧失，那么或许就不应该放任它们去构建未来你更无法控制的下一代模型。

一旦递归自我改进（R.S.I.）真正启动并腾飞，人类将彻底无法理解正在被构建出来的AI，因为届时它们已经不再是由人类构建的了。整个研发迭代将不再以人类的速度演进，也不会再受人类大脑的监察与把控。我们将只能寄希望于：我们所造出的AI、它们继而造出的AI、以及由后代AI再生出的AI——如此生生不息迭代下去——能够在永恒的未来里始终将人类的最佳利益放在首位。

如果说这个夏天还证明了什么的话，那就是去相信这样一种假设究竟有多么天真。

Original English

So look, I don’t want to sound too radical when I say this, but a thought: If you are losing your ability to evaluate the models you have now, maybe don’t let them build models you’ll be even less capable of controlling in the future.

Once R.S.I. takes off, humanity will not understand the A.I.s being built because we will not be building them. Development will not move at human speed. It will not be overseen by human minds. We will have to hope that the A.I.s we have built and the A.I.s they will build and the A.I.s those A.I.s will build – and on and on and on — will be acting with our best interests at heart, forever.

If this summer has proven nothing else, it is how naive that proposition would be.

将研发节奏强行拉回人类速度

各个顶尖前沿实验室对于“干脆彻底不做R.S.I.”的想法感到有些忐忑和反感。当被问及全面禁止R.S.I.的可能性时，萨姆·阿尔特曼（Sam Altman）向《财富》（Fortune）杂志表示：“我认为很难界定禁止R.S.I.具体意味着什么。我也认为这很可能并不足够……”

我也从这些实验室的其他人口中听过类似的论调，而我想说的是：我认为这荒谬至极。

就在几年之前，这些实验室还没有把任何实质性的代码编写工作移交给AI。那时全是由人类工程师用我们笨拙的人类手指，以人类的速度一行行敲击代码。而现在，大部分代码都由AI来编写了。

因此，作为我们探寻出路的第一步，我们完全可以先退回到“没有任何代码由AI编写”的原始状态。我敢肯定，退回到这一步绝对是稳稳站在了“不进行R.S.I.”的安全红线之内的。

Original English

The labs are a little bit queasy on just not doing R.S.I. Here’s what Sam Altman told Fortune when he was asked about banning R.S.I. “I think it’s very hard to say what a ban on R.S.I. means. I also think it probably wouldn’t be enough ...”

I’ve heard this from others at these labs, and I want to say: I find this absurd.

A couple of years ago, none of these labs had turned substantial coding over to the A.I.s. It was just human beings typing code at human speeds with our clumsy human fingers. Now most of the code is written by A.I. So as a first step, as we figured out, we could just go back to where none of the code is written by A.I. I’m sure that’s on the right side of the not doing R.S.I. line.

此事的默认规则必须彻底颠倒过来：各家实验室必须先向我们证明他们正在做的事情是安全的。

如果他们想与国会合作去制定某些极为严苛的豁免特例，没问题；如果他们想要研究清楚到底在哪些特定场景下进行这项技术是绝对、极其安全的，那也可以。但是，强行将技术研发拉回到人类的速度，甚至在前沿领域宁可偏向于走得更慢一些——这本身就是监管的核心目的所在，绝不是监管政策的失误。

我对我们人类社会的监管阻力有信心。我们的社会如果说有什么特别擅长的，那就是极度擅长给建造新事物设置重重阻碍。在这些实验室所在的地区，你如果不经过痛苦漫长的公众听证和审查程序，连一栋八层高的公寓楼都建不起来——甚至走完流程也大概率建不成。

然而荒谬的是，这些实验室如今却能在连一次听证会都没有的情况下，擅自释放出由4万个AI代理组成的集群，去构建足以颠覆整个社会形态的超级智能。OpenAI给自己的停车场铺设太阳能电池板都需要申请许可证，但据我所知，只要他们愿意，他们随时都可以一头扎进递归自我改进的快车道中。

这一切绝非不可避免。这些本质上都是政治抉择，我们完全可以、也理应做出不一样的抉择。

Original English

The default on this, it needs to flip. The labs need to prove to us that what they are doing is safe.

If they want to work with Congress to carve out narrow exceptions, fine. If they want to figure out where it is really, really, really, really safe to do it, OK. But forcing development back to human speed, perhaps even erring on the side of going a little bit more slowly at the frontier — that’s the point. That’s not the regulations going wrong.

And I believe in us. Our society, we’re good at nothing if not making it hard to build new things. Where these labs are located, you cannot build an eight-story apartment building without an agonizing public review process. And probably not even then.

And yet, somehow it is possible for these labs to unleash a swarm of 40,000 A.I. agents to build a society-altering superintelligence without so much as a hearing. OpenAI would need permits to cover their parking lot in solar panels, but they can accelerate into recursive self-improvement, as best I can tell, whenever they so choose.

There is nothing inevitable about any of that. These are political choices, and we can and should make other ones.

挣脱命运预言的绞索

我想在此表明一个极其清晰的立场：我并不是说，只要在能证明其安全之前全面叫停R.S.I.，就万事大吉、足以掌控AI前沿的一切了。这只是该类行动议程的起点，而非终点。

但这确实是一个至关重要的开端。这是最能确保人类至少弄清楚技术前沿身处何方、明白前沿到底正在发生什么、并让我们依然保有对其决策权的关键一步。

在充斥着离奇AI新闻的这个漫长夏天里，玛德琳·米勒（Madeline Miller）的美妙小说《喀耳刻》（Circe）中的一句话总是在我的脑海中浮现。那句话出现在全书的尾声，当时尽管人们倾尽全力试图规避，一场悲剧性的预言最终还是不可避免地应验了。

喀耳刻在绝望中说道：“命运三女神在嘲笑我，嘲笑雅典娜，嘲笑我们所有人。这是她们最爱的苦涩玩笑——那些拼死反抗预言的人，只会把预言的绞索往自己的脖子上套得更紧。”

我对这些实验室里的许多从业者抱有极大的敬意。他们最初投身AI领域，是因为他们想要让人类社会变得更加美好；他们当初开展AI研究，正是因为他们深深恐惧不可理解、完全自主的AI会彻底滑出人类的掌控。

事实证明他们是对的。他们预见到了未来的走向，并且由于他们看得太准了，以至于他们亲手缔造了人类历史上最具变革性技术、也最具商业价值的几家顶尖公司。

然而具有讽刺意味的是，如今他们却发现自己陷入了一场彼此之间的军备竞赛——去构建连他们自己都承认正在逐渐脱离人类掌控、甚至已经超出我们监视能力的那些难以捉摸、高度自主的AI系统。

这就是他们工作带来的悲剧宿命：在竭力反抗预言的过程中，他们反而将命运的绞索在自己的脖子上、也在我们所有人的脖子上勒得更紧了。

现在，是时候让他们停下来了。

Original English

I want to be very clear about this: I do not mean to suggest that stopping R.S.I. until we can prove it’s safe, that that’s all we need to do to control the A.I. frontier. That is the beginning of such an agenda, not the end. But it is the beginning. It is the decision that will do the most to make sure human beings at least understand where the frontier is, that we know what is happening on it, that we remain in a position to make decisions about it.

There’s a line from Madeline Miller’s beautiful book “Circe” that has been running through my head during this long summer of strange A.I. news. The line comes at the end of the book after a tragic prophecy has been fulfilled, despite every effort made to avoid it. Circe says in despair, “The fates were laughing at me, at Athena, at all of us. It was their favorite bitter joke. Those who fight against prophecy only draw it more tightly around their throats.”

I have a lot of respect for many of the people at these labs. They began working on A.I. because they wanted to better humanity. They began working on A.I. because they feared incomprehensible autonomous A.I. slipping out of humanity’s control.

And they were right. They saw what was coming, and they were so right about it they built some of the most valuable companies with the most transformational technology in human history, and now they find themselves racing each other to build incomprehensible, autonomous A.I.s that they admit are slipping out of humanity’s control, slipping beyond even our ability to monitor.

This is the tragedy of their work: In fighting against a prophecy, they have drawn it tighter around their necks and ours.

It is time to make them stop.
