摘要:

特斯拉前总裁约翰·麦克尼尔深度拆解埃隆·马斯克打造特斯拉与SpaceX的核心方法论：质疑一切要求、删除冗余步骤、极简化优化、加速周期时间，并坚守“最后才进行自动化”的原则，揭示创业与制造创新的第一性原理。

特斯拉险遭破产的教训

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我们当时差点破产，因为我们没有获得预期的来自 Model 3 的现金流。而我们拯救自己的唯一方法就是退回到人工流程。我们真切地在工厂外面的空地上搭起了一顶帐篷，纯靠手工组装汽车。起初是一周造100辆，然后是一周500辆等等，直到我们能够真正建造出一条反映现实情况的自动化生产线。当我们事后回顾并反思：“看，我们差点把公司搞垮了。如果我们重新来过，会有什么不同的做法？”我们的结论是：把自动化放在最后一步。你必须在自动化之前彻底完善流程。否则，你只会把自己活埋——在那次危机中，我们差一点就万劫不复了。

Original English

John McNeill: We almost went bankrupt because we didn't have the cash flow that we had predicted coming off a Model 3. And the only way we saved ourselves was to go back to the manual process. We literally built a tent in the factory outside and produced cars by hand. First 100 a week, then 500 a week, etc. until we could actually build an automated line that reflected reality. When we looked back on that and said, "Look, we almost killed the company. What would we do differently?" We said automate last. You've got to perfect the process before you automate. Otherwise, you just might bury yourself. And in that case, we almost did.

尼古拉·坦根: 大家好，我是挪威主权财富基金的首席执行官尼古拉·坦根。今天非常荣幸能与约翰·麦克尼尔对话。约翰曾作为 Tesla 总裁直接向 Elon Musk 汇报长达三年，随后担任过 Lyft 的首席运营官，如今在 DVx Ventures 孵化并创立企业。很多人都曾为埃隆工作过，但约翰写了一本关于如何做事的方法论书籍《算法》（The Algorithm），系统阐述了马斯克用于打造特斯拉和 SpaceX 的底层逻辑。今天我们将逐一拆解这些核心步骤，帮助大家理顺业务并取得巨大成功。约翰，非常感谢你的到来。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Hi everyone, I'm Nicolai Tangen, the CEO of the Norwegian Sovereign Wealth Fund and today I'm in really good company with John McNeill. Now, John spent three years reporting directly to Elon Musk as president of Tesla, then was chief operating officer of Lyft, and now he builds companies at DVx Ventures. Lots of people have worked for Elon, but John actually wrote a book about how to do things, The Algorithm, which basically lays out how Musk used to build Tesla and SpaceX. And so, today we are going to go through this, you know, point by point so that you also can sort out your business and make it a huge success. So John, big thank you for coming on here.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 能够与你对话是我的荣幸，尼古拉。

Original English

John McNeill: It's an honor to be on with you, Nicolai.

第一步：质疑每一个需求

尼古拉·坦根: 那么，第一步是质疑每一个需求。请跟我们详细讲讲，有哪些需要被质疑的最简单例子？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Now, step one, question every requirement. Tell us about it. What are some of the simplest examples of things you need to question?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我认为最简单的做法就是从质疑一切开始。因为很多时候，那些在某个行业深耕已久或长期面对某一问题的人，往往从未质疑过底层假设。因此，我们在特斯拉迈向创新的第一步，就是寻找那些长久以来从未被审问或质疑过的环节，并真正开始拷问它们。因为当你开始质疑这些假设时，你会发现它们当中的绝大多数其实都是毫无根据的。

Original English

John McNeill: I think the simplest example is you start to question everything, because often times people who have been in a business or looking at a problem for a long time have not questioned the base assumptions. And so one of the first steps we took towards innovation at Tesla was look for those places that hadn't been touched in a long time by interrogation or questioning and really start to question them, because when you start to question these assumptions, you find out that most of them are really groundless.

尼古拉·坦根: 给我举几个具体的例子，讲讲你们当时质疑过的具体事项。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Give me some examples of some of the stuff that you questioned.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 比如，我们当时试图在线上销售价值10万欧元的汽车，此前从未有人这么做过。我们不得不去质疑整个汽车购买体验：为什么买车非得去实体经销商？为什么买车非得经历四五个小时的折磨，忍受各种推销与讨价还价？难道不能把买车做成像在电商网站上下单那样简单吗？

而在制造端，我们质疑了零件的必要性。比如，埃隆经常问：“为什么这辆车上需要这个支架？这个零件是干什么的？”如果工程师回答：“这是为了防止某个振动频率”，埃隆就会追问：“是谁设定的这个振动规范？我们能把这个支架彻底去掉吗？”很多时候大家才发现，那个要求最初只是某个部门在多年前基于过时条件写下的，根本没有实际物理必要性。如果你不能说出提出该要求的具体自然人姓名，这个要求就是无效的。

Original English

John McNeill: Yeah. So like we were trying to sell 100,000 euro cars online for the first time anybody had done that. We had to question the whole buying experience: why do you have to go to a dealership? Why does it take four or five hours of agony with haggling and finance managers? Why can't it just be like buying something online with a few clicks?

And in manufacturing, we questioned the existence of parts. Elon would ask, "Why is this bracket in the car? What does it do?" And if an engineer said, "Well, it's for noise and vibration," Elon would say, "Who specified that requirement? Can we delete the bracket entirely?" And often people realized the requirement came from a department years ago for a reason that no longer applied. If you can't name the person who created the requirement, the requirement is bogus.

尼古拉·坦根: 供应链负责人也是如此吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: And then the head of supply,...

尼古拉·坦根: 这确实很有启发。但为什么对大多数人来说，打破规则、改变规则会如此困难？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: ...not a bad thing. Why? Why is it so difficult for people to break rules to change rules?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我认为第一点在于，人类天生是复杂化的制造者，而不是简化者。马克·吐温曾有名言：“如果我有更多时间，我会写一封更短的信。”把事情变复杂很容易，而把事情变简单极其艰难。第二点是组织惯性与心理安全感。遵守既定规则永远是最安全的防御机制——“因为以前就是这么做的，即使搞砸了我也不会被解雇”。一旦你要去打破规则，你就必须承担责任与风险。

Original English

John McNeill: I think number one, humans are natural complicators, not simplifiers. You know, Mark Twain had that great line, "I didn't have time to write a short letter, so I wrote a long one instead." Making things simple is really hard work. And number two is organizational inertia and fear. Following a rule is safe; nobody gets fired for following an existing procedure even if it's dumb. The moment you challenge a rule, you take personal ownership and risk.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 比如在汽车金融贷款方面，这是传统购车过程中体验最差的环节之一，充斥着几十页繁琐的纸质文件和漫长的审批。我们当时直接介入并重构了汽车金融。我们质疑为什么审批需要几天时间，为什么需要那么多签字。我们与银行合作开发自动化接口，将审批流程缩短到几分钟之内。当你敢于打破既有流程时，你就能创造出降维打击般的客户体验。

Original English

John McNeill: We touched auto financing. It's one of the worst parts of the customer journey, but it's full of dozens of pages of paper and waiting around. We went in and questioned every piece of paper and requirement. We asked why it takes days to approve a loan, why there are so many manual handoffs. We worked with lenders to automate underwriting and cut the time down to minutes. When you challenge those legacy constraints, you create an exponentially better experience.

如何区分愚蠢的规则

尼古拉·坦根: 那么你该如何区分愚蠢的规则与真正合理的规则？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: How do you tell a dumb rule from a rule that makes sense?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 在我们的认知框架中，真正合理的规则只有三类约束：安全要求、法律法规，以及物理学定律。除了这三者之外，其余的一切要求都只是建议或历史遗留产物。

Original English

John McNeill: Rules that make sense in our world are requirements of safety, of law, or physics. Everything else is open for debate.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 物理学定律是不可逾越的边界。如果一个限制是建立在热力学第一定律或材料极限之上的，那它是坚固的；但如果一个限制仅仅是“某某部门制定的规章”或“行业标准实践”，那它随时可以被挑战和废除。

Original English

John McNeill: And those are kind of immutable. So if there's an assumption based on physics, it's real. But if it's based on a corporate policy or industry convention, it's just a convention and can be discarded.

第二步：删除所有非必需步骤

尼古拉·坦根: 明白。你的书中的第二步是删除每一个非必需步骤。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Yeah. Okay. Step number two in your book, delete every step.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 没错。

Original English

John McNeill: Yeah.

尼古拉·坦根: 这一步具体是如何执行的？在实际中是什么样的？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: So how does that what does that look like?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我们要求管理者采取的实际动作是：逐项列出流程中的每一个步骤，然后激进地进行删除。马斯克有一个著名的原则：如果你最终没有被迫把被删掉步骤的10%再加回来，就说明你删得还不够狠。你必须删到临界点，直到系统稍微出现问题，你才知道什么是真正不可或缺的。

Original English

John McNeill: The practical step that we had our managers take was to literally list every single step in a process and aggressively delete them. Elon's rule of thumb was: if you don't end up adding back at least 10% of what you deleted, you didn't delete enough. You have to push past the comfort zone to see where the real edge is.

尼古拉·坦根: 跟我讲讲你们当时具体砍掉了哪些步骤。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Tell me about some of the stuff you cut.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 在制造环节和贷款审批流程中，我们删除了大量的冗余交接和中间检查。我们去掉了不必要的缓冲库存区，去掉了多重质检站，把质量控制直接内嵌到工人的装配工序中。

Original English

John McNeill: Yeah. In manufacturing processes and loan processing, we eliminated massive amounts of handoffs and intermediary checks. We removed staging areas and redundant inspection stations, putting the quality check directly into the assembly step itself.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 这帮助我们消除了很多传统组织里无处不在的独立质检环节。传统的做法是生产线上做完，再拉到后面让一帮人检查挑毛病。我们推行的是“制造者即质检者”，砍掉后续全部冗余的返工检查环。

Original English

John McNeill: And that helped us eliminate separate quality inspection stations which exist in every traditional manufacturing organization worldwide, where you build something poorly and pay another team down the line to inspect it. We forced quality to be verified at the source and deleted the separate inspection steps.

尼古拉·坦根: 但很显然，如果没有零件，你就无法生产。有没有删过头导致不得不加回来的例子？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: But I mean clearly if you got no part, you got no business. So you know examples where you went too far?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 当然有，这种例子数不胜数。例如在车身制造中，我们曾删掉过某种隔音垫材料，结果导致车辆在特定路况下的噪音超出了可接受范围；或者删掉了某个螺栓，后来在极端耐久性测试中发现结构出现疲劳。我们随后立刻把它们加了回来。但这种测试边界的过程，让我们明确知道了每一个零件存在的绝对必要性。

Original English

John McNeill: Yeah, plenty of those. For instance, we deleted certain sound-dampening acoustic foam pads in the car body, and then found noise levels in certain road conditions were unacceptable, or removed a fastener that caused fatigue in extreme track testing. So we added them back. But that's the point: testing the limits proves exactly what is genuinely necessary.

第三步：极简化与优化

尼古拉·坦根: 第三条规则：简化与优化。这很好，你肯定很欣慰我认真读了你的书。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Rule three, simplify. It's good. You must be impressed that I read the book...

约翰·麦克尼尔: 是的，非常感动！

Original English

John McNeill: Yes.

尼古拉·坦根: 我几乎把书背下来了，约翰。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Yeah. No, I know it by heart, John, you know.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 这太棒了。[笑声]

Original English

John McNeill: Yes. This is wonderful. [laughter]

尼古拉·坦根: 那么，简化与优化与之前的“删除步骤”有什么本质区别？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Okay. Simplify and optimize. So, how is kind of simplification different from deleting?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 当你完成删除之后，你面对的是一个被剥离到极致的新流程。极简化是对剩余的必要步骤进行重构与融合，使其路径最短、摩擦最小。常见的致命错误是去优化一个本该被彻底删除的流程。你必须先删干净，再去简化剩下的核心。

Original English

John McNeill: Once you've deleted, now you have a stripped-down process. Simplification is about streamlining and integrating the remaining necessary steps so they have minimal friction. The most common error is optimizing a process or part that should not exist in the first place. You delete first, then you simplify what remains.

尼古拉·坦根: 什么时候才算“足够简单”？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: When is simple simple enough?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 在我们的实践中，我们的量化标准是追求行业平均利润率的两倍，并将交付周期缩短至行业平均水平的几分之一。当你能用一半的步骤和一半的成本完成相同甚至更好的产出时，简化才算初见成效。

Original English

John McNeill: In our case, we tried to achieve margins that were twice the margins of the industry, and cycle times that were a fraction of the industry benchmark. When you can deliver the outcome with half the steps and half the cost of anyone else, you're getting close to simple enough.

第四步：加速周期时间

尼古拉·坦根: 接下来是速度。第四步是加速周期时间。首先，为什么必须把速度放在第四步？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Now speed. Step four, accelerate cycle time. So first of all, why does it come fourth?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 因为如果你加速一个糟糕的流程，你只是在更快地制造灾难。如果你在一个未经精简、充满错误的流程上追求速度，只会让系统更快地崩溃。

Original English

John McNeill: Basically, because if you speed up a bad process, you're just getting into trouble faster. Speeding up waste simply produces chaos at high velocity.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 因此，只有在我们完成了质疑、删除和简化之后，我们才会全力推行提速。速度不仅能降低在制品库存，还能极大地加快反馈循环，暴露一切潜在问题。

Original English

John McNeill: And so we introduce speed after we've insisted on simplification and deleting. Speed reduces working capital and tightens feedback loops, exposing bottlenecks immediately.

尼古拉·坦根: 在提速方面，你们的核心抓手是什么？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Get the finger out in what sense? In...

约翰·麦克尼尔: 就是全方位提升运转速度。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: ...just get the speed up.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 关键在于两件事：第一是消除一切等待时间与工序间的摩擦停滞；第二是追求极致的精益协同。

Original English

John McNeill: Yeah. The key is really two things. One is eliminating idle wait time and friction between steps, and the other is tight operational synchronization.

尼古拉·坦根: 我记得有位 F1 赛车手曾经说过，一台完美的赛车在冲过终点线的那一刻应该恰好散架，因为任何多余的强度都是多余的重量。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: I can't remember which Formula 1 driver said that for the perfect machine, it should fall apart the moment it crosses the finish line...

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我记得是舒马赫，或者科林·查普曼。

Original English

John McNeill: I think it was either Schumacher or...

尼古拉·坦根: 很多名言都归功于舒马赫，但道理确实如此。

尼古拉·坦根: 为什么让组织和员工加快节奏会如此困难？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Why is it so difficult to get people to hurry up?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 我认为这是一种心智模式。我们从日本丰田的精益生产中学到了很多，但很多西方企业习惯了宽松的排期与从容的节奏。

Original English

John McNeill: I think it's a mindset. We actually learned a lot from the Japanese lean manufacturing, but many corporate cultures are conditioned to accept long lead times and built-in slack.

中国制造速度与工作节奏

尼古拉·坦根: 那中国是如何将这种极速变成全社会的思维方式的？比如中国的节奏。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: How have they managed to make it a mindset in the whole of China? China, I think...

尼古拉·坦根: 能解释一下所谓的 996 吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: ...explain the 996.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 996 就是早 9 点上班，晚 9 点下班，每周工作 6 天。这是一种硬核的蛮力推进。但中国企业不仅投入工时，他们迭代硬件与制造产线的速度也是全球罕见的。

Original English

John McNeill: 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., 6 days a week. That's brute force effort. But they also iterate manufacturing lines and supply chains with incredible agility.

尼古拉·坦根: 但这种工作制在许多其他国家是通不过劳动法的。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: That doesn't really pass labor laws in many other countries.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 确实如此。因此真正的出路不能单靠人力蛮力，而是要转向极致高效的流程与合理的自动化。

Original English

John McNeill: Exactly. Exactly. So you can't rely solely on sheer human brute force; you have to shift towards process velocity and intelligent engineering.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 在特斯拉，我们建立了“每周心跳机制”（weekly heartbeat）。我们不按季度来审视进度，而是每周都要看到实质性的交付与产线提速，确保任何卡点在几天内就能被暴露和解决。

Original English

John McNeill: At Tesla, we used what we called a weekly heartbeat. We didn't manage on quarterly rhythms; we wanted to know week by week that we were making measurable progress, ensuring bottlenecks were surfaced and cleared within days.

第五步：自动化必须放在最后

尼古拉·坦根: 最后一步：自动化。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Last step, automate.

约翰·麦克尼尔: 没错。

Original English

John McNeill: Yes.

尼古拉·坦根: 为什么自动化一定要排在最后？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Why is it last?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 自动化就像是浇灌在流程上的水泥。一旦你把一个流程做成了自动化软硬件系统，任何微小的修改都将变得极其昂贵和痛苦。如果你在没有彻底理顺、验证和简化流程前就急于自动化，你就把所有的缺陷用混凝土永久浇筑固定了下来。

Original English

John McNeill: This is last because automation is like concrete that you pour over a process. Once you automate, changing that workflow or hardware line becomes extraordinarily expensive and rigid. If you automate a flawed process, you cement all the flaws into place.

尼古拉·坦根: 那么什么应该被自动化，什么应该保留为人工？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: What should be automated and what should be kept manual?

约翰·麦克尼尔: 在流程被人工彻底跑通、达到极其稳定且完全可预测之前，都应该保留人工。著名的例子是 DoorDash 的创始人们，作为斯坦福大学的计算机系毕业生，他们在创业初期全靠自己人工接单、手工打电话给餐馆订餐并亲自开车配送，完全没有写自动化派单算法。
