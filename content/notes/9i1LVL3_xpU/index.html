炸毁四座大坝：克拉马斯河的生态复苏与原住民抗争史

摘要

起源指令与生态平衡的破裂

在尤罗克族（Yurok Tribe: 北加州原住民部落）的创世神话中，万物之初并无具体形体，漂泊于宇宙的灵体向造物主祈求陪伴。造物主应允为它们创造安身之所，但提出了一个唯一条件：必须照料并守护这片土地。灵体应允后化作大地、流水、植物、动物与人类，赋予世间万物以生机。造物主留给祖先的不仅是精神寓言，更是维系生存的原生指令（Original Instructions: 原住民世代传承的生活与生态伦理），其核心在于取用不超所需、常怀感恩，并誓死守护赋予人类生命的自然体系。

千百年来，尤罗克人世居于北加州的克拉马斯河（Klamath River: 流经俄勒冈与加州的著名河流）下游，这条河流是部落赖以生存的生命线。在原住民的悉心照料下，克拉马斯河的鲑鱼洄游规模一度发展为美国大陆第三大鱼道。然而，自19世纪殖民者涌入以来，掠夺性的资源开发模式彻底打破了生态平衡。淘金热、木材砍伐、水资源掠夺以及针对原住民的暴力洗劫接踵而至，截至1910年，加州超过90%的原住民遭到屠杀。进入20世纪后，克拉马斯河上修筑了多座缺乏过鱼设施的水坝，阻断了超过400英里的传统产卵地，导致鲑鱼数量骤降至历史水平的5%，大坝水库蓄水更是产生了等同于每年6.4万辆汽车碳排放的巨量甲烷。

In the beginning, there were shapeless spirits roaming the universe. And they went to the creator and said, "We are lonely. Please help us." And the creator said, "I will make you a place to be together, on one condition. You must take care of it." The spirits agreed. And the creator made the Earth and sent the spirits down to the Earth to become land and water, plants, animals and humans, making everything on Earth alive. The creator told my ancestors, "You will never want for anything, so long as you live in a balance with the natural world. Never take more than what you need. Give thanks. And protect what gives you life.” That is a version of my nation, the Yurok tribe's, creation story. It's not spiritual parable. It's a set of instructions. Simple, practical rules about how humans are meant to live on Earth.

I want to tell you a story of loss, resilience and victory, and what it means to remember. Since time immemorial, my nation has lived by these instructions. The lower Klamath River, in what is now known as Northern California, is our home and our lifeblood. Under our stewardship, the Klamath salmon runs grew to be the third largest in all the continental US. We had an abundant fishing way of life, caring for all that gave us life. Then in the 1800s, everything changed. Newcomers arrived, and they viewed our world through the lens of extraction: gold, timber, water, land and people. The balance was disrupted, and suffering ensued in this place. By 1910, over 90 percent of California's Indigenous population had been slaughtered. It was genocide.

By the 2000s, the Klamath River, our ancient relative, had been transformed by dams, habitat loss, water diversions and warming polluted waters. The dams, built without fish passage, blocked the salmon from accessing over 400 miles of historical spawning grounds. Our salmon runs fell to five percent of their historical abundance. The hydropower from the dams wasn't green either. The reservoirs behind the dams emitted methane, the equivalent to 64,000 cars a year on the road.

生态灾难触发的法律与制度反击

殖民掠夺与水利工程的负面效应在2002年集中爆发。当年，克拉马斯河尤罗克保护区内发生了美国历史上最大规模的鲑鱼死亡事件，超过7万条成年鲑鱼在两周内相继毙命，河岸上堆满了腐烂的鱼尸，散发着如战场废墟般的恶臭。尤罗克古老谚语曾警示：若鲑鱼灭绝，尤罗克人亦将随之消亡，因为部落的使命与生存完全依附于这些生灵。面对族群濒临绝境的现实，作为现场渔业工作人员的演讲者感受到先辈责任的召唤，毅然决定攻读法学院，立志用现代法律武器终结此类生态灭绝（Ecocide: 大规模破坏自然生态系统的行为）。

毕业后，她成为尤罗克部落历史上首位出任总法律顾问的女性族人，并与卡鲁克族活动家莫莉·迈尔斯（Molly Myers）联合创立了“山脊到激流”原住民保护组织（Ridges to Riffles Indigenous Conservation Group）。鱼群的大规模暴死唤醒了原住民践行祖训的责任感，克拉马斯河流域的原住民部落率先发起了空前的大坝拆除倡议。尽管面临来自财阀集团与工业巨头的强烈阻力，原住民将宪法保护的部落保留权利（Reserved Rights: 美国联邦法律承认的原住民条约保留权利）与现代环境及能源法相结合，建立起涵盖原住民部落、商业渔民、非政府组织、州政府与农场主的广泛联盟，在法律诉讼中取得节节胜利。

In 2002, the legacy of colonization produced a horrific outcome. Over 70,000 adult salmon died on the Klamath River within the Yurok Reservation, the largest salmon kill in US history. I was working for tribal fisheries that summer, and the salmon died for two weeks. By the end of it, their bodies lined the banks of the river, and the river looked and smelled like a war zone. Standing in a Yurok tribal fisheries boat, I recalled a Yurok myth. If the Klamath salmon die, so too will the Yurok people. Because our purpose, our way of life depends on those salmon. Was I witnessing the end?

In that moment, I felt my great grandmother move through me like electricity. I feel it now. And she told me, "It's your turn to fight." And my response? I'll go to law school to prevent this kind of ecocide from ever happening again. Now I did not know a lawyer, and I thought they were all rich and famous. But that's what I did. And I became my nation's general counsel, the first tribal member and woman to do so. Thank you. (Cheers and applause)

And then I cofounded, with Molly Myers, a real tough Karuk activist, the Ridges to Riffles Indigenous Conservation Group. And we moved money and power to better serve the river. And that was important. The fish kill triggered our duties as Indigenous people to share the original instructions and work to renew the balance. Indigenous peoples on the Klamath were the first to call for dam removal. People thought it was impossible. A dam removal of this scale had never been done before. Powerful corporations, some of the richest corporations and industries were against us. They said removing the dams would release toxic sediment and destroy the river for good.

The odds were against us. But we had something they did not. The original instructions, thousands of years of traditional knowledge, ancient legal rights, vested in the US Constitution as the supreme law and our duties and responsibilities to the river, its creatures and our people, making the Un-Dam the Klamath Movement an unstoppable Indigenous force. Yeah. We protested. (Applause) There we go, guys, come on. (Cheers and applause) We protested. We built coalitions between Indigenous nations, the Karuk, the Hoopa, the Klamath tribes, commercial fishermen, NGOs and states and farmers. We litigated. We asserted the tribes’ reserved rights and combined them with modern environmental power and energy law. We won on almost every count. Yeah. (Cheers and applause)

破坝重生与生态记忆的归位

随着法院判令电力公司必须加装昂贵的过鱼设施才能继续运行水坝，诉讼双方找到了核心利益的重叠点：直接拆除大坝的经济成本反而低于安装改造鱼道。这一契机促成了价值5亿美元的历史性和解协议，不仅确立了自然权利、商业利益与原住民权利的平等地位，更启动了全球历史上规模最大的鲑鱼恢复工程与全美最大的水坝拆除项目（Dam Removal Project）。在2023至2024年间，克拉马斯河上的四座大坝相继爆破拆除，跨越数千英亩的生态系统修复工程全面铺开。

随着最后一座铁门水坝（Iron Gate Dam）被挖开缺口，奔涌的河水在被截断百年后重新汇合贯通。令人震撼的是，大坝拆除仅三天后，鲑鱼群便凭着本能的生态记忆，自发游过了封锁一个世纪的旧址，重返上游传统产卵场。水坝拆除第一年便有约7,000条鲑鱼通过旧坝址，2025年这一数字进一步激增至13,000条。与此同时，原住民部落开展了标志性的土地归还（Land-Back: 将传统土地管辖权归还原住民的运动）行动，吸纳部落与非部落成员参与生态修复，不仅创造了高薪就业岗位，还人工播种了200亿颗本土植物种子，预计随着生态重建可长期实现每年减排80万吨二氧化碳的潜力。这场胜利印证了一个真理：尊重原住民权利不是发展的阻碍，而是解决复杂生态危机的基石。

And the power company was required to install fish ladders to continue to operate the dams. Empathy overcame fear. And perhaps for the first time, we shared the original instructions with the newcomers, and they accepted their shared responsibilities to work to renew the balance. With our allies and our former enemies, we found a common interest. It was cheaper to remove the dams than it was to install the fish ladders. Yeah, right? Yeah, there's that common interest. So we worked together to find a path forward, to restore the river and the balance.

And the outcome was historic. A 500 million-dollar settlement agreement for dam removal that equally valued the rights of nature, business and Indigenous peoples. And it produced the world's largest salmon restoration project in world history. Yeah. Feel that, like, oh my God. And also the largest US dam removal project in history. In 2023 and 2024, we removed four dams on the Klamath River, and restoration across thousands of acres has already begun and will continue through 2028.

I stood among tribal leaders when the first dam was breached through a series of explosions. The explosion released a freedom wave, a surge of water into a stretch of river that had been dewatered for more than a century. (Applause) The water moved with such force and confidence, knowing exactly where to flow and how to go, and it seemed to declare, "Do not call this a comeback, I have been here for years." (Cheers and applause) Later, at Iron Gate, the lowest dam, an excavator scooped some final dirt from the dam and the water began to go over the structure, first as a sparkle, then as a ribbon, and then the river reconnected with itself for the first time in 100 years, in a brilliant stream of prosperity.

Three days later, the salmon came home and went past historical spawning grounds not accessed in 100 years. The salmon remembered. (Applause) They remembered. Many did not think that the river could heal, but its recovery has been remarkably rapid. A year after dam removal, the river is cleaner and colder and fiercer and smells sweeter than I have ever known it to be. And the salmon, they are literally jumping for joy. And if I wasn't wearing heels, I would jump for joy too.

The first year, about 7,000 salmon passed the former dam site. In 2025, 13,000 salmon passed the former dam site and went further into their historical spawning grounds they'd been denied. The many Indigenous peoples of the Klamath, whose relationship with salmon had been severed by the dams, are welcoming the salmon home and returning to their homelands in historic, trend-setting land-back efforts. The Yurok tribe and many other tribes are employing tribal members and non-members, providing living-wage jobs, restoring land in the river. These crews have planted -- get this -- 20 billion native seeds, bringing back -- yeah, yes, -- 20 billion native seeds. And look at these poppies, aren't they gorgeous? These new ecosystems are bringing back plants and animals, foods and medicines that anchor Indigenous cultures into the future. And these ecosystems have the potential to prevent 800,000 metric tons of CO2 a year over time. Yeah. (Applause)

So I know many feel that there is no hope for a future on planet Earth because the Earth is too out of balance, too sick. And in that sense of urgency is real. But on the Klamath, the river and all life it supports are remembering how to be in balance. And this project has helped us remember an important truth. We can heal nature, and when we work with it, it works with us. And it starts with believing in yourself. So I want you all to remember that what you do matters. What you do with your life force makes a difference. Treat Indigenous rights not as obstacles to progress but as critical to it. Build mutually beneficial solutions to complex problems. Value ecosystem restoration as a strategic priority to the most pressing problems of our time. When we do that, balance is restored, life returns and so does hope. (Speaking in Yurok) Thank you. (Cheers and applause)
