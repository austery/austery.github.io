折纸的乐趣与无限可能

我热爱折纸。我喜欢将一张普普通通的纸变成几乎任何东西的感觉——比如一只轮蝽、我的祖父母（笑声），甚至是一个神经元细胞。我今年14岁，住在纽约市，目前读九年级——所以通常我都忙于家庭作业或课外活动。但在空闲时间，我真的很喜欢折纸。

英文原文 I love origami. I love the way I can transform a simple piece of paper into almost anything -- like a wheel bug, my grandparents (Laughter) or even a neuron cell. I’m 14 years old. I live in New York City. And I’m in ninth grade -- so I'm usually busy with homework or after-school activities. But in my free time, I really enjoy doing origami.

折纸很有趣，但摆弄纸张的过程也让我看到，折纸具有激发我的创造力、帮助他人，甚至促成新发现的力量。今天，我想分享我所做的一个小发现，以及我是如何走到这一步的。

英文原文 Origami is fun, but playing with paper has also shown me that origami has the power to stretch my creativity, help others and even lead to new discoveries. And today, I'd like to share one small discovery that I made and how I got there.

我是七年多以前开始折纸的，那时是为家里微小的圣诞树做装饰物，还因为在课堂上把习题纸折成忍者飞镖和纸爪而惹上了麻烦。你可以想象，老师们一点也不感到高兴，但我告诉他们我正在制作**“学术武器”**。（笑声）

英文原文 So I started folding origami over seven years ago making ornaments for my family’s tiny Christmas tree and getting in trouble in class for turning my worksheets into ninja stars and paper claws. As you can imagine, my teachers were less than overjoyed, but I told them I was making academic weapons. (Laughter)

随着时间的推移，我开始折更复杂的折纸，甚至自己设计模型。但无论如何，我总是在折纸。随便给我一张废纸，我都会挑战自己把它变成某种东西。例如，如果我在 Trader Joe's 排队，我可能会把试吃杯折成千纸鹤，或者甚至把超长的 CVS 收据——（笑声）折成一条蜈蚣。观众：哇！甚至把《纽约时报》折成一只蜥蜴。正是这种将“虚无”化为实质的过程，点燃了我的想象力，并吸引我走向折纸。

英文原文 Over time, I began folding more complex origami and even designing my own models. But no matter what, I'm always folding. Give me any scrap of paper, and I'll challenge myself to turn it into something. For example, if I'm waiting in line at Trader Joe's, I might turn a sample cup into a crane or even a super-long CVS receipt -- (Laughter) into a centipede. Audience: Wow! Or even the New York Times into a lizard. And it’s this process of turning “nothing” into something that sparks my imagination and draws me to origami.

用折纸回馈社区

我也发现折纸有帮助他人的力量。在疫情期间，八岁大的我手写卡片，并将折纸鸟邮寄给当地养老院的老人，在隔离期间让他们振作起来。最近，我折了更多的纸鸟。两年前，我折了一些折纸鸽子。我说的“一些”，指的是200只。（笑声）如果你看不出来的话，鸽子是我最喜欢的鸟类。接下来的那一年，我折了麻雀，这是另一种常被忽视但我个人非常喜欢的鸟。我折了100只麻雀，并与鸽子一起出售，筹集了超过4000美元，捐赠给我当地的救济厨房，以及一家在纽约市救助受伤和成为孤儿的鸟类的非营利组织。（掌声）因此，通过这个项目，我看到了最不起眼的材料——一张单薄的纸——所蕴含的真正帮助社区的力量。

英文原文 I've also found that origami has the power to help others. During the pandemic, eight-year-old me wrote cards and mailed origami birds to seniors at my local nursing home to cheer them up during quarantine. And more recently, I've been folding even more origami birds. Two years ago, I folded some origami pigeons. By some, I mean 200 of them. (Laughter) If you couldn't tell, pigeons are my favorite birds. And then the following year, I folded sparrows, another bird that’s overlooked but that I personally love. And I folded 100 of them and sold them alongside the pigeons to raise over $4,000 to donate to my local soup kitchen, as well as a nonprofit that rehabilitates injured and orphaned birds in New York City. (Applause) And so through this project, I was able to see the power of the most humble material, a simple piece of paper, to really help my community.

探索三浦折叠与科学工程

最近，我对折纸与科学及工程学的交叉领域产生了兴趣。我想这始于初中，当时我开始深入钻研，了解科学家、研究人员和工程师们是如何应用折纸折痕的。例如，在太空领域，甚至用于帮助病患。但有一种折法特别引起了我的兴趣，那就是三浦折叠（Miura-ori）。

英文原文 More recently, I've been interested in the intersection of origami and science and engineering. I think it started in middle school when I began going down rabbit holes to see how scientists and researchers and engineers were using origami folds. For example, in space or even to help sick people. But one fold interested me in particular. And this was the Miura-ori fold.

它是由日本天体物理学家三浦公亮（Koryo Miura）在20世纪70年代发明的。它是一种镶嵌结构（tessellation），意味着它是由平行四边形组成的重复图案。根据这些平行四边形的排列方式，它们能在一个流畅的动作中折叠成极其紧凑的尺寸。这使得三浦折叠成为一种高效的可展开结构。它甚至曾被用于折叠送入太空的太阳能电池阵列。

英文原文 It was invented in the 1970s by Koryo Miura, a Japanese astrophysicist. It’s a tessellation, which means it’s a repeating pattern of parallelograms. And the way these parallelograms are arranged, they're able to fold down to an extremely compact size in one smooth motion. This makes Miura-ori an efficient deployable structure. It’s even been used to fold a solar array that was sent into space.

去年，我也在阅读有关自然灾害的新闻，例如飓风海伦（Hurricane Helene）。我看到许多人流离失所。这让我思考，三浦折叠是否可以用来改进应急可展开避难所，使它们更坚固、更轻便。

英文原文 And last year, I was also reading the news about natural disasters like Hurricane Helene. And I saw so many people displaced. And it made me wonder if Miura-ori could be used to improve emergency deployable shelters by making them stronger and lighter.

强度重量比的科学实验与发现

因此，针对八年级的科学展项目，我决定研究三浦折叠，以及我该如何优化它的强度重量比。我首先设计了18种不同的折叠图案。这些图案在平行四边形的高度、宽度以及角度上各不相同。我在三种不同的纸张克重下对每种图案进行了测试，且每种变体测试两次，总共折叠并测试了108个三浦折叠模型。对于每一个三浦折叠，我不仅想观察它们能承受多少重量，还想了解它们能承受相当于自身重量多少倍的负重。

英文原文 And so for my eighth-grade science fair project, I decided to study Miura-ori, and how I could optimize its strength-to-weight ratio. I started out by designing 18 different folding patterns. These had different heights, widths and angles of the parallelograms in the pattern. I tested each of these patterns across three different paper weights, and each of those variations twice, for a total of 108 Miura-ori that I folded and tested. And for each of these Miura-ori, I wanted to see not only how much weight they could hold, but also how many times their own weight they could hold.

于是整整两个月，我把家里在纽约市狭小的公寓变成了我的测试实验室，到处都摆满了三浦折叠。刚开始的时候，我基本上随手拿来每一本厚重的书和物品堆叠在三浦折叠上面。但我很快意识到，我其实没有足够的重物来准确测试这些模型。所以我请父母买了一些非常重的健身杠铃片。经过250个小时——以及大量的重物搬运之后——我发现：面板面积最小、锐角角度最小且用最轻的纸制成的三浦折叠，具有最大的强度重量比。

英文原文 And so for two months, I transformed my family's small New York City apartment into my testing lab with Miura-ori all over the place. When I was first starting out, I grabbed basically every heavy book and item, and I stacked them on top of the Miura-ori. But I quickly realized I didn't actually have enough weights to accurately test the models. And so I asked my parents to buy some really heavy exercise weights. 250 hours later -- and after a lot of heavy lifting -- I found that the Miura-ori with the smallest and least acutely-angled panels made out of the lightest paper had the greatest strength-to-weight ratio.

最坚固的图案承受了近200磅的重量，而另一个更轻的版本承受了超过自身重量10,000倍的重量。我发现更坚固的图案更加紧凑，而且它们还形成了能够均匀分散压力的类桁架结构。令人惊叹的是，某种东西竟然可以如此坚固，同时又如此轻巧。想到这种折法可能拥有的潜在应用——甚至有朝一日可能有助于构想出更好的应急避难所，这令人无比兴奋。

英文原文 The strongest pattern held almost 200 pounds, and another lighter version held more than 10,000 times its own weight. I found that the stronger patterns were more compact, and they also created truss-like structures that were able to distribute pressure evenly. It's amazing that something can be so strong and yet so lightweight at the same time. And it's exciting to think about what potential applications a fold like this could have -- maybe even helping to imagine a better emergency shelter one day.

荣誉与未来的展望

这项完全由我自己在家完成的研究，让我去年在一项全美 STEM 竞赛中获得了最高奖。（掌声）如你所见，我真的很震惊……（笑声）仅仅因为玩纸就获得了这么大的一个奖项。因此在未来，我肯定想继续探索和学习。但最重要的是：继续摆弄折纸，以便我能继续去发现一张单薄的纸所能容纳的一切可能性。谢谢大家。（欢呼与掌声）

英文原文 And so this work that I did by myself at home led to me receiving the top prize at a national STEM competition last year. (Applause) As you can see, I was really shocked ... (Laughter) to receive such a big prize for simply playing with paper. And so in the future, I definitely want to keep exploring and learning. But most of all: to keep playing with origami so I can continue to discover all the possibilities a single piece of paper can hold. Thank you. (Cheers and applause)
