进化悖论：以风险换取跃升

过去几年中，我们的研究团队针对高绩效团队的运作模式进行了深度调研。我们对数千名职场人士提出了两个核心评估维度：团队实现目标的有效性，以及相较于同业的相对表现。那些在两项评估中均获得满分的极少数群体，我们称之为超级团队（Superteams）。通过解构他们的日常运作——从日程排布、会议机制到周末恢复模式，我们发现超级团队普遍具备三大核心优势：精细化的精力与注意力管理、赋能型的深度协作，以及绝不满足于现状的持续进化能力。

在大多数企业中，推动团队持续进化是一项异常艰难的挑战。这种困难的根源在于：任何实质性的进步都必然要求引入企业通常极力规避的两大要素——风险承担与失败容忍。尝试新路径本质上与犯错相伴共生。如果在建立这种心理防线和组织安全感之前，员工无法安心地承担试错成本，那么团队层面的学习与进化根本无从谈起。

RON FRIEDMAN: Over the last few years, my team and I have done a deep dive on what high performing teams do differently. We surveyed thousands of workers, and asked them two key questions about their teams. Number one, how effective is your team at meeting its goals? And number two is, compared to other teams in your industry, how would you rate your team's performance? Then we took the teams with a perfect score, a very tiny group we call super teams, and looked at what are the super teams doing differently on a day-to-day basis? And we looked at everything from how they structure their day, to how they run their meetings, to even how they recover in the evenings and weekends. The patterns were striking. [MUSIC PLAYING] Superteams share three key strengths. The first is they get more done by better managing their time, energy, and attention. The second is they don't just collaborate well, they actively make one another better. And the third is that they're not satisfied doing great work. They're constantly building their skills and improving over time. In today's discussion, what we're going to cover is the latter of the three strengths how super teams get better over time. At most companies, getting better is surprisingly difficult, and that's because improvement requires two things that most businesses are not particularly comfortable with-- and that is risk taking and failure. Trying something new often involves making mistakes. Unless the team feels comfortable making those mistakes, learning simply becomes impossible.

常态实验：85%胜率的容错区

在具体的落地策略上，超级团队的首要特征是高频试错。我们在这些团队中观察到三种核心战术：持续实验、建立主动反馈机制，以及重奖创新。在此机制下，产生新想法的员工拥有更多机会将其探索成果融入个人职业发展路径，从而激发了广泛的试错意愿。

实验（Experiment: 在做出最终决策前，用于获取新知的小规模、低风险测试）的本质，是用局部的效率换取全局的增量信息。如果一个举措的成功是完全可预见的，那它就不具备实验价值。这意味着，即便实验最终失败，团队也能从中萃取到优化未来决策的经验。

为了将这种实验思维无缝嵌入团队日常，领导者必须建立“失败去敏化”的常态机制。例如，通过每周一次的进展复盘，公开透明地分享所有正在进行的实验结果——无论成败。这种做法能向全员传递一个强烈信号：试错不是异常，而是本团队的标准工作流。有趣的是，我们的研究发现了一个关键的健康指标：85%的成功率。如果你能在保持85%的胜率的同时，允许剩余15%出现试错带来的失败，这恰恰证明了团队既维持了高水准的输出表现，又在不断向外突破自身的舒适区边界（Stretching）。

英文原文

There are a few tactics that I often see on superteams. The first is that they are constantly experimenting. The other thing that they are very high on, is they're very high on something called feedback seeking. And the third thing is that they reward innovation. And so we tend to see more people trying new things, because there's more of an opportunity for people who come up with new ideas to incorporate what they find into their career path. An experiment is a low, small risk test that allows your team to learn something new before committing to a decision. By definition, if you know it's going to work, it's not an experiment. You're exchanging efficiency for information. So even if it doesn't work out, even if the experiment is a failure, you're going to learn something that helps you improve in the future. I think that there are simple things that any leader can do to incorporate this learning and experimentation mindset, and part of it is having a weekly review of experiments that are ongoing, regardless of whether they were successful or failures. The idea is to socialize some of these findings so that people appreciate that this is something that is a routine way in which this team operates. One of the things we from the research is that 85% turns out to be a pretty good indicator. You're looking for 85% success. It tells you you're generally successful, but it also tells you you're failing a small portion of the time-- and that is suggestive of someone who is stretching, while still performing at a high level.

前置反馈：重塑评价时空坐标

在建立实验文化后，超级团队通过高强度的主动寻求反馈（Feedback Seeking: 员工自发向周围网络获取评价以校准表现的行为）来加速迭代闭环。相较于平庸团队，他们接收到的反馈总量呈指数级增长。这不仅得益于领导者更高频、更具启发性而非审判性的介入，更重要的是，他们重塑了反馈发生的时间节点。在超级团队中，员工之间形成了密集的同级协作网络，但这绝不是为了互相挑错，而是因为他们习惯于尽早分享半成品，通过吸纳同侪视角的建议，在成果正式提交给老板或客户之前就完成前置的内部修正。

为了激发这种主动寻求反馈的团队本能，管理者需要对反馈话术进行结构性改造：

  • 锚定未来：将讨论重心从“你过去做错了什么”转移到“下一次如何优化”。
  • 单一焦点法则：摒弃长篇累牍的问题清单，每次复盘只聚焦一个最具体、可执行的微小改进点。
  • 对话式重构：将单向指令（“我需要你这么做”）转化为探讨式提问（“我认为这样也许对未来有帮助，你怎么看？”）。这种赋权型沟通能有效卸除员工的心理防御，让他们在接收反馈时感受到被赋能（Energized）而非被审判（Judged）。
英文原文

One of the fascinating findings in our research on superteams is that the quantity of feedback that they receive on a regular basis is much higher than those on average teams. And part of it is because the leader is delivering feedback more frequently, and they're doing it in a way that is motivating rather than judgmental. But on superteams, people get more feedback from their colleagues. And it's not because their colleagues are pointing out mistakes that they're making. It's because they're sharing their work early, they're getting input from their colleagues, and they're making revisions before the work gets to the boss or to the client. There are a couple of things that leaders can do to get their team higher in feedback seeking. The first thing is, focus on the future, not the past. The second thing is, don't provide a laundry list of all the things they need to change. Focus on one small thing they can change to get better in the future. And the third thing is, when possible, try to make it a conversation. Don't just say, here's what I need you to do. Say, here's what I think might help in the future. What do you think? That's the kind of feedback that gets people feeling energized rather than judged.

创新溢价：为聪明失败买单

最后，系统层面的激励机制决定了团队行为的持续性。超级团队无一例外地设立了硬性的创新奖励系统。以发明了便利贴（Post-Its）和透明胶带的百年企业 3M公司（原讲者口误为GM）为例，他们能够长盛不衰的秘诀在于一项经典的“30%法则”：一个事业部要想获得全额奖金，其总营收的至少30%必须来源于过去四年内推出的新产品。这种制度设计通过人为制造生存危机感，倒逼团队在顺境中依然保持警惕，彻底杜绝了躺平吃老本（Coast）的可能性。

更深层次的制度内核是为聪明的失败（Intelligent Failure: 经过深思熟虑且风险可控，最终未能达成预期但提供了关键认知的尝试）支付溢价。当有人承担了真实的业务风险并以失败告终时，他们应该获得与创造了巨大商业成功的实验者同等体量的嘉奖。

如果在你的团队中没有出现任何形式的挫折或失败，这并非意味着一切运转完美，而是暴露出你们已经停止了技能扩展和系统进化。因此，卓越的管理必须剥离对绝对完美的执念，转而拥抱一种更具韧性的“进度思维”（Progress Mentality）。这就要求领导者具备投资组合思维（Portfolio Thinking: 将资源分散配置于不同风险梯度的项目中以博取长期收益的策略）——必须配置一定比例的风险资产，因为这是长期优化与成长的唯一源泉。在当今残酷的竞争格局中，零风险绝不代表万无一失，它只意味着你正在加速落后。

英文原文

One of the things that superteams do really well is they reward innovation. And my favorite example of this is GM, the company that is best known for Post-Its and for Scotch Tape. They've been around for over 120 years. The question is, how do you create a company that is so effective for so long? One of their secrets is rewarding innovation. At 3M, they have a policy called the 30% rule. In order for a division to earn its bonus, at least 30% of its revenue must come from a product introduced in the last four years. And I love this approach, because it ensures that even if things are going well, your team will not coast, because they're constantly worried about what's next. One of the keys is rewarding intelligent failure. And what that refers is the idea that when someone takes a real risk and it doesn't work out, they get the same level of reward as someone whose experiment resulted in a giant success. I think unless you're seeing some degree of failure or setbacks, what it suggests is not that everything is going perfectly, but you're not stretching your skills or developing in an ongoing way. Part of what leaders need to let go of is the desire for perfection, and instead embrace the progress mentality-- and that requires portfolio thinking. You're going to have some degree of risk, and that's what's going to lead to ongoing improvement. But if you have no risk and everything is going perfectly, it doesn't mean you're getting everything right. It means you're falling behind. [MUSIC PLAYING]
