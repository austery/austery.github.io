来源: Harvard Business Review

原文: 查看

发布时间: 2026-09-24

摘要:

哈佛商学院前院长尼廷·诺里亚对话迪士尼首席执行官鲍勃·艾格。艾格深度剖析了执掌迪士尼的关键经验：收购皮克斯重振动画核心、平衡传统尊崇与激进创新、在常态化危机中物色具备坚韧毅力的接班人，以及如何警惕高管的结构性自恋，始终保持敬畏与清醒。

破局：收购皮克斯

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): 我在书中提出的核心论点是，**首席执行官（CEO）之所以至关重要，并非因为他们亲力亲为或掌控一切，而是因为他们塑造了所有真正重要的事情。你的前任在位时间非常漫长，你在成为 CEO 之前也经历了一段漫长的等待。当你接任这个职位时，你已经做好了充分的准备。而你上任后首先不得不做的事情之一，就是告诉迪士尼极具传奇色彩的部门——动画业务（Animation Business）——他们需要一次彻底的重置。你通过引入皮克斯（Pixar）**来帮助公司重构未来蓝图。你能向我们详细讲讲，你是如何让迪士尼直面这一现实的，以及让大家齐心协力接受这一变革付出了什么？

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: The main argument that I make in my book is that CEOs matter, not because they do or control everything, but because they shape everything that matters. You had a predecessor who also had a very long tenure. You'd waited a long time to become CEO. You were very ready for it when you got the job. One of the first things that you had to do was to tell the very storied part of Disney, which was the animation business, that they needed a reset. And you brought in Pixar as a way to help the company reimagine its future. Can you walk us through how you had Disney confront this reality, and what it took to get people to get on board?

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 当时所有的迹象都已经表明，迪士尼动画正在走向衰落，而它对公司的重要性又极其重大，因此必须迅速得到修复。这并不是说公司上下处于否认现实的状态，我认为核心问题在于他们根本找不到拯救迪士尼动画的方案，所以也就没有采取任何实质性的扭转举措。在这方面，大家几乎陷入了一种空转停滞的状态——人人都清楚存在问题，却不知该如何破局走出困境。

我非常细致地研究了公司。对我而言显而易见的是，至少在我出任 CEO 的 2004 年至 2005 年期间，动画业务对整个公司来说，尤其是动画电影本身，可能比任何其他业务板块都更为重要——这不仅关乎财务底线，更直接决定了外界对整个迪士尼的认知与口碑。因此，我感受到了一种强烈的紧迫感，并立即采取果断行动来解决这一危机。具体策略就是收购皮克斯，不仅要把皮克斯纳入迪士尼旗下，更要将皮克斯的管理核心团队引入迪士尼动画，从根本上扭转迪士尼动画的颓势。

幸运的是，这一举措收到了丰厚回报。加盟而来的皮克斯领军人物，约翰·拉塞特（John Lasseter）和埃德·卡特穆尔（Ed Catmull），确实在相对较短的时间内彻底扭转了迪士尼动画的局面。剩下的便已成为历史。自那以后迪士尼动画所取得的辉煌成功，包括《海洋奇缘》（Moana）、《冰雪奇缘》（Frozen）、《魔发奇缘》（Tangled）以及《疯狂动物城》（Zootopia）等现象级作品，不仅为公司的利润表带来了巨大的收益，对整个迪士尼品牌的全球声誉更是产生了极其显著的拉动作用。

Original English

ROBERT IGER: The signs were all there that Disney Animation was faltering, and that its importance to the company was rather significant and therefore had to be fixed and fixed fast. It wasn't that the company was in denial, but I think because they had no solutions in terms of how to fix Disney Animation, there wasn't any real movement to try to do so. They were kind of stuck in neutral in that regard, knowing there was a problem, but not knowing how to get out of it. I studied the company very carefully. It was very clear to me that at least in 2004, 2005, when I became CEO, the animation business to the company, and animation in particular, was probably more important than any other business in terms of not just the bottom line, but the perception of the company. And so I felt a sense of urgency, and I acted very quickly to address it. And that was to buy Pixar and to not only bring Pixar into Disney, but bring the leadership of Pixar into Disney Animation to turn Disney Animation around. And fortunately, it paid off. The people at Pixar that came in, John Lasseter and Ed Catmull, in fact, turned Disney Animation around and relatively quickly. And the rest is history. The success of Disney Animation since then, with movies like Moana and Frozen and Tangled and Zootopia has been very, very obvious not just to the bottom line, but to the overall perception of Disney.

传承与创新的平衡

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): 作为首席执行官必须面对的核心使命之一，是在公司既有的历史传承之上继续开拓。你在接任时继承了丰厚家底——我们在**哈佛商学院（Harvard Business School）**讲授关于迪士尼的经典案例课程，探讨其令人惊叹的商业飞轮效应：迪士尼多年来打造了令人叹为观止的优质知识产权（IP），并能够通过主题乐园及各类衍生业务进行全方位放大。然而，CEO 同时也必须带领公司开辟全新领域。你究竟是如何在深耕迪士尼的核心资产与向全新领域扩张之间找到平衡点的？你在任内下了几手极大的战略赌注，你当时是如何权衡这种平衡的？

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: One of the tasks that CEOs have to do is to both build on the heritage of the company, and you inherited-- we teach courses at Harvard Business School about Disney, about the extraordinary flywheel of how Disney for years had built amazing IP that it was able to leverage through theme parks and everything else. And yet CEOs have to also grow the company into new areas. How did you balance both the exploitation of the core of Disney as well as its movement into new areas? You made some very big bets. How did you think about that balance?

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 甚至到了 2004 年、2005 年那段时期，迪士尼内部的许多人在很多方面依然深陷于过去的泥淖之中——确切地说，是困在属于**华特·迪士尼（Walt Disney）**个人的历史荣光里。而我认为非常耐人寻味的一点在于：华特·迪士尼本人其实是一位真正的未来主义者和开拓创新者，他时刻渴望挑战既有现状、打破陈规、制定新规则、突破一切边界。

但当时公司里存在着这样一种执念：认为华特本人的形象太过于高大、太具标志性、成就太辉煌，以至于他所创造的几乎所有东西不仅不应止步不前，反倒被推崇到了甚至有些过头的地步。我经常谈到这种“盲目崇拜”（revered）。我当时非常笃定地认定，这种对过去的极度依恋已经产生了太大的负面阻力，我们需要做的是“由衷尊重”（respected）。这意味着，让我们尊重他的历史功绩，尊重他作为开拓者的精神本色；但不要把他所做的事情当成某种博物馆陈列柜里的展品，只能远观和赞叹。我们应当以其为根基进行革新与迭代——而这恰恰正是华特当年自己会做的事情。

因此，我当时立即强调的关键点在于：我们绝不忽视历史，我们深怀敬意，但绝不能盲目崇拜到画地为牢的程度。我们必须把优秀的核心品质传承下去，同时坚决捍卫勇于创新和承担风险的企业文化。

Original English

ROBERT IGER: Many people, Disney, even into the 2004, 2005 period, were still stuck in its past in many respects, stuck in the past that really was Walt Disney's past. And what I found interesting about that is that Disney, Walt Disney himself, was a real futurist and an innovator, and someone that was willing to challenge the status quo all the time and move forward, create new rules, break boundaries, et cetera, and so on. But there was this belief that he was so larger than life and so iconic and so successful that almost everything that he created should not necessarily stand still, but be honored to a fault, actually. I talk often about it being revered. And I actually determined that that was way too powerful in terms of adherence to the past and it should be respected. Meaning, let's respect what he did. Let's respect who he was. Let's not relegate it to a museum case of some sort to be looked at and appreciated. Let's use it to build on it and to change from it, which is exactly what Walt would have done. So it was immediately basically emphasizing the need not to ignore our past, to respect it, but again, not to revere it to a fault and to enable us to bring the qualities forward, but also maintain a culture of innovation and risk taking.

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): 我自己一直以来的一个观察是：人们往往忘记了，所谓的传统在最初其实全都是创新。世上绝不存在任何一个从一开始就不是创新的传统。正因如此，身为领导者的天职——正如你所做的那样——就是要在今天勇敢创新，唯有如此，今天创造出的事物在明天才有机会沉淀为新的传统。

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: One of my own observations has been that people forget that traditions were once innovations, that you could not have a tradition that wasn't innovation once. And the obligation of a leader, as you did, is to innovate today something that then has the opportunity to become a tradition tomorrow.

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 我们有时会因为更换主题乐园里那些建于 20 世纪 50 年代或 60 年代的古老游乐项目而遭到外界的猛烈批评，没有游人愿意看到我们动手去改。他们会抗议说：“那是华特亲手打造的，你们怎么能擅自改动？”然而，如果华特本人还在世，他也一定会毫不犹豫地对其进行改造。大家往往忘记了，华特自己从来不会抱残守缺，更不会死守过去的辉煌不放。他永远在眺望未来，永远愿意去推动根本性的变革，即便这种变革在起初可能会招致争议与非议。

Original English

ROBERT IGER: We get criticized at times for changing out older attractions in our theme parks that sometimes were built in the 1950s and 1960s, and no one likes us to do that. And they say, well, Walt created that. How can you touch it? Well, Walt himself would have changed it. They forget that he wasn't wedded to his past or to Disney's past. He was always looking ahead and always willing to essentially create change, even if it was unpopular initially.

塑造企业文化与权威

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): 请谈谈你是如何重塑与影响迪士尼企业文化的。因为很多人嘴上都理解“文化”这个词，但他们并不真正清楚一位 CEO 究竟是通过怎样的具体路径对一家公司的文化产生深远影响的。

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: Say a word about how you influenced the culture of Disney. Because lots of people understand the word, but they don't quite understand how a CEO actually has an impact on the culture of a company.

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 我希望建立一个高度协同的环境，打造一家充满合作精神的公司。绝不是所有事情都必须自上而下地强推。但与此同等重要的是，我也要让所有人都清楚：身为 CEO，我是无可置疑的权威领袖（authority figure），拥有最终的裁决权。这实际上是在“独裁者”与某种“民主践行者”之间寻找微妙的平衡。

我们让决策的前期过程充分协作，让尽可能多的人参与进来，广纳来自不同层级、不同视角的意见和输入；但在流程终点，大家都明白我有一锤定音的权力，可以决定最终的战略走向，或者在适当时刻终止争论、切断意见分歧，拍板带领全员坚定前行。这无疑是关键的第一点。

第二点，我极其看重可接近性（accessibility）。之所以说我推崇它，是因为我觉得它至关重要。我希望自己是一个员工能够直接沟通、产生互动的人。为此，我在个人时间分配上作出了非常明确的倾斜——我确保自己经常出现在公司一线。我大幅削减了几乎所有的外部事务：我只保留了一个外部董事席位，极少参加各类行业峰会论坛，也推掉了绝大部分的公开演讲。我把绝大部分宝贵的时间都留在了公司内部，用来陪伴我们的员工，与各个层级的员工深入交流。

这具有极其重大的意义：其一，这赋予了我深入施加领导力与影响力的话语场；其二，它让基层员工切身感受到被尊重与被重视，因为通过面对面的相处，我有机会直接向他们表达最真诚的赞赏与支持；其三，这让我能够向全员充分传递热情与乐观精神，并向他们不断阐明我认为对于公司及其未来而言最为关键的战略愿景。

Original English

ROBERT IGER: I wanted to be a collaborative environment, a collaborative company. Not everything should be top down. But I also wanted everybody to know that as CEO, I was an authority figure and that I had the final say. And kind of a balance between being an autocrat and being a democrat of sorts. Allowing a process to be collaborative and with many people engaged and a lot of people and hearing input from many different sources, but at the same time, everybody knowing at the end I could dictate what we wanted to do or when the debate should be shut off, that opinion should be shut off, and we should proceed or move forward. So that certainly was one. I loved accessibility. I say I love it because I thought it was important. I was someone people could engage with. And so I made sure that, for instance, in time allocation, that I was there a lot. I cut off most extracurricular activities. I was only on one outside board. I attended very few conferences. I didn't give that many speeches. I spent my time at the company, with our people and with our people at all levels. That was really important. One, it gave me an ability to influence. Two, it gave people an ability to feel respected and appreciated, because I had the opportunity by seeing them to actually express appreciation and support. And I think it also gave me an ability to demonstrate enthusiasm and optimism and also to preach to them what I thought was the most important about the company and its future.

CEO的时间精力分配

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): CEO 面临的最稀缺资源莫过于自己的时间。我和同事**迈克尔·波特（Michael Porter）**曾专门针对 CEO 如何管理时间展开过深度课题研究。许多新晋 CEO 经常对我们倾诉：“天哪，我整个职业生涯中一直都算是个极具时间管理能力的高手，但这份工作对我提出的挑战之严苛，完全超出了我以往的想象极限。”请谈谈你是如何应对 CEO 身上看似无穷无尽、层出不穷的时间需求的？工作体量可以无止境地膨胀，但一天所拥有的时间绝不会增加。

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: One of the CEO's scarcest resources is their time. My colleague Mike Porter and I did a study of how CEOs manage their time, because new CEOs would say to us, oh my God, I've been a good person at managing my time my entire career, but this job is challenging me in ways that I never could have imagined. So talk a little bit about how you dealt with what seemed to be the infinite and myriad demands on a CEO's time, and yet it's not like-- the job expands, but the hours in a day don't.

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 我很庆幸自己在出任 CEO 之前担任了整整五年的首席运营官（COO），这让我得以近距离观察前任是如何管理他的日程与精力的。我很快察觉到，身为迪士尼的掌门人，尤其是长期执掌这家巨头，外界的各色力量会源源不断地把你往公司外部拉扯。所有人都会邀请你发表演讲、公开露面，而在不知不觉中，无论你是有心还是无意，你的注意力极易被严重分散，你的时间分配也会面临前所未有的危机。

因此，在上任之初我就对自己立下誓言——特别是在担任 CEO 的前五年起步阶段，我必须将除去家庭陪伴之外的 99% 的时间完全奉献给迪士尼。

那么，如何具体分配这 99% 的精力？我将其拆解为几个核心维度： 第一类，是 CEO 职责范围内必须完成的规定动作：你必须主持董事会会议、出席季度财报电话会议，并密集会见关键利益相关者，包括核心投资者、基层员工以及执行高管团队。这是刚性要求，极其明确。 第二类，任何涉及重大资本配置（Capital Allocation），或者可能对公司公众形象和核心品牌资产产生深远影响的重大事项，CEO 必须全程深度介入把关。这至关重要。 第三类考量则是：我究竟能在哪里真正带来实质性的改变？换句话说，如果某件事邀请我参与，但我客观评估后认为自己的参与并不会产生实质性不同，那我就绝不应该在上面浪费时间，我必须彻底授权并依赖他人去推进。

随着公司运转越来越步入正轨、愈发成功，我开始拥有一定自由度去挑选一些我真正热爱的事情。尽管这个自由支配的精力份额非常有限。例如，我虽然始终认为自己能对创意生产流程产生积极的赋能作用，但在很多日子里，由于被各种行政事务裹挟撕扯，我整天都无法涉足任何创意项目。每当遇上这种日子，我下班离开办公室时内心都非常沮丧。因此从那时起我便给自己定下铁律：必须确保在我度过的每一天里，都必须包含参与创意事务的具体时刻。

Original English

ROBERT IGER: I was fortunate to have been COO for five years before I became CEO, and I was able to observe how my predecessor managed his time. And I quickly discovered that as CEO of Disney, particularly for an extended period of time, you're drawn outside the company constantly. You're asked to speak and appear and you can easily, either wittingly or unwittingly, become distracted and your time gets even more challenged. And so I promised myself when I came in, particularly in the early stages, first maybe five years of being CEO, I would devote 99% of my non-family time to Disney. Now, how do you allocate the 99% of your time? One, there are things a CEO must do. You have to preside over board meetings and earnings calls and meet with critical constituents or constituencies, investors and employees and your senior team. So that's very, very clear. Second, anything that required either significant capital allocation or could have a significant impact on the perception of the company and its brand, as a CEO, you must be involved with those matters. Really important. The third bucket was where could I make a difference? In other words, if I was asked to engage in something and I really didn't think that my involvement mattered, then I shouldn't be spending my time doing it. I should delegate and rely on other people to do that. And the company got more successful. I had the freedom to choose things that I actually enjoyed doing. Now, that discretionary bucket was small. But for instance, even though I mentioned I thought I could have a positive influence on the creative process, there were days when I had no engagement in creative processes, because I was drawn in so many other directions. Those were days that I left work not happy, and so I made sure that there was something creative in every single one of my days.

危机时代的接班人选拔

尼廷·诺里亚 (Nitin Nohria): 在商界历史上，一位 CEO 主动自愿退居幕后，随后又不得不临危受命重返帅位，这是极其罕见的现象。你在亲历了第一次未能如愿的接班过程后，如今又极其审慎地主导了新一轮的权力交接，从这一系列切肤之痛的实践中，你学到了关于**接班人计划（Succession Planning）**的哪些本质教训？对于那些正在竭力确保接班流程——作为 CEO 职业生涯“终章大戏”——能够平稳收官的领导者，你能分享哪些宝贵的真知灼见？

Original English

NITIN NOHRIA: It's rare to have a CEO who both at some point voluntarily decided to step down and then had to come back in. What have you learned about the succession process from your own experience of having done it once, didn't go as you hoped for, now you've thoughtfully done it again? What lessons could you share for someone else who thinks about making sure that succession, which is the last act of a CEO, goes as well as it possibly could?

罗伯特·艾格 (Robert Iger): 回溯到 2019 年和 2020 年，我们当时挑选了我的继任者。现在回过头来看，整个考察评估过程或许并没有我们自以为的那么周密严谨。其中很大一部分原因在于，我们误以为自己对这个人已经了如指掌：他在公司任职多年，长期直接向我汇报工作，我们深知他的优势所在，也清楚他的部分短板。

我并不是说我们故意忽视了他的弱点，但在当时急于完成接班人考核指标、好让我顺利交棒脱身（先转任执行董事长直至彻底退休）的迫切心态下，我们虽然没有对问题视而不见，但确实在一定程度上低估了这些缺陷的潜在危害。

我认为我们当时遗漏的另一项更重大的变量在于：宏观外部环境正在发生剧烈剧变。我们如今身处一个危机几乎无所不在的动荡时代。企业仿佛生活在一种“持久常态化危机”（perpetual crisis）之中，突发性危机以极高的频率接踵而至。我认为这种残酷的现实对领导力模型提出了完全不同的诉求，而我们在当时并未深刻意识到这一点。
