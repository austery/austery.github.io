巅峰背后的至暗时刻：当成功无法填补内心空洞

在创办 TOMS 帆布鞋之初，我住在加州码头一艘 45 英尺长的帆船上。那时我的全部家当不过几袋衣服、一辆自行车、一块冲浪板和一台笔记本电脑。那是我一生中最快乐的时光之一：清晨在海浪的轻摇中醒来，光着脚煮咖啡，内心澄澈、充满灵感且生机勃勃。

快进二十年，TOMS 已经成长为一个全球知名品牌。我们的“买一捐一”（One-for-One）商业模式为全球贫困儿童捐赠了超过 1 亿双鞋子；就个人而言，我赚到的财富也远超最初的想象。然而，就在 23 个月前，我却陷入了前所未有的迷茫、绝望与重度抑郁，甚至制定了结束生命的计划。我选择了那艘曾经承载快乐的帆船作为终点——并非因为我想死在那里，而是不想让我的家人成为最先发现遗体的人。站在这里公开说出这段经历，是准备这场演讲中最艰难的部分。

英文原文

When I started TOMS Shoes, I lived on a 45-ft sailboat in Marina del Rey. I didn't have much, a few bags of clothes, a bike, a surfboard, and a laptop. That was it. And those were some of the happiest years of my life. I woke up to the ocean rocking me. I made coffee barefoot. I felt clear, inspired, and alive.

Now, let's fast forward 20 years. TOMS had become a global brand. Our one-for-one model had given over 100 million shoes to children in need around the world. And personally, I had made more money than I ever thought was possible. And yet, 23 months ago, I had become so lost, so hopeless, and so depressed, that I made a plan to end my life. And I chose that boat as the place to do it on. Not because I wanted to die there, but because I didn't want my family to be the ones who found me. Knowing that I was going to say that today, here on the stage, is definitely the hardest part of preparing for this talk.

戳破生存策略：向真实求助与重写潜意识神经通路

幸运的是，我没有执行那个计划。相反，我向身边的家人和朋友坦白了一切。我没有粉饰说自己“只是压力大”或“失去了目标”，而是直截了当地告诉他们：我撑不下去了。正是这个决定拯救了我的生命。随后，我遇到了一位出色的精神科医生（Psychiatrist: 专门从事精神障碍诊断与治疗的医学专家），他引导我慢下来，不仅探究当下的感受，更深入探究陷入绝境的根源。我们花了五个月时间，逐渐戒掉了此前因误诊为双相情感障碍而服用的四种药物。在停药过程中，焦虑和脑海中飞驰的杂念愈演愈烈，几乎令人难以承受。但随着心绪逐渐平复，一个潜藏在最底层的核心创伤彻底浮现出来——那是童年留下的极深创伤，它一直在耳边低语：“你不够好。”

回望过去 48 年的人生，我不得不痛苦地承认：我所做的一切，本质上都是在挣扎着赢取自我价值。成为网球冠军、连续创业、将 TOMS 打造成有良知的资本主义（Conscious Capitalism: 强调兼顾商业利润与社会价值的商业理念）典范、登上各大商业杂志封面、站上演讲台接受荣誉与掌声……我曾深信这些成就能够抚平内心的自卑声音，但现实是成就反而让这种声音愈发喧嚣，直至把我逼上绝路。在一次深度心理治疗中，认知发生了根本性的转变：我开始意识到，自己的前半生不是一个成功故事，而是一套为了证明自我价值而苦心经营的“策略”。当这套策略失效时，我的整个世界便彻底坍塌了。

面对困境，治疗师提出了一个最初听起来甚至有些荒谬的方案：“四十多年来，你的大脑一直在告诉你‘你不够好’。除非我们重塑潜意识（Subconscious Reprogramming: 改变深层无意识思维定势的心理训练）并重构你的神经通路（Neural Pathways: 大脑神经元之间传递信号的连接网络），否则你下半辈子都会被这种限制性信念绑架。”于是我们立下约定：在接下来的 40 天里，每天早晨醒来立刻在床上端坐，闭上双眼，连续 20 分钟不断重复一句话：“我很充盈，我生来就已足够”（I am enough. I've always been enough）；入睡前再重复同样的练习 20 分钟。

刚开始的几周痛苦不堪，20 分钟漫长得如同两小时，整套练习显得微不足道且毫无起色。在坚持到第三周时，因为感觉不到任何变化，我一度想要放弃并更换治疗师。但出于对摆脱痛苦的极度渴望，我硬撑了下来。直到大约第 28 天，当我照例坐在床上默念这些话时，突然感到一股类似电流的震颤传遍全身，随之而来的是前所未有的轻盈与宁静。过去数周机械重复的口号在这一刻化作了真实的信念：我的价值不需要任何外在成就、企业规模或外界评价来定义，仅仅因为我的存在本身，我就是完整的。

英文原文

Thankfully, I did not act on that plan. Instead, I reached out to my friends and my family, and I told them the truth. Not that I was stressed, not that I had lost my purpose. I told them I wasn't okay. And that decision saved my life.

I then met an incredible psychiatrist who helped me really slow down and understand how I got there. Not just what I was feeling, but why. We then spent 5 months weaning me off four different medications I had been put on when I was misdiagnosed as bipolar. 5 months. And during that time, the anxiety did not get better, it got worse. And the racing thoughts in my head almost became unbearable. But eventually, my mind started to settle and something became impossible to ignore. There was a core wound underneath it all. A very very old one. And it said this, "You are not enough."

The truth is, I had never felt that I was enough. Not as a kid, not as an athlete, not as a founder, not as a human. And when I looked honestly at my life, I saw something that was really painful. Everything I had done for the first 48 years was an attempt to earn my worth. Becoming a championship tennis player, starting multiple businesses, building Toms and becoming the poster child of conscious capitalism. I mean, there were the magazine covers, the stages, just like this one, the awards and the accolades because I believed that achievement would quiet that voice. Instead, it made it louder. And it got so loud inside my mind that I didn't want to be alive anymore.

But one day, during a deep healing session with my therapist, something finally shifted. I began to see my life not as a success story, but as a strategy. A strategy to earn worth. And when that strategy failed, my whole world had collapsed.

So, my therapist suggested something one day that honestly sounded ridiculous at first. He said, "Blake, for over 40 years, your mind has been telling you that you're not enough. So, you are going to live with this limiting belief the rest of your life unless we reprogram your subconscious and rewire your neural pathways."

So, we made a commitment. For the next 40 days, I would wake up every morning, immediately sit up in bed, and repeat one sentence over and over again for 20 minutes with my eyes closed. "I am enough. I've always been enough." And then before going to bed later that night, I would do it again. "I am enough. I've always been enough." 20 minutes in the morning, 20 minutes at night for 40 days.

Now, the first few weeks honestly were brutal. 20 minutes felt like 2 hours. And the whole practice felt so trivial. After 3 weeks, I didn't feel any different. So I almost quit and changed therapists. But I was so damn desperate to feel better and nothing else had worked, so I decided to stick with it. And on the 28th day, around there, I was sitting up in bed saying the words and all of a sudden I felt almost like an electric shock go through my body and a lightness and a peace I had never felt. And what had been a generic mantra for the past couple weeks actually felt true. I am enough and I always have been. Not because of what I've done, not because of what I've built, and definitely not because of what the world thinks of me, but just because I exist.

打破沉默链条：将心理健康视同生理疾病的常态

当这种认知从口头念诵转变为身体层面的切实感受，我的人生彻底改变了。我重新简化了生活，剥离外界杂音，不再为了证明自己而表演。我深刻体会到：如果连我这样的人都会花费数十年试图证明自我价值并险些丧命，那么这种“不够好”的创伤必然广泛存在，没有人能够真正免疫。

在现实社会中，一旦有人遭遇焦虑、抑郁或心理疾病，往往会被视作一种令人震惊的失败。然而面对骨折、感冒等生理疾病时，我们不仅将其视为常态，更会坦然讨论并做好应对准备。在现场互动中，约有 40% 的人举手表示自己曾经历过骨折；但当问及是否被诊断出严重焦虑、抑郁或双相情感障碍时，举手的人明显稀少。然而据美国卫生部数据，50% 的人在一生中都会经历某种心理健康问题。这暴露了一个严峻的现实：羞耻滋生沉默，沉默导致孤立，而孤立催生了致命的自杀风险。研究表明，只要能向哪怕一个人袒露真实的生存状态，就能显著降低自杀风险。

为了打破这种致命的沉默链条，我发起了一项名为 We Are Enough Giving Chain 的公益行动。起初，这只是一条用来提醒我自己的串珠手链；但我很快意识到，手链真正的力量在于传递给身边所爱之人，向对方传递“有我支持你、你并不孤单、我们生来充盈”的信号。手链的规则非常简单：每位佩戴者都要将手链传递给下一位需要关怀的人。

这种自我确认的心理机制在于：当我们感到“自己不够好”时，要么深陷对过去过失与内疚的执念，要么焦虑地在未来追逐更多成就来填补空白；而一旦清晨以“我已足够”来开启一天，我们便能扎根于当下（The Present Moment: 摆脱过去懊悔与未来焦虑的即刻心智状态）——这是古往今来几乎所有灵性先哲公认的通往平静与喜悦的唯一途径。佩戴这条手链不仅是在去污名化（Destigmatization: 消除社会对特定群体的偏见与羞耻标签），更是向周围人释放一个信号：“你可以对我敞开心扉，告诉我你最真实的感受。”我们渴望的世界，是一个不再掩饰脆弱、以关怀替代耻辱的世界，因为真诚的对话能够拯救生命。

英文原文

And when that stopped being something that day that I said out loud and something I felt in my body, everything changed. I simplified my life again, I stripped away the noise, and I stopped performing. And I realized this, if I could spend decades trying to earn my worth, and if I could almost lose my life trying to earn it, then this wound is everywhere. And no one is immune to it.

Right now, when someone experiences anxiety, or depression, or a mental illness, we treat it like a shocking failure. Like something went wrong. But think about physical illness. We expect it. We expect colds. We expect broken bones. We plan for them. We talk about them. But mental illness, we whisper. We isolate. We pretend that we are fine. And I'm telling you that silence is deadly. I almost became part of a statistic not because I was weak, but because I was too ashamed to tell my friends how I was really doing.

Today, I'd like to ask everyone here two questions. Please, raise your hand if you have ever broken a bone. Thank you. So, looks like about 40% of the room, which is interesting cuz that's what my dad said, who's actually an orthopedic surgeon, said it would be. So, he got that one right. A harder question now. If you've ever been diagnosed with severe anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, or a mental health condition, would you please raise your hand? Thank you for your bravery. Notice a lot fewer hands went up. But according to the US Department of Health, we should have seen about the same number of hands for both questions. In fact, 50% of all people will be diagnosed with a mental health condition during their lifetime.

So why do we plan for one and are ashamed of the other? What if we expected mental illness the same way we expect physical illness? When you expect it, you're less ashamed when it arrives. You reach out sooner. You get help faster. And fewer people die. This is not abstract. The research is absolutely clear. Talking about how you're really doing with at least one person greatly reduces suicide risk. Silence does not protect us. It isolates us. Shame creates silence. Silence creates isolation. Isolation creates risk. I know because I lived that chain reaction.

And now I'm dedicating my life and my resources to starting a new chain. It's called the We Are Enough Giving Chain. It started with me creating a simple beaded bracelet to wear every single day as a reminder to myself that I am enough. But then I realized something. The real power of this bracelet was not wearing it myself. It was sharing it with someone I love. So that they would know every day that I got their back. That they're not alone. That we are all enough. And today the way it works is simple. Every person that is given an enough bracelet we ask them to send it to just one more person.

Now I'd like to ask you to do something with me real quickly. Please close your eyes. And I'd like you to think of the one person in your life right now that could use this message. Or think of the one person in your life that has always made you feel enough. Okay, open your eyes. I ask that you take 60 seconds and send one to that person. Because if we all take this simple task and wear these bracelets, I believe we will see a reduction in suicides and an improvement in our collective mental health.

And here is why. When you do not feel enough you are constantly living in the past. Obsessing over the mistakes that you've made and the shame and the guilt you carry because of them. And also, when you do not feel enough and very much like me, you're constantly living in the future. Thinking what can I do, achieve, or obtain to finally feel enough? But when you start your day and you ground your day in the fact that you are already enough just as you are, you get to live in the present. And almost every spiritual teacher throughout all of history agrees on only one thing and that is the only place we can access peace and joy consistently is in the present moment.

Now, when you wear this bracelet, you are helping to destigmatize mental health. But I would say it's much more personal than that. When you wear this bracelet, you're sending a signal. A signal to those around you that says, "You can talk to me. You can tell me how you're really doing." And just that is proven to save lives.

Now, my vision is not just about a bracelet. It's about a world where we stop hiding. It's about a world where we expect mental illness and respond in care instead of shame. And it's about a world where we tell each other how we're really doing. Cuz honest conversation saves lives. I'm here today because I reached out. I'm also here because someone listened. And I'm so, so thankful to be here today to share this with you. You are not broken. You are not weak. You're not alone. You are enough. And you always will be. Thank you.
