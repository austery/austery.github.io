职业的真相：从“安静辞职”到找到让你痴迷的毕生事业 TED 2026-06-15

领域: work-career

分类: career-development

主讲人: TED

来源: 查看

发布时间: 2026-06-15

摘要:

在这场演讲中，比尔·古尔利通过多个生动案例，深入剖析了驱动职业卓越的核心要素：不是“追随激情”，而是找到让你“自动魔力般”学习的“痴迷”所在。在AI加速时代，找到痴迷能让你成为不可被替代的终身学习者和领域工匠。

卓越的驱动力：持续学习

Bill Gurley: 我要从一个故事讲起，一个好故事，一个真实的故事。1983年，我的朋友丹尼25岁。几年前，他从三一学院毕业，拿到了政治学学位。他四处漂泊了一段时间，但在销售行业找到了一份非常酷的工作。他负责推销百货商店里挂在衣服上的那种防盗扣。他在这方面做得很出色。他赚了很多钱。但作为一个学政治学的学生，他总是计划好下一步要干什么：那就是去读法学院。所以就在他参加法学院入学考试（LSAT）的前一天晚上，他和他的理查德叔叔在上东区一家名叫Elios的餐厅共进晚餐。理查德叔叔能看出来有点不对劲。"丹尼，什么事让你这么心烦？" "啊，我明天得去考法学院入学考试，但我真的不想去考。" 理查德叔叔追问："那你为什么还要去考呢？"

Original English

Bill Gurley: I'm going to start with a story, a good story, a true story. In 1983, my friend Danny was 25 years old. A few years earlier, he had graduated from Trinity College with a poli sci degree. He bounced around for a while but fell into a really cool job in sales. He sold those doohickeys they attach to clothes in the department store so you can't steal them. He was good at it. He was making a lot of money. But as a poli sci student, he’d always planned to take the next step: law school. So the night before the LSATs [he was] out for dinner with his uncle Richard [at] a place called Elios on the Upper East Side. And Uncle Richard can tell something's not right. "Danny, what's eating you?" "Ah, I have to take the LSAT tomorrow, and I don't really want to." Uncle Richard probed. "So why are you?"

Bill Gurley: 我待会儿再来讲丹尼和理查德叔叔的故事，但请允许我先讲讲我为什么会站在这里。在过去的六年里，我一直在研究是什么驱动了职业生涯的卓越表现。我和一位合著者、一位研究员一起梳理了一百多部传记。我们采访了这个领域一些顶尖的学者，研究了他们的科研成果，我们甚至和沃顿商学院一起做了一项调查——然后我们把这些成果写成了一本书。我们发现了什么？在这些卓越者身上有许多共同的特征，但有一点比其他所有特征都更为突出：那就是持续且痴迷的学习。他们都是终身学习者。他们了解自己所在领域的历史。他们理解自己领域中细微的差别——正是这些细微之处将伟大与优秀区分开来。他们知道自己领域的边界在哪里。那就是创新所在的地方。而且他们在整个职业生涯中——从开始、中间到结束——都在不断地学习。我倾向于认为他们是用一种工匠的心态、像工匠一样去思考他们的手艺。而且我也逐渐相信，在每一个领域都存在着这样的工匠。

Original English

Bill Gurley: I'll get back to Danny and Uncle Richard in a minute, but let me tell you why I'm here. I spent the past six years studying what drives career excellence. A co-writer, a researcher and I combed through over 100 biographies. We talked to some of the leading academicians in the field, looked at their research, and we even did our own survey with Wharton -- we turned that into a book. What did we find? There were many common traits, but one thing stood out above everything else: continuous and obsessive learning. They were all lifetime students. They knew the history of their field. They understand the nuance of their field -- the thing that separates great from good. They know the edge of their field. That's where innovation lies. And they study throughout their entire career, beginning, middle and end. I'd like to think they thought about their craft as an artisan with an artisan mindset. And I've come to believe these artisans exist in every field.

从激情到痴迷

Bill Gurley: 举个有趣的例子。2015年，在冰岛举行的年度国际象棋比赛上，他们办了一件好玩的事：他们举办了一场历史知识问答比赛。猜猜谁赢了？马格努斯·卡尔森 (Magnus Carlsen)，也就是世界冠军。你看，他不仅国际象棋下得好。他还懂历史。但是如果你研究过马格努斯·卡尔森，你就会知道这是必然的。在他职业生涯中，他不太可能会突然说："哎呀，为了让我的国际象棋下得更好，我需要去学习历史了，我这就去学。" 它是通过一种完全不同的机制发生的。而这就是我今天要讲的核心观点，也是大家最应该记住的一点，在一开始就告诉大家。痴迷而持续的学习不是一种输入，它是一种输出。 它不是原因，而是结果。那原因到底是什么呢？是什么驱使一个人去终身学习？

Original English

Bill Gurley: Here's a fun example. In 2015, at the annual chess competition in Iceland they did something fun: they held a history trivia contest. Guess who won? Magnus Carlsen, the world champion. See, he's not just great at chess. He knew the history. But if you study Magnus Carlsen, you'd know this to be true. It’s very low likelihood that he got to a place in his career where he says, "Oh, boy, to be even better at chess, I need to study the history, I'm going to go do it." It was a different mechanism. And this is my key point, the key takeaway, right here at the beginning. Obsessive and continuous learning is not an input -- it’s an output. It's not the cause, it's the effect. What's the cause? What drives someone to learn for a lifetime?

Bill Gurley: 2024年，喜剧演员杰瑞·宋飞 (Jerry Seinfeld)在杜克大学的毕业典礼上发表演讲。他在狠狠嘲讽了"追随你的激情 (follow your passion)"这句话之后——嘲讽是他擅长的——他提出了一个不同的词，一个更好的词，一个更精确的词。他说，你在这个世界上应该去追随你的痴迷 (fascination)。我真的非常喜欢这个区分。你要明白，激情并不会直接引发实际的工作。你可以对辛辛那提红人队充满激情，然后坐在椅子上喝着啤酒看三个半小时的比赛。（笑声）但是痴迷本身就自带一种机制。当你对某件事痴迷时，你会"自动魔力般地 (automagically)"去学习它。顺便说一句，我知道那不是一个真正的词。（笑声）

Original English

Bill Gurley: In 2024, Jerry Seinfeld, the comedian, gave the commencement speech at Duke University. And after making fun, which he's good at, making fun of the phrase "follow your passion," he came up with a different word, a better word, a more precise word. He said, you should follow your fascination. I really love this distinction. You see, passion doesn’t invoke work. You could be passionate about the Cincinnati Reds and sit in a chair for 3.5 hours, drinking beer. (Laughter) But fascination comes with the mechanism. When you’re fascinated, you study “automagically.” By the way, I know that's not a real word. (Laughter)

Bill Gurley: 回到丹尼的故事。理查德叔叔继续追问。"丹尼，你这辈子想过的、谈论过的所有东西都是关于食物和餐厅的。你为什么不开一家餐厅呢？" 丹尼听进去了。他第二天早上还是去考了法学院入学考试，但他后来再也没有去法学院注册。相反，他报名参加了一个在杂志上看到的、收费300美元的餐厅管理课程。然后他接受了90%的降薪，只为了能进入当地的一家餐厅工作，在那里他可以轮岗体验不同的工作岗位。接着，他计划了一趟欧洲之旅，一趟学习之旅，他要在那里前往许多不同的国家，去许多不同的餐厅里进行“stage”。“Stage”是一个高级的法语词汇，意思就是“白干活不要钱”。（笑声）之后他回到了纽约。他还需要再去研究一些关于建筑选址的知识。然后在1985年，距离那顿具有重大意义的晚餐整整一年之后，丹尼开办了联合广场咖啡馆 (Union Square Cafe)。后来，联合广场咖啡馆被Zagat杂志八次评为纽约最受欢迎的餐厅。在这之后，丹尼在纽约陆续创办了十几家高端餐厅，包括 Eleven Madison、Gramercy Tavern 和 The Modern。对于在座的年轻人们来说，他之后还创立了 Shake Shack，这家快餐店如今在全球有400家分店，市值高达40亿美元。

Original English

Bill Gurley: Back to Danny. Uncle Richard kept pressing. "Danny, all you've ever thought about and talked about your whole life is food and restaurants. Why don't you open a restaurant?" Danny listened. He took the LSAT the next morning, but he never enrolled in law school. Instead, he enrolled in a $300 restaurant management course that he found in a magazine. He would then take a 90 percent pay cut to get his foot in the door at a local restaurant, where he could rotate through the different jobs. And then he planned a trip through Europe, a learning trip, where he would stage in many different countries, many different cuisines. “Stage” is a fancy French word that means work for free. (Laughter) He then went back to New York. He had to study some more location buildings. Then in 1985, a full year after that momentous dinner, Danny opened Union Square Cafe. Union Square Cafe would be recognized by Zagat magazine as New York's favorite restaurant eight times. And Danny would go on to launch over a dozen high-end restaurants in New York, including Eleven Madison, Gramercy Tavern, The Modern. For you younger folks out there, he would then, after that, found and launch Shake Shack, which has 400 locations worldwide and a $4 billion market cap.

Bill Gurley: 每次丹尼想要启动一个新概念之前，他都会先进行为期一年的学习和研究，然后再去推出它。去年秋天我跟他通电话的时候，他刚从欧洲结束又一轮的学习之旅回来。四十多年过去了，这种持续学习的劲头依然刻在他的DNA里。理查德叔叔那天晚上做了一件极其了不起的——或者说做了两件极其了不起的事情。第一，他把这个世界从“又多出一个律师”的灾难中拯救了出来。（笑声）第二点，也是更重要的一点，他帮助丹尼围绕着他所拥有的这种令人惊叹的痴迷，释放了他整个职业生涯的潜力。一顿晚餐，一句话，一个从0到1的观念转变，剩下的就都是历史了。这感觉非常神奇。

Original English

Bill Gurley: Every time Danny started a new concept, he'd do a year of learning and study before he'd launch. When I talked to him last fall, he was just back from Europe on another learning tour. Over 40 years later, still in his DNA. Uncle Richard did something really -- Did two things really amazing that night. First, he saved the world from another lawyer. (Laughter) But second, and more importantly, he unleashed Danny's career around this amazing fascination that he had. One dinner, one comment, and a bit flipped from 0 to 1, and the rest is history. It feels magical.

痴迷带来的复利

Bill Gurley: 我认为，当你把你所痴迷的事物与你的事业结合起来时，许多神奇的事情就会发生。首先，很显然，你会感到更加充实，也更加快乐。第二，学习变成了一件免费且自然而然的事情。我这么说是什么意思呢？意思是它不需要你刻意去费力。当你在学习自己不喜欢的东西时，它会消耗你的精力，你会感到疲倦，你需要休息。但当你在学习你所痴迷的事物时，你反而会获得能量，你甚至会忍不住微笑。这种对比是巨大的，这也就解释了为什么丹尼非常清楚自己该做什么，这同样也是为什么马格努斯·卡尔森会去了解那么多历史。

Original English

Bill Gurley: I think a lot of magical things happen when you combine fascination with a career. First, obviously, you're more fulfilled and you're more happy. Second, the learning comes for free. What do I mean by that? Zero conscious effort. When you're learning about something you don't like, it saps your energy, you get tired, you need a break. When you learn about something you're fascinated by, you get energy, you want to smile. That contrast is massive, and it’s why Danny knew exactly what to do, and it's precisely why Magnus Carlsen knew the history.

Bill Gurley: 当你将职业与你的痴迷结合在一起时，发生的第三件事情就是：人们会注意到你。如果你充满热情，并且掌握着超出常人的知识，你将在职业生涯的每一个维度上都表现得更加出色。你会在面试中所向披靡，你会更容易获得晋升，你会吸引到愿意指导你的导师，而且，或许最重要的是，机会会主动找上门来。“哦，听说你对做纪录片感兴趣。那你一定得去找萨莉聊聊，她对拍纪录片简直着迷了。”这种引荐在现实中每时每刻都在发生。

Original English

Bill Gurley: The third thing that happens when you combine career with fascination is people notice. If you're enthusiastic and have extra knowledge, you're going to do better in every dimension of your career. You're going to crush it in interviews, you’re going to get promoted, you’re going to attract mentors, and maybe, most importantly, opportunity comes at you. "Oh, I hear you're interested in starting a documentary. You have to talk to Sally, she's obsessed by them." Those introductions happen all the time.

Bill Gurley: 最后会发生的一件事情是，被痴迷驱动的人会留下巨大的印记。理查德叔叔并不只是帮助了丹尼一个人。你想想成千上万在丹尼的餐厅里工作过、学习过的人。你想想感受过他那份热情好客的数以百万计的顾客，再想想那些读过他那本《摆桌子》（Setting the Table）的餐厅老板和小企业主们。这确确实实是一种极其庞大的影响力。

Original English

Bill Gurley: The last thing that happens is the fascinated people leave big footprints. Uncle Richard didn't just help Danny. Think of the thousands and thousands of people that have worked in and learned in Danny's restaurants. Think about the millions of customers that have felt his hospitality and think about the restaurant owners and small business owners that have read his book, "Setting the Table." It's really, really a massive impact.

安静辞职与教育危机

Bill Gurley: 那么，究竟有多少人能抵达这个神奇的境界呢？2023年，盖洛普（Gallup）进行了一项民意调查，他们询问人们有多大比例的人在工作中感到充满活力并且全情投入。只有23%的人给出了肯定的回答。他们把足足59%的人归入了一个被称为安静辞职者 (quiet quitters) 的类别。这些人表示，他们对自己的工作感到矛盾，在情感上与工作是脱节的。在我们自己所做的一项调查中，我们问人们：“你现在做的是你梦寐以求的工作吗？”以及“你想不想重新来过？”只有**20%**的人回答说：“是的，我现在做的就是我梦想的工作，我不需要重新来过。”所以，能够做到的人比例可能低至五分之一。也就是20%。情况为什么没能更好呢？我认为这里面存在几个问题。

Original English

Bill Gurley: So how many people make it to this magical place? In 2023, Gallup did a poll where they asked what percentage of people are thriving and engaged in their job. Only 23 percent said yes. A full 59 percent they put in this category they called quiet quitters. They said they were ambivalent about their job, emotionally disconnected. In our own survey, we asked people, “Are you in your dream job?” and “Do you want [a] do-over?” And only 20 percent said, “Yes, I’m in my dream job and I don’t need a do-over.” So maybe it's as low as one in five. 20 percent. Why isn't it better? I think there are a few issues.

Bill Gurley: 首先，我认为考上大学和读大学的这条路本身就出了问题。我觉得这主要是因为现在想考进好学校实在是太难了。从六年级开始，我们就陷入了**乔纳森·海特 (Jonathan Haidt)所说的“简历军备竞赛 (résumé arms race)”**中。我们让孩子上中文课、长曲棍球课、大提琴课、做志愿服务——而且这才刚到星期二呢。（笑声）孩子们感到压力很大，父母们也觉得压力山大。我想大家都知道，这其中肯定哪里出了问题。

Original English

Bill Gurley: First, I think the path to and through college is broken. I think it’s because schools are so damn hard to get into. In sixth grade, we begin with [what] Jonathan Haidt has termed the “résumé arms race.” We do Mandarin lessons, lacrosse lessons, cello lessons, volunteering -- and that’s just by Tuesday. (Laughter) The kids feel pressure, and the parents feel pressure. And I think we know something's wrong there.

Bill Gurley: 其次，我们提前了做决定的目标门槛。在我年轻的时候，学校是不允许你在大学二年级结束之前选定专业的。而在今天，在许多学校里，你在填写入学申请的时候就必须得申请具体的专业。我们把做这个人生重大决定的年龄从20岁提前到了17岁。你有没有试过去问一个17岁的孩子，他们余生想做什么？他们是真的不知道。他们完完全全不知道。

Original English

Bill Gurley: Second, we've moved the decision goalposts. When I was young, they wouldn't allow you to declare a major until the end of your sophomore year of college. Today, at many schools, you have to apply to the major when you fill out the application. We made the life decision from 20 years to 17 years old. Have you ever asked a 17-year-old what they want to do the rest of their life? They really don't know. They really don't know.

Bill Gurley: 最后一件事是，有一些本意是好的父母和顾问——我想再次强调一遍，他们的本意是好的——会推动孩子们去追求那些所谓的安全工作：比如医学、法律、金融、计算机科学。但是，如果那些安全的工作现在已经不再安全了呢？**人工智能（AI）**已经到来了。你们在学校里学过的、帮你们应付考试的那些公式和算法？它们全都被写进大模型里了。如果你在大学毕业后不能继续推进你的学习，那些模型终将追上你。而且我认为，受到威胁的并不是我们热爱的工作。受威胁的恰恰是那些人们本来就感觉模棱两可、心生矛盾的工作，是那59%的“安静辞职者”的工作。

Original English

Bill Gurley: The last thing is, several well-intentioned parents and advisors, and I want to repeat, well-intentioned, have pushed kids towards the safe jobs: medicine, legal, finance, comp sci. But what if the safe jobs aren't safe anymore? Along comes AI. You know all those formulas and algorithms you learned in school that helped you take the test? They're all in the model. If you're not advancing your learning after you leave college, they're catching up. And I don't think it's the jobs we love that are under threat. It's the ones people were ambivalent about already, the 59 percent, the quiet quitters.

AI时代的破局之道

Bill Gurley: 但也许，真正受到威胁的其实是那种静止僵化的思维模式。那么那些工匠呢，那些充满痴迷的工匠呢？**马克·库班 (Mark Cuban)**几天前说了一句话。他说：“世界上有两种人：一种是利用大语言模型（LLMs）来让自己以前所未有的速度学习的人，另一种是利用大语言模型来彻底逃避学习的人。”现实情况是，对于这些满怀痴迷的工匠来说，AI其实是一个喷气式背包 (jetpack)。他们会学得更快，飞得更高。所以，我们到底该如何让更多的人走上这条路呢？我担心现在的教育机构并不是为此而设立的。他们是为了大规模、高产量的流水线生产而设立的，而不是为了提供那种量身定制的、个性化的、针对个人的痴迷去进行探索的服务。但是，如果机构做不到，那谁能做到呢？

Original English

Bill Gurley: But maybe, maybe what's really under threat is the static mindset. What about the artisans, the fascinated artisans? Mark Cuban said something the other day. He said, “There’s two types of people in the world: those that use LLMs to learn faster than ever, and those that use LLMs to skip learning altogether.” The reality is, for these fascinated artisans, AI is a jetpack. They learn faster, they soar higher. So how do we get more people in this lane? I fear the institutions aren't set up for it. They’re set up for high-volume mass manufacturing, not bespoke, individualized, customized fascination discovery. But if they can't, who will?

成为点燃他人的火花

Bill Gurley: 最后，我想用两个简短的故事来收尾，也许它们能给我们指明正确的方向。四年前，我和妻子搬到了奥斯汀，我们有幸在那里认识了一位非常有名的演员。你们可能认识他。“好吧，好吧，好吧（Alright, alright, alright）。”你们猜对了。当**马修·麦康纳 (Matthew McConaughey)**还小的时候，他非常擅长在争论中赢过别人，于是他全家人都告诉他：“你以后应该去当个律师。”他也觉得这是个很棒的主意。于是他去上大学了。在大二那年，他结识了一些电影学院的朋友。他非常热爱电影，想要转专业，但他又很害怕他那位严厉父亲的反应。他最终还是鼓起勇气给父亲打了个电话，把他的逻辑理了一遍。电话那头是一阵漫长的沉默，然后他的父亲说道：“既然要做，就别半途而废（Don't half-ass it）。”（笑声）

Original English

Bill Gurley: I'm going to close with two short stories that might point us in the right direction. My wife and I relocated to Austin four years ago, and we've had a chance to meet this very famous actor that lives there. You may know the one. "Alright, alright, alright." You got it.
