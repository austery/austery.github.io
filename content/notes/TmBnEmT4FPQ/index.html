中国诺贝尔奖困境：战时思维与创新抑制 夸克说 10/31/2025

领域: geopolitics

分类: geopolitics

主讲人: 夸克说

来源: 查看

发布时间: 10/31/2025

摘要:

本文深入探讨了中国在诺贝尔奖方面与日本的显著差距，并分析了其背后的深层原因。文章驳斥了“诺奖滞后论”和“制度论”的表面解释，提出“战时思维”是中国社会从上到下推崇效率、忽视原创性基础研究的根本原因，这种思维模式扼杀了创新，导致中国难以在诺贝尔奖级别的科学突破上取得成就。文章还通过历史案例和数据对比，进一步论证了这一观点。

日本诺奖井喷与中国的反思

大家好，欢迎收看夸克说，我是夸克。过去的一周，瑞典皇家科学院陆续公布了本年度的诺奖名单。

View/Hide Original English

Hello everyone, welcome to Quark Says, I am Quark. In the past week, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences successively announced this year's Nobel Prize winners.

相比于去年，今年的名单在简中网络明显引发了更多讨论，原因也很简单：今年的名单里出现了两位日本科学家的名字。

View/Hide Original English

Compared to last year, this year's list clearly sparked more discussion on the simplified Chinese internet, and the reason is simple: two Japanese scientists appeared on this year's list.

一位是获得了医学奖的板口志文（Tasuku Honjo: 日本免疫学家，因发现PD-1而获得2018年诺贝尔生理学或医学奖），一位是刚拿了化学奖的北川静（Susumu Kitagawa: 日本化学家，因在金属有机框架材料MOF领域的贡献而获得2024年诺贝尔化学奖）。

View/Hide Original English

One is Tasuku Honjo, who won the Nobel Prize in Medicine, and the other is Susumu Kitagawa, who just won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

算起来日本已经总计拿到了32座诺奖，其中光在本世纪，也就是2000年以后获得的就高达24座。

View/Hide Original English

Japan has accumulated a total of 32 Nobel Prizes, with 24 of them awarded in this century alone, meaning after the year 2000.

要知道，2001年日本政府曾经提出要在50年内拿30个诺奖，当时中国官媒曾经不屑一顾，并在报道中用“口出狂言”来形容。

View/Hide Original English

It's worth noting that in 2001, the Japanese government proposed to win 30 Nobel Prizes within 50 years. At the time, Chinese state media dismissed this, describing it as "boasting" in their reports.

如今时间才过了一半，人家的目标已经完成了80%，狂言眼看就要成真了，怎么能不让某些U型锁门（U-shaped lock gate: 网络流行语，指极端民族主义者，典故源于2012年西安U型锁砸人事件）着急上火呢？

View/Hide Original English

Now, only half the time has passed, and their goal is 80% complete. The "boasting" is about to come true, so how could it not make some extreme nationalists anxious and angry?

于是乎，简中世界各种声音都出来了：有指责日本人靠公关的，有酸葡萄说诺贝尔奖是西方人的奖，都偏袒自己人，这种奖不拿也罢。

View/Hide Original English

Consequently, various voices emerged in the simplified Chinese world: some accused the Japanese of relying on public relations, others, with sour grapes, said the Nobel Prize is a Western award, biased towards their own people, and that it's not worth winning.

还有强行赢的说，虽然我们拿不到奖，但一点不耽误我们登月、六代机和东风导弹啊！

View/Hide Original English

There were also those who claimed victory by saying, even if we don't win the prize, it doesn't stop us from landing on the moon, developing sixth-generation fighters, and Dongfeng missiles!

等啥时候我们的导弹给美国、欧洲全世界都打服了，他们求着给我们颁奖还来不及呢！

View/Hide Original English

When our missiles have subdued the US, Europe, and the entire world, they'll be begging to give us awards!

以上这几种都属于比较反智，咱们就不浪费时间介绍了。

View/Hide Original English

These types of arguments are rather anti-intellectual, so we won't waste time discussing them.

但与此同时，也有不少人在认真地探讨为什么中国拿诺奖那么难，以及中国的诺贝尔奖什么时候才会爆发。

View/Hide Original Original English

But at the same time, many people are seriously discussing why it's so difficult for China to win Nobel Prizes, and when China's Nobel Prize "explosion" will occur.

接下来我也会根据自己的理解，谈谈对这两个问题的看法。

View/Hide Original English

Next, I will also share my understanding and views on these two questions.

“某国诺奖”的定义与中国的实际情况

在讨论这些问题之前，咱们得先理性地给个定义，那就是“某国拿诺贝尔奖”这个说法其实不准确的。

View/Hide Original English

Before discussing these issues, we first need to rationally define what "a country winning a Nobel Prize" actually means, as the phrase itself is not entirely accurate.

因为诺奖是颁发给个人的嘛，所以你只能说“拥有某国国籍的人拿诺贝尔奖”，也就是他在得奖时是哪国人。

View/Hide Original English

Since the Nobel Prize is awarded to individuals, you can only say "a person with a certain nationality wins a Nobel Prize," meaning what their nationality was at the time of receiving the award.

但仅仅只有个国籍也不能证明你这个国家就有多强的教育和科研实力。

View/Hide Original English

However, merely possessing a nationality does not prove the strength of that country's education and scientific research capabilities.

最典型的例子就是刚和北川进一起获得了诺贝尔化学奖的奥马尔·亚吉（Omar Yaghi: 约旦裔美国化学家，因在MOF和COF领域的开创性工作而闻名）。

View/Hide Original English

The most typical example is Omar Yaghi, who just won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry alongside Susumu Kitagawa.

理论上他依然拥有巴勒斯坦的公民权，算是巴勒斯坦人。

View/Hide Original English

Theoretically, he still holds Palestinian citizenship and is considered a Palestinian.

但一查履历你就会发现，他被看作是巴勒斯坦人唯一的原因是他的父母都是巴勒斯坦难民，而他本人则是在约旦出生的，15岁跟随父母移民到美国。

View/Hide Original English

But upon checking his resume, you'll find that the only reason he's considered Palestinian is that his parents were Palestinian refugees, while he himself was born in Jordan and immigrated to the United States with his parents at age 15.

他的高中、大学教育以及此后的研究工作都是在美国完成的，他这辈子可能都没去过巴勒斯坦。

View/Hide Original English

His high school and university education, as well as his subsequent research work, were all completed in the United States; he may never have even visited Palestine in his life.

这种情况下，巴勒斯坦如果站出来宣称亚吉是为国争光、与有荣焉，似乎也有点过于牵强。

View/Hide Original English

In this situation, if Palestine were to come forward and claim Yaghi brought glory to the nation and shared in its honor, it would seem a bit far-fetched.

所以当我们讨论某国拿到诺贝尔奖时，其实包含了三个要素：第一，得奖人得奖时是不是该国国籍；第二，他的主要受教育经历发生在哪个国家；第三，他得奖的研究成果是在哪个国家做出的。

View/Hide Original English

So, when we discuss a country winning a Nobel Prize, it actually involves three elements: first, whether the laureate held that country's nationality at the time of the award; second, in which country their primary education took place; and third, in which country their award-winning research was conducted.

其中第二、第三点尤其重要。

View/Hide Original English

The second and third points are particularly important.

其实按照这个标准，日本的32座诺奖也要打部分折扣，因为1987和2010年的历根川静（Ryoji Noyori: 日本化学家，2001年诺贝尔化学奖得主）和根岸英一（Ei-ichi Negishi: 日本化学家，2010年诺贝尔化学奖得主）虽然都还是日本籍，但他们的博士学位和主要研究工作都是在美国完成的。

View/Hide Original English

In fact, by this standard, Japan's 32 Nobel Prizes also need some discounting, because Ryoji Noyori (1987) and Ei-ichi Negishi (2010), though still Japanese nationals, completed their PhDs and primary research work in the United States.

而南部杨一郎（Yoichiro Nambu: 日裔美籍物理学家，2008年诺贝尔物理学奖得主）等三人呢，获奖时已经是美国籍了。

View/Hide Original English

As for Yoichiro Nambu and two others, they were already US citizens when they received their awards.

至于2017年获得诺贝尔文学奖的石黑一雄（Kazuo Ishiguro: 日裔英籍小说家，2017年诺贝尔文学奖得主），则是五岁就已经移居英国，他创作小说时用的也都是英文。

View/Hide Original English

As for Kazuo Ishiguro, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2017, he had already moved to the UK at the age of five, and he wrote his novels in English.

但除开这几位，实打实的由日本培养、在日本做出研究成果、获奖时依然是日本籍的诺奖得主依然有26位。

View/Hide Original English

However, excluding these individuals, there are still 26 Nobel laureates who were genuinely educated in Japan, conducted their research there, and remained Japanese nationals at the time of their awards.

并且南部杨一郎等三人虽然是美国籍，但考虑到他们的博士学位和研究工作都是在日本完成的，也基本上可以算是日本诺奖。

View/Hide Original English

Moreover, although Yoichiro Nambu and the other two were US citizens, considering their doctoral degrees and research work were completed in Japan, they can largely be counted as Japanese Nobel laureates.

如果按上面这个标准，地地道道“中国制造”的诺奖得主尤其只有屠呦呦、莫言、刘晓波三位。

View/Hide Original English

If we apply the above standard, there are only three genuinely "Made in China" Nobel laureates: Tu Youyou, Mo Yan, and Liu Xiaobo.

因为达赖喇嘛目前的国籍状况存疑嘛。

View/Hide Original English

Because the current citizenship status of the Dalai Lama is questionable.

这个数量在全球各国排第31位，和白俄罗斯、克罗地亚、墨西哥相当。

View/Hide Original English

This number ranks 31st among all countries globally, comparable to Belarus, Croatia, and Mexico.

当然前提是中国承认刘晓波的这个和平奖啊，如果不承认那就只剩下两座，直接调到38位，和伊朗、巴基斯坦、突尼斯和阿尔及利亚相当了。

View/Hide Original English

Of course, this is on the premise that China recognizes Liu Xiaobo's Peace Prize; if not, only two remain, dropping directly to 38th place, on par with Iran, Pakistan, Tunisia, and Algeria.

如果再考虑到人口规模，那中国毫无疑问是全球人均诺奖数量最少的国家之一。

View/Hide Original English

If we further consider population size, China is undoubtedly one of the countries with the fewest Nobel Prizes per capita globally.

要知道，哪怕是粉红们最看不上的印度也拿了十座，这显然跟墙内某些人一直吹嘘的“中国国力领先美国100年”和“中国人平均智商世界第一”形成了强烈反差。

View/Hide Original English

It's worth noting that even India, which some nationalists disdain, has won ten. This clearly contrasts sharply with the claims made by some within China about "China's national strength leading the US by 100 years" and "Chinese people having the highest average IQ in the world."

诺奖滞后论与制度论的局限性

关于这一现象，除开粉红说的各种阴谋论以外，常见的解释有这么几种。

View/Hide Original English

Regarding this phenomenon, apart from various conspiracy theories put forth by nationalists, there are several common explanations.

第一种，可能也是支持者最多的，叫诺奖滞后论（Nobel Prize Lag Theory: 认为诺贝尔奖的成果通常有较长的滞后期，中国科研投入增加后，诺奖数量会在未来井喷）。

View/Hide Original English

The first, and perhaps most widely supported, is the Nobel Prize Lag Theory.

这种理论认为，诺奖级的成果是需要拿钱砸的，过去中国拿的少是因为穷、底子薄、科研投入少。

View/Hide Original English

This theory posits that Nobel-level achievements require significant financial investment, and China's past low count was due to poverty, a weak foundation, and insufficient research funding.

而中国经济真正开始起飞也就是最近二十几年，考虑到诺奖的科学类奖项通常有二十年左右的滞后期，可以预见到2030年以后中国的诺奖数量将会出现井喷。

View/Hide Original English

China's economy only truly began to take off in the last two decades, and considering that scientific Nobel Prizes typically have a lag of about twenty years, it can be predicted that China's Nobel Prize count will surge after 2030.

支持这一观点的人还举了日本的例子，因为日本本世纪以来拿到的诺奖大都是上世纪90年代经济腾飞时期的成果。

View/Hide Original English

Proponents of this view also cite Japan as an example, as most of Japan's Nobel Prizes this century are the result of its economic boom in the 1990s.

需要说明的是，这种理论不是今天才出现的，事实上每隔几年都会有专家站出来预测说若干年内中国会获得多少多少个诺奖。

View/Hide Original English

It should be noted that this theory is not new; in fact, every few years, experts emerge to predict how many Nobel Prizes China will win in the coming years.

比如2007年开始，杨振宁（Chen-Ning Yang: 华裔美籍物理学家，1957年诺贝尔物理学奖得主）就在多个场合公开预测过，说10到20年内中国就会有人拿到诺贝尔奖。

View/Hide Original English

For instance, starting in 2007, Chen-Ning Yang publicly predicted on multiple occasions that someone in China would win a Nobel Prize within 10 to 20 years.

他说的还算比较保守的，因为也没说拿几个，过了几年屠呦呦也确实拿了，人家没预测错。

View/Hide Original English

His prediction was relatively conservative, as he didn't specify how many. A few years later, Tu Youyou indeed won, so his prediction wasn't wrong.

但接下来的预测就比较离谱了。

View/Hide Original English

However, subsequent predictions became quite exaggerated.

比如2013年中国科学院院士黄维（Huang Wei: 中国有机电子学家，中国科学院院士）对媒体表示，说十年之内，也就是到2023年，中国人拿诺奖就应该如家常便饭。

View/Hide Original English

For example, in 2013, Huang Wei, an academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, told the media that within ten years, meaning by 2023, Chinese people winning Nobel Prizes should become commonplace.

2015年首都医科大学的校长饶毅（Rao Yi: 中国神经生物学家，曾任首都医科大学校长）也预测说，中国将在2035年以后出现诺奖井喷，到2050年左右诺奖总数将全面超过日本。

View/Hide Original English

In 2015, Rao Yi, the president of Capital Medical University, also predicted that China would experience a Nobel Prize surge after 2035, and by around 2050, its total number of Nobel Prizes would completely surpass Japan.

最夸张的还得属清华大学高等教育研究所所长王小阳（Wang Xiaoyang: 清华大学高等教育研究所所长）。

View/Hide Original English

The most exaggerated prediction came from Wang Xiaoyang, the director of Tsinghua University Institute of Education.

他的看法是到2050年中国可能会出现100个诺奖，秒杀日本。

View/Hide Original English

His view is that by 2050, China might produce 100 Nobel Prizes, completely outperforming Japan.

至于中国教育体制扼杀创新导致拿不到诺奖的说法，则完全属于“公知抹黑”。

View/Hide Original English

As for the claim that China's education system stifles innovation, leading to a lack of Nobel Prizes, he dismissed it as "smearing by public intellectuals."

以上还是目前网上能查到的，事实上在我的印象里，类似的观点从上世纪八九十年代就开始了。

View/Hide Original English

These are still the views that can be found online; in fact, in my memory, similar opinions started appearing in the 1980s and 1990s.

我小的时候媒体的说法是到2000年，就连前学生都做过类似的论断。

View/Hide Original English

When I was young, the media's narrative was that by 2000, even former students had made similar assertions.

到我上大学的时候，普遍预测时间又推到了2010年左右，只是因为年代久远，那会儿的信息现在查不到了而已。

View/Hide Original English

By the time I went to college, the general prediction had been pushed back to around 2010, but because of the passage of time, that information is now untraceable.

似乎你只要不停地把这个井喷时间往后推，牛皮就能一直吹下去，反正聊的都是20年以后的事，那会儿年轻人还有几个记得并且跟你翻旧账较真呢？

View/Hide Original English

It seems as long as you keep pushing back the "surge" date, the boast can continue indefinitely. After all, it's about things 20 years in the future; how many young people will remember and hold you accountable then?

没准网上都查不到了。

View/Hide Original English

Perhaps it won't even be traceable online.

除开这种有点自欺欺人的诺奖滞后论，另一种制度论则显得更加务实。

View/Hide Original English

Aside from this somewhat self-deceptive Nobel Prize lag theory, another institutional theory appears more pragmatic.

这种理论认为，中国人拿诺奖难最根本的原因还在于中国的教育制度和学术氛围很大程度上抑制着创新。

View/Hide Original English

This theory posits that the fundamental reason for China's difficulty in winning Nobel Prizes lies in its education system and academic atmosphere, which largely suppress innovation.

更具体的说，诺奖更看重基础科学和技术的原创性，而这些研究成果具有相当程度的偶然性，只有好奇心驱动、忍得住寂寞、愿意坐冷板凳的科研人员才有可能成功。

View/Hide Original English

More specifically, the Nobel Prize places greater emphasis on the originality of basic science and technology, and these research achievements have a considerable degree of serendipity. Only researchers driven by curiosity, who can endure solitude and are willing to "sit on the cold bench" (work on unpopular or long-term projects), have a chance of success.

而中国的情况是从义务教育阶段开始就扼杀学生的好奇心，灌输标准答案，崇尚实用主义。

View/Hide Original English

In China, however, from the compulsory education stage, students' curiosity is stifled, standard answers are instilled, and pragmatism is glorified.

到了科研阶段，考核标准更是严重的功利导向。

View/Hide Original English

At the research stage, assessment standards are even more heavily utilitarian-oriented.

比如之前聊过的“飞升即走”（Fly up and leave: 指学术界为了晋升而追求短期、数量化的成果，而非长期、原创性研究），要晋升就必须在顶级期刊发多少篇论文等等，以至于搞出了一个庞大的付费发论文的产业链。

View/Hide Original English

For example, as discussed before, the "fly up and leave" system means that to get promoted, one must publish a certain number of papers in top journals, leading to a massive industry of paid paper publications.

这相当于鼓励所有的研究人员都扎堆去搞那些热门、容易出成果的方向，和学术精神背道而驰了。

View/Hide Original English

This effectively encourages all researchers to flock to popular, easily achievable research areas, which runs counter to the spirit of academic inquiry.

再有就是学术领域严重的官僚风气，比如一篇论文十几二十个作者，真正做出贡献的人反而排在导师后面。

View/Hide Original English

Furthermore, there's a serious bureaucratic atmosphere in academia; for instance, a paper might have a dozen or twenty authors, with the actual contributors often ranked behind their supervisors.

再比如70%的科研经费都是竞争性的课题，平均周期不超过三年，支撑不了需要长期沉淀的重大项目等等。

View/Hide Original English

Another example is that 70% of research funding comes from competitive projects with an average cycle of no more than three years, which cannot support major projects requiring long-term accumulation.

以上这些观点有道理吗？有，但似乎还有一点流于表面，因为它描述的更多是现象而不是背后的成因。

View/Hide Original English

Are these points valid? Yes, but they seem a bit superficial, as they describe phenomena more than underlying causes.

战时思维：抑制创新的深层根源

接下来就说说我自己的观点，也就是为什么中国的学术和研究氛围会呈现出这种急功近利的取向。

View/Hide Original English

Next, I'll share my own perspective on why China's academic and research environment exhibits such a utilitarian and short-sighted orientation.

在阐述观点前，我们需要先了解诺贝尔奖是干嘛的，它的评奖标准是什么。

View/Hide Original English

Before elaborating on my views, we need to understand what the Nobel Prize is for and what its judging criteria are.

根据诺奖官网上的解释，诺贝尔奖是根据阿尔弗雷德·诺贝尔（Alfred Nobel: 瑞典化学家、工程师、发明家，诺贝尔奖的创立者）先生的遗嘱设立的，它最高的核心标准是“对人类做出最大的贡献”。

View/Hide Original English

According to the Nobel Prize website, the Nobel Prize was established according to the will of Mr. Alfred Nobel, and its highest core standard is "to have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind."

注意这个定语啊，是“为人类做出最大贡献”，而不是为某个国家、某个政权或者某个政党。

View/Hide Original English

Note this qualifier: it's "the greatest benefit to humankind," not to a specific country, regime, or political party.

所以墙内经常有人讨论什么钱学森（Qian Xuesen: 中国著名科学家，两弹一星元勋）邓稼先（Deng Jiaxian: 中国著名核物理学家，两弹一星元勋）贡献那么大凭什么拿不到一个诺奖，甚至有人夸张地表示“不是钱学森拿不到诺奖，而是诺奖配不上钱学森”，这都属于是笑料了。

View/Hide Original English

So, it's often discussed within China why figures like Qian Xuesen and Deng Jiaxian, with their immense contributions, didn't receive a Nobel Prize. Some even exaggeratedly claim, "It's not that Qian Xuesen couldn't win a Nobel Prize, but that the Nobel Prize isn't worthy of Qian Xuesen," which is frankly laughable.

因为钱、邓干的事儿不过是为中共党卫军添砖加瓦，唯一的贡献就是帮助巩固党的独裁统治，防止老百姓造反。
