小黄鸭调试法与“通过思考来学习”

软件工程师有一项非常有趣的实践，叫做“小黄鸭调试法”。当他们的代码遇到问题时，会向一只橡胶小黄鸭进行解释。他们一步一步、一行一行地推进，有时候就能在这个过程中发现新东西。但这怎么行得通呢？小黄鸭并不会回应，所以程序员实际上是在向自己解释。他们并没有获得任何新信息。然而，这个过程中的某些机制却切实帮助了他们学习。

英文原文 Software engineers have a delightful practice called rubber ducking. When they run into a problem with their code, they'll explain it to a rubber duck. They'll work step by step, line by line, and some of the time they'll learn something new. But how can this work? The ducks don't talk back, so the programmers are explaining to themselves. They're not getting any new information. And yet, something about this process helps them learn.

小黄鸭调试法正是被我称为“通过思考来学习”的一个例子。而理解通过思考来学习是如何运作的，正是成为一名更优秀学习者的关键所在。我们大多数人都渴望成为更优秀的学习者，因为我们希望自己的思维变得更好、更有效、更见识广博。但学习往往很艰难。在未来的岁月里，我们将通过互联网、社交媒体和人工智能接触到越来越多的工具与信息。然而，更多的信息可能会让学习变得更难，而不是更容易。我们面临着信息过载的风险。

英文原文 Rubber ducking is an example of what I call learning by thinking. And understanding how learning by thinking works is the key to becoming a better learner. Now most of us want to be better learners. That’s because we want to make our minds better, more effective and more informed. But learning can be hard. And in the years ahead, we'll have access to more and more tools and to more and more information through the internet and social media and AI. And more information can make learning harder rather than easier. We run the risk of information overwhelm.

部分问题在于，我们往往倾向于向外寻找更多信息来开展学习。但这只会加剧这种过载。相反，我们可以通过思考来进行学习。我们可以从自己已经掌握的信息开始。我是一名认知科学家，研究学习已有25年之久。我逐渐体会到的一点是，我们大脑的丰富与深邃程度往往超出了我们原本给予它的评价。事实上，我们的大脑中包含着庞大的知识宝库——这些知识就蕴藏在我们的记忆与技能之中。通过思考来学习，就是调动并利用这些知识以成为更优秀学习者的过程。

英文原文 Part of the problem is that we tend to approach learning by looking outside of ourselves for more information. But that can just exacerbate the overwhelm. Instead, we could be learning by thinking. We could be starting with the information we already have. I’m a cognitive scientist, and I've been studying learning for over 25 years. One thing I've come to appreciate is that our minds are richer and deeper than we often give them credit for. In fact, our minds contain vast repositories of knowledge -- knowledge that’s contained in our memories and our skills. Learning by thinking is the process of tapping into that knowledge to become a better learner.

发掘头脑中沉睡的知识

为了让大家感受“通过思考来学习”的实际运作，我想问你们几个问题。一旦有了答案，请举起手来。准备好了吗？

  • 你的房子或公寓正前方向共有多少扇窗户？
  • 字母表中的第五个字母是什么？
  • 在标准键盘上，字母 Z 位于左侧还是右侧？
英文原文 To give you a sense of learning by thinking in action, I'd like to ask you a few questions. As soon as you have an answer, please raise your hand. Are you ready? How many windows are along the front of your house or apartment? What is the fifth letter of the alphabet? Is the letter Z on the left or the right side of a typical keyboard?

我敢打赌，你们中的很多人并没有立刻知道这些问题的答案。但你们拥有得出答案所需要的知识：

  • 你们可以调动视觉记忆来数出家门正前方的窗户数量；
  • 你们可以运用听觉记忆推导出 E 是字母表中的第五个字母；
  • 你们可以凭借运动技能判断出字母 Z 位于标准键盘的左侧。

这些简单的例子突显了你们既有知识的丰富度与深邃度，也展示了通过思考来学习的过程。

英文原文 I'll bet that many of you didn't know the answers to these questions immediately. But you had the knowledge you needed to figure them out. You could use your visual memories to count the number of windows along the front of your home, you could use your auditory memories to figure out that E is the fifth letter of the alphabet, and you could use your motor skills to figure out that the letter Z is on the left side of a typical keyboard. These simple examples highlight the richness and depth of the knowledge you already have. And they illustrate the process of learning by thinking.

转化与重组已有知识

然而，仅仅意识到自己拥有未被调用的知识，并不足以让你成为更优秀的学习者。因为你还需要知道在需要时如何提取这些知识。假设你决定烘烤巧克力曲奇，但食谱要求使用巧克力豆和有盐黄油，而这两样东西你目前都没有。幸运的是，你可以把你确实拥有的一整块巧克力切碎，变成食谱所需的巧克力碎片；你也可以将无盐黄油与食盐结合，得到有盐黄油。

英文原文 But recognizing that you have untapped knowledge is not enough to become a better learner. That's because you also need to know how to extract that knowledge when you need it. Suppose you decide to bake chocolate chip cookies. But the recipe calls for chocolate chips and for salted butter, neither of which you already have. Fortunately, you can transform that bar of chocolate you do have into the chocolate pieces you need for the recipe, and you can combine unsalted butter with salt to get salted butter.

这很常见。我们在烹饪时，经常会转化和组合现有的食材，以获取我们需要的成分。学习也是一模一样的道理。当我们在通过思考进行学习时，我们正是在转化和组合我们既有的信息。为了看清这是如何运作的，请想想信息可以以各种各样的方式被表征。直到今天之前，关于你家正前方的窗户，你脑海里的表征大概不是一个单一的数字，而是一连串的视觉记忆。因此，当你转化并组合那些记忆以提炼出一个具体数字时，你就学到了新的东西——你得知了家门正前方到底有多少扇窗户。这样，你就能回答一个此前无法回答的问题。这就是通过思考来学习的神奇之处。

英文原文 This is probably familiar. When we’re cooking, we often transform and combine the ingredients we have to get the ingredients we need. It's just the same for learning. When we're learning by thinking, we transform and combine the information we already have. To see how this works, consider that information can be represented in all sorts of ways. Until today, you probably represented the windows along the front of your home, not in the form of a single number, but as a series of visual memories. So when you transform and combine those memories to extract a single number, you learn something new. You learn how many windows are along the front of your home. And so you can answer a question that you couldn't answer before. That's the magic of learning by thinking.

不过这里有一个限制。并非所有的学习都能仅凭思考来完成。如果我从未去过你家，我无论如何思考也无法知道你家有多少扇窗户。我需要新信息。但是，问自己你家有多少扇窗户，能为我随后的学习做好铺垫；因此当我真正来到你家时，我就能确切知道该寻找什么信息。这就是为什么通过思考来学习往往是最好的起点。回到做巧克力曲奇的例子，在着手列采购清单之前，先查看自己已有的食材才是明智之举。

英文原文 There is a catch, though. Not all learning can be done by thinking alone. If I've never been to your home, I can't think my way to how many windows you have. I need new information. But asking myself how many windows you have sets me up to learn so that when I do make it to your home, I know precisely what information to look for. That's why learning by thinking is so often the best place to start. Going back to our chocolate chip cookies, it makes sense to look at the ingredients you have already before you start making a shopping list.

自我解释效应与认知表征

遗憾的是，在学习这件事情上，我们并没有现成的菜谱。没有任何一种单一的学习策略能够在每种情况下都保证最佳结果。但有一种策略非常接近于此：它是“通过思考来学习”的一种形式，涉及寻求解释。

英文原文 Unfortunately, though, when it comes to learning, we don't have a recipe. There's no single strategy for learning that guarantees the best outcome in every case. But there is one strategy that comes close. It's a version of learning by thinking that involves seeking explanations.

在1980年代末，科学家们试图了解到底是什么让成绩最出色的学生与表现较差的同龄人区别开来。即使学生接触的是完全相同的信息、同样的教科书、考试中同一段文本，他们学到的内容也会有巨大的差异。因此，让那些最优秀学习者脱颖而出的，不单单是大脑外部可获取的信息，而必定与他们如何处理这些信息有关。科学家们发现，许多最成功的学生都有一个共同点：在学习时，他们会向自己解释。他们向自己提问，并竭尽所能给出良好的解答。他们成了自己的橡胶小黄鸭。

英文原文 In the late 1980s, scientists wanted to understand what it is that differentiates the most successful students from their less successful peers. Even when students have access to exactly the same information, the same textbooks, the same passage of text on an exam, how much they learn can vary enormously. So what sets those best learners apart can't just be the information available outside their heads. It has to be something about what they do with that information. The scientists found that many of the most successful students had something in common. As they studied, they explained to themselves. They asked themselves questions, and they did their best to offer good replies. They were their own rubber ducks.

自那以后，大量研究发现，在各种背景和各个领域中，向自己解释都能对学习产生显著的影响。当儿童或成年人向自己解释时，与那些不解释的人相比，他们往往学得更好，并且能够将学到的知识应用得更广泛。这被称为自我解释效应，它是“通过思考来学习”的一种强大策略。

英文原文 Since then, lots of studies have found that explaining to yourself can have a dramatic impact on learning across a range of contexts and a range of domains. When children or adults explain to themselves, they tend to learn better and can apply what they learned more broadly than those who don't explain. This is known as the self-explanation effect, and it is a powerful strategy for learning by thinking.

让我从自己的研究中给你们举一个例子。这个例子展示了儿童如何通过解释来改变他们表征信息的方式。为了好玩，我们用鸭子来做说明。我想请你们想象一张印有两只鸭子的卡片，我们称之为参考卡。现在再想象两张新卡片：一张印有两只青蛙，另一张印有一只鸭子和一只狐狸。你必须选择这两张新卡片中的哪一张与参考卡归为一组。请花点时间思考一下你会选择哪一张。

英文原文 Let me give you an example from my own research. It's an example that shows how children can transform the way they represent information by explaining. Just for fun, we'll illustrate with ducks. I'd like you to imagine a card with two ducks on it. We'll call it the reference card. Now imagine two new cards. One has two frogs, the other has one duck and one fox. You have to choose which of the two new cards goes with the reference card. Take a moment to consider which you would choose.

包括我自己在内的大多数研究发现，在这种情况下，成年人通常会选择印有两只青蛙的卡片来与两只鸭子的卡片配对。这是因为他们不仅仅把卡片表征为“一只青蛙和一只青蛙”以及“一只鸭子和一只鸭子”，还将它们表征为“相同的物体与相同的物体”。当你以这种方式表征卡片时，自然就会把这两组具有相同配对特征的物体放在一起。

英文原文 Most research, including my own, finds that in a case like this one, adults will typically choose the card with two frogs to go with [the] card with two ducks. That's because they don't only represent the cards as having a frog and a frog and a duck and a duck, they also represent them as having the same objects and the same objects. When you represent the cards in this way, it's natural to pair the two sets of matching objects together.

那么小孩子呢？五六岁的儿童通常会选择印有一只鸭子和一只狐狸的卡片来与两只鸭子的卡片配对。这是因为他们将参考卡表征为“有一只鸭子和一只鸭子”，因此印有一只鸭子的卡片会比完全没有鸭子的卡片与它更相似。即便给孩子们展示了大量示例，明确说明在这项任务中正确的规则是成年人那种“同类与同类匹配”的模式，他们依然会这么做。

英文原文 So what about kids? Five- and six-year-old children will typically choose the card with one duck and one fox to go with a card with two ducks. That's because they represent the reference card as having a duck and a duck. So a card with one duck on it will be more similar to it than a card with no ducks at all will. Children do this even when they're shown plenty of examples to make it clear that in this task, the right answer is the adult pattern of matching same with same.

但在我们的一项研究中，通过引导五六岁的儿童进行解释，我们能够让他们像成年人一样思考。在看到两只青蛙的卡片与两只鸭子的卡片配对的相关示例后，我们引导其中一半的孩子解释为什么那些卡片会归在一起。我们没有给他们任何反馈，因此他们并没有获得任何新信息。然而，那些进行了自我解释的孩子，随后在对新的卡片组进行配对时，就像成年人一样，做到了“同类与同类匹配”。通过解释，他们改变了表征信息的方式，从而解决了问题。

英文原文 But in one of our studies, we were able to make five- and six-year-old children think like adults by prompting them to explain. After seeing examples equivalent to the card with two frogs being paired with a card with two ducks, we prompted half the children to explain why those cards went together. We didn't give them any feedback, so they didn't have any new information. Yet those kids who explained went on to pair new sets of cards just like adults, matching same with same. By explaining, they changed the way they represented the information to solve the task.

解释深度错觉与识别认知缺口

解释还有另一个非凡的特性，这也是我前面已经暗示过的：解释能为你随后的学习做好准备。我的朋友 Roxanna 有个年幼的儿子，有一天问她龙卷风是怎么形成的。她在大学里上过气候与自然灾害的课程，所以以为自己能解释得很清楚。她开始描述气压是如何存在差异的，接着大脑就一片空白了。是什么引发了它？她不知道。低气压究竟是如何形成漏斗云的？她说不上来。她告诉我，她在那一刻意识到自己其实什么都不知道。

英文原文 Explaining has another remarkable property. It's one I've hinted at already. Explaining sets you up to learn. My friend Roxanna has a young son who asked her one day how tornadoes start. She'd taken a class on climate and natural disasters in college, so she thought she could explain it pretty well. She started to describe how there's a difference in pressure, and then she drew a blank. What sets it off? She didn't know. How exactly does low pressure produce a funnel? She couldn't say. She told me that she realized she knew nothing.

我猜想我们所有人都曾有过类似的经历——试图去解释某件事，却在解释的过程中发现自己所理解的远比原先以为的要少得多。这种现象被称为解释深度错觉。而这种错觉揭示了另一个原因：为什么从内部、从我们已经掌握的信息开始学习是如此有价值。当我们开始转化和组合自己知道或自以为知道的内容来构建解释时，我们常常会暴露出盲区，从而明确自己缺失了哪些信息。这样，我们就知道了该问什么问题，以及如何将获得的答案与我们已有的认知连接起来。

英文原文 I suspect that all of us have had an experience like this one -- an experience of trying to explain something and realizing in the course of doing so that we understand much less than we thought we did. It's a phenomenon called the illusion of explanatory depth. And this illusion points to another reason why it's so valuable to start the process of learning from within, with the information we have already. When we start to transform and combine what we know or what we think we know, to craft an explanation, we often uncover gaps that point us to the information we're missing. So we know what questions to ask and how to connect the answers to what we know already.

通过尝试去解释，Roxanna 发现了一个缺口——一项她需要了解的知识：低气压到底是如何与龙卷风的漏斗云产生关联的。她知道了自己的“信息采购清单”上该写上什么。弄清楚我们缺少什么、该去寻找什么，可以成为应对信息过载的第一步。

英文原文 By trying to explain, Roxanna identified a gap - something she needed to know: how it is that low pressure relates to a tornado’s funnel. She knew what needed to go on her information shopping list. Knowing what we're missing, what to look for, can be a first step in navigating information overwhelm.

在AI时代守住思考的边界

这一点至关重要，因为在未来的年代里，这种过载只会愈演愈烈。用于获取和综合信息的工具将变得无处不在，而我们也会受到诱惑，试图先从大脑外部可获取的信息入手来解决问题。我们会倾向于依赖对搜索查询给出的 AI 生成回复，以及从聊天机器人那里获得的解释。这些都是不可思议的工具，但它们不应取代“通过思考来学习”。

英文原文 That's crucial because in the years ahead, that overwhelm will only become worse. Tools for getting and synthesizing information will be all the more pervasive, and we'll be tempted to solve problems by turning first to the information available outside our heads. We'll be tempted to rely on AI-generated responses to our search queries, and on the explanations we can get from chatbots. These are incredible tools, but they shouldn't replace learning by thinking.

如果我们不从自己已有的信息开始，我们就会把转化和组合这些信息的过程外包出去——而正是这个过程为我们指引了寻找正确信息的方向，并在获得新信息时帮助我们去理解其意义。我们就会变得不善于厘清自己脑海中真正懂得的内容与外部仅仅能够访问到的内容之间的界限，沦为理解错觉的牺牲品。

英文原文 If we don't start with the information we have already, we outsource the process of transforming and combining that information that sets us up to seek the right information and to make sense of that information when we get it. We become poor at policing the boundaries between what we know inside our heads and what we merely have access to outside them. We fall prey to illusions of understanding.

但现在你们知道了如何打破这些错觉：那就是去寻求解释。当你向自己解释时，你转化并组合了自己拥有的信息以回答新问题；当你向自己解释时，你更有可能识别出自己不知道的东西。因此，当需要从“通过思考来学习”转向“从新信息中学习”时，你就知道该去寻找什么了。

英文原文 But now you know how to break free from those illusions: by seeking explanations. When you explain to yourself, you transform and combine the information you have to answer new questions. When you explain to yourself, you're more likely to recognize what it is you don't know. And so you know what to look for when it's time to shift from learning by thinking to learning from new information.

遗憾的是，通过思考来学习并不会让学习变得轻松，但这能确保你的努力不被浪费。它为你提供了一条穿透信息过载走向深刻洞见的路径。而且，通过思考来学习并不能消除对新信息的需求——至少在很多情况下是如此。但它能帮你明确去哪里寻找，帮你搞清楚自己缺少什么，以及在获得新信息时该如何处理它们。

所以，要成为一个更优秀的学习者，不要先从你大脑之外的信息开始——先看看你的内心。花点时间向自己提问，向自己解释。做你自己的橡皮小黄鸭。

谢谢大家。 （掌声）

英文原文 Unfortunately, learning by thinking will not make learning easy. But it means your effort isn't wasted. It gives you a path through information overwhelm to insight. And learning by thinking won't eliminate the need for new information, at least in lots of cases. But it helps you know where to look. It helps you know what you're missing and what to do with new information when you get it. So to be a better learner, don’t start with the information outside your head -- look inside first. Take a moment to ask yourself questions, to explain to yourself. Be your own rubber duck. Thank you. (Applause)
