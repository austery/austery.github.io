死亡倒计时的决策力：用 10 年视角过滤人生噪音

Yun 的创业底色源于一次意外的生命冲击。在高中毕业后，他被诊断出患有甲状腺癌（Thyroid Cancer）。尽管这种癌症通常治愈率很高，但在 18 岁这个本该规划未来数十年的年纪，死亡的可能性迫使他重塑了世界观。他建立了一个核心决策框架：如果我 10 年后就会死去，我现在还会做这些事吗？

这个 10 年生命轴（10-year timeline）是一个极度高效的信息过滤器。它能瞬间消除那些由于追求资历、证书（如硕士或博士学位）或寻找借口而产生的愿景迷雾。Yun 认为，虽然利用 AI 自动化处理人类的机械繁琐工作（Grunt Work）很有意义，但更紧迫的问题是：如何利用 AI 赋能人性（Empower Humanity）。如果人类不能通过 AI 进化，随着 AI 变得越来越聪明，人类将失去目标。因此，他敦促年轻的创业者去挑战那些更大胆、更艰难的问题，通过行动获取超额回报。

英文原文

Right after I graduated from my high school, I went to a local hospital that said, "Oh, there's a 1 cm tumor in your thyroid." And the doctor confirmed that it's actually thyroid cancer. Hearing this unpredicted way really changed my mind of viewing things in the world. One of the core ways I make decisions right now or after that incident is if I were to die after 10 years, would I be doing the things that I'm doing right now? If you're putting 10 years of a timeline, you can eliminate all the other side tracks and excuses that was clouding your vision and the things you want to achieve in life. Obviously, helping AI to automate grunt work of humanity is meaningful. But at the same time, I don't think there's enough founders who are thinking to use AI to empower humanity. If we cannot empower humanities with AI, we lose purpose. There's no meaning as humanity to evolve as AI gets smarter and smarter than us. I urge all the smart and young founders to go after bolder and harder problems to solve. Act on it. Be bolder than others. Make bold bets. If you act on these bold opportunities, you'll be getting surprisingly bigger returns.

从街头卖背包到寻找“创始人与市场契合点”

Yun 的创始人基因在韩国的教育竞争压力下萌芽。为了在看重学历的社会中突围，他曾学习到凌晨 2 点，直到 14 岁时开始思考“学习究竟是为了什么”。受 Steve Jobs 和 Bill Gates 故事的启发，他渴望成为改变世界的创始人，尽管父亲认为他缺乏那种“天才基因”。

为了证明自己，他曾拿着 100 美元去韩国露天市场卖背包。在最初两小时一无所获后，他意识到不能单纯卖产品，而要卖故事（Storytelling）。他告诉潜在买家，他卖的不是背包而是“回忆”和“勇气”——每当顾客遇到挑战时，看到这个背包就会想起那个在寒冬街头努力的少年。这次经历让他明白，要创办一家长青的全球化公司，必须具备硬技术能力。进入伯克利（UC Berkeley）后，他在一年内经历了 8 次转型（Pivots）。他发现，即便遵循了所有 YC 的教条（如验证假设、构建 MVP、与用户交谈），如果缺乏创始人与市场契合点（Founder-Market Fit），也很难在长达 10 年的征程中保持热忱。

英文原文

I was born in a small island in South Korea. In Korea, which university you are graduating from matters a lot in civil society success. So when I look back in my elementary schools and in middle school years, I would study by myself until 2:00 a.m. to really keep up and really be competitive with my peers. But then when I was turning 14, it kind of hit me. What am I getting after studying hours and hours and being stressed? And I would just read books in different subjects to really try to find an answer with this very question. Why am I studying so hard? What is it for? That's the time when I first found out about founders, people like Steve Jobs, Bill Gates who would create something new making impact globally. And I was just fascinated by their stories. I want to be like them. I want to be like those legendary founders who would make great impacts in the world. And my dad's first reaction was founders like Steve Jobs and Bill Gates. They are geniuses and you don't have the DNA. Yun, you just have to study hard, get to a good university in Korea and then just become a lawyer. Looking back, I do agree in the claim that founders are special people. But at the same time, I didn't think that I'm unccapable of becoming a founder. So other than just obeying to his words were kind of coming down, I really thought if I make this $100 as an allowances that I have saved so far in my piggy bank to $200 and bring back to my dad, he would now believe that I'm worthy of the founder. So I went to a Korean street market and then try to sell backpacks and it was freezing cold and no one wanted to buy a backpack. So I remember I failed selling a single backpack for 2 hours. My hand was frozen like this because I was grabbing my back like this. So now I try to sell in different ways and using a different strategy where what is unique to myself. Oh, it's my story like why am I selling these packbacks in the first place? I was mainly targeting people who seemed like who would have a son like me. So I'll reach out to them and say the thing I'm selling to you right now isn't a backpack. It's a memory. Every time you're wearing this backpack, you'll think of like myself selling these backpacks in a freezing winter in the street market even though you are down or you have some challenges like you are going throughout the day you'll be thinking of those memory and then getting some courage. So I sold my first backpack with that story. But then one thing hit me once again where is this one I wanted to do as a founder? I wanted to found a company that is longlasting creating something durable and something new. That point on I decided if I want to be a founder I need to be technical. I need to learn the hard skills so that I can build the softwares and I can build a product so that I can sell because I really wanted to start a generational global company. Coming to Berkeley I founded my first company after a month I entered to the college. Prior to the current direction of founding Pensive, I was pivoting eight times throughout the whole year and going through a lot of these processes. There was something missing. The biggest thing that was missing was a founder market fit. Am I a founder who can actually pursue this idea for the next 10 years?

Pensive 的诞生：重塑高等教育的成本与效率

Yun 使用 Ikigai（生き甲斐：结合热爱、擅长、世界需要、能获报酬的框架）来定位自己的创业方向。在学习、能源和医疗三个备选领域中，他在教育领域（EdTech）拥有最深厚的积累，曾作为研究科学家在高速增长的初创公司工作。他发现伯克利等顶尖学府也面临挑战：一个 2000 人的班级里，助教（TAs）每天需要机械化地批改上千份作业，这种重复性的繁重工作是教育质量的痛点。

Pensive 平台由此诞生，旨在通过 AI 自动化处理这些机械化评分任务，将评分时间缩短 60% 至 90%，让教师能专注于办公时间、小组辅导和深度反馈。Yun 展现了极强的资源利用能力（Resourcefulness）：他曾仅凭一个 Figma 原型就获得了哥伦比亚大学教授的认可，并通过给全美各大学教授发冷邮件、亲自飞往校园见面的方式拿下了前 10 家合作院校。目前 Pensive 已获得 Mayfield Fund、Sequoia Capital 等机构 680 万美元的种子轮融资。

英文原文

I love a framework called Ikigai. Ikigai is a framework where when you're choosing something to do you have to consider four different things altogether: you should do a thing that you love, you should do a thing that you're good at, you should do a thing where the world needs and you should do a thing which you can paid for. Learning and education, energy and climate change and healthcare and curing cancer. Now the thing that I was good at, I thought I was particularly good at experience at learning and education. Prior to founding Pensive, I worked on a lot of machine learning models and language models and adopting them inside education at a company called Grid. Coming to Berkeley, the first class I took was called CS61A. It had 2,000 students. Professors obviously wouldn't even remember my name. Imagine yourself, you're an instructor. Now you are grading 1,000 students worth submissions in a single day. When you were over 500 submissions, you're just mechanically circling. I think that was my biggest pain point when I was a tutor at Berkeley. So that's really the point the pain point that Pensive wanted to eliminate. How can we remove these routine mechanical and repetitive grading tasks so that faculties and TAs can actually focus on giving more feedback. We quickly just designed the landing page in a Figma mockup. We didn't even deploy the landing page. And we show that mockup to the professor. He was saying actually the AI looks cool but AI is the thing that I need the most. Can I try AI first? And that was when we had this aha moment. One of my core strength and the things I think I did well in the past is being resourceful. I would send emails to UCLA faculties. If the full day is actually booked, I would actually fly in and then meet the instructors. That's how we got the first 10 colleges. Basically me flying around the entire nation, meeting these faculties in person and persuading them to use Pensive.

AI 时代的唯一性：在不确定性中积累“命运”

展望 2026 年，Pensive 的目标是覆盖全美数百所大学，并最终直接面向学生，成为一个 AI 原生教育机构（AI-native learning institution）。Yun 认为，新一代人在出生时就会发现 ChatGPT 比自己聪明。在这种背景下，人类最独特的技能将是在不确定环境中做出决策的能力（Ability to make good decisions in uncertain environments）。

他的人生座右铭是：“如果你知道自己的路径，每一个事件都感觉像是命中注定。” 这种“命运感”并非天定，而是源于在信息不完全的情况下，一次次有意识地选择向前迈进。这些无数次自主决策累积形成的独特路径，是任何 AI 都无法复制的。他鼓励大家保持胆识（Be bolder），因为大胆的尝试往往会带来意想不到的丰厚回报。

英文原文

Pensive's goal in 2026 is to be licensed over hundreds of universities across the nation. After that milestone, we want to go after direct to consumers and become an ideal AI native learning institutions. I think in the future AI native schools will be transferring more as a socialization environment whereas most of the components of getting some knowledge will be happen very intimately with 101 AI tutors. The new generation they'll be never have a single chance to be smarter than ChatGPT. That's why I'm founding Pensive. If we cannot empower humanities with AI, we lose purpose. The most unique skills that I think people should learn is an ability to make good decisions in uncertain environments. My personal life motto is that every event feels like a destiny if you know your path. As you practice making those conscious decisions in uncertain situations, you are consciously making a step forward to your own path. As these conscious choices accumulate that forms your unique path. No one including AI can replicate yourself and your path that you have walked on. So act on it. Be bolder than others. Make bold bets. If you act on these bold opportunities, you'll be getting surprisingly bigger returns.
