摘要:

文章探讨了“超政治”（hyperpolitics）这一核心概念，描述了当代政治活动在高度政治化与机构化程度脱节的时代特征。核心观点包括政治活动缺乏持久的后果、短视主义的盛行，以及大众政治素养的提升。文章通过历史对比，将当前政治形态定位为一种缺乏机构支撑的、狂热而短暂的混合体，并提出了超越线上极化的制度构建需求。

政治时代的质感与“超政治”的兴起

埃兹拉·克莱因：描述一个政治时代的大体形态、质感与节奏，是最困难的事情之一。我们总在谈论各种具体的现象：我们会谈论“觉醒文化”（wokeness），谈论“所有警察都是混蛋”（ACAB），谈论“觉醒派简直不可理喻”；我们也会谈论特朗普主义，谈论“我承蒙上帝拯救，必将让美国再次伟大”。但要真正触及这一切赖以发生的宏观总体形式，却是极其困难的。最近有一个逐渐流行开来、我也在周围频繁听到的概念，那就是“过度政治化”（hyper-politicization）。

“超政治”（Hyperpolitics）是安东·耶格尔（Anton Jäger）提出的一个核心概念。耶格尔是一位历史学家，也是《纽约时报》观点版的特约撰稿人。他同时还是《超政治：没有政治后果的极端政治化》（Hyper-Politics: Extreme Politicization Without Political Consequences）一书的作者。他在书中所描述的，正是这样一个政治无处不在的时代：政治观点与政治立场的狂热度和激烈程度异乎寻常地高涨，社会情绪从一端剧烈地摆动到另一端；但与此同时，这些政治诉求并未稳固地附着在政治机构和组织结构之上。因此，这些声势浩大、动员极度密集的时期，似乎并未产生任何持久的政治后果。我认为，这是一种非常深刻的视角，有助于我们洞察所有人切身感受到的政治现状背后的深层逻辑。现在他来到了我们的节目中。安东·耶格尔，欢迎来到本期节目。

狂热的节奏与短视的政治消费主义

就像在某一年，大家都在全身心投入“黑人的命也是命”运动和“觉醒文化”之中；转眼之间，舆论氛围似乎就完全倒向了特朗普；然后不知不觉间，情绪又再度反弹回来。似乎没有任何事情能够长久维系，但每一个当下发生的事物又显得极其至关重要。你在书中的行文风格非常深思熟虑、典雅克制，但我很想探讨这种狂热动荡的本质，而你在书中也确实对其有所描绘。

Original English

Anton Jäger: Yeah, so I think the volatility or the frenzy you're pointing at is a quintessential feature of the phenomenon I'm describing, because what do institutions do with political activity? They stabilize it and they give it longevity over a longer time period.

And if you don't have these institutions to tie your political action to, and also not, let's say, the mechanisms of delegation or representation that come with these institutions, I'd say also maybe discipline. That means that short-termism is the de facto mode of a lot of political action.

Because you can be enormously interested and invested in one question for a couple of weeks, you follow the media cycle, but then once, let's say, the popularity fades or it doesn't seem as urgent, you move on to the next thing. And there's no institution that's actually there to set the agenda to help you articulate a long-term vision that might unite your politics across all these short-term issues.

So the hyper has both a spatial and a temporal side to it. Spatial insofar as it can go everywhere and it can gather loads and loads of people. I mean, there are record numbers in terms of turnout, not just in the U.S., but across the West for protests in the last 10 to 15 years, but it's also hyper in that it doesn't cover a very long time span and almost like a political market, you can invest in a product. And then when you see that its value is plunging, you can dump your shares and you can move on to the next issue.

机构内爆与数字时代的政治过激

Guest: The second book is Willem Elsschot’s 1933 novella, Cheese (Kaas). It is a relatively concise and accessible story about an ordinary clerk who, burdened by growing family expenses, attempts to become a successful businessman by setting up a wholesale cheese enterprise. The comic tragedy is that he utterly detests cheese and never eats it, yet devotes his entire life to marketing it. It serves as a brilliant parable for 21st-century hyper-capitalism, where the compulsion to buy, brand, and sell has become an all-consuming obsession, entirely decoupled from any genuine care or regard for the actual commodities being traded.
