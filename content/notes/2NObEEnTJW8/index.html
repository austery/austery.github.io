From the New York Times, it's the headlines. Today's Thursday, December 4th. Here's what we're covering today.

儿童疫苗政策面临重大转变

我正在亚特兰大参加一个非常重要委员会的会议。这是一个疫苗委员会，负责为美国成人和儿童接种的所有疫苗提供建议，实质上是制定指导方针。本周，他们似乎即将做出一些重大改变。

I'm in Atlanta for the meeting of a very important committee. This is a vaccine committee that makes recommendations, essentially guidelines for all the shots that adults and children in America get. And this week it seems like they are about to make some very big changes.

我的同事阿波拉·曼达维尔（Apora Mandaville）正在报道CDC（Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: 美国疾病控制与预防中心）委员会今天的会议，该委员会似乎准备对一项几十年来一直推荐给美国所有新生儿的疫苗——乙型肝炎疫苗（Hepatitis B vaccine: 预防乙型肝炎的疫苗）——做出重大调整。这种疫苗可以预防一种高度传染性疾病，该疾病可能严重损害肝脏。自20世纪90年代初婴儿开始接种疫苗以来，儿童急性感染率下降了99%。

My colleague Apora Mandaville is covering the meeting of a CDC committee today that seems poised to make a major switch up involving a vaccine that's been recommended for all newborns in the US for decades: the hepatitis B vaccine. It protects against the highly contagious disease that can severely damage the liver. And after babies began getting vaccinated in the early 90s, acute infection rates for kids dropped by 99%.

然而，该委员会现在似乎很可能决定推迟接种此疫苗，甚至完全不再向儿童提供，因为卫生部长小罗伯特·F·肯尼迪（Robert F. Kennedy Jr.）对此疫苗的必要性提出了质疑。阿波拉表示，他们还将讨论修订其他常见儿童疫苗的使用建议。

View/Hide Original English

But the committee now seems likely to decide that vaccine should be delayed or no longer offered to children at all, as health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has cast doubt on whether it's necessary. AORVA says they're also going to discuss revising the use of other common childhood vaccines.

该委员会不制定法律或规则，但他们提出的指导方针非常重要，因为它们实质上决定了保险公司和政府保险项目是否会覆盖这些疫苗的费用。因此，他们的决定可能会影响人们支付这些疫苗的能力，甚至影响他们获得疫苗的途径。

View/Hide Original English

The committee doesn't make laws or rules, but the guidelines that they come up with are very important because they essentially determine whether insurance companies and government insurance programs will cover the shots. So, what they decide could affect people's ability to afford these shots or even have access to them.

更广泛地说，我采访的公共卫生专家担心，该委员会正在助长一些已经流传数十年的反疫苗论（Anti-vaccine tropes: 质疑疫苗安全性或有效性的观点）。该委员会成员均由小罗伯特·F·肯尼迪亲自挑选，他多年来对疫苗提出了许多毫无根据的主张，声称疫苗未经适当测试、对儿童大脑有毒，并会导致自闭症等疾病。

View/Hide Original English

More broadly, the public health experts that I talked to are concerned that this committee is really elevating some antivaccine tropes that have been circulating for decades. The committee members were all handpicked by Robert F. Kennedy Jr. who has made many unfounded claims about vaccines over the years that they were not tested properly, that they are toxic to children's brains, that they cause conditions like autism.

因此，除了该委员会可能做出的任何决定之外，仅仅是这样一个重要委员会以如此公开的形式进行这些讨论，就赋予了这些边缘理论以合法性，这使得公共卫生专家担心，此举将进一步侵蚀人们对疫苗的信心，并可能导致一些我们很久未见的疾病卷土重来。

View/Hide Original English

So beyond any decisions that this committee makes, just having these conversations in this very public format by this important committee gives these fringe theories legitimacy, and that makes public health experts worry that it will further erode confidence in vaccines and we'll start to see the return of some diseases that we have not seen in a very long time.

美国军事行动的伦理质疑

今天在国会山，两名美国军事指挥官预计将面临尖锐的质询，内容涉及美军在加勒比海一次致命船只袭击事件，以及这是否构成战争罪。数月以来，美国政府一直在打击其声称运载毒品的船只，迄今已造成80多人死亡。尽管许多武力使用专家和立法者批评这项整体行动是非法的，但今天的焦点将集中在一次具体的袭击上。

View/Hide Original English

Today on Capitol Hill, two US military commanders are expected to face sharp questions about one of the US's deadly boat strikes in the Caribbean and whether it was a war crime. For months, the administration has been targeting boats it claims are carrying drugs, killing over 80 people so far. While many use of force experts and lawmakers have criticized that overall campaign as illegal, today the focus will be on one strike in particular.

9月2日，海豹突击队第六分队（Seal Team 6: 美国海军特种作战部队）的队员对一艘载有11人的委内瑞拉船只发动了袭击。硝烟散去后，监控视频显示残骸中有两人仍然生还。据《纽约时报》采访的官员称，军方截获了幸存者的通讯，他们正在向涉嫌贩毒集团成员求助。官员表示，军方将这一求救电话解读为幸存者仍在抵抗。于是，负责此次行动的弗兰克·布拉德利海军上将（Admiral Frank Bradley）下令进行后续袭击，导致两人死亡。

View/Hide Original English

On September 2nd, Seal Team 6 operators launched a strike on a vessel from Venezuela carrying 11 people. After the smoke cleared, surveillance video showed two people still alive in the wreckage. According to officials the Times talked with, the military intercepted communications from the survivors who were radioing suspected cartel members for help. Officials say the military interpreted that distress call as meaning the survivors were still in the fight. And Admiral Frank Bradley, who was overseeing the operation, ordered a follow-up strike that killed both people.

立法者预计将仔细审查这次袭击的细节，因为五角大楼的**《战争法手册》（Law of War manual: 规定武装冲突中行为准则的法律文件）禁止向未积极抵抗的遇难者开火。此外，还有谁应为第二次袭击承担最终责任的问题：是下令的布拉德利，还是更广泛地批准了这次致命行动的赫格斯**（Hegsth）。国防部长赫格斯通过视频直播观看了任务，他声称自己“没有留下观看第二次袭击”，并否认是他下令的，但表示布拉德利做出了正确的决定。

View/Hide Original English

Lawmakers are expected to scrutinize these details of the attack since the Pentagon's Law of War manual prohibits firing on shipwrecked people who aren't still actively fighting. There's also the question of who bears the ultimate responsibility for the second strike. Bradley, who ordered it, or Hegsth, who signed off on the lethal operation more broadly. The defense secretary, who was watching the mission live on video, said he quote didn't stick around for the second strike and denied that he ordered it, but said Bradley made the right call.

五角大楼“信号门”丑闻

与此同时，经过长达八个月的调查，五角大楼定于今天发布关于**“信号门”丑闻**（Signalgate scandal: 指美国国防部长在不安全群聊中讨论军事行动的事件）的报告。《纽约时报》获悉，调查发现赫格斯在三月份讨论也门军事行动时，在一个意外包含了记者的群聊中，危及了美国军队的安全。调查人员还表示，并非所有信息都按照联邦法律得到了妥善保存，而且赫格斯在调查期间拒绝接受采访。

View/Hide Original English

Meanwhile, after an eight-month investigation, the Pentagon is set to release its report today on the Signalgate scandal. The Times has learned that the inquiry found that Hegsth risked endangering US troops back in March when he discussed military operations in Yemen in a group chat that accidentally included a journalist. Investigators also said that not all of the messages were properly preserved in compliance with federal law and that Hegsth refused to sit down for an interview during the inquiry.

特朗普政府削弱燃油效率标准

昨天在白宫，特朗普总统（President Trump）采取行动，大幅削弱了联邦燃油效率标准（Fuel efficiency standards: 衡量汽车燃油消耗效率的规定），废除了拜登时代一项鼓励汽车制造商销售更高效车辆和更多电动汽车的规定。这是本届政府旨在削弱应对气候变化政策、并加倍支持燃油车的最新举措。

View/Hide Original English

At the White House yesterday, President Trump moved to significantly weaken federal fuel efficiency standards, getting rid of a Biden era rule that had encouraged automakers to sell more efficient vehicles and more electric vehicles. It's the administration's latest move to gut policy aimed at addressing climate change and double down on gas powered cars.

白宫声称，新规定将通过降低制造成本为消费者省钱。经济学家告诉《纽约时报》，这在一定程度上可能实现，但代价是公共健康和环境。此外，特朗普自己的关税政策以及通货膨胀，也助推了许多汽车成本的上涨。

View/Hide Original English

The White House claims the new rules will save consumers money by lowering manufacturing costs. Economists told the Times that could happen to some extent, but it would come at the expense of public health and the environment. Additionally, Trump's own tariff policies along with inflation have helped drive up the cost of many cars.

当特朗普宣布新的降低标准时，他身旁站着各大汽车公司高管，他们公开庆祝这些变化。然而私下里，许多人对联邦政策的反复无常表示担忧。因为在拜登政府期间，他们已经投入了数十亿美元转向电动汽车。

View/Hide Original English

As Trump announced the new lowered standards, he was flanked by top auto executives who publicly celebrated the changes. In private though, many have expressed concerns about the whiplash of changing federal policies. As during the Biden administration, they already spent billions of dollars pivoting to electric vehicles.

AI聊天机器人与健康咨询

今年早些时候，一位26岁的女性收到血液检查结果，显示荷尔蒙失衡，她将完整的医疗报告粘贴到ChatGPT（OpenAI开发的大型语言模型）中，询问可能存在的问题。聊天机器人告诉她“很可能与肿瘤有关”。这个回答是错误的。

View/Hide Original English

Earlier this year, when a 26-year-old woman got some blood work results back showing hormone imbalances, she pasted her full medical report into chat GPT to ask what the problem could be. The chatbot told her it was quote most likely related to a tumor. That was wrong.

但当一位63岁的男性在锻炼时感到不适，向ChatGPT寻求建议时，他利用聊天机器人提供的信息说服医生仔细检查他的心脏，结果发现了一处严重堵塞。这种好坏参半的结果，是全球数百万开始使用聊天机器人寻求健康建议的人们所面临的现实。

View/Hide Original English

But when a 63-year-old man who'd been feeling some discomfort while exercising asked ChatGpt for advice, he used the info the chatbot gave him to persuade his doctor to take a closer look at his heart where they found a significant blockage. These kinds of mixed results are the reality for millions of people around the world who have started using chat bots for health advice.

考虑到聊天机器人在各种问题上都可能出错，专家们担心潜在的错误信息，例如人们在没有病的情况下以为自己快死了，或者在情况严重时却以为不严重。围绕这一趋势的另一个主要担忧是隐私问题。有些人正在将他们的完整病史上传到聊天机器人，其中可能包含大量敏感信息。而HIPAA（Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act: 美国联邦医疗信息可携性与责任法案）这项美国联邦健康隐私法，并不适用于运营这些聊天机器人的公司。

View/Hide Original English

Considering the bot's well-deserved reputation for getting all kinds of things wrong, experts are concerned about the potential for misinformation, people thinking they're dying when they're not, or thinking that something's not serious when it might be. The other big concern around this trend is privacy. Some people are uploading their whole medical histories to chat bots, which can include a ton of sensitive information. And HIPPA, the federal health privacy law in the US, doesn't apply to companies that operate the bots.

一位专门研究生物医学技术的教授告诉《纽约时报》：“你基本上放弃了你在医疗隐私方面的任何权利。”尽管如此，人们也指出了一些可能意想不到的好处。一位患有转移性阑尾癌的女性告诉《纽约时报》，ChatGPT能即时提供她的检测结果概述，尤其是在结果不好的时候，这很有帮助。她觉得这样可以在就诊前处理毁灭性的消息，然后更有效地利用与医生交流的时间。

View/Hide Original English

One professor who specializes in biomedical tech told the Times, "You're basically waving any rights that you have with respect to medical privacy. Still, there are some perhaps unexpected benefits that people point to." One woman who has metastatic appendix cancer told the Times it's been helpful to get ChatGpt's immediate overview of her test results, especially when they're bad. She felt like she could process the devastating news before her doctor's visit and then use her time with the doctor more effectively.

抢救音乐历史：磁带修复的挑战

最后，一场抢救音乐历史的竞赛正在进行中。许多艺术家的录音室录音最初都是用磁带（Magnetic tapes: 用于记录音频或视频信息的存储介质）录制的，而这些磁带现在正在衰变。《纽约时报》追踪了新泽西州的一位名叫凯利·普里布尔（Kelly Prible）的男子，他一直走在音频修复的最前沿，致力于抢救鲍勃·迪伦（Bob Dylan）和布鲁斯·斯普林斯汀（Bruce Springsteen）等标志性艺术家的独特录音。

View/Hide Original English

And finally, there is a race on to save music history. A lot of artists studio recordings were originally captured on magnetic tapes which are now decaying. And the times followed how one man in New Jersey, Kelly Prible, has been at the forefront of audio restoration, working to save unique recordings from icons like Bob Dylan and Bruce Springsteen.

所有模拟录音都会随着时间变得越来越脆弱，但最易受损的一些录音来自20世纪70年代和80年代，当时制造工艺的变化引入了一些问题，这些问题只是随着时间的推移才变得明显。普里布尔有自己的一套词汇来描述这些问题，从“润滑剂流失综合症”到“粘连综合症”，后者指的是磁带卷轴粘合在一起，变成一大块固体。

View/Hide Original English

All analog recordings get more and more fragile over time, but some of the most vulnerable recordings are from the 70s and 80s when changes in manufacturing introduced problems that have only become obvious over time. Pribble has his own glossery for how to describe those problems. Everything from loss of lubricant syndrome to adhesion syndrome where spools of tape get fused together into a huge solid chunk.

普里布尔一直在开创各种鲁布·戈德堡式（Rube Goldbergesque: 形容复杂、间接且不必要的装置或方法）的技术来抢救它们，例如使用通常在脱毛沙龙中找到的产品，或者在一个精心倾斜的自制吹风机架上烘干潮湿的磁带。国会图书馆（Library of Congress: 美国国家图书馆）的一位音频专家称普里布尔为“魔术师”。他从数百位艺术家那里抢救了磁带。

View/Hide Original English

And Pribble's been pioneering various Rube Goldbergesque techniques to save them using products that you'd usually find in a waxing salon, for example, or drying out wet tape on a handbuilt rack of carefully angled hair dryers. One audio expert at the Library of Congress said Pribble is known as the magician. He salvaged tapes from hundreds of artists.

但这项工作非常昂贵。一位唱片公司高管，其公司拥有大量旧录音的目录，表示处理和保存所有旧磁带将花费数百万美元。因此，这变成了一个难题：什么值得保存，什么不值得。

View/Hide Original English

But the work is expensive. One record executive whose company owns a huge catalog of old recordings said it would cost millions of dollars to process and preserve all of their old tapes. So, it becomes a difficult question of what is and isn't worth saving.
