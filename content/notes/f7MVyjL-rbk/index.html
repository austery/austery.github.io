主持人：在那种体制下，如果考砸了或者没有背景，人生轨迹就会被彻底固定。

Original English

对他而言，心里始终憋着一股不服输的劲头：“为什么别人能做到，而我不能去追求心中的理想？”直到今天，他依然保持着这种性格，我也正是因此深深敬佩着他。

来到美国后，对他来说，这里就是一块真正拥有自由的土地。我父亲不是那种只空谈口号的人，但在他看来，能够拥有各项公民权利、享有投票权、成为民主制度运转的一部分——这些绝非理所当然的琐碎事物。我经常思考，他是否曾经想过，自己的儿子有朝一日会决定竞选公职，去角逐代表人民的立法席位。

Abdul El-Sayed: Exactly. At the bottom of the list, people were simply drafted into the army under an authoritarian military dictatorship. That was the regime governing the country. So when the opportunity arose for him to go abroad to study, he said, "I'm going. I have to take this chance." For him, it came down to a fundamental belief: "Why can't I pursue what I believe in?" He still lives with that same spirit today, and I love him deeply for it.

America, to him, was a place defined by real, tangible freedom. My father wasn't someone who made grand rhetorical speeches, but having rights, participating in voting, and being an active part of a functioning democracy were things he never took lightly. I often wonder whether he ever imagined that his own son would choose to run for political office.

美国理想的真谛：从移民经历看公民社会的凝聚力

主持人：移民来到这个国家的人，往往对“美国梦”有着最深刻的体会。无论你来自哪里，无论你的肤色如何、口音怎样，你都能在这个国家成为真正意义上的美国人。

Original English

Host: You know, that realization that no matter where you come from, what color your skin is, or what accent you speak with, you can become an American—that is profoundly powerful.

阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德：是的，我一直极其郑重、无比严肃地看待这份身份认同。我至今清清楚楚地记得，当我父亲正式宣誓入籍成为美国公民的那一天。那时我还很年幼。我记得我的继母——她是个地道而淳朴的美国人——开玩笑地指着我说：“照这么说，布巴（Bubba）明天要是走到这里，是不是也能变成一个金发碧眼、操着正统美国本土口音的公民？”而当时只有几岁的我心里困惑地想：“这怎么可能呢？他明明还有着浓重的老家口音啊！”然而那一纸入籍证书下来，他就是法律意义上完整且平等的美国人了。

这让我从小就明白了一个道理：美国从来都不是由某种单一的长相、族裔或口音来定义的。美国是一整套神圣不可动摇的理想与信念的集合。它建立在一套共同的价值基石之上，只要你全身心投入其中，你就是这个国家不可分割的一分子。这也是我长久以来不断反思的核心问题。

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: Yes, and I've taken that responsibility with the utmost seriousness throughout my life. I still distinctly remember when my father became naturalized as an American citizen. I was very young at the time. I remember my stepmother playfully saying, "So does this mean Bubba is going to walk in here tomorrow with blonde hair, blue eyes, and no accent at all?" And as a kid, I thought, "How can that be? He still has his thick accent!" But just like that, he was naturalized. He was fully American.

That taught me early on that being an American isn't defined by what you look like or whether you speak with an accent. America is a set of ideals. It is a shared civic faith. That principle is something I have reflected on for many years. When you are fully committed to those ideals, you are entirely part of this community.

主持人：这一点非常关键。我自己的父亲来自巴西，我也亲眼见证了他宣誓入籍的全过程。那种情感冲击力是无与伦比的：从法律和公民权利的角度看，你就是一个百分之百的美国人，与其他任何人没有任何高低之分。

这正是“何谓美国人”的精髓所在。世界上很多国家依然依靠单一民族、特定种族或特定宗教来划定国族界限。比如在英国，即使几代人生活在那里，来自巴基斯坦等背景的家庭仍常被主流社会区分对待，人们会问：“你究竟是英国本土人，还是英籍巴基斯坦人？”但在美国最震撼人心的地方在于，我们不仅拥有宪法保障的公民身份，更有包容多元的宪政文化。

当然，现在依然有一部分人试图收紧对“美国人”的定义，试图把特定少数群体排斥在外，筑起一道排他的高墙。但我们必须坚信，美国之所以伟大，就在于我们能够把来自不同背景、拥有不同分歧的人群团结在一起，共同守护民主协商的过程，从而创造出一个远远大于各个组成部分总和的强大国家。

Original English

Host: And that hits home deeply. My own father was an immigrant from Brazil, and I vividly remember when he was naturalized. It moves you to your core: in every civic sense, you are an American, with no asterisk attached.

This speaks directly to what being an American means. In many countries around the world, national identity is still anchored in ethnic uniformity, tribal belonging, or religious homogeneity. Take Great Britain, for instance—people whose families emigrated from Pakistan might say they reside in the UK, but society often categorizes them separately from "indigenous" British citizens. What makes America truly exceptional is that our identity is not merely about having citizenship papers; it is fundamentally about our shared constitutional culture.

Yes, there are factions today who wish to shrink the definition of what it means to be an American, seeking to exclude various outside groups and enforce rigid cultural conformity. But our greatest strength has always been our grand democratic conversation: bringing disparate people together across differences, trusting the collective process, and building a union that is vastly greater than the sum of its individual parts.

阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德：你说得太对了。这确实很有意思——几十年过去了，我父亲至今仍然带着一口浓郁的埃及口音，但我看着他的眼睛，我看到的是一个对这个国家充满感激与信念的美国人。

我们年轻的时候常会开善意的玩笑。在节日聚会上，大家聚在一起，我父亲会笑着讲述当年在埃及生活的种种艰难，而我总在心里默默感叹：在埃及，你或许怀揣无数宏伟的构想与才华，却因为没有特权和出身而寸步难行；而在这片土地上，只要你付出汗水与智识，无论你的起点多么卑微，你都有机会去践行抱负、去追求公平。对我而言，这就是美国最神圣、最珍贵的核心价值。

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: This is so true. It's funny, all these years later, my father still carries that distinctive accent. But you look at him, you look into his eyes on patriotic holidays, and you realize how deeply he cherishes this country.

We used to laugh together as a family about the contrast. He would tell stories about growing up in Egypt, and I would think: back there, you could have all the brilliant ideas and talent in the world, yet find yourself completely shut out by rigid social barriers; but here in America, you are given the space to turn ideas into reality, to build a life based on your merits. To me, that promise is the very soul of what America represents.

穿梭于两个世界：童年视角下的阶层反差与公共卫生初心

主持人：当你还是个孩子的时候，你是否敏锐地察觉到了自己生活的世界与你在埃及的表亲、亲属们的生活环境之间的悬殊差距？那种巨大的现实反差对你的三观产生了怎样的冲击？

Original English

Host: When you were growing up, did you clearly see the stark contrasts between your everyday life in the United States and the lived experiences of your relatives and cousins back in Egypt? How did navigating those two worlds shape you?

阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德：是的，那段经历对我的人生产生了决定性的影响。我的父母当时非常希望我能够牢牢掌握阿拉伯语，深刻了解家族的文化根脉，因此在他们能够负担得起的时候，他们会让我回去探亲。我清晰地记得，在开罗的机场里，当我走出舱门时，迎接我的是一大群堂表亲和祖母。

我当时穿着典型的美式夏装，行李箱里塞满了带给亲戚们的各种礼物。在埃及的街头巷尾，我坐在亲戚颠簸的面包车里，看着窗外街道上为了生计奔波苦熬的人们。

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: It was profound. My parents were determined to make sure I learned their native language and understood where my family came from. So whenever they could manage, they arranged for me to visit relatives overseas. I distinctly remember arriving at the Cairo airport, being greeted by a massive crowd of aunts, uncles, cousins, and my grandmother.

I was there in typical American summer clothes, bringing over suitcases loaded with gifts and clothing for everyone. And as we drove through the bustling, dusty streets in crowded vehicles, looking at families struggling through daily hardship, the sheer contrast hit me immediately.

主持人：你当时大概多大年纪？

Original English

Host: Around how old were you when you first experienced that contrast?

阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德：我那时大概十三岁。十三岁，正好是一个少年开始审视世界、构建自我价值体系的关键时期。所有这一切都让我感受到了强烈的心理震撼。

在美国密歇根的学校里，作为一个少数族裔，你必须时刻向同学和老师解释自己的名字怎么发音、自己的肤色为何不同、家庭饮食为何不一样——你总是处于需要为自己的存在向外界解释的状态中。可是，当我踏上埃及的土地，身处与我流着相同血液的亲族之间时，我却看到了完全另一维度的现实：这里的物质匮乏、医疗短缺和生活艰辛是如此真切。人们常问：“你怎么能把不同世界的生活摆在天平两端去衡量？”

我在美国长大，享受着健全的现代公共设施、充足的教育资源与优渥的发展机会；而我那些同样聪明、同样勤奋的亲人们，却困在医疗匮乏和生存压力的泥潭里。每当我带去那些在美国随处可见的日常物品和药品时，亲人们眼中流露出的渴望与感激，甚至让我感到内疚与心碎。

这让我从小就真切体会到了生命的随机性与社会制度对普通人命运的深刻塑造。这并不是个人是否努力的问题，而是底层的制度安排、公共卫生资源与公平保障决定了成千上万人的生死与尊严。正是在面对这种强烈的对比时，我坚定了自己后来投身公共卫生、投身医学、并最终走上为弱势群体争取制度性公平与医疗权利的政治道路。

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: I was around thirteen years old. At thirteen, you're at that formative age where you begin processing how the world functions, and the contrast was stunning.

In Michigan, as a kid with an unfamiliar name and background, you spend so much energy navigating differences—explaining how to pronounce your name, fielding questions about your skin color, constantly feeling like you have to justify your identity. But then you arrive in Egypt, among family members who look like you and share your heritage, and suddenly you witness deep systemic hardship and material deprivation. How do you reconcile those realities?

Back home in the United States, I had access to stable infrastructure, top-tier schools, and boundless institutional support. Meanwhile, my cousins—who were every bit as smart and capable—were trapped under economic stagnation and a complete lack of basic social protections. When I would arrive with suitcases of ordinary household goods, playing the role of Santa Claus, and see how much those simple resources meant to them, it fundamentally altered my perspective.

It made me realize how much of human destiny comes down to the luck of where you happen to be born. It showed me that individual effort alone cannot compensate for broken, unfair systems. That realization is what drove me directly into medicine, into epidemiology and public health, and ultimately into the fight to reform our healthcare policies and political institutions so that every human being is afforded basic dignity and protection.

埃及祖母的对话与对“优绩主义”的反思

Abdul El-Sayed: 最后，我和祖母之间有了这样一段对话。我对她说：“好了，泰塔（Teta，阿拉伯语‘祖母’），我到底拥有什么？这里又有什么呢？”她看着我说：“你所拥有的，全都是最好的。”我当时说：“得了吧，怎么可能。”那是炎热的夏天，我的其余亲人全部都在这里。这让我久久不能平静。你知道，回头去审视她所度过的一生，即便抛开那些沉重的历史大环境不谈，仅仅是出生在这里这件事——正是这一点深深触动了我。

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: And at the end, I had this moment where I was like: "Okay, Teta, what do I have? What is there?" And she says: "Everything you have is the best." I said, "Come on." It was summer, and the rest of your family is right here. And it really got to me. You know, you look back and see the life she lived, even putting aside the historical circumstances, just to be born here. That really got me thinking.

Abdul El-Sayed: 这到底意味着什么？我不断回想起那段经历。你知道，面对周围所有其他的人，同时也通过对自己更深层的理解，去反思：在那里度过的那四个月，究竟带给了我什么？而最重要的是，让每个人都能真正理解这种处境。我竭尽全力去理解这一点。不管怎样，置身于截然不同的境遇之中，你是否对他人抱有那样的敬意？

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: What does that really mean? I remember how it happened, you know, toward everyone else, but also through self-understanding, asking myself: what did I experience after being there for those four months? And most of all, everyone understands. I tried so hard to understand that. In whatever different circumstances, do you have that respect for people?

Abdul El-Sayed: 随着岁月的流逝，当你深知世上有那么多人都在拼尽全力地工作，甚至比你更加专注、更加勤勉，去完成他们命中注定或被召唤去承担的责任，去往他们必须去的地方。那么紧接着第二个问题来了：你究竟为什么要做你现在所做的一切？你做这些只是因为别人夸赞你吗？因为你知道，很多上了年纪的人常说：“噢，你真聪明，你工作真努力。”

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: As time passes, when you know how many people are out there working, working even harder, striving with everything they have to fulfill what they are called to do and go where they need to go. And then second, why do you do what you do? Do you do it because, you know, older folks look at you and say, "Oh, you are smart, you are working hard"?

Abdul El-Sayed: 当时我给出的回答是：“我的祖母不仅极其聪明，而且一辈子都在辛勤操劳，付出了无比艰辛的劳动，但她却从未得到过我如今所拥有的任何一样东西。”那么，所谓的“能力本位”或优绩主义到底算什么？这种感觉究竟意味着什么？

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: And I responded: "My grandmother was smart, and she worked hard, grueling, back-breaking work every single day." And she never got any of these things that I have. So then, what is that feeling? What does that say about this concept of merit?

公共服务、制度责任与道德评判

Abdul El-Sayed: 我认为，当我投身于政府公共服务领域时，我常思考我们所肩负的责任。那不仅是说：“你看，承担责任意味着在任何资源的调配使用上，在谈判桌前，你必须始终保持清醒与笃定，为那些本身一无所有的人去据理力争。”在我看来，每个结果都在为某些人塑造着实质性的影响，不是吗？

Original English

Abdul El-Sayed: And I think that when I came into government service, I thought about your responsibilities, about saying: "Look, being responsible means that at the table, whenever resources are being used, you have to be confident and ensure access for the people who have none." In my view, results create something real for someone, right?
