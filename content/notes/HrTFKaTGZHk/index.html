地缘政治的博弈：美伊停火协议与全球经济影响

美国总统特朗普昨日发布了对伊朗的毁灭性威胁，但仅在十小时后便戏剧性地撤回，宣布与伊朗达成一项为期两周的停火协议。该协议旨在为最终的永久和平协议铺平道路。此次突破得益于巴基斯坦的紧急外交斡旋，该国敦促特朗普避免冲突，并促使伊朗接受了由巴基斯坦起草的提议。根据协议条款，伊朗将允许载有石油、天然气和其他货物的船只通过霍尔木兹海峡（Strait of Hormuz: 连接波斯湾与阿曼湾的关键水道，是全球石油运输的重要通道）。在此期间，双方将停止一切军事行动。然而，截至今日早晨，波斯湾地区仍有零星袭击报告，引发了对协议信息是否已传达到伊朗分散的军事力量的疑问。

以色列虽与美国一同对伊朗进行了打击，但也表示支持该协议。但以色列特别指出，停火不适用于黎巴嫩，以色列此前一直在黎巴嫩攻击伊朗支持的真主党（Hezbollah）民兵并试图夺取领土。特朗普本人称昨日是“世界和平的大日子”，他这种在发表极端威胁后寻求达成协议并宣称胜利的策略，被视为其惯用剧本。

《纽约时报》白宫记者David Sanger分析指出，若此次停火能永久持续且不发生改变，白宫将未能实现其大部分主要目标。尽管战争已对伊朗的领导层和军事力量造成了沉重打击，但伊朗与美国之间的主要紧张点依然存在。Sanger提到，伊朗仍由一个恐吓国内民众的军事和神权精英集团统治，并依然拥有其核材料、导弹和无人机。更重要的是，伊朗可能认为自己成功地对抗了美国和以色列这一更强大的侵略力量。David的详细报道将在今日的《The Daily》节目中播出。

媒体的深入调查揭示了特朗普最初决定发动战争的细节。Maggie Haberman和Jonathan Swan通过匿名采访，获得了关于特朗普下达作战命令之前，白宫内部闭门会议和讨论的内幕。他们发现了三个关键点。首先，2024年2月与以色列总理Benjamin Netanyahu的关键会晤。Netanyahu飞往华盛顿，在白宫总统办公室与特朗普及其团队会面，并向特朗普“推销”战争。他描绘了一个近乎确定的胜利计划，预计仅需几周，报复性袭击将微乎其微，霍尔木兹海峡将保持畅通。Netanyahu及其团队还表示，以色列情报机构MSAD将会在伊朗内部煽动起义，作为推翻该政权的最后一击。特朗普对此表示“听起来不错”。

接下来，美国分析师们被要求评估以色列提出的计划，他们紧急连夜进行了分析。报告指出，计划的部分内容是可行的，例如可以消灭伊朗最高领袖及其威胁邻国的能力。但其他部分，如煽动起义和彻底粉碎伊朗政权，被CIA局长形容为“荒谬”，或被国务卿 Marco Rubio称为“胡说八道”。最后，总统需要做出决定，但Haberman和Swan发现，特朗普基本上处于一个“回音室”效应中，缺乏足够的反对意见。特朗普在委内瑞拉的快速军事行动后士气高涨，认为伊朗也会如此。国防部长 Pete Hegth对此表示支持，认为“我们最终总是要对付伊朗人，不如现在就做”。参谋长联席会议主席 General Dan Kaine虽持续提出风险，但亦反复表示这不是他的职责去指导总统。在特朗普的核心圈子中，副总统 JD Vance是唯一一位强力反对战争的人。尽管如此，他最终还是告诉特朗普：“我认为这是个坏主意，但如果你想这么做，我会支持你。”次日，特朗普下达了最终命令。

战争对全球经济造成了巨大冲击。市场对昨日的停火协议反应积极，油价大幅下跌，投资者谨慎乐观地认为石油和天然气将重新开始通过霍尔木兹海峡流动。然而，油价和航空燃油成本不太可能很快恢复到战前水平，因为至少10%的世界石油供应已被切断。重新开放关闭的油井和修复波斯湾受损的炼油厂仍需时间。

From the New York Times, it's the headlines. I'm Tracy Mumford. Today's Wednesday, April 8th. Here's what we're covering.

At 8:06 a.m. yesterday morning, President Trump issued an apocalyptic threat to Iran, saying he was prepared to order widespread attacks against civilian infrastructure and that a whole civilization will die. Just over 10 hours later, he backed down from that threat, announcing on social media that the US and Iran have reached a two-week ceasefire deal. The idea is that negotiators will spend that time trying to finalize a permanent peace agreement.

The breakthrough came after a frantic diplomatic effort by Pakistan, which had been urging Trump not to carry out the devastating attacks and pushing Iran to accept the proposal, which Pakistan had drafted. Under the terms of the deal, Iran will let ships carrying oil, gas, and other goods through the Strait of Hormuz. During those two weeks, all fighting is supposed to stop. Though, as of this morning, there have been a number of strikes reported across the Persian Gulf, raising questions about whether news of the deal has reached all corners of Iran's decentralized military.

Israel, which has been carrying out strikes on Iran alongside the US, said it also supports the agreement, but explicitly noted that the ceasefire doesn't apply to Lebanon, where it has been attacking the Iranbacked Hezbollah militia and trying to seize territory. When it comes to Trump, who called yesterday "a big day for world peace," the whole head-spinning situation appears to be the latest example of a kind of playbook that he has come to rely on, of making increasingly drastic threats before securing some kind of deal that he can claim as a victory.

"If this ceasefire essentially becomes permanent with no change, then the White House will have accomplished virtually none of its major goals." Times White House correspondent David Sanger says that while the war has dealt crushing blows to Iran's leadership and military, the major points of tension between Iran and the US remain.

"The country is still run by a military and theocratic group of elites who have terrorized their own population. They still have their nuclear material. They still have missile supplies and drones. And most importantly, I think they still have a sense that they were able to stand up to the United States and Israel and resist against a much larger invading force."

David has more on the last-minute ceasefire on today's episode of The Daily.

Meanwhile, the Times has learned new details about President Trump's decision to go to war in the first place. My colleagues Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan conducted extensive interviews on the condition of anonymity to get the inside story of the closed-door meetings and internal White House discussions that led up to Trump's go order. They found three key things. First, there was a crucial meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in February. Netanyahu flew to Washington and sat down with Trump and his team in the Situation Room at the White House. And he pitched Trump on war. He laid out what he portrayed as a near certain plan for victory. The whole thing would take a couple weeks. Retaliation would be minimal and the Strait of Hormuz would stay open.

Netanyahu and his team said that MSAD, Israel's intelligence agency, would also spark an uprising inside Iran as a final blow to take down the regime. "Sounds good to me," Trump said. The next step was for US analysts to assess the plan Israel had presented, which they scrambled to do overnight. They reported back that parts of the pitch were achievable. They could kill the Supreme Leader and Iran's ability to threaten neighboring countries. But other parts, like sparking an uprising and crushing the regime entirely, were farcical, as the CIA director put it, or as Secretary of State Marco Rubio said, "It's bullshit."

Lastly, the president had to make a decision, and my colleagues found that he was basically operating in an echo chamber with not a lot of push back. Trump was riding high off the quick operation in Venezuela, thinking Iran would go the same. And Defense Secretary Pete Hegth was for it, saying, "We're going to have to take care of the Iranians eventually, so we might as well do it now." The chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, General Dan Kaine, persistently flagged risks, but also repeatedly said it wasn't his job to tell the president what to do. In Trump's inner circle, Vice President JD Vance was the only one to make a forceful case against the war. Though he ultimately told Trump, "you know, I think this is a bad idea, but if you want to do it, I'll support you." The next day, Trump gave the final order. You can find Maggie and Jonathan's full reporting in the Times app or at nytimes.com.

And one last update on the war, which has shaken the global economy. Markets surged in response to yesterday's ceasefire agreement and the price of oil dropped sharply as investors are cautiously optimistic that oil and gas will start moving through the Strait of Hormuz again. Still, gas prices and jet fuel costs are unlikely to return to pre-war levels anytime soon since at least 10% of the world's oil supply has been cut off. And it will take time to reopen shuttered wells and repair damaged refineries in the Persian Gulf.

AI 领域的‘大 Reckoning’：安全模型与潜在风险

在硅谷，各大科技公司纷纷推出最新、最强大的AI模型（大语言模型 (Large Language Model: 基于海量文本训练的AI系统)），宣称其性能比前代更为精准、强大且实用。昨日，Anthropic公司宣布推出一款新模型，但声称该模型过于强大，不适宜公开发布。原来，这款名为“Clawude Mythos Preview”的模型在发现软件安全漏洞方面表现极其出色。理论上，这有助于开发者识别和修补系统中的薄弱环节。然而，这也意味着它可能被用于识别和利用这些相同的弱点。

Anthropic公司表示，该模型已发现数千个bug和漏洞，涵盖了几乎所有主流操作系统和浏览器。因此，Anthropic决定仅将该模型提供给包括苹果 (Apple)、亚马逊 (Amazon)和微软 (Microsoft)在内的少数科技公司，以协助它们加固自身防御体系。尽管来自AI公司的强大模型的消息需要谨慎对待，但Anthropic认为这可能预示着整个科技行业的一次“Reckoning”（清算/审判）。参与该模型的研究人员已将其评估为一项“重大风险”。一家网络安全公司的负责人称：“想象一下，一群代理人 (agents) （受控的AI实体，能够自主执行任务）不断地、有条不紊地对您的技术基础设施进行弱点编目。”

In Silicon Valley, major tech companies have been rushing to release the latest and greatest AI models, promising each one is more accurate, more capable, more useful than the last. Yesterday, Anthropic announced it has a new one, but claimed that it's too powerful to be released to the public. It turns out the model, known as Clawude Mythos Preview, is really good at finding security vulnerabilities in software. In theory, that's great. It can help developers identify and patch weak points in their systems. But that means it could also be used to identify and exploit those same weak points.

Anthropic claims the model has already identified thousands of bugs and vulnerabilities, including in every major operating system and browser. For that reason, Anthropic says it's only giving the model to a limited group of tech companies, including Apple, Amazon, and Microsoft, so that they can shore up their defenses. Now, claims about a wildly powerful new AI model from an AI company should be taken with a grain of salt, but Anthropic is saying this could be a reckoning for the whole tech industry. And researchers who've been given access to the model have called it a significant risk. 开心果的崛起：从利基产品到全球之王

20年前，加州农民大胆押注开心果，种植了成千上万英亩的这种小绿色坚果。当时，开心果被视为一种利基产品，虽然早已存在，但在美国并非一项大生意。当时全球供应主要来自伊朗。起初，开心果的投资并未获得预期回报。一位开心果农场高管表示，开心果作为咸味零食有市场，但仅此而已，是“单轨马车”。对于剥壳后的开心果仁，他表示“简直没人要”。但随后，情况发生了转变。

“哦，我的天哪，这是我吃过的最好的迪拜巧克力。” 随着迪拜巧克力（一种内含开心果奶油的巧克力）在2023年左右爆红，开心果开始受到广泛关注。健康趋势也推崇开心果为富含蛋白质的食物。这些因素共同推动了全球开心果进口量猛增60%。如今，加州的开心果种植园面积是21世纪初的六倍。

与此同时，伊朗失去了其在开心果领域的主导地位，这甚至发生在战争开始之前，战争已经扰乱了航运。这一切使得美国成为世界上最大的生产商和出口商，加州更是成为了“开心果之王”。
