摘要:

在本期访谈中，币安创始人赵长鹏（CZ）深度回顾了他的传奇经历：从随父母移民加拿大的艰辛童年，到在东京和纽约磨练交易系统开发技术，再到抓住比特币机遇创立币安。CZ 首次详细披露了与美国司法部的博弈内幕、四个月的监狱生活细节，以及他卸任 CEO 后对教育项目 Giggle Academy 和 AI 代理驱动加密生态的未来愿景。

低延迟交易的技术挑战

Chamath: 在高频交易组织中，他们为了节省几毫秒的延迟，甚至会定制电路和物理光纤。在软件层面，你如何针对这些边界条件进行优化？

Original English

Chamath: In high frequency trading organization, they go to such a degree to basically optimize for efficiency and low latency all the way down to their own physical fiber infrastructure. How does that manifest in the software? How do you actually optimize for those boundary conditions in code?

CZ: 有几个层面。首先，你要写出高效的代码，消除所有数据库查询，将一切都放在内存中进行。你要减少任何额外的计算，简化风险检查。更高级的版本会使用 FPGA（现场可编程逻辑门阵列），这是一种直接在网卡上的芯片，这样数据就不必经过内存再回到处理器。十年前，那个往返过程大约是 100 微秒，通过 FPGA 可以减少到 20 微秒。

Original English

CZ: Well, there's quite a few levels, right? The first level you do is basically you write a software to be efficient and fast. You want to remove all the database lookups. So you want to do everything in memory and then you want to reduce any sort of additional computation. You want to simplify the risk checks. And then the more advanced versions you move on to like the FPGA which is a network chip card that's on the network card. So that you don't have to go all the way up to memory and come all the way back down to the processor. Back then that round trip is about 100 microseconds and by avoiding that you reduce down to 20 microseconds.

Chamath: 既然高频交易涉及这么多资金，为什么他们不尝试开发自己的定制芯片 (ASIC)？

Original English

Chamath: Why when so much money is on the line for high frequency trading, why did they never try their own custom silicon (ASIC)?

CZ: 我认为是因为算法变化太快了。设计自己的芯片虽然非常高效，但修改起来需要很长时间。FPGA 提供了两全其美的方案，尽管它的编程周期也比软件长十倍。

Original English

CZ: I think the algorithms change too often. So when you want to design your own chip, it is very efficient, but when you want to change it, it takes a long time. I think FPGA already offers kind of the best of both worlds.

The same partners went to do a different company, but they rented a very fancy office. That company only lasted a year. It means that previous success doesn't guarantee future success. In early 2001 I was looking for new jobs and then Bloomberg was hiring. This is right before 911. I kind of got an offer before 911.

Chamath: 911 之后你还是去了纽约？

Original English

Chamath: So after 911 you went to New York?

CZ: 是的，我在 2001 年 11 月去了纽约。当时的街道非常安静。我在彭博工作了四年，担任高级开发人员，负责期货交易系统。当时我还没有创业的念头，只是一个想体验世界、寻找出路的年轻人。我在两年内被提拔了三次，开始管理一个 60 到 80 人的团队。我不再写代码，变成了经理，那是“最糟糕的转型”。

Original English

CZ: I went to New York in November of 2001. I worked at Bloomberg for four years. I joined as a senior developer. I was put into a team called Tradebook Futures. Still no inkling of that entrepreneurial bug. I was just a young guy trying to get a job, experience different parts of the world. I got promoted three times in two years and then I was leading a team of 60 initially and then grew to about 80 people. That's when I became sort of a manager. I no longer wrote code. The worst transition.

Chamath: 然后你辞职去了中国？

Original English

Chamath: And then you quit and moved to China.

CZ: 是的，2005 年初。我在日本认识的朋友想在亚洲创办一家新的金融科技公司。他们认为上海是未来金融科技最热门的地方。我们六个人去了上海，我是唯一会说中文的，虽然当时我的中文已经很生疏了。我们的想法是把华尔街的交易技术带到中国。

Original English

CZ: Yes. Beginning of 2005. The same friends I made in Japan, they were talking about starting a new fintech company in Asia. They said Shanghai is most likely the most happening place for the future of fintech. So the six of us went there to do a new IT startup in Shanghai. Our idea was let's bring the Wall Street trading technology into China so we can service the brokers and exchanges in China.

Chamath: 你们当时知道怎么分配股权吗？

Original English

Chamath: Did you know to even ask the question like okay guys what's our equity split?

CZ: 并不是平分的。大老板占了大约 40%，剩下的五个人平分，我拿了大约 11%。我当时对股东权利、优先股和普通股一无所知。到了上海后，我开始和客户谈，才发现我们注册为外商独资企业 (WFOE)，而中国的券商和金融机构是不允许与外商独资企业合作的。

Original English

CZ: It wasn't one-sixth. The top guy had like 40% and the rest five of us split equally so I had about 11%. I didn't know any of the ins and outs about shareholder rights. I had no idea what common stock is versus preferred. Once we landed in China, I found out we are registered as a wholly foreigner owned enterprise (WFOE). The Chinese brokers and financial institutions are not allowed to work with a WFOE.

Chamath: 所以你们转型了？

Original English

Chamath: So you pivoted?

CZ: 是的，我们变成了“外包开发”。我们为各种公司做 IT 系统，甚至修打印机或做 SAP 实施。我们做了几年，活了下来。我们有很多汽车行业的客户，比如上海通用、上海大众。后来我们在香港开了办公室，开始与摩根士丹利、德意志银行合作。我在那家公司待了八年，直到 2013 年。

Original English

CZ: Yeah, the company pivoted towards doing any IT system for any company. Work for hire. We can be fixing printers or we can be writing SAP implementations. We did that for a few years and we actually made a living. We had a number of automotive clients, Shanghai General Motors, Shanghai Volkswagen. Three or four years in we started having offices in Hong Kong and then we were dealing with Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank. I left in 2013 after 8 years.

比特币的启示

Chamath: 在那八年里，你结婚生子了。到 2013 年你 36 岁时，你还是个拿薪水的初级合伙人，孩子上私立学校，生活很稳定。然后发生了什么？

Original English

Chamath: In the eight years you worked at that company, you got married and had kids. By 2013 you're 36, a salary guy, junior partner, kids in private school. So then what happens?

CZ: 我接触到了比特币。我的一位朋友告诉我一定要看看这个东西。我花了大约六个月才完全理解它。我读了白皮书，一遍又一遍地读。

Original English

CZ: I came across Bitcoin. One of my friends tells me, "Look, CZ, you got to look at this thing called Bitcoin." I look into it. It took me about roughly six months to fully understand it. I read the white paper and I read it again and read it again.

Chamath: 我在 2012 年写过一篇关于比特币的文章，说每个人都应该把净资产的 1% 投入比特币作为“混蛋保险”。比特币白皮书最吸引我的一点是，它是极少数我能从头读到尾，且写得极其优雅、非技术人员也能读懂的技术文档。

Original English

Chamath: I wrote this thing in 2012 about Bitcoin and I said everybody should put 1% of their net worth into Bitcoin as "schmuck insurance". The thing that captivated me was it was the only white paper that I read end to end where I thought this is one of the most elegantly written pieces of non-technical prose. You could literally give it to somebody non-technical and they would understand it.

CZ: 是的，它只有九页。写得短小精悍且优雅是非常困难的。向我推荐它的是曹大容 (Ron Cao)，他当时是光速创投在中国的董事总经理。我们在玩私人扑克局，他说：“CZ，你应该看看比特币。”第二天我和李启元 (Bobby Lee) 吃了午饭，他当时正要加入 BTCC 担任 CEO。Bobby 告诉我，把净资产的 10% 投进去，如果归零你只损失 10%，但如果涨 10 倍，你的净资产就翻倍了。

Original English

CZ: Yeah, and it's only nine pages. It's much harder to write it very short and elegantly. The friend that showed it to you was Ron Cao. He was a managing director of Lightspeed Ventures in China. We have a poker house game. Ron says, "CZ, you should look at this thing called Bitcoin." I had lunch with Bobby Lee the next day and Bobby said put 10% of your net worth into Bitcoin. He said there's a small chance you will go to zero then you lose 10%. There's a much higher chance you will go 10x and you'll double your net worth.

Chamath: 你当时觉得入场晚了吗？

Original English

Chamath: Did you feel late?

CZ: 到 2013 年底我完全信服时，比特币已经从 70 美元涨到了 1000 美元。我觉得太晚了。但在比特币行业，无论你什么时候进来，你总会觉得自己晚了。

Original English

CZ: By end of 2013 I started like I'm convinced now. But then Bitcoin went from $70 in mid 2013 to like $1,000 by end of 2013. I was like, I'm too late. Doesn't matter when you get into Bitcoin, you always feel late.

卖房梭哈与币安诞生

Chamath: 你当时还在那家公司工作，是利用业余时间研究这些吗？

Original English

Chamath: Were you still working at that company while you were doing all of this moonlighting?

CZ: 是的。我去拉斯维加斯参加了一个比特币会议，见到了 Vitalik、查理·李等所有人。当时媒体都在说比特币是毒枭用的，但在会议上我看到的是一群极客和非常好的人。回来后，我告诉合伙人我们要写一个比特币支付系统，但他们不感兴趣。对我来说，互联网我太年轻没赶上，比特币我不能再错过了。于是我决定辞职，卖掉了上海的房子，全部换成比特币。

Original English

CZ: Yes. I flew to Las Vegas for a Bitcoin conference. Met Vitalik, Charlie Lee, everyone. The media was like Bitcoin is only used by drug lords. But when you go to the conference it was like a bunch of geeks and they're very nice people. I told my partners we should do a Bitcoin payment system. For me, the internet I was too young to do too much with it, and there was this Bitcoin thing and I wasn't going to miss it. So I told my partners I'm going to quit. I sold my apartment in Shanghai and buy Bitcoin.

Chamath: 你卖了多少钱？

Original English

Chamath: How much did you sell it for?

CZ: 大约 90 万美元。我分批买入，第一批是在 800 美元买的，然后价格跌到了 600、400 美元，最后均价大约 600 美元。当时我还没有工作，但我只想在比特币行业找工作。几周后，我加入了 Blockchain.info 担任技术副总裁，后来又去了 OKCoin 担任 CTO。但在 OKCoin 待了八个月后，因为文化差异和一些我不认同的做法，我离开了。

Original English

CZ: Roughly $900,000. Every trench I get, I just buy. The first trench at $800 and then Bitcoin is dropping $600, $400. Kind of averaged to about $600. I was looking for a job in the Bitcoin industry. I joined Blockchain.info as VP of Engineering. Then I joined OKCoin as CTO. That didn't last long either because there were culture differences.

Chamath: 币安是怎么开始的？

Original English

Chamath: So how do we get from there to Binance?

CZ: 2015 年，我和几个同事想在东京做一个比特币交易所，但投资者认为我不懂日语，不会成功。他们建议我把技术卖给其他交易所。于是我们转型做“交易所即服务 (SaaS)”，为 30 多家交易所提供系统。直到 2017 年 3 月，中国政府关闭了我们大部分客户。我们必须再次转型。2017 年 6 月，我决定做自己的币币交易所，并进行 ICO。

Original English

CZ: In 2015, I with a couple of my old colleagues said we're going to do a Bitcoin exchange in Tokyo. But investors said you won't be successful because you don't speak Japanese. They said why don't you sell the technology to other exchanges. So we pivoted to an exchange systems provider. In March 2017 the Chinese government shut down most of our clients. By end of May we said why don't we do our own crypto-to-crypto only trading. In June 2017 I said we're going to do an ICO.

Chamath: 币安的 ICO 筹了多少钱？

Original English

Chamath: How much did you raise?

CZ: 筹了 1500 万美元。我们发行了 BNB 代币，承诺持有者可以享受 50% 的手续费折扣。2017 年 7 月平台上线，但 9 月中国就发布了禁令。我们决定全员搬迁到东京。当时团队只有 30 人，几乎都没成家，大家收拾行李就走了。

Original English

CZ: Raised $15 million. We launched the token BNB. September 4th, Chinese government issued a memo saying crypto exchanges are no longer allowed. I said let's move to Tokyo. We had like 30 people then. Everybody just packed up and moved.

CZ: The product was growing pretty well. Placing an order on Binance is so much faster than on competing platform. By that New Year's Eve 2017 we were number one exchange already. CZ: 钱很重要，但不是一切。我 40 多岁了，性格很稳，不会去买兰博基尼或开疯狂派对。我现在的房子还是三四手的旧房子，客厅每个月都会漏水，但它大小合适，功能齐全。我是一个功能驱动的人，只要能解决问题，我不关心它是否奢华。

认罪、监狱与未来

Chamath: 你最终同意了认罪协议。

Original English

Chamath: You eventually agreed to the final terms.

CZ: 是的，我承认了一项违反《银行保密法》的指控，即未能注册为金融服务公司。这在历史上从未有人因此入狱。政府还想增加两项指控，说我个人协助了非法交易，但法院驳回了，因为他们没有任何证据。

Original English

CZ: Yeah, I validated a single charge of a banking secrecy violation, which is a registration failure. No one went to jail for this in history. The government want to add two additional charges, they call enhancements. The court outright rejected those because they couldn't provide any evidence.

Chamath: 在监狱里的生活是怎样的？

Original English

Chamath: What was it like in prison?

CZ: 那是一个非常糟糕的经历。监狱里按种族分群，这其实避免了很多冲突。我所在的群组只有六个人，包括亚洲人和太平洋岛民。我被送进了一个低安全级别的监狱，那里大多是毒贩。我学会了如何保持低调，不要随便接受别人的好意。出狱后，我做的第一件事就是洗个痛快的澡，吃一盘新鲜水果。在监狱里，我几个月没见过完整的水果。

Original English

CZ: It's a really bad experience overall. They organize prisons by race. This actually does avoid a lot of conflicts. I was sent to a low security prison where all the drug laws are. The first thing I did when I got out was a good shower and a good meal. I haven't seen a whole fruit for like months.

Chamath: 你现在不能再经营币安了，这让你难过吗？

Original English

Chamath: You couldn't run Binance per se. Did that make you sad?

CZ: 卸任时我确实哭了，那是我人生中极少数流泪的时刻。但现在我挺开心的，我有更多自由时间。我现在专注于 Giggle Academy，这是一个免费的数字化教育平台，旨在为全球 12 亿无法接受教育的人提供服务。我不打算在这个项目里发币，我希望它是一个纯粹的教育平台。

Original English

CZ: Stepping down was really really hard. I actually cried. But after a while I was like I'm actually quite happy not to run Binance. I have a lot more free time. I do Giggle Academy, this free education platform. I will for a very long time resist issue a token on the Giggle Academy. I want it to be a real free education platform instead of a token platform.

Chamath: 你对未来的 AI 代理和加密货币有什么看法？

Original English

CZ: Soon each of us going to have like hundreds or thousands of agents working for us in the background and they will be transacting. They're not going to use banks. Banks won't onboard and AML KYC an agent. Agents will transact at a transaction volume and at a rate that'll make the traditional network's head spin.

CZ: 我只是个普通人，并不超级聪明。成功需要价值观、情商和大量的运气。你无法改变环境，只能改变自己。每天逼自己进步一点点，坚持 30 年，如果你运气够好，你就会成功。金钱只是生活的一个维度，一旦够用了，更多的钱并不会让你更快乐。健康、家庭和对社会的积极影响才是最重要的。

Original English

CZ: I'm a normal dude. I don't think I'm super smart. There's a lot of luck as well. You usually cannot change the situation, so what you can only do is change yourself. If you just push yourself a little bit every day for like 30 years and you get lucky, you will be successful. Money is only one tangent. Once you have enough, more doesn't make you happy. Health, family, and positive impact are extremely important.
