超级智能时间线的重大更新

如果你在今年早些时候问我距离超级智能还有多远，我大概会说至少还要大半个十年。但过去三个月里，我的观点发生了翻天覆地的变化。无论在播客中还是私下里，我一直在与人们辩论这个问题；当然，我也一直在密切关注 AI 在可验证领域的进展，并思考这对 AI 研究本身意味着什么。

英文原文 >> If you had asked me earlier this year how far away  we are from superintelligence, I'd probably have   said at least the better part of a decade. But over the last three months,   I've updated my views a ton. I've been debating people both   on and off the podcast about this question, and  of course, I've been watching the progress of   AI on verifiable domains and thinking about  what that implies for AI research itself. 

我现在认为，我们距离出现与最优秀人类水平相当的 AI 研究智能体只有两三年，甚至可能只有几个月的时间了。我也逐渐相信，AI 研发的自动化将在一年内压缩如此巨大的进展，以至于在此之后不久——大约在 2030 年之前，甚至可能最早在明年——我们就会拥有在各个领域至少能比肩最优秀人类专家的 AI。我知道这是一个极其惊人的断言，甚至在几个月前我自己都不会相信。让我来为你梳理一下我的思维转变过程。

英文原文 >> And I've come to the view that we're only  a couple of years away, and possibly only   a couple of months away, from AIs that are  as good as the best humans at AI research.  And I've also come to believe that automating  AI research will compress so much progress into   a single year that soon thereafter, so by  2030, and plausibly as early as next year,   we'll have AIs that are at least as good  as the best human experts in every field.  I know this is a wild claim, and it's one that I  myself didn't believe even a couple months ago.  Let me walk you through how  my thinking has changed. 

前沿研发中的 AI 加速现状

在我们深入探讨关于未来走势的推测性论点之前，我想先指出：在前沿阵地上，AI 已经在极大地加速进一步的 AI 进展。例如，独立评估组织 METR 曾在 Anthropic 进行了为期三个月的实地调研，随后得出结论：Anthropic 在目标明确任务上的 AI 进展速度是一年前的两倍，并且“当前这种加速的大部分可能归因于 Anthropic 广泛使用 AI 进行研发”。

英文原文 >> Before we get into the more speculative arguments  about what will happen in the future, I want   to point out that AI is already dramatically  speeding up further AI progress at the frontier.  For example, METR, which is an independent  evaluation organization, spent three months   embedded inside of Anthropic, and afterwards  concluded that Anthropic's rate of AI progress   on well-scoped tasks is 2x what it was a year ago,  and that a, quote, majority of this acceleration   today is likely attributable to Anthropic's  extensive use of AI to do R&D, end quote. 

OpenAI 在九月份发表了一篇相关的博客文章，展示出在 OpenAI 研究人员中，使用 AI 程度最高的前 10% 用户，其内部编程智能体的使用成本从夏初的大约每天 1,000 美元激增至八月底的每天 7,000 美元以上。同样在九月，Anthropic 也发布了一篇关于自身 AI 提效的类似文章。他们报告称，Claude 现在主导了 Anthropic 26% 的 AI 研发工作，而在今年二月时这一比例还不足 1%。

英文原文 >> OpenAI published a blog post about this in  September, where they showed that the heaviest   10% of AI users amongst OpenAI's researchers  went from using about $1,000 a day of internal   coding agents at the beginning of the summer  to over $7,000 a day by the end of August.  And also in September, Anthropic put  out a similar post on its own AI uplift.  They reported that Claude now leads  26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work,   up from under 1% as recently as February.

数学突破及其对机器学习研究的启示

在思考让 AI 自动化 AI 研究会产生多大的影响、以及这会如何加快 AI 进展时，对我而言一个非常重要的认知更新，就是亲眼目睹了 AI 模型在数学领域能力的爆发式增长。现在的 AI 解决目标明确的数学问题，就像热刀切黄油一样轻松自如。仅仅两年前，我还曾问过 Dario，为什么大语言模型无法利用它们跨多个领域的庞大知识库把不同想法联结起来，从而做出新的发现。

英文原文 >> In thinking about how big a deal it would  be for AIs to automate AI research and  how much that would speed up AI progress,   a really important update for me has just been   seeing how fast the capabilities of AI  models in mathematics have exploded.  Current AIs are slicing through well-scoped  problems in math like a hot knife through butter.  It was only two years ago that I asked  Dario why LLMs aren't able to use their   vast knowledge across so many different  fields in order to connect together different   ideas and make new discoveries.

看吧，现实已经来到了眼前。在数学这样一个完全可验证的领域，AI 进行搜索和匹配式发现的规模如此之大，以至于世界顶尖数学家和计算机科学家苦思冥想数十年试图解决的数百个重要成果，现在被当成烘托未来模型发布的支线任务源源不断地产出。这一进展发生的速度实在惊人。就在 2023 年，这些模型还无法稳定地进行四位数加法。2024 年，它们在高中数学竞赛题面前几乎束手无策。到了 2025 年，它们已经能在国际数学奥林匹克竞赛中摘得金牌。再到今年早些时候，它们终于开始攻克一些小众的开放式未解问题；而就在一个月前，OpenAI 用 88 小时解决了一个千禧年大奖难题。

英文原文 >> And lo and behold, here we are.  In a wholly verifiable domain like mathematics,  AIs are doing the search-and-match discovery to   such a great extent that hundreds of important  results, which the top mathematicians and computer   scientists in the world had spent decades  trying to resolve, are being churned out   as side quests to hype up future model releases. It's crazy how fast this progress has happened.  As recently as 2023, these models  couldn't reliably add four-digit numbers.  In 2024, they could barely solve any  problems in a high school math competition.  In 2025, they could win a gold medal  at the International Math Olympiad.  To earlier this year, where they finally  started to solve some niche open problems,   to a month ago, where OpenAI solved a  Millennium Prize problem in 88 hours. 

这让我想起小说《伊甸之东》里的一句话：“诸神的陨落唯有一点可以笃定。他们绝非缓缓跌落，而是轰然崩塌、粉身碎骨。”诚然，目前的进展极大地偏向于数学中重在验证的部分，比如寻找反例，而不是提出全新的理论。Toby Ord 最近就此写了一篇非常出色的文章，他写道：“虽然当前的 AI 系统正开始实现数学证明的自动化，但目前极少有证据表明它们能够提出有力的问题，或者开辟深刻的全新数学探索领域。”然而，同一句话换一种方式表达其实就是：当前的 AI 系统在不具备提出有力问题或开辟深刻新探索领域的能力下，依然解决了一个千禧年大奖难题。

英文原文 >> I'm reminded of a line from the novel East  of Eden, quote, "And there is one sure   thing about the fall of gods. They do not fall a little.  They crash and shatter." End quote. Now, it's  true that progress has skewed disproportionately   towards the verification-laden parts of  mathematics, like finding counterexamples,   rather than, say, coming up with new theory. Toby Ord recently wrote a great essay about this.  He writes, quote, "While current AI systems  are starting to automate mathematical proof,   there is currently very little evidence  that they can ask powerful questions or   find profound new fields of mathematical enquiry." End quote. But another way to say that same exact   sentence would be this: Current AI systems have  solved a Millennium Prize problem, all without   the ability to ask powerful questions or find  profound new fields of mathematical inquiry. 

这种解决界定清晰问题的能力，对机器学习研究的影响将远远大于对广义数学的影响。因为数学研究的目标不仅在于证明或推翻长串的猜想，而是在于拓展人类对数学概念的理解。提出拓扑学或群论的思想，甚至更早的笛卡尔坐标系，其意义远胜于准确知晓从 n 个点中能构成多少对距离为 1 的点对。我们之所以在意这些深奥问题，主要是因为它们可能催生新的数学理论。

英文原文 >> And this ability to solve well-scoped  problems is going to be an even bigger   deal for machine learning research than it  has been for mathematics broadly construed.  That's because the goal of mathematics research  is not just to prove or disprove long lists of   conjectures, but rather to extend the human  understanding of mathematical concepts.  Coming up with the idea of topology or  group theory, or going back even further,   the Cartesian grid, matters far more than  knowing exactly how many one-unit-apart   pairs you can make from n dots. We mainly care about such abstruse   questions insofar as they might  elicit new mathematical theory. 

但在 AI 研究中，优先级完全颠倒了过来。AI 实验室最关心的，是任何新的算法配方在经验上能否让训练更高效，或者诱导出更聪明的模型。他们并不在乎为了理论本身的纯理论研究，而且过去十五年来我们在深度学习领域取得的所有进展，本来也没有需要多少新理论。那种仅仅去硬啃各类随机猜想的做法，在数学研究里或许价值有限，但在机器学习中却具有无比巨大的价值。

英文原文 >> But in AI research, the  priorities are exactly reversed.  AI labs mainly care about how any new  algorithmic recipe empirically makes   training more efficient or elicits smarter models. They don't care about theory for its own sake,   and all the progress that we've made in  deep learning over the last decade and a   half has not required much new theory anyways. The equivalent of just grinding through random   conjectures would be enormously  valuable in machine learning in   a way that it just isn't for mathematics.

实际上，AI 研究与当前 AI 格外擅长的这类数学进展惊人地相似，那就是：凭借难以置信的执着，加上联结众多邻近领域想法的超人类能力，收割海量的低垂果实。

英文原文 >> AI research is actually surprisingly similar  to the kind of mathematical progress which  current AIs are disproportionately good at,   which is unlocking a ton of low-hanging fruit by  applying a combination of incredible persistence   and a superhuman capacity to connect  ideas from lots of adjacent domains. 

从 Transformer 的发明看拼图与实验能力

例如，我们来谈谈 Transformer 架构。正是这项重大突破开启了现代大语言模型的时代。我的朋友 Sholto Douglas 本身就是一名 AI 研究员，他有一篇常年半成品的博客文章，解释了人们本可以在实际发明 Transformer 的两年前就把它创造出来。或许这个视频能促使他别再拖延，把文章写完。不过眼下，我先简要总结一下我和他探讨过的文章内容。

英文原文 >> For example, let's talk about  the transformer architecture.  This is the big breakthrough  that enabled modern LLMs.  My friend Sholto Douglas, who is an AI  researcher himself, has this perennially   half-finished blog post where he explains  how you could have invented the transformer   two years earlier than people actually did. And maybe this video will finally get him   to quit stalling and complete his essay. But in the meantime, I'll summarize a bit   of the discussion I had with him  about the contents of his essay. 

在 2017 年 Transformer 论文发表之前，许多研究人员就已经确定将注意力机制作为更好地并行化训练并在更长序列间传递信息的方式。当时活跃的研究员 Chris Olah 曾说过：“在当时，我的感觉是 Transformer 架构是许多想法的自然融合，比如注意力机制和残差网络，它们原本各自都很有效，只是这次被推向了相当激进的极致。”换句话说，对于活跃在该领域的研究者而言，核心模块没有一个是出乎意料的。

英文原文 >> Before the transformer paper was published  in 2017, many researchers had already   identified attention as a way to  better parallelize training and   communicate information across longer sequences. Chris Olah, who was an active researcher at the   time, has said, quote, "At that time, my sense is  that the transformer architecture was a natural   combination of many ideas like attention and  residual networks, which had independently been   effective, taken to quite aggressive lengths." End quote. In other words, none of the   core pieces were a surprise to  researchers active in the field. 

那么人们当时为什么没能早点发明出 Transformer 呢？原因之一在于，只有当你把半打额外的调整与微创新拼合在一起时，这种新架构才明显优于当时的最佳技术（SOTA）。尽管这些零散机制在文献中几乎都已经公开了数年，但没有人知道如何恰到好处地将这些拼图组合起来。要做到这一点，当然需要出色的研究品味，但它同样需要做海量细微的实验。

英文原文 >> So then why didn't people  invent the transformer sooner?  One reason was that this new architecture  was only obviously better than the state of   the art at the time once you connected together  half a dozen additional tweaks and innovations.  And though almost all of them had been  known in the literature for years,   no one knew how to combine these puzzle  pieces together in just the right way.  Doing so required great research  taste, of course, but it also required   tons and tons of small experiments.

而我上面所描述的——持之以恒且智能地在数十种不同想法组合中进行匹配——正是 AI 在数学贡献中所展现出的那种超常技能。总而言之，即便像 Transformer 这样自 2012 年 AlexNet 开启深度学习革命以来可称机器学习领域最大突破的成果，如果当年 Chris Olah 让 Claude 7 竭尽全力去把注意力机制做得更好用，它也是能被 Claude 7 发明出来的。

英文原文 >> But what I've just described above,  which is persistently and intelligently matching  dozens of different combinations of ideas,   is exactly the kind of capability that AIs  have shown themselves disproportionately   skilled at in their contributions in mathematics. This is all to say that even the transformer,   which is arguably the biggest breakthrough  in ML since AlexNet kicked off the deep   learning revolution in 2012, is the kind of  thing that Claude 7 could have invented if   Chris Olah asked it to try really hard to  get this attention thing to work better. 

时间预测：2028 年中位数与起飞概率

当然，我想澄清的是，AI 研究的某些部分在可验证性上确实不如数学。这就是为什么 OpenAI 即使手握显然能自主解决千禧年难题的模型，也并没有解雇所有的研究人员。但我认为，AI 研究的可验证性已经足够高，AI 很快就能在其中达到顶尖人类的水平。解释其中的原因需要深入探讨 AI 研究实际运作的技术细节；因为这些细节虽有趣但偏技术性，我把它们留到了结论之后。所以如果你感到好奇并想了解，可以一直看到视频结尾。

英文原文 >> Now, I wanna clarify that some parts of AI  research are, in fact, less verifiable than math.  That's why OpenAI hasn't  fired all of its researchers,   despite having a model that can apparently  autonomously solve Millennium Prize problems.  But I think that AI research is  verifiable enough that AIs will   soon be as good at it as the best humans. Now, explaining why requires getting into   the nitty-gritty of how AI research  actually works, and because those   details are interesting but technical,  I've saved them for after the conclusion.  So if you're curious and you  wanna hear them, you should just   keep watching until the end of the video.

不过现在，我先给出关于何时能出现此类 AI 的估计。如果要我选出自动化研究员在进行 AI 研究方面比肩最顶尖人类 AI 研究员的中位数年份，我猜是 2028 年——前提是各大 AI 实验室全速前进，没有自我有意放慢脚步，也没有受到政府的阻碍。

英文原文 >> But in the meantime, I'm gonna give you my  estimate of when we're gonna have such AIs. If I had to pick my median year for when   automated researchers will be as good as the very  best human AI researchers at doing AI research,   I'd guess 2028, and that's assuming that  the AI labs go full steam ahead and don't   slow themselves down intentionally or  aren't slowed down by a government. 

为什么我认为 AI 能在两年内达到这种水平？因为在每一个我们能够核验答案并让 AI 进行成千上万轮练习的领域——例如编程竞赛或界定清晰的未解数学题——它们都在短短几年内从弱于大多数人类飞跃为超越最顶尖人类。正如我将在结论之后论证的那样，AI 研究恰好具备完全相同的可验证属性。

英文原文 >> Why do I think that AIs could  get this good within two years?  Well, in every domain where we can check answers  and give AIs thousands of rounds of practice,   for example, competitive programming  contests or well-scoped open math problems,   they've gone from worse than most humans to better  than the best humans within just a few years.  And as I'll argue after the  conclusion, AI research has   exactly the same verifiable properties.

当然，顶尖人类 AI 研究员之所以能够表现优异，很大一部分能力是无法被直接验证的，例如他们提前数年押注正确研究方向的能力。但正如我们在数学领域已经看到的那样，模型所能泛化的范围远远超出了它们被直接训练的内容。OpenAI 的模型显然不是靠解决其他千禧年大奖难题训练出来的；它是靠训练大量野心较小、更易下手的数学题，然后从这些题目一路泛化，最终攻克了此前从未有人类解决过的难题。我认为在 AI 研究中也是同理：在可验证的研究任务上进行成千上万轮练习，加上新一代模型普遍比上一代更聪明的事实，两相结合将使模型即便在无法直接验证的 AI 研究环节中也变得出类拔萃。

英文原文 >> Of course, a big part of what makes the  top human AI researchers really good at  their job can't be directly verified,   for example, their ability to bet years in  advance on the right research direction.  But as we've already seen in  mathematics, models generalize   far beyond what they were directly trained on. OpenAI's model was obviously not trained by   solving other Millennium Prize problems. It was trained on lots of less ambitious,   more tractable math problems, and from those,  it generalized all the way to solving a   problem which no human had ever cracked before. I'd expect the same in AI research, where the   combination of thousands of rounds of practice on  verifiable research tasks, plus the fact that each   new generation of models is broadly smarter than  the last, will make models excellent even at the   parts of AI research that we can't directly check.

这里我想明确一点：达到这一里程碑耗时更久的可能性相当大，因为 AI 研究的某些重要部分可能依然难以自动化。这种事情提前预测本就极为困难，我猜测至少有 25% 的概率要到 2032 年之后才会发生。但相反地，我也认为进展速度可能快得多，我们在 2027 年就能达到这一里程碑。

英文原文 >> Now, I wanna be clear that there's a pretty decent  chance that hitting this milestone takes  longer because some important parts of AI  research remain hard to automate. This is just a very hard thing to   try to predict in advance, and I'd guess  that there's at least a 25% chance that   it doesn't happen until after 2032. But conversely, I also think that   things could happen much faster,  and we hit this milestone in 2027. 

我认识的一些消息最灵通的研究者——他们在过去几年中的预测记录非常好——预计 AI 最早在明年就能在 AI 研究上击败最顶尖的人类。此外，如果把 METR 指出 Anthropic 的 AI 进展速度相比一年前翻倍的最新报告代入起飞模型，直接得出的推论也是 2027 年。正如往期嘉宾 Ajeya Cotra 在关于该报告的博客文章中所说：“如果我们按字面意义接受 AI 目前几乎使 AI 能力进展速度翻倍，并假设来自 AI 的加速是平滑的，这在朴素推演下意味着我们将在一年内达到极度超级智能。”因此，我的中位数年份之所以不是 2027 年，只是因为我无法确定这种直接建模到底有多准确。但对于明年就可能达成这一里程碑的世界，我们不应感到意外，并且应当做好准备。

英文原文 >> Some of the most well-informed researchers  I know, people who have a very good track   record of making predictions over the  last few years, expect AIs to beat the   best humans at AI research as early as next year. And 2027 is also the straightforward implication   of putting the new report from METR that says  that Anthropic's rate of AI progress has doubled   from a year ago into a takeoff model. As former guest Ajeya Cotra put it in   a blog post about this report, quote, "If we  take it at face value that AI is now nearly   doubling the rate of AI capabilities progress  and assume that acceleration from AI is smooth,   this naively implies that we would reach extreme  superintelligence within a year," end quote.  So the reason my median year is not 2027 is just  that I'm uncertain about how accurate this kind   of straightforward modeling is. But we should not be surprised,   and we should be ready for a world in  which we get to this milestone next year.

数据中心训练如何泛化至现实世界

英文原文 >> Now, at this point, you might say, "How will  even superintelligence, which is trained in  a datacenter, beat human experts in domains  where the basis of that human expertise is   years and years of real-world experience?" 从可验证任务到通用能力的迁移

英文原文 >> Now, why do I think we'll see lots  of transfer from verifiable tasks   to these more general skills? What this involves is designing your model and   the surrounding software to get more juice out of  your compute, and this is especially verifiable.  For example, you can easily measure GPU  utilization or KV cache size per token or total   FLOPs in a forward pass or latency and so on.

反驳之一：持续学习能力的缺失

现在我想回应一下针对我关于 RSI 论点的几大最强反驳。我想表明的是，这些瓶颈确实很严重，它们确实压低了以最激进形态实现起飞的概率。这也是为什么我认为我们在 2028 年更有可能迎来一次相当于从 GPT-3.5 到 GPT-6 规模的跃升，而不是明天就开启某种不可思议的暴风骤雨般的一夜奇点。但我认为这些瓶颈不至于造成致命的瘫痪，这个瓶颈的瓶颈口实际上相当开阔。

英文原文 >> Now I wanna deal with the strongest  objections to my arguments about RSI. I wanna be clear that these bottlenecks are   serious, and they do tamp down the probability  of the most aggressive possible takeoff.  This is why I think we'll probably  make something like a GPT-3.5 to   GPT-6-sized jump in 2028 rather than some crazy  overnight singularity that starts tomorrow.  But I think these bottlenecks won't  be especially crippling, and that   the neck of the bottle is actually pretty wide.

让我解释一下原因。第一个质疑点（这也是我直到最近都持有的异议）：我们会被持续学习能力的缺失所卡住。过去一年半的时间里我一直在主张：当今 AI 最缺乏的关键能力，就是从实际工作经验中学习的能力。每一次新的会话都是它们上班的第一天；而在每次会话中，它们只能保留几个小时的信息，因此根本无法积累那些需要在数周或数月时间里逐渐形成的高阶反馈和专有诀窍。

英文原文 >> Let me explain why. Okay, so the first objection,  and this is an objection that I had until  recently, is that we'll be bottlenecked   by continual learning or the lack thereof. I've spent the past year and a half arguing   that the main thing missing from today's AIs is  this ability to learn from on-the-job experience.  Every new session is their first day on the  job, and in each session, they can only retain a   couple hours' worth of information, so they don't  accumulate the kind of feedback and know-how that   arrives over the course of weeks or months.

去年十二月，这也是我怀疑我们能否在短期内实现 AI 研究自动化的主要原因。正如我当时所写：在不具备小孩子所拥有的基础学习能力的情况下，这个自动化研究员不知怎的就要去找出实现 AGI 的算法——而这是人类长达半个世纪以来撞得头破血流的问题——这在我看来极其令人难以置信。但在这个问题上，我一定程度上改变了主意。以下是我认为这些模型当前缺乏持续学习能力并不会阻碍在 2028 年前实现顶尖 AI 研究员全面自动化的原因。

英文原文 >> Last December, this was my main reason for  doubting that we'd automate  AI research anytime soon.  As I wrote then, somehow this automated researcher  is gonna figure out the algorithm for AGI,   which is a problem that humans have been  banging their head against for the better   half of a century, while not having the basic  learning capabilities that children have.  I find this super implausible. But I've somewhat changed my mind on this.  Here's why I don't think that the current lack of  continual learning in these models will stop full   automation of the best AI researchers by 2028.

首先，AI 公司到时候可能仅凭与现有架构非常相似的结构就解决了持续学习问题。此前我曾认为我们受制于某种不可思议的突破性架构，需要能够让模型在每次会话结束时自我更新权重；但现在我认为，扩大上下文学习（in-context learning）的规模就已经足够了。这样一来，你就能拥有在实际效果上长达数月的会话，从而允许单个实例在工作中切实有效地学习。人们已经发表了大量非常有前景的思路，能让实验室实现极其漫长的有效上下文窗口。

英文原文 >> First, AI companies might just solve continual  learning by then with architectures that  are much like the ones they already have.  Previously, I thought that we were  bottlenecked by some crazy breakthroughs   that would allow models to update their  own weights at the end of every session.  But now I think that scaling up  in-context learning is enough,   and so you'll have sessions that are effectively  many months long, and this will allow a single   instance to learn effectively on the job. And people have published many, many   promising ideas that would let the labs achieve  dramatically longer effective context windows. 

第二个原因在于：你未必需要一个具备持续学习能力的系统去攻克持续学习。正如我前面所解释的，在线学习本身就是一个拥有清晰、可验证代理指标的机器学习研究课题。如果在两年前问我 Navier-Stokes 方程和持续学习哪个更难解决，答案是 Navier-Stokes 对我而言绝非显而易见。现在看来，鉴于各大实验室尚未攻克持续学习，它确实更难一些。但我们真的有十足把握断定它就难到那种程度，以至于比当前解决千禧年难题的模型再聪明一两年的非在线学习模型即使像现有模型一样如同失忆症患者，也依然无法攻克它吗？

英文原文 >> And the second reason is that you might not need  a continual learner to solve continual learning.  As I explained earlier, online learning is an ML  research problem with clean, verifiable proxies.  If you'd asked me two years ago, which would  be harder to solve, Navier-Stokes or continual   learning, it wouldn't have been obvious  to me that the answer was Navier-Stokes.  Now, of course, it turns out that continual  learning did turn out to be harder, given the   fact that the labs haven't cracked it yet. But are we so confident that it's that   much harder that the non-online learning  models a year or two smarter than the ones   that are currently solving Millennium Prize  problems won't be able to crack it, even if   they're amnesiacs like the current models are?

这些模型目前在没有长期记忆、依然表现得像笨拙博学实习生的情况下，就已经在接连解决千禧年难题了。这就像看着一个人在跑道上一边翻着侧手翻，一边还能跟尤塞恩·博尔特并驾齐驱。如果真的允许他们放开奔跑，我相信他们肯定会跑得更快；但谁能说他们翻着侧手翻就绝对赢不下这场比赛呢？话虽如此，我认为我们未来几年无法解决持续学习、且模型无法通过其他优势来弥补这一缺陷的概率仍然不容忽视。这也是为什么我给出了 25% 的概率：在 2032 年之后，我们才能实现达到 Ilya 级别的顶尖人类研究员的自动化。

英文原文 >> These models are out here solving Millennium  Prize problems while still being these derpy  savant interns that have no long-term memory.  It's like watching somebody do cartwheels down  the track and still keep pace with Usain Bolt.  If they were actually allowed to run,  I'm sure they could go even faster.  But who's to say that they can't win  this race while still cartwheeling?  Now, all this being said, I think there is a  serious chance that we don't solve continual   learning within the next few years and that  models are not able to compensate for this   missing capability through their other advantages. That's why I put a 25% probability that we won't   automate Ilya-level top human  researchers until after 2032. 

反驳之二：范式转变与研究品味

人们提出的另一个重要质疑是：我们受制于寻找合适的可验证代理指标。他们会辩称：我上面列出的所有可验证代理指标都是以现有范式为前提的；然而在最初去搞清楚究竟该验证什么目标、决定让 GPT-7 去爬哪座山，这本身就是 AI 研究至关重要的一部分。这也就是人们所说的“研究品味”，你又如何针对这一点进行训练呢？在 2018 年之前，几乎没人会说将下一个 Token 的预测损失降到最低将是实现 AI 进展最可行的路径；因此当时没有人会费心去构建 nanoGPT 风格的速通环境，要求智能体集群以最短的时间达成某种预训练损失。难道从当前走到超级智能，我们不需要跨越多个全新范式吗？难道每个新范式不都需要某些我们在事后才能知道的关联代理指标吗？自动化研究员在 RSI 期间又如何能找到这些指标？

英文原文 >> One important objection people  have is that we'll be bottlenecked   by finding the right verifiable proxies. All the verifiable proxies that I've listed above   assume the current paradigm, they would argue. But figuring out what objective to verify in   the first place, choosing which hill to have GPT-7  climb, is itself an important part of AI research.  This is what one might call research taste,  and how are you gonna train for that?  Before 2018, almost nobody was saying that  minimizing loss on next-token prediction is gonna   be the most tractable path to AI progress. So nobody would have bothered to build   nanoGPT-style speedrun environments where the  agent swarm has to minimize how much time it   takes to get to a certain pretraining loss. Wouldn't there be multiple new paradigms   that we have to cross to get  from here to superintelligence?  And won't each new paradigm require some  associated proxy that we don't know ex ante?  How will the automated  researchers find these during RSI? 

对这一质疑我有两点回应：首先，Transformer 在当时已有的翻译基准测试上是清晰可见的，事实上它就是为了刷爆现有的翻译基准而发明的。推理模型以及先前存在的代码与数学基准测试之间也呈现出完全相同的规律。在范式飞跃之前建立的代理指标，似乎完全能够捕捉到性能上的巨大阶跃变化。这是因为 AI 的学习算法具有很强的通用性。

英文原文 >> I have two responses to this objection. First, the transformer was visible on,   in fact, invented to beat the  existing translation benchmarks.  A similar story is true for reasoning models  and the pre-existing coding and math benchmarks.  Proxies made before a paradigm leap seem  perfectly capable of capturing large step   changes in performance. This is because AI   learning algorithms are quite general.

其次，提出与范式无关的外循环代理指标并不困难。例如你的新模型解决了多少个千禧年难题？如果你用 1905 年之前的数据训练你的模型，它需要多久能发现广义相对论？它掌握完全未见过的电子游戏速度有多快，等等。总体而言，我认为针对 RSI 影响的其他反驳要有力得多；如果这一条最终成了 RSI 未成大器的原因，我会感到非常意外。

英文原文 >> And second, it's not that difficult to  come up with paradigm-agnostic outer loop proxies. For example, how many Millennium Prize problems   does your new model solve? And if you train your model on   data before 1905, how long does it take  before it discovers general relativity?  How fast does it master totally  unseen video games, and so on?  All in all, I think the other objections  to the impact of RSI are much stronger,   and I'd be very surprised if this ended up  being the reason that RSI was not a big deal. 

反驳之三：数据瓶颈与合成环境

人们指出的另一个重大瓶颈是数据。数据难道不是 AI 进展的核心投入要素之一吗？如果数据不能以同样的速度扩张，RSI 又如何能够发生？我的回答是：数据在 RSI 期间将继续增长，但届时将是由 AI 来负责编译它们。无论为预训练还是后训练产出更优质的训练数据，这本身就是一个机器学习研究课题，而 AI 自己正在越来越擅长做这件事。

英文原文 >> The other big bottleneck that  people point out is data.  Isn't data one of the main  inputs into AI progress?  How will RSI happen without that  scaling up at the same rate?  My answer is that data will  continue growing during RSI,   but the AIs will be the ones compiling it. Producing better training data for both   pretraining and post-training is itself an ML  research problem, and AIs are getting increasingly   better at doing it themselves.

诚然，数据对于 AI 的进展是极其关键的投入。我和普林斯顿大学的学生 Jerry Han 实际上就此做过专门的研究：我们发现在过去的六年里，针对预训练而言，源自数据改进的算法进展是源自模型改进的 3 倍以上。然而我认为人们没有意识到，预训练中的这些数据改进工作与常规的 AI 研发工作高度契合：本质上就是去摸索哪些过滤现有数据或生成新合成数据的技术，在经验上能让模型在保留语料库上获得更低的损失。

英文原文 >> Now, it is true that data is an  extremely important input into AI progress. Together with Jerry Han, who's a student at   Princeton, I actually did some research into this. We found that for pretraining, over the last six   years, more than three times the  algorithmic progress has come from   data improvements than model improvements. However, I don't think that people realize   that these data improvements in pretraining are  very much in line with normal AI research work.  It's basically figuring out which techniques  for filtering existing data or generating   new synthetic data empirically lead  to lower loss on a held-out corpus. 

类似的情况在强化学习中似乎也越来越明显。DeepSeek 刚发表了一篇论文，阐述了他们是如何对新模型进行后训练的。他们表示，对基础强化学习算法的修改远没有那么重要，并且“合成数据与环境在规模、多样性和可验证性上的系统性提升，几乎贡献了观察到的全部收益”。而且，这类环境的构建方式本身也变得越来越自动化。他们写道：“我们对用于数据合成和环境构建的大规模自动化流水线进行了投资。利用难度和正确性作为奖励信号，我们迭代式地训练模型以构建出更优秀的任务。”

英文原文 >> A similar thing seems to be  increasingly true for RL.  DeepSeek just published a paper where they talk  about how they post-trained their new model.  They say that changes to the basic RL algorithm  have not mattered nearly as much, and that,   quote, "Systematic improvements in the scale,  diversity, and verifiability of synthesized data   and environments account for essentially  all of the observed gains," end quote.  And the way in which these environments are  made is itself getting more and more automated.  They write, quote, "We invest in  large-scale automated pipelines for   data synthesis and environment construction. Using difficulty and correctness as reward   signals, we iteratively train the model  to construct better tasks," end quote. 

反驳之四：实验算力瓶颈与小模型科学

现在我想谈谈“AI 进展将受制于实验算力”这一观点。前面我把当前数学领域的加速与我们在 AI 研究中应当预期发生的事情做了一个长篇类比。但 AI 研究与数学之间存在一个巨大的差异：在数学中，你可以仅凭纸和笔推导构思出新发现；而在 AI 研究中，你必须运行真实的实验来验证你的想法，而这需要算力和时间。随着自动化研究员数量的增加，每个人分到的总实验算力蛋糕份额会变得越来越小。

英文原文 >> Now I wanna talk about the idea that AI  progress will be bottlenecked by experiments.  So earlier, I made this extended analogy between  the current acceleration in mathematics and what   we should expect to happen in AI research. But there is one big difference between   AI research and mathematics. In math, you can simply reason   your way with pen and paper to new  discoveries, whereas in AI research,   you need to run actual experiments to validate  your ideas, and these take compute and time.  And as the number of automated researchers  grows, each gets access to a smaller and   smaller slice of the total experiment compute pie.

对此我的回答是：基本上，每个智能体仍然会获得大量的算力支持。前沿实验室每年将算力翻两番以上，如果这一趋势持续下去，到 2027 年底，OpenAI 几乎可以给 10,000 个智能体（相当于解决千禧年难题所动用的智能体规模）每个智能体每天分配足够的算力来运行一次 GPT-3 规模的训练。别忘了，2017 年初代 Transformer 论文中训练的最终模型，仅在 8 块 P100 上跑了三天——这在今天花大约 50 美元就能租到，仅相当于 GPT-3 训练算力的万分之一左右。如果顶尖人类 AI 研究员（如 Noam Shazeer 和 Ilya Sutskever）每天都有足够的实验算力训练一个 GPT-3 规模的模型，他们的进展可能确实会比现在慢，但如果他们直接两手一摊、抱怨集群太小而彻底无法取得任何有意义的进展，那对我来说将是极其不可思议的。

英文原文 >> My answer to this is that each agent will still  get access to a ton of compute, basically. Frontier labs have been more than quadrupling   their compute every single year, and if this  trend continues, then by the end of 2027,   OpenAI could almost give 10,000 agents, so  the amount that were involved in solving the   Millennium Prize problem, each enough compute to  run a GPT-3-sized training run every single day.  Keep in mind that the final model trained in the  original transformer paper in 2017 took three   days on eight P100s, which would cost about 50  bucks if you went to rent it today, or about one   ten-thousandth of the training compute of GPT-3. If the top human AI researchers, like Noam Shazeer   and Ilya Sutskever, had enough experiment compute  to train a GPT-3-sized model every single day,   they might make slower progress than they  currently do, but it would be extremely   surprising to me if they failed to make meaningful  progress altogether and just sat down and started   complaining about how small their cluster was.

那么，如果一家 AI 实验室拥有 10,000 名优秀的研究员，每人都拥有如此多的算力，且每人的思考速度都是原来的 10 倍，你认为这家实验室的生产力会有多高？难道你不想给这家新型实验室开支票吗？如今各大实验室的研究和实验模式反映出：相对于算力而言，研究员的时间极其稀缺。顶尖 AI 研究员个人的年薪高达数亿美元。因此，实验室今天使用算力的方式，根本无法说明如果关键瓶颈是算力而非研究员时间的话，他们能够将算力运用得多有效率。

英文原文 >> Now, how productive do you think an AI lab  would be if it had 10,000 great  researchers, each with this much   compute and each thinking 10 times as fast? You wouldn't wanna write a check to this neolab?  The nature of research and experimentation at  the labs today reflects that researcher time   is extremely scarce relative to compute. Top AI researchers individually get paid   hundreds of millions of dollars a year. So the way labs use compute today tells   us very little about how efficiently  they could use it if compute rather   than researcher time was the key bottleneck.

在使用实验算力方面，RSI 智能体集群能比当今研究人员高效得多的主要方式有两点：首先，它们可以立刻以人力置换算力。为了节省宝贵的研究人员时间，如今的实验对算力往往非常浪费，而且理当如此。因此，今天的人类研究员可能不会去耗费心力建立并仔细监控大量小规模预训练实验，而是直接并行启动几次大规模的多智能体强化学习运行。但如果你受到了实验算力的制约，你完全可以多做前者。当这些自动化研究人员在进行强化学习运行时，它们可以阅读每一份展开记录，一旦发现某个任务出现故障，就能立即暂停并重建整个环境；它们还可以对模型进行海量的小规模消融实验，只为让它们好上那么一点点。当一个智能体团队决定把算力集中起来做一次更大规模的运行时，它们可以花上主观上的数十年时间去设计最具信息量的合并实验；一旦实验运行完毕，它们还可以花上主观上的数十年时间进行全方位的深入分析，然后再去设计新的实验。

英文原文 >> There are two main ways in which the RSI agent  swarm will use experiment compute much more  efficiently than researchers today use it.  First, immediately they can  trade labor for compute.  To save valuable researcher time,  experiments today are, and should be,   very wasteful with compute. So instead of painstakingly   setting up and carefully monitoring tons  of small-scale pretraining experiments,   a human researcher today might instead kick off  a couple of huge multi-agent RL runs in parallel.  But if you're bottlenecked by experiment  compute, you can just do more of the former.  And when those automated researchers are doing  an RL run, they could read every single rollout   transcript and immediately pause and rebuild  a whole environment if some task is broken.  They could be doing crazy amounts  of small-scale ablations of their   models to make them oh-so-slightly better. And when a team of agents decides to combine their   compute together in order to do a much bigger run,  they could spend many subjective decades designing   the most informative pooled experiment. And once it's done running, they could   spend further subjective decades comprehensively  analyzing it before designing a new experiment. 

庞大的智能体集群在单个副本分配算力减少的情况下依然能保持高效产出的另一大途径在于：这些自动化研究人员可以开创一套更好的“从小规模实验中学习”的科学体系。缩放定律（Scaling laws）本身就是极佳的例证：GPT-4 的论文中指出，他们能够利用少花费 10,000 倍算力训练出的模型来预测最终模型的最终损失。更聪明、更快速的自动化公司所能做的事情之一，就是尝试发现更多类似于 muP 的缩放现象——后者能让研究人员从缩小 100 倍的模型中预先推知更大规模运行的最佳超参数。像这样的相似发现还有多少？我记得曾与一位 AI 研究员交谈，他认为从极小规模的神经网络中学习可以带来极其巨大的信息量。他是这么说的：“天上的诸神，所遵循的想必是和地上的蝼蚁相同的法则。”

英文原文 >> The other big way that this huge agent swarm  could remain productive, despite the fact that   each individual copy will have access to less  compute, is that these automated researchers   could come up with a better science for  learning from small-scale experiments.  Scaling laws themselves  are a good example of this.  The GPT-4 paper says that they were able  to predict the model's final loss using   models trained with 10,000x less compute. One of the things that the smarter and faster   automated company could be doing is trying  to discover more scaling phenomena like muP,   which lets researchers anticipate the  optimal hyperparameters for a larger run   from 100x smaller models. How many more similar   discoveries like this are there? I remember chatting to an AI researcher   who thought that learning from extremely small  neural networks could be incredibly informative.  He put it this way, "The gods above must work  from the same principles as the ants below."
