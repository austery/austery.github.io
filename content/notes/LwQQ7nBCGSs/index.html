征服阿兹特克与印加：历史上的“降维打击”与现代类比

主持人: 今天我邀请到了军事历史学家西·谢泼德（Si Sheppard）教授。我想和你探讨阿兹特克帝国与印加帝国的征服史，因为这是人类历史上最令人震惊的事件之一，在人类有史以来的所有军事与外交成就中可以说是绝无仅有的。区区几百名西班牙征服者踏上一片他们一无所知的全新大陆；短短数年之内，他们就彻底征服了拥有数百万人口的庞大帝国。在这一过程中，他们经常赢得兵力悬殊达 100 比 1、甚至 1000 比 1 的战役，而且在许多情况下自己连一人伤亡都没有。更令人难以置信的是，他们作战部队中高达 99% 以上的兵力，实际上正是由他们即将征服的本土原住民所组成的。

英文原文

Host: Today I’m chatting with professor and military historian Si Sheppard. I want to talk to you about the conquest of the Aztec and the Inca Empires because these are some of the most shocking events in human history, unparalleled among all the military and diplomatic achievements ever made. A few hundred conquistadors show up to a new continent they know nothing about. Within a matter of years, they subjugate empires which have millions of inhabitants in them. In the process of doing so, they are often winning battles where they are outnumbered 100 to 1, 1,000 to 1, and in many cases not even taking a single casualty. They’re doing this while upwards of 99% of their fighting forces are made up of the very natives that they’re going to subjugate.

主持人: 我这期节目邀请你来，其实心里还挂着一件事。我们今天主要谈历史，不会把焦点全放在这上面，但现在很多人把“人工智能接管人类”看作纯粹疯狂的科幻场景。然而这并不是历史上第一次发生这样的事：一个拥有完全不被土著理解的动机的外来异质力量，凭借外交权谋和压倒性的技术优势，成功运用策略击溃并接管了一个规模远比自己庞大、根基深厚的既有秩序体系。我特别想请你来帮我们厘清这里面究竟发生了什么。首先，我很想听听具体的故事：科尔特斯到底做了什么？皮萨罗又做了什么？这些征服在现实中究竟是如何一步步发生的？

英文原文

Host: I wanted to have you on to talk about something this episode. We’re not going to focus on it, we’re mostly talking about history here, but people think of AI takeover as this crazy sci-fi scenario but this would not be the first time in history that an alien force, with motivations that the natives don’t understand, is able to use diplomatic cunning and technological advantages to outmaneuver and then take over a much larger and well-established order. I wanted to have you on to understand what happened here. First, I would love to hear the stories. What did Cortés do? What did Pizarro do? How did these conquests actually go down?

地缘困境与大航海时代的开启

Si Sheppard: 谢谢你，非常荣幸能来到这里。是的，我们现在谈论的是现代世界的诞生，它发生在 500 到 600 年前，塑造了我们今天所身处其中的现代全球化共同体。这一切始于 15 世纪：当时的欧洲背靠大西洋的绝壁，在外部地缘力量的巨大挤压下陷入困境，被迫切断了与东方核心经济基地的联系——也就是历史上贯穿整个欧亚大陆、推动全球经济运转的香料与丝绸发源地。在走投无路之下，欧洲终于被迫走上创新之路。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: Thank you. It’s a great pleasure to be here. Yes, we’re talking about the birth of our modern world, which played out between 600 to 500 years ago and created the modern globalized community that we all participate in today. It began in the 15th century when Europe, backed up against the wall of the Atlantic, under immense pressure from external forces and cut off from the core economic power bases of the East — the sources of the spice and silk that had driven the global economy historically throughout Eurasia — was finally forced to innovate.

Si Sheppard: 葡萄牙的恩里克航海王子等关键人物开始发力，致力于把大西洋从天堑障碍转变成海上超级高速公路。历代船长们不断带回潮汐、风向、洋流以及他们所侦察勘测到的陆地信息，这种世代积累的航海知识让他们在摸索中不断完善地图，最终成功找到了绕过非洲南端直达印度的航线，从而彻底甩开了中间商。与此同时，西班牙人则选择向西航行。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: Key names like Prince Henry the Navigator of Portugal work on turning the Atlantic Ocean from a barrier into a superhighway. Generational knowledge accrued from captains reporting on tides and wind currents and whatever land they’ve managed to reconnoiter, building out the map as they go along, enables them to find a way around the southern tip of Africa directly to India and therefore cut out the middleman. The Spanish, meanwhile, are heading west.

Si Sheppard: 我们都知道克里斯托弗·哥伦布的故事：“一四九二，扬帆破浪渡重洋”（In 1492, he sailed the ocean blue）。那实际上是一场创业冒险，是一次试图通过向西航行抵达东方市场的探险。当然，他的计算数据完全错了。但他之所以能死里逃生并名垂青史，纯粹是因为在他原本以为是日本、中国和香料群岛的地方，竟然不可思议地横亘着一整块全然未知的大陆。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: We all know the story of Christopher Columbus: "In 1492, he sailed the ocean blue." That was entrepreneurship. This was an expedition to arrive at the Oriental markets by traveling west. Of course, his figures were completely wrong. He was saved by the fact that, unknown to him, there was an entire continent in the place where he thought Japan, China, and the Spice Islands would be.

Si Sheppard: 具有讽刺意味的是，最初人们甚至把发现这块大陆当成一种失望。但随着时间的推移，大家越来越清楚地意识到，这里蕴藏着巨大的土地、财富与人力潜力，完全可以加以开发掠夺。关键的转折点发生在 1511 年，一位野心勃勃的西班牙贵族迭戈·贝拉斯克斯（Diego Velázquez）夺取了古巴的控制权。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: Initially, that was perceived, ironically, as a disappointment. But over time, it became increasingly apparent this was a source of potential in land, in wealth, and in people that could be exploited. A key turning point happened in 1511 when an ambitious Spanish nobleman, de Velázquez, seizes control of Cuba.

科尔特斯的野心与脱轨出航

Si Sheppard: 贝拉斯克斯以古巴为权力基地，派出探险队去勘察我们今天所说的中美洲（中部美洲）沿岸地区。随后在 1518 年，他把这项任务交托给了一个和他关系颇为微妙波折的人——埃尔南·科尔特斯（Hernán Cortés）。科尔特斯早在 1511 年就随军来到了新大陆的古巴。他受过一些法律训练，先是担任公证人，后来一路升迁，成为了古巴的首席地方行政长官（alcalde）。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: He sends out from this power base expeditions exploring the coast of what we would call Mesoamerica, Central America. Later in 1518, he assigns that task to a man with whom he’s had a somewhat tempestuous relationship, a man named Hernán Cortés. He’d arrived in the New World in Cuba in 1511. He had some legal training, so he served as a notary and worked his way up to being alcalde, which is chief magistrate, on Cuba.

Si Sheppard: 但科尔特斯的野心远不止于此。他拿着总督签发的手令便开始大展拳脚。他着手招兵买马，瞄准了一个规模宏大得多、也危险得多的目标：不仅要勘探海岸线，还要直插内陆，探明那里究竟有什么，并彻底夺取其控制权。当古巴总督意识到科尔特斯的真实图谋时，急忙试图阻拦他。在 1518 年 11 月 18 日上演了极其精彩的一幕：总督匆忙赶到海滨码头时，科尔特斯恰好正登上小艇，准备划向停泊在近海的舰队。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: But he had much greater ambition. He takes this warrant from the governor and runs with it. He begins recruiting men to a much wider and much more ambitious goal: not just to explore the coast, but to penetrate into the interior, to find out what’s there, and to seize control of it. When the governor realizes what Cortés is up to, he tries to stop him. There’s this great moment on 18th November 1518. He arrives at the waterfront just as Cortés gets into a boat to go out to his ships offshore.

Si Sheppard: 总督隔空冲他大喊：“你想干什么？！”科尔特斯向他保证说：“别担心，一切尽在我的掌控之中。”但实际上，此时此刻总督已经彻底失去了控制。现在，这场探险完完全全属于科尔特斯了。这就是时代背景。正如你刚才所说的，他是在字面意义上驶出了已知地图的边界，闯入了一片欧洲人此前完全未知的无名之境（terra incognita）。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: The governor calls out to him, "What are you up to?" Cortés assures him, "Don’t worry. I have this all under control." But of course, the governor had lost control at that point. Now it was Cortés’s expedition. This is the background. Again, as you say, he is literally going off the side of the map. He’s entering terra incognita, unknown to Europeans previously.

利用帝国裂隙：托托纳克人与特拉斯卡拉人

Si Sheppard: 在加勒比群岛摸爬滚打多年所磨炼出的敏锐政治嗅觉，让科尔特斯在极其早期的阶段就洞察到两点：这片内陆大陆上存在着一个拥有霸权的庞大帝国势力；同时，在其统治下的附属部落臣民对这位霸主极其痛恨、怨声载道。他首先抵达了托托纳克人（Totonacs）的土地。这群人是不久前才刚刚被强行纳入帝国的势力范围。这个帝国的统治者自称为墨西加人（Mexica）——这正是我们今天“墨西哥”一词的由来——但在历史上，人们根据他们起源于阿兹特兰（Aztlán）的神话故土，将他们统称为阿兹特克人（Aztecs）。为了简便起见，我们也用阿兹特克人来称呼他们。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: His political antenna, honed by years in the islands, enables him to appreciate at a very early stage that there is a significant imperial power which has hegemony here in the mainland, and that its subject peoples chafe under the authority of whoever this lord or imperial power is. He arrives in the lands of the Totonacs, who had recently been incorporated into the imperial orbit of a people who call themselves the Mexica — hence Mexico as we know it — but who have come down to history based on their mythical origins in a land called Aztlán as the Aztecs. For simplicity’s sake, that’s what we’ll refer to them as.

Si Sheppard: 就像所有被征服和奴役的臣属民族一样，托托纳克人对阿兹特克的残暴统治怀恨在心。在他们眼中，这群神秘外来者的到来，正是一个摆脱强加在自己头顶枷锁的千载难逢的潜在机遇。与托托纳克人建立起同盟关系后，科尔特斯率军向内陆挺进，进入了特拉斯卡拉人（Tlaxcalans）的地盘。特拉斯卡拉人就像人体的自身免疫系统一样，立刻全军动员向他发起进攻，企图击溃他并将他逐出自己的领地。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: The Totonacs resented this authority, as all subjugated and subordinated peoples do, and they saw the arrival of these outsiders as a potential opportunity to throw off the authority that had been imposed on them. Having established this relationship with the Totonacs, Cortés begins to march inland, and he enters the territory of the Tlaxcalans, who, like an autoimmune system, immediately rallied to defeat him, to expel him from their territory.

Si Sheppard: 然而，科尔特斯顶住了狂轰滥炸，硬生生把特拉斯卡拉人打成僵局，交战持续了相当长一段时间，以至于特拉斯卡拉人最终选择与他达成了某种全新的政治契约。特拉斯卡拉人同样看清了他的强悍力量、战斗力以及巨大的潜在价值。他们彻底倒向了科尔特斯一方，从那以后便充当向导和生力军，护送他的探险队继续向内陆深处挺进。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: But he fights them to a standstill for such a period of time that the Tlaxcalans and he arrive at a new kind of arrangement. The Tlaxcalans again appreciate his strength, his power, his potential. They swing over to his side and will henceforward serve as guides and reinforcements for his expedition deeper into the interior.

兵力悬殊与技术代差的本质

主持人: 我们能不能在这里稍停一下，具体聊聊兵力数字？科尔特斯当时到底带了多少人马？要知道，即便是特拉斯卡拉人，其兵力规模也远超科尔特斯。所以光是能与他们打成平手僵局，就已经是一项极其惊人的军事壮举了，更何况在这个时间节点上，科尔特斯身边根本还没有任何大规模本土盟军的支援。在那个阶段，双方的实际兵力对比究竟是怎样的？

英文原文

Host: Can we pause here and talk about the numbers? How many men had Cortés brought with him? Even the Tlaxcalans had much larger numbers than Cortés did. So even the standstill is an impressive military achievement, especially because he’s fighting without native support. What are the numbers even at this point?

Si Sheppard: 科尔特斯这边只有区区几百人，而特拉斯卡拉一方则有数千上万之众。正是在这一刻，旧大陆与新大陆之间决定性的技术代差开始真正显现出毁灭性的威力。最初让我对这整段历史深陷其中的经典著作，是贾雷德·戴蒙德（Jared Diamond）的《枪炮、病菌与钢铁》（Guns, Germs, and Steel）。他在书名中列出了西班牙人所拥有的那几项至关重要的核心优势。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: A few hundred on Cortés’ side and multiple thousands on the Tlaxcalans’ side. It’s at this point that the critical technological differentials from the Old and New World really begin to come into effect. The classic text which got me invested in this whole scenario in the first place is Jared Diamond’s book, Guns, Germs, and Steel. He enumerates in the title those critical advantages that the Spaniards had.

Si Sheppard: 但在战场现实中，枪炮其实并没有想象中那么举足轻重。原住民部队在最初的震撼惊骇过去之后很快便意识到，考虑到当时早期火器那慢得令人绝望的重新装填速度，火器在实战战场上并不是决定性的王牌资产。然而，钢铁却是不可逾越的致命壁垒。当地土著的武器是用木材、石块和黑曜石打造的。黑曜石是一种火山玻璃，打磨锋利后削铁如泥，作为劈砍武器非常危险致命。但在征服者身上坚不可摧的精钢板甲、尤其是钢制头盔面前，黑曜石武器的威力大打折扣。

英文原文

Si Sheppard: Guns in reality weren’t so significant. Once the Native peoples got over the shock value, they appreciated, given the very slow reloading times involved in firearms at that time, that they weren’t so significant as assets on the battlefield. But steel was critical. Local weapons were carved from wood and from stone and from obsidian, that volcanic glass which is razor sharp when honed to a point and quite dangerous as a slashing weapon. But against the steel armor, and especially the steel helmets that the conquistadors were wearing, it was much less effective.

Si Sheppard: 征服者继承了旧大陆延续数代的深厚军事战斗传统，配备了著名的托莱多钢造长剑。他们将长剑用作突刺武器，能够一击毙命直插敌害；而土著武士习惯于大开大合地挥舞武器进行劈砍，这种动作不可避免地会提前暴露出身体躯干的侧翼薄弱点，从而在交锋伊始就被刺穿。这种技术代差与几代人传承下来的战术训练和实战技巧相结合，赋予了征服者在战场上压倒性的绝对优势。
