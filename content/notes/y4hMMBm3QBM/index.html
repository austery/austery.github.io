气候危机与联邦撤离：地方治理的“断链”时刻

在全球气候变化的背景下，突发性洪水（Flash Flooding）的频率正显著上升。然而，在地方社区最需要预警支持时，美国联邦政府却在缩减相关项目。在艾奥瓦州的河谷市（Riverdale），市长 Anthony Huddleston 发现，原本由联邦政府运行、用于监测鸭溪（Duck Creek）水位的关键传感器（Gauge）已被撤除。

这一变化的背后是复杂的政治与人事动荡。联邦机构实施了所谓的延迟辞职计划（Deferred Resignation Program: 一种允许员工停止工作但继续领取数月薪水直至离职的激励政策），导致大量资深技术专家如 Gary Johnson 离岗，原本由联邦运作的水位监测服务随之瘫痪。对于只有550人的小镇而言，重新安装一套联邦标准的监测系统需花费超过 10 万美元，这在地方财政上是难以承受的沉重负担。

英文原文

I'm David Leheart of New York Times Opinion. Last summer, one of my colleagues became a little obsessed with one problem in particular, flash flooding. Climate change is increasing the number of floods, just as President Trump is cutting government programs that help communities deal with those floods.

The story begins in Riverdale, Iowa. It's home to just 550 people. Mayor Huddleston had help from the federal government. The feds ran a gauge on Duck Creek, a little brick house with a sensor inside. It measured the height of the creek and posted the information online. Local officials would use that information to make decisions about evacuations during storms. But the mayor no longer had that crucial service at his disposal.

He went to check the height of the creek online and said, "This gauge has been discontinued. Please email Gary Johnson if you'd like to ask any questions." I typed an email. Hey Gary, what's going on with this gauge? And I immediately got a response back and it said, "Hey, this is Gary Johnson. I've taken the deferred resignation program and I'm not available to help you." Deferred resignation is what the Trump administration offered tens of thousands of federal employees last year. It allowed them to stop working but continue getting paid for a few months while they looked for a new job. He discovered that the feds had ripped the whole thing out and it would have eventually cost the town more than $100,000 to replace it.

技术下沉：低成本水位计与艾奥瓦州洪水中心

面对联邦服务的真空，艾奥瓦州展示了一个独特的解决方案。由 Larry Weber 领导的艾奥瓦州洪水中心（Iowa Flood Center: 全美首个州立洪水研究中心）开发出了一款低成本的水位计。与联邦政府建造的大型砖结构建筑不同，这款传感器仅有“大号鞋盒”大小，通过简单的螺栓即可固定在桥梁侧面。

这款传感器的核心优势在于其极致的性价比。其单价仅为 7500 美元，不到联邦系统成本的十分之一。传感器内置的探测器能够实时测量水深，并将数据同步至在线预测模型（Predictive Model）。这种模型不仅能显示当前水位，还能根据降雨量预测溪流在未来几小时内的走势。尽管它未被纳入国家气象预报系统，但对于处理像鸭溪这样“脾气暴躁”的小型支流，其响应速度和精准度远超陈旧的联邦方案。

英文原文

But the mayor was very lucky because Iowa is different. Engineers at the Iowa Flood Center have developed an incredible tool. Larry Weber is the director there. He showed me the gauge in his lab. I would say that this is at the size of a big shoe box. Pretty straightforward really.

Cities and towns can buy the gauge from the flood center. Local officials like Anthony can attach it to the side of a bridge over a body of water and the sensor inside measures the height of that water. It then posts that information online with a predictive model so local officials can see how, say, a rainstorm is likely to affect their creek. The data from the federal gauge is part of a national weather forecasting system. The Iowa gauge is not, but otherwise it does pretty much what the federal one can do for way less money. The Iowa made gauge cost about $7,500.

深夜生死局：专家实时介入与政府公信力

技术在危机时刻的价值，不仅在于硬件，更在于其背后的“人”。在去年 7 月的一个暴雨之夜，河谷市的预测模型显示水位将上升 8 英尺，这意味着洪水将齐平防洪墙，危及周边带有氧气瓶的体弱老人。在联邦机构无人回应、洪水中心办公室已下班的深夜，市长 Huddleston 通过 LinkedIn 紧急联络到了工程师 Felipe。

在接到求助后，工程师在远程运行了更精细的模型，得出了“水位仅会上升 4 英尺”的关键结论。这一分析排除了模型之前的过度预测，成功阻止了一次耗费巨大、且可能引发社区恐慌的无效撤离。这种实时专家支持机制，不仅挽救了潜在的生命财产损失，更通过避免“狼来了”式的误报，巩固了地方政府与民众之间的信任。事后，洪水中心甚至将主要负责人的私人手机号公布在官网上，确保艾奥瓦人能随时获得支持。

英文原文

The predictor model attached to the gauge said that given the rain, Duck Creek was likely to rise 8 ft sometime in the middle of the night. Mayor Huddleston thought of one resident in particular, an older man who lives right next to the creek who uses an oxygen tank. And if the creek hit 8 ft in the middle of the night, that is a tough time to evacuate.

It's Friday night. They're not a 24/7 operation, right? And so there's nobody there to answer the phone. "It's so heavy... I was furious that evening that I couldn't get a hold of people that they were gone." Thanks to the magic of LinkedIn, Anthony tracked down an engineer at the flood center named Felipe. He agreed to run a different model to see if he could get a more precise answer. At around 11:30 p.m., Felipe calls me back and he says, "Hey, it's only going to be 4 ft. I did the math. The model's overpredicting a little bit."

If Mayor Hedleston hadn't been able to reach the flood center, he might have had to evacuate the neighborhood in the middle of the night for no reason. That risks a real loss of trust in local government. Those officials want to get evacuations right to preserve their relationship with their residents.

数据驱动的防御：从城市规划到精准救援

艾奥瓦州系统的应用已远超紧急避险，渗透到了长期的城市治理中。紧急协调员 Rick Wolfkull 利用这套在线制图系统，成功否决了一个计划在洪涝区兴建 30 户住宅的开发方案。其直言不讳的评价——“这地方只要青蛙放个屁就会被淹”——背后是详实的动态数据支撑。此外，该系统在救援中也能提供关键的水流流速数据，指导救援船只选择最安全的路线。

相比之下，联邦政府提供的 FEMA 洪水图（FEMA Maps）被指责为界面停留在“互联网 1.0 时代”，数据陈旧且仅覆盖大江大河，忽略了在气候变化下更具破坏力的暴雨径流（Flash flooding）。Larry Weber 提出，任何一个州只需投入约 200 万美元的初建费和每年 50 万美元的维护费，即可建立类似的防御体系。这一支出在数百亿规模的州政府预算面前显得微不足道。在联邦支持不断蒸发的当下，艾奥瓦州的“地方自主防灾”模式正在成为全美各州应对气候变化的新蓝图。

英文原文

The gauges are connected to an online mapping system they use all the time. Here's Rick Wolfkull from Buchanan County: "We just had a proposal from a developer to put a 30-home subdivision right in the floodway. I said, 'You realize this area has been underwater every time a frog farts.'" This mapping system lets officials game out where to build and whether to risk lives to rescue stranded residents. "We run our model. We give water velocities in all the streets and say you're better to go down Third Avenue."

City officials elsewhere have to rely on data from the National Weather Service and FEMA maps, which are notoriously limited. The interface looks like it's from internet 1.0. The FEMA maps don't account for intense bursts of rain that lead to flash flooding. Larry said the average state could do it for about $2 million to start and then about half a million a year to maintain it. That is a small fraction of the tens of billions of dollars most states spend. As federal officials continue to disappear, Iowa has a plan, a blueprint for other states to follow.
