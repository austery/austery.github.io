约会经济史：从公共空间自由到订阅制的萌芽

约会模式在过去一个世纪经历了翻天覆地的变化。1920年代，随着城市化的推进，约会从父母监管下的家庭内部转移到了电影院、餐厅等公共场所。这种转变重构了权力动态（Power Dynamic）：男性开始承担财务责任，而女性则掌握了决定权。在那个年代，一场约会通常花费不到 1 美元，约占当时工人周薪的 4%。到了 1960 年代，尽管两份汉堡和电影票的价格看起来仅为 2.65 美元，但相对于当时每周 108 美元的家庭中位收入，约会支出已攀升至周收入的 23% 左右。

直到 1980 和 1990 年代，付费墙（Paywall）首次出现在约会领域。对于无法通过社交圈找到伴侣的人，最早的视频约会服务 Great Expectations 开始收取 1,000 到 2,000 美元的预付费用，用于访问其视频资料库。这标志着约会服务正式进入订阅时代，植入了“为获取潜在伴侣池而付费”的商业逻辑。尽管当时中端餐厅的晚餐加电影可能需要 30 到 50 美元，但四位数的约会服务费用开始逐渐盖过实际约会的成本。

英文原文

The dating era in the sense that we need it today started in the 1920s. After a long period of urbanization, families moved to cities. Dating moved out of the family home under the supervision of parents and young people got the freedom to go to public places without chaperones. They started visiting restaurants and movie theaters, dance halls and this changed the power dynamic. Men now paid for the experience, taking the financial responsibility that was previously held by the hosting family and women decided the terms. A typical date... cost well under a dollar. For context, a typical weekly family budget... was around $25 in 1920... So, a proper date would cost around 4% of a weekly spending.

During the post-war era, the dinner and movie template became the default date format... A cheap date in 1960s was typically two hamburgers, two sodas, and two movie tickets... that comes out to about $2.65 total. Roughly $2 for two tickets, 30 cents for two burgers, and 30 cents for sodas. A nicer restaurant date... would run $8 to $12... the median American family income in 1960 was about $108 per week before tax. That means that a cheap date was around 23% of a week's income... So dating was cheap in absolute dollars compared to today. But it got a lot more expensive compared to the previous era relative to earnings.

In the 80s and 90s... was the first time when the haywall showed up. People who couldn't find partners through social circles would turn to newspaper personal ads... But this time, the first video dating service showed up. The service called Great Expectations, and it charged $1 to $2,000 upfront for access to video libraries of potential batches. This was the first time when dating had a subscription fee attached to it. The service planted the seed that paying for access to a curated pool of potential partners should cost money.

网络约会的演进与“约会通胀”的全面爆发

1995 年 Match.com 的上线开启了第一波互联网约会浪潮，其订阅模式直接借鉴自杂志订阅。2000 年 e-Harmony 则以严肃、高端的定位入场，月费高达 45 至 65 美元，宣称这是对未来伴侣的一种投资。直到 2004 年 OkCupid 作为免费替代品出现，市场才正式分化为“严肃付费”与“休闲免费”两大阵营。2012 年 Tinder 的出现带来了颠覆性的滑动机制（Swiping Mechanic）和源自老虎机的多巴胺循环（Dopamine Loop）。

进入 2026 年，约会经济学呈现出惊人的图景：单次约会的平均成本已飙升至约 200 美元，比前一年增长了 12%，这种现象被称为约会通胀（Dateflation）。更显著的变化在于成本分布的逆转：过去昂贵的是约会本身，而现在软件订阅费占据了巨大比重。一个典型的约会者如果同时使用 Tinder Gold、Bumble Premium 和 Hinge Plus，每月仅订阅费就需支出约 100 美元；若再加上 e-Harmony，年支出将超过 2,000 美元。研究显示，超过 47% 的单身人士认为约会在财务上已难以为继。

英文原文

Moving into 1995... the first internet wave, match.com, launched in 1995... this subscription model was borrowed from magazine subscriptions... E-Harmony launched in 2000 with a different positioning... long for questionnaires, and premium pricing, $45 to $65 a month. And they justified that price by positioning it as an investment in your future life partner. Then OKCID showed up and launched in 2004 as the free alternative... Tinder's 2012 launch borrowed the swiping mechanic from a card sorting exercise in user experience studies and the reward mechanism was the dopamine loop borrowed from slot machines.

The economics of today's dating ecosystem are strikingly different from the old dinner and movie model. The average date in 2026 costs roughly $200. And mind you, that is a 12% jump from 2025. And costs keep rising. There is even a separate term coined for it called dateflation. And that's before the app costs. A typical dator today that sits on three different apps with a paid subscription... spends around $100 a month. And if, for example, you're on e-harmony... you're looking at more than $2,000 just for the subscriptions. And if you look at the studies, more than 47% of singles say that dating is no longer financially viable. Historically, the date itself was the expensive part. But today, if you're serious about dating, you have to get on premium versions of those apps.

平台利益冲突：AI 生成配置文件与活跃度模拟

约会软件行业与其核心用户之间存在着天然的利益冲突：当用户成功脱单并离开平台时，平台实际上失去了一个付费客户。AI 的介入加剧了这一矛盾。平台利用 AI 生成配置文件（AI Generated Profiles）来延长用户在平台上的停留时间。即使这些资料并非真实存在，它们也能创造和刺激活动。

这些 AI 资料被设计得极其逼真，足以引导用户进行互动。当用户获得多巴胺快感（无论是收到消息还是匹配成功）时，这种价值具有持久的“成瘾”效果。即使互动最终没有转化为现实约会，用户也会因为持续的聊天而选择续费，担心错过那个“对的人”。这种机制本质上是在利用用户的孤独感获利，通过虚假的互动维持平台的繁荣表象。

英文原文

The dating app industry has always had a conflict of interest with its core audience. It is obvious that a platform technically profits when users don't find a match because a successful match removes paying customers. You may ask, "But how's that different from any other business... like a car mechanic?" Well, that comparison is not fair... Unlike a car mechanic that delivers value all at once, the app delivers some value at certain points. And when a user gets a dopamine hit, be it a date or... even a message, that value has a lasting effect.

So AI profiles... the point of AI profiles is that they extend time on the platform. Yes, dating apps reportedly benefit from AI profiles despite the fact that they're not real because those profiles create and stimulate activity. If an AI profile looks believable enough for you to engage with... it doesn't matter if you end up going on a date or not. You keep staying on the app because the interaction keeps going and all of a sudden it's the last day of your subscription. But hey, all those chats can't just sit there. What if he or she is the one... and the next moment you find yourself paying for the next month hoping that something may pan out?

AI 辅助配置文件的同质化与“猫鱼”升级版

在更显性的层面上，AI 辅助配置文件（AI Assisted Profiles）正在改变用户的呈现方式。这些工具可以优化个人简历、润色对话提示，甚至分析照片库以筛选最能吸引人的形象。这虽然帮助了表达困难的人群，却导致了一个显著后果：档案同质化（Homogeneity）。当数百万用户使用相同的底层大模型（LLM）、相同的温度设置和人格设定来润色资料时，个性的差异性迅速坍缩。

与此同时，传统的虚假社交（Catfishing: 利用他人照片伪造身份）在生成式 AI 的加持下迎来了规模化增长。由于 AI 生成的照片是“原创”的，传统的以图搜图验证手段彻底失效。结合可以维持自然语境对话的聊天机器人、声音克隆乃至深度伪造视频（Deepfake），虚假资料的真实性达到了前所未有的高度。这种虚假资料由于能够精准迎合算法的审美偏好，往往占据了平台的流量顶端。

英文原文

AI assisted profiles are different... they optimize your bio, your description, your prompts. They suggest how to write a response... analysis of your photo gallery to gauge what you're into and so on. Now, on one hand, this can be a life-cher for people who struggle to present themselves... On the other hand, the obvious consequence of this is that it works universally across all profiles. So when millions of users run their bios and conversation openers through the same LLMs... the variance in dating profiles collapses and they become very homogeneous.

Another problem is catfishing. Pre-AI catfishing was fairly straightforward... You could reverse image search... After GenAI, catfishing really grew in scalability because AI profile photos are in fact original. They're not copies. You can't reverse image search them. And on top of that, add the LLM bots that can sustain a natural and contextually appropriate conversation. They can do voice cloning. They can be even used during video chats. And deep fake videos are also getting pretty convincing.

算法围猎：普及偏见与稀缺性驱动的收费逻辑

约会软件中存在明显的普及偏见（Popularity Bias），即算法倾向于推荐极少数高活跃、高颜值的资料。这些“头部资料”往往与高互动率正相关，却与最终成功匹配负相关。AI 生成的虚假资料正是针对这一机制进行了优化：它们极其符合社会审美标准，成功吸引大量新老用户留存。美国联邦贸易委员会（FTC）曾起诉 Match.com，证据显示该公司通过虚假账号发送“有人喜欢你”的通知，仅此一项就驱动了近 50 万用户重新订阅。

在异性约会市场中，软件的经济学逻辑是利用稀缺性驱动男性付费。数据显示，Tinder 的男性用户占比高达 75%，Bumble 为 64%，Hinge 为 60%。在匹配率上，男性的平均匹配率仅为 3%，而女性则高达 45%。因此，算法的首要任务是保持女性用户的活跃度，因为她们的参与创造了男性的竞争压力和付费动力。这种两面市场（Two-sided Marketplace）的不对称性，使得平台缺乏动力去彻底清除虚假资料，因为维持用户池的规模表象直接关系到其核心营收。

英文原文

There are multiple studies... that describes a pattern of dating apps exhibiting a measurable popularity bias. Popularity bias is a phenomenon in recommendation systems... the algorithm disproportionately suggests popular items... especially when you're just starting to use them or coming back after a long time. You get the cream of the crop at the very top to lure you in. And the interesting thing is that these profiles directly correlate with maximum engagement and reverse correlate with successful matching. The FTC established that Match Group sent emails from fake accounts notifying users that someone likes them. And that notification alone drove nearly half a million people to resubscribe.

Dating apps... work as a two-sided marketplace... but in dating apps, there is heavy supply-demand asymmetry. Tinder is 75% male. Bumble is 64% male. Hinge is 60% male. On Tinder, men swipe right on about 60% of profiles. Women swipe right under around seven. Men's match rate is around 3%. Women's is 45. This is why Tinder's algorithm is specifically designed to keep women engaged and active because dating marketplaces create extreme scarcity for men and scarcity is what drives spending. This is why the economics of the dating apps is skewed towards keeping fake profiles because removing them has immediate costs.

未来边界：AI 代聊、叙事化档案与元宇宙的局限

约会技术的下一个前沿是 Volkmatch（一个由 AI 驱动的新型平台），它允许 AI 代理替用户创建资料并启动浪漫对话。与此同时，Bumble 正在测试取消滑动机制，转而采用纵向叙事格式（Vertical Storytelling Format），通过生活故事的篇章化呈现来减少“滑动疲劳”。虽然滑动动作可能被取代，但核心的连接问题依然存在。

关于元宇宙约会（Metaverse Dating）的讨论也日益增多，用户通过虚拟化身（Avatar）进行 VR 约会。然而，这一方向面临两大阻碍：首先是性别失衡，VR 领域由于其游戏基因而严重偏向男性，再次陷入稀缺性陷阱；其次是物理临场感的缺失。外貌和物理特征在人类择偶行为中是不可分割的组成部分，虚拟化身无法从根本上解决真实身体接触的需求。约会软件越是精密，越是暴露出孤独感这一本质问题：人们渴望连接，而约会无论在线上还是线下，始终是那件充满困惑、挫败但也偶尔浪漫且令人兴奋的人类本能行为。

英文原文

The newest thing... is mold match, an offshoot of OpenClaw... allowing AI agents to create dating profiles and initiate romantic conversations on behalf of their users. Bumble explicitly plans to test removing the swipe mechanic entirely and replace it with chapter-based profiles in a vertical storytelling format... there's also some talks about metaverse dating... the idea is that there would be no human faces and users would swipe on avatar profiles... If you want my personal view on it, I don't think this is going to go too far... I think that looks and appearance and physical traits are integral in mating behavior.

The biggest problem in metaverse dating is the gender imbalance. VR demographics skew heavily male... you run into the same problem with scarcity. The irony that the more sophisticated these apps get, the more they expose a problem that has nothing to do with technology. We are getting lonier and we long for connection and dating is dating whether online or not. It's confusing, frustrating, entertaining, romantic, and occasionally humiliating as it's always been.
