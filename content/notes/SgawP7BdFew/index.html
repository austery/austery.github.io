对话欧洲央行行长拉加德：地缘政治碎片化、AI 生产力与货币政策的未来 Norges Bank Investment Management 2026-03-24

领域: geopolitics

分类: macro-economy

主讲人: Norges Bank Investment Management

来源: 查看

发布时间: 2026-03-24

摘要:

欧洲央行行长克里斯蒂娜·拉加德在访谈中深度解析了当前全球经济与 20 世纪 20 年代的惊人相似性，探讨了地缘政治碎片化、能源危机以及人工智能对欧洲生产力的潜在影响。她强调了央行独立性的核心价值，分享了其在法律、政府与国际组织任职中的领导力智慧，并对数字欧元的演进和个人成长驱动力给出了独到见解。

历史的类比：20 世纪 20 年代与当下

尼古拉·坦根: 大家好，我是挪威主权财富基金的 CEO 尼古拉·坦根。今天我们有幸邀请到一位重量级嘉宾，我们现在身处法兰克福，与欧洲央行 (ECB) 行长克里斯蒂娜·拉加德在一起。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Hi everyone, I'm Nicolai Tangen, the CEO of the Norwegian Sovereign Wealth Fund and today we are in particularly good company because we are in Frankfurt with Christine Lagarde, president of the European Central Bank.

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 很高兴见到你，尼古拉。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Lovely to see you, Nikolai.

尼古拉·坦根: 见到你太棒了。克里斯蒂娜，你拥有传奇般的职业生涯。你曾执掌全球性律师事务所，担任过法国财政部长，领导了 IMF (国际货币基金组织) 十年，而现在你正掌舵欧洲的货币政策。非常感谢你能抽出时间。我最近听到你说，现在的世界看起来有点像 1929 年。相似之处在哪里？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Fantastic. Now Christine, you've had a tremendous career. You were running a global law firm. You've been the finance minister of France. You ran the IMF for a decade and now you are steering the monetary policy over Europe. So thanks for seeing us. It's a great pleasure. I heard you recently say that the world now looks a bit like 1929. What is similar?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 我想我当时做的类比是关于 20 世纪 20 年代。因为那是一个重大技术突破层出不穷的时代，虽然我们现在觉得理所当然，但在当时那是全新的，比如内燃机、生产流水线，各种事物在那个时间点汇聚在一起。同时，那个时代也开始了碎片化 (Fragmentation)，显著改变了世界的运作方式。在那之前，世界经历了一段开放贸易和“第一次全球化”的时期。

我们现在也看到了一点当下的影子：一方面是技术突破，我会将其与 AI (人工智能) 的发展和普及联系起来；另一方面是地缘政治挑战，挑战着我们熟悉了几十年的国际秩序。这就是那个类比。我认为我们需要从历史中汲取经验，努力避免 20 世纪 20 年代之后的结局——首先是金融危机、欧洲银行的破产，最终，由于当时处理不当，演变成了摧毁许多发达经济体的全球冲突，让当时的所有人都变得更贫穷、更稀少。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: I think the analogy I made was with the 20s because it's a time when there were major technology breakthrough. That of course we take for granted but which were new at the time. You know, the combustion engine, the manufacturing line, all sorts of things that just came about together at a time when fragmentation started to also significantly change the way the world worked because it was preceded by a period of open trade and the first globalization if you will. And we are seeing a bit of that at the moment technological breakthrough which I would associate with the development of AI and the diffusion of AI on the one hand and fragmentation challenges to the international world order as we have known him for decades. So that's the analogy and I think that we have to be informed by history and try to avoid what came after those developments in the 20s that ended up with number one financial crisis, bankruptcies of banks in Europe and eventually because the matter was not handled very well at the time a global conflict that destroyed many of the advanced economies and made all of us at the time poorer and fewer.

碎片化对欧洲的冲击

尼古拉·坦根: 那你认为这对金融市场有什么启示？因为我们知道 20 年代末发生了什么。

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: What do you think are the implications for financial markets then? Because we know what happened at the end of the 20s.

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 我认为这对各类政策制定者都有启示。这意味着要把重点放在外交而非战争上，放在公共预算的良好财务管理上，并关注真实的变革而非过度的炒作。这就是我所期待的有利发展。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: I think there are implications for policy makers of all stripes. So it means putting the onus on diplomacy rather than war, the onus on good financial management of public budgets and being attentive to the real transformation and not so much hype. That's what I would see as favorable developments.

尼古拉·坦根: 我们现在看到的这种全球碎片化，对欧洲有什么影响？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: The fragmentation we are seeing in the world just now, how does it impact Europe?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 在所有发达经济体中，欧洲可能是最开放的。这对你熟知的一些北欧国家非常适用，对整个欧洲也是如此。因此，任何对贸易的冲击对开放经济体的影响，都会超过那些并非自给自足但对进出口依赖度不高的经济体。除此之外，欧洲自身的资源和能源非常有限，除了我们正在推动的可再生能源和我们作为一个大陆试图资助的绿色转型。除此之外，能源来源很稀缺。这使我们更加脆弱。一方面是开放性，另一方面是稀缺的化石能源，两者结合使得我们在当前局势下处于明显的劣势。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Europe is of all advanced economy is probably the most open. This is very true for some of the Nordic countries that you know well, but it's true for the whole of Europe. So any shock to trade for instance is going to impact an open economy more than an economy that you know is not self-sufficient but not particularly vulnerable to trade whether export or import. Added to that, Europe has very limited own resources and own energy sources with the exception of the renewable energies that we are pushing and the green transition that we are also trying to finance as a continent. But other than that, sources of energy are rare and that exposes us even more. So it's openness on the one hand, rare fossile energy sources on the other hand and the two combined exposes us significantly in the current situation.

中东冲突与绿色转型

尼古拉·坦根: 我们看到的中东局势增加了哪些复杂性？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: What's the added complexity from what we are seeing in the Middle East?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 除了那些折磨人们生活、家庭和最终成为战争目标的平民的惨剧之外，它通过能源来源（石油、天然气）受到威胁来影响全球经济。运输和航运首当其冲，我想到的就是霍尔木兹海峡 (Strait of Hormuz)。结果是价格大幅上涨，我们看到了过去几十年从未见过的波动——油价可能在一天内上涨 30% 紧接着下跌 30%。这就是当前中东战争给世界其他地区带来的：航运中断、保险成本飙升，以及能源价格的显著上涨。而我们知道能源是所有经济体的必需品，几个月内就会产生连锁反应。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Well, I would say the constraint, apart from the horrible drama that afflicts the life of people, the situation of families, the civilian population that eventually end up being the target of those wars. So putting that aside which is a big put aside, it impacts the global economy in that energy sources, oil, gas are under threat in terms of transportation, shipping, the straight of Hormuz comes to mind right away and as a result of that we see prices increasing significantly and we see a volatility that is unprecedented in the last few decades where suddenly the price of oil can go up by 30% and go down by 30% in a matter of one day. So that's what the Middle East current war development is creating for the rest of us. Disruption in shipping, disruption in insurance cost and obviously significant increases in the price of energy which as we know is necessary for all economies and has ripple effects in a matter of months.

尼古拉·坦根: 欧洲应对这种不确定性的正确方式是什么？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: What's the right way of Europe to dealing with all this uncertainty?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 我认为必须区分短期和长期。长期来看，专注于几年前由欧洲委员会和欧洲理事会确定的绿色转型最符合欧洲利益。非常有意思的是，就在几周前，这还几乎被束之高阁，仿佛不再是优先事项。显然，中东发生的事情让这一优先级重回各国领导人的视野。这种绿色转型将增加可再生能源的比例，减少对化石能源的依赖，是一个必须加速追求的长期目标。短期来看，显然应尽一切努力重新开放霍尔木兹海峡，并促进占全球 20% 到 25% 的石油贸易顺利通过。此外，我认为我们也会看到核能在欧洲的发展速度加快。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: I think you have to distinguish between the short term and the long term. In the long term it is in Europe's best interest to focus on that green transition that has been identified a few years ago by the European Commission and the European Council which comprises all the leaders of Europe. It's actually fascinating to see how only a few weeks ago this was almost put on the back burner as if it was not a priority. Clearly what has happened in the Middle East is bringing this priority back to the front of the mind of leaders and this green transition that would increase the volume of renewable energies versus the dependency on fossil energy is a long-term objective that has to be pursued and at an accelerated pace. In the short term you know obviously every effort should be made in order to reopen the straight of Hormuz and facilitate the passing of this 20-25% oil traffic that goes through that particular straight. I think there is, you know, it's neither nor, but the focus on nuclear energy is also one that we will see developing faster in Europe as well.

价值观、社会民主与央行使命

尼古拉·坦根: 美国政策最近发生了一些变化。我们一起参加了达沃斯的开幕晚宴，你就坐在我旁边的桌子，当时你看起来并不开心。你当时在想什么？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Yeah. Now there's been some changes in policy in the US and we attended the same opening dinner in Davos and you were sitting at a table next to where I was and you didn't seem very happy at the time. So what went through your mind?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 你看出来了。我不擅长掩饰，没有所谓的“扑克脸”。我认为当时不能接受的是，一位美国政府代表在没有辩论和对话的情况下，就在晚宴结束时对着包括许多欧洲领导人和皇室在内的听众，大肆抨击欧洲。我认为所有的观点都是受欢迎的，只要有辩论，但他表达的方式让我无法接受，所以我离开了房间。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: You could see that. I'm very bad at covering up and having a poker face. So what I thought was not acceptable is the fact that a US government representative without contradiction and without any kind of dialogue could in at the end of a dinner which had the participation of many European leaders including royalties and representatives of those countries that he could just bash Europe in the way he did and I thought okay well all opinions are welcome as long as there is a debate but the way in which this came about was to me not acceptable so I left the room.

尼古拉·坦根: 欧洲还能负担得起过去几十年的那种制度吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Do you think we can afford the type of system that we've had in Europe for the last decades going forward?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 我认为，欧洲人和欧洲必须珍惜并重视社会民主 (Social Democracies) 所带来的价值。这是否意味着所有的社会福利都能保持原样？这需要根据我们面临的人口挑战随时间评估和修改。但民主原则、独立性、言论自由、个人权利以及它所促进的创业层面的经济发展，这些都是极其珍贵的原则。如果为了保卫这些代代相传的财富，我们需要减少某些领域的福利，或者在军事开支和社会福利之间做出权衡，我认为我们应该准备好面对现实，共同决定我们想要什么。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: I think it's imperative for Europe and for the Europeans to appreciate value and vote for the value delivered by social democracies. Does that mean that the entire social benefits can be maintained as is with the same financing and the same benefits that is to be assessed, determined and possibly modified over the course of time given the demographic challenge that we are facing. But the principles of democracy, the independence, the freedom of speech, the individual rights, the economic development at entrepreneurial level that it facilitates. I think those are precious precious principles that we have to secure and if it requires reduced benefits in some areas I think we should be prepared to consider that if we have to referee and trade off between military expenses because we want to secure that precious treasure that has been acquired over the course of time and generations and if we have to have less of those social benefits that of which cost increases as the demographic changes take place in Europe, aging societies, less working population. I think we should face it and decide what we want collectively.

尼古拉·坦根: 在这种地缘政治、人口和国防开支的变化下，你作为欧洲央行行长的工作变得更复杂了吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: So to which extent has your job become more complicated or the job as the president of the ECB become more complicated with the change in geopolitics, demographics, defense spending. There are just a lot more things going into the mix than in the past.

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 幸运的是，我们有明确的法定授权 (Mandate)。我将其视为欧洲央行的使命，即物价稳定 (Price Stability)。这是我们对欧洲人民的职责。我们要确保价格保持稳定，让人们能在稳定的环境中决定投资、消费或就业。为了完成这个使命，我们必须考虑所有影响欧洲经济的发展因素。地缘政治冲击是外部因素，人口结构、人工智能的影响以及生产力的提高是内部因素。我们需要考虑越来越多的因素来决定如何履行使命，这确实让我们的工作变得更复杂，需要极强的专注力和分析能力。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Well, we are lucky that we have driven by a mandate. Which I regard as the mission of the ECB which is price stability. That's our duty to the people of Europe. We have to make sure that prices remain stable and that people can decide to invest, to consume, to be employed in a landscape where prices are stable. That's the mission. And to deliver on that mission, we have to take into account all the developments that affect European economies and that has internal elements and external elements. The geopolitical shocks is an external elements that has ramification in our economies. the internal element such as demographics for instance, such as the impact of artificial intelligence, such as the rise in productivity, we have to take more and more factors into account in order to determine how we deliver on the mission. That is true and it makes our life more complicated and it requires a very strong focus and strong analytical skills in order to deliver.

AI、生产力与数字欧元

尼古拉·坦根: 欧洲在生产力数据上表现疲软。我们是怎么走到这一步的？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: So we've had some quite weak numbers in terms of productivity in Europe including recently in Germany. How just how did we get to this point?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 这不是最近的现象。我认为欧洲错过了互联网革命。当你追溯欧洲和美国生产力的演变时，可以明显看到分歧就是从那时开始的。美国继续保持生产力增长，而我们则停滞不前且处于低位，从未赶上。现在我们面临的问题是：人工智能的使用能否帮助我们提高生产力？我们正开始看到一些迹象。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: This is not a recent phenomenon. It goes back to, you know, I would say that we in Europe have missed the internet revolution and when you trace back the evolution of productivity between Europe and the United States it's pretty obvious that this is where it started diverging but the US continued to increase productivity not at a very fast pace but increased whereas we remained stable and low and we never caught up with that. The real question we need to ask ourselves now is is the use of artificial intelligence not in a pioneer way as the US does... but the real question for us is will the diffusion and use of that help us move our productivity up? We're beginning to see some of that.

尼古拉·坦根: 你认为我们在 AI 领域现实地能与美国竞争吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Do you think we realistically can compete with the US in AI?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 在先锋阶段可能不行。无论是芯片制造的精密度、数据积累、能源价格，美国都有显著优势。但先锋是一回事，普及 (Diffusion) 是另一回事。在普及层面，欧洲并不落后，甚至在 AI 渗透到制造过程和服务开发方面还略领先于美国。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Not in the pioneering phase. Whether you look at chips manufacturing and sophistication, whether you look at accumulation of data, whether you look at the price of energy, I think the US has a very significant advantage. But the pioneering is one thing, the diffusion is another one. And when we look at the diffusion, even at memes levels, Europe is not lagging behind. Europe is actually a little bit ahead of the US in terms of penetration of AI in the manufacturing process in the services elaboration.

尼古拉·坦根: 什么时候能看到 AI 对生产力的实质性影响？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: When do you think we could start to see the AI effect on productivity?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 很难说，因为 AI 的普及速度比我们之前见过的任何技术都快。如果拿电力和 AI 相比，AI 的迭代是以周或月计的。三年前 ChatGPT 刚出现，现在我的机构里几乎人人都在用。电力的普及用了 30 年。所以 AI 的影响会比我们想象的快得多。至于 AI 是否会带来通缩，如果它能显著提高生产力，它应该会。但还要考虑能源成本，训练一个顶尖的大语言模型 (LLM) 耗资巨大且消耗海量能源。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Hard to say because this AI creation, diffusion, penetration is so much faster than anything we've seen before. You know when you compare electricity and AI, AI it new iterations and changes come about in 3 weeks, well maybe I'm exaggerating let's say 3 months... electricity the diffusion of electricity 30 years. So when do we see the productivity impact how do we measure it probably a lot faster than what we think. Will AI be deflationary? I wouldn't say at this point in time it will to the extent that It improves significantly productivity. It should. But you know what other factors do we need to take into account? Energy. Well, you know, to teach an LLM at the pioneering edge where they are now cost an absolute fortune, right? And it consumes masses of energy as well.

尼古拉·坦根: 为什么数字欧元 (Digital Euro) 对维持欧元的地位如此重要？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Why is a digital euro so important when it comes to making the euro an important or continue to keep euro as an important currency?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 如果我和我的子孙后代谈话，他们还会随身携带纸币吗？很少了。他们是用数字化支付吗？是的。所以我认为，为了结构化和维护金融系统，央行货币必须有数字化形式。此外，数字货币运行的“轨道”是一种公共产品，我们应该建设它。我们计划在 2027 年进入试点阶段，2029 年全面推出。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: If I talk to my children and my grandchildren, do they carry bank notes? Not much. Do they pay digitally? Yes. So, do I think that central bank money is critical in order to structure, organize, and hold the financial system? Yes. So my conclusion from those two elements is we should have a central bank currency which is available in digital form. Added to which I think that the the rail guards on which digital currencies and digital assets travel is a public good and I think that we should be building it. It will be in pilot phase in 2027 and then complete roll out in 29.

领导力、多样性与个人驱动力

尼古拉·坦根: 作为一个领导者，你是什么样的？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: How are you as a leader?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 这个问题你得问别人。但我相信倾听、学习、工作，并最终通过这些来实现领导。我更倾向于做一个包容型领导者 (Inclusive Leader)，而不是那种“我在山顶，跟我冲”的类型。我不从发号施令或行使权力中获得快感，那既不让我满足也无效率。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Oh, you have to ask the other people not me. But what are your principles? I believe in listening, in learning, in working, and ultimately in leading as a result. But am I an inclusive leader rather than a sort of I'm on the top of the hill, follow my flag. I'm more of an inclusive leader. Don't take personal pleasure out of, you know, I instruct, I order, I exercise power. This is not something that I find neither satisfying nor particularly productive.

尼古拉·坦根: 现在的你和执掌律师事务所时的你有什么不同？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: To which extent are you a different leader now compared to when you led the law firm?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 我想我比以前更没耐心了。随着时间流逝，你会感觉到紧迫感。有时我不得不克制自己对进展过慢的沮丧。

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Um, I think I'm more impatient than I was. Passing off time, you see. And how does that materialize itself? I have to sometimes refrain my frustration and my annoyance with things moving too slowly.

尼古拉·坦根: 《福布斯》将你列为全球第二大权势女性。你会思考这件事吗？

Original English

Nicolai Tangen: Forbes rank you as the second most powerful woman in the world. Do you reflect on that?

克里斯蒂娜·拉加德: 完全不会。第一次发生时，我的小儿子对我说：“才第二？为什么不是第一？”我想，“天哪，这已经不错了。”他说：“现在你知道我们拿回一个 B 的成绩单时，你问‘为什么不是 A’时的感受了吧。”

Original English

Christine Lagarde: Not at all. But the first time it happened, my youngest son said to me, "Only second. Why aren't you first?" And I thought, "Oh, bloody hell. This is not bad." And he said, "Well, now you know how it felt when we brought back a B and you say, why not an A?"

尼古拉·坦根: 你提到 16、17 岁是你人生中非常重要的阶段。你 16 岁时失去了父亲，17 岁去了美国。那段经历对你有什么影响？
