艺术的基石与规则的打破

David Lee Roth：大卫·李·罗斯（David Lee Roth）乐意效劳。 Alex Van Halen：亚历克斯·范海伦（Alex Van Halen）。 DLR：哇，来这里真开心。这也是迈向世界和平的一步。（笑声）这是个重大且充满力量的时刻。罗布（Rob），祝贺你在这里完成的精彩作品。 Robert Vargas：谢谢，很高兴来到这里。想聊聊艺术、创作过程以及我们的方式吗？

英文原文 David Lee Roth: David Lee Roth at your service. Alex Van Halen: Alex Van Halen. DLR: Wow, it's a gas to be here. This is also a step towards world peace. (Laughter) This is momentous, a mighty time. Congratulations on [your] amazing work here, Rob. Robert Vargas: Thank you, it's great to be here. Want to talk about art, creative process and our approach?

DLR：那么，这种方式到底是什么？谁想先说？因为我们的观点截然相反，而且谁又真正明白那究竟意味着什么呢？ AVH：正是这一点让事情奏效。 DLR：你先请。 AVH：我认为在艺术中最重要的事，是你必须做好功课，掌握基础，然后打破所有规则。我们就是这样运作的。

英文原文 DLR: Well, what is the approach? Who wants to go first, because we're diametrically opposed and who even knows what that really means? AVH: That's what makes it work. DLR: Go ahead. AVH: I think that the most important thing in art is that you do your homework and you learn the basics, and then you break all the rules. That's how it works for us.

DLR：什么样才算做好了功课？ AVH：让自己沉浸其中，去倾听、去观察你能接触到的一切、你双眼所能看到的一切，尽可能多地去体验。 DLR：你得去体会心碎的滋味，然后还得用一门外语来体验心碎，接着你得离家出走去加入法国外籍军团。这时候你就写得出歌了。这时候你就画得出画了，对吧？ RV：心碎越深，画幅就越大。

英文原文 DLR: What qualifies as homework? AVH: Immerse yourself into and listen and watch everything you can get your hands on, your eyeballs on, and experience as much as possible. DLR: You've got to go get your heart broken, then you've got to get it broken in a foreign language, and then you have to run away and join the Foreign Legion. Now you've got a song. Now you've got a painting, right? RV: A bigger painting, the bigger the heartbreak.

DLR：等一下，这还挺有禅意的。 AVH：是啊，哇。我能引用你这句话吗？你真该去组个乐队的。 DLR：我想，我们当年的理论和管弦乐配器教授曾经告诉昆西·琼斯（Quincy Jones）和伦纳德·伯恩斯坦（Leonard Bernstein）的话——小时候我墙上贴着他们俩的大海报——那就是：你的音乐，你所有的平面艺术拓展，你的摄影，你的戏剧与舞台艺术，永远不可能超越你作为一个人本身的境界。我把这句话理解为：我想走遍这本《国家地理》杂志里的每一个地方；我想在每个地方至少结识三个人；而且我想吃第26页上他们正在吃的东西。

英文原文 DLR: Wait a minute, that's some Zen. AVH: Yeah, wow. Can I quote you on that? You should have been in a band. DLR: I think what one of our theory and orchestration professors told Quincy Jones and Leonard Bernstein -- a couple of big posters that were on my wall as a kid -- is that your music, your graphic arts all expanded, your photography, your theater and stagecraft will never be anything more than you are as a human being. And I translated that as: I want to go everywhere in this National Geographic magazine. I want to get to know at least three people in each place, and I want to have whatever they're eating on page 26.

人类体验如何滋养创作过程

RV：没错，我是说，正是这些构成了创作过程。所有这些属于人的体验。我刚完成一次世界艺术巡回回来，光是这个月就去了五个国家。 DLR：哪些国家？ RV：我去了巴黎、伦敦、希腊、柏林、东京。在所有这些地方画画，不带任何预设的想法，让亲身经历去引导创作过程，品尝当地的美食，与人交谈。一旦我真正想透了我为什么要做这件事，接下来的就是我该如何去做。

英文原文 RV: That's right, I mean, it informs the process. All of these human experiences. I just came back from a world art tour, five countries just this month. DLR: Which countries? RV: I was in Paris, London, Greece, Berlin, Tokyo. And, you know, painting in all of these places and coming in without having a preconceived idea and letting the experience kind of inform the process, you know, eating the foods, having the conversations. And once I really wrap myself around why I'm doing it, then how I'm going to do it.

DLR：你做这个多久了，罗布？ RV：我的一生，我不记得自己有什么时候是不画画的。 DLR：你是什么时候开始从涂鸦走向复杂得多的创作的？ RV：我的第一幅壁画是在我10岁的时候画的。接着我的下一幅大幅壁画是在15岁完成的，当时吉恩·奥特里（Gene Autry）还亲自参加了我在洛杉矶西部遗产博物馆举行的揭幕仪式。

英文原文 DLR: How long have you been doing this, Rob? RV: My whole life, I don't remember when I wasn't painting. DLR: When did you start moving beyond scrawl into something way more complicated? RV: Well, I mean, my first mural, I was 10. You know, and then the next big mural I did, I was 15, and Gene Autry came to my unveiling at the Western Heritage Museum down in LA.

DLR：我至今还保留着五岁时妈妈送给我的吉恩·奥特里手表。我今天早上还看了它。我有大约五样带来好运的小物件。我有老爸给的一颗棋子，我有妈妈的眼镜，你明白吗？这就像在你最喜欢的泰国餐厅里看到的神龛一样。它通常指代着某个特定的人。 AVH：每个人都需要一些他们认为能带来好运的东西。不知怎么的，它能提升你的振动频率，达到你平时达不到的状态。正是在那些时刻，创意的火花才会超越你体内原有的东西迸发出来。

英文原文 DLR: I still have the wristwatch my mom gave me of Gene Autry when I was five years old. I looked at it this morning. I have like five different little good-luck things. I have a chess piece from Pop, I have Mom’s glasses, you follow? It's like seeing a shrine at your favorite Thai restaurant. It means somebody specific, usually. AVH: Everybody needs something that they perceive to be good luck. Somehow it raises your vibration to where you normally wouldn't be. And it's in those moments that these sparks of creativity go beyond what's already in you.

直觉、锚定与现场的连结

DLR：有些人用念珠来做到这一点。那你用什么呢？ RV：其实，我是通过墙壁、通过表面来让自己沉下心来的。我有一种触摸那个空间的方式，真正让自己在身处的地方安定下来，因为我不用网格，不用投影，也不用任何模板。不管是一幅这么小的画，还是有20层楼那么高，我完全是靠肉眼目测。

英文原文 DLR: Some people have a rosary for that. What do you use? RV: You know, I actually ground myself with the wall, with the surface. I have a way of kind of touching that space and really kind of centering myself where I'm at because I don't use any grids, I don't use any projections, any stencils. I literally will eyeball something, whether it's this big or 20 stories big.

DLR：你是静音画，还是伴着音乐画？ RV：两者兼有。但我喜欢听城市的背景声。我喜欢把人群保持在一臂可及的近距离内，而不是拒人于千里之外。 DLR：我试着把那种感觉体现出来。我们最近演了《巴拿马》（"Panama"），亚历克斯和我一直在争论有调性与无调性音乐。结果就是，有些东西你可以跟着哼唱，或者有些东西则是更多声音的层层叠加。我试着用一种无调性的方式去写歌词，好让你能感受到你刚才描述的那种状态。那是城市的声音，也是你在高速前行时脑海里回响的声音。那里面其实并没有什么具体的故事情节。

英文原文 DLR: And you do that silently or do you do it with music? RV: A little bit of both. But you know, I like to listen to the soundtrack of the city. I like to keep people kind of within arm's reach instead of at arm's length, you know. DLR: I tried to exemplify that. We played “Panama” recently, and Alex and I are always arguing about tonal versus atonal music. Consequently, something you can hum or something that is more sounds layered on. I tried to take an atonal approach to the lyrics so that you would feel like what you're describing. It's the sounds of the city and the sounds of what's going on in your head when you're moving forward at velocity. There's no actual story there.

RV：当我在作画时，无论是在这里还是在其他语言不同的地方，这都是一个把人们凝聚在一起、把社区连接起来的创作过程。所以当他们看到我在做的事情时，即便彼此语言不通，他们也会全情投入想看到成画。也许有人不会说英语，但他们却知道他唱的每一句歌词。 DLR：等等，你是说在创作过程中，有大家在现场见证并成为这个过程的一部分？ RV：亲眼见证，绝对如此。

英文原文 RV: When I'm painting, you know, whether I'm painting here or somewhere else with different languages, it's a creative process that's bringing people together, bringing the community together. So when you see what I'm doing, they’re invested in seeing it through even if we don’t speak the same language. Someone may not speak English, but they know every lyric that he's singing. DLR: Hold on, you're talking about the process and you have folks witnessing and being part of the process? RV: Witnessing, absolutely.

排练与演出的真谛：在错误中磨合

DLR：无论如何，我一直都更喜欢排练，远胜过正式演出。演出时总会出岔子，大象迟到了，灯光掉下来了。（笑） AVH：事实就是这样。 DLR：脚踏板把大象给电击了。 AVH：你要排练的是那些可能出错的事，而不是那些顺理成章的事。如果一切顺利，你到底还有什么好排练的？ RV：没错，全在于过程。我只是让我的创作过程变得可以让人接近。

英文原文 DLR: I've always liked rehearsal way better than the show. No matter what. The show goes wrong, the elephant is late, the light falls. (Laughs) AVH: That's just it. DLR: The foot pedal electrocutes the elephant. AVH: You rehearse the things that go wrong, not the things that go right. If it goes right, what the hell do you need to rehearse it for? RV: That's right, it's all about the process. I just make my process accessible.

DLR：那你是否希望大家能在身体上、直觉上感受到你在画画时的那种感受？他们能产生完全相同的感受吗？还是说你做好了心理准备：你画了一辆别克车，而我却看出了一台冰箱？ RV：我喜欢这样，太喜欢了。我认为这就是一种唤起。 DLR：但这需要花一段时间才能适应。 RV：是的，这里面有一种炼金术。是的，有一种炼金术存在。

英文原文 DLR: And are you hoping that folks feel viscerally, physically, the way you feel when you're painting? That they feel that same feeling? Or are you prepared to accept that you painted a Buick, and I see a refrigerator? RV: I love that, I love that. I think that it’s about evocations. DLR: But that takes a while to get used to. RV: Yeah, there's an alchemy there. Yeah, there's an alchemy there.

AVH：音乐中真正的绝妙技巧是在一言不发中达成这种效果。这就是做音乐时所蕴含的振动力量之美。当年埃德（Ed，指埃迪·范海伦）弹奏时，带给我的就是这种感觉。我们从来不讨论我们要怎么做或要做什么。他就只是弹奏。 DLR：我们从来没向外展示过厨房后堂。这是我和艾尔（Al）第一次在台前讨论后堂的“地狱厨房”，因为前厅餐馆讲究的是风平浪静时就哼哼《兔八哥》。（哼唱）（笑声）可在后台，妈妈在尖叫，爸爸在大吼，那是以前的家庭，如今也是演艺圈的常态。我和亚历克斯在这方面可是出了名的。天哪，好莱坞应该有三家不同的俱乐部前立着星星，纪念我们为了曲目单在里面练巴西柔术。

英文原文 AVH: Now the real trick in music is to do that without saying anything. That's the beauty of the power of the vibrational things that are involved in making music. And when Ed played, that's what it did for me. We never talked about how or what we were going to do. He just played. DLR: We never showed behind the Kitchen. These are the first times Al and I are discussing Hell's Kitchen in the back because the restaurant out front is like, let's hum the "Bugs Bunny" when things are calm. (Humming) (Laughter) And in backstage, Mom screams, Dad yells, that was family and now showbiz as well. And Alex and I are famous for that. Oh my God, there should be a star in front of three different clubs in Hollywood where we practice Brazilian jiu-jitsu over the set list.

核裂变与核聚变：范海伦乐队的协作之道

AVH：但恰恰是这种张力成就了这一切。没有任何单一的创作风格占据压倒性主导地位，我们对这一点都非常清醒。而且我们愿意共享，比如我和埃德写出的素材，戴夫（Dave）随后加入并添砖加瓦，它就变得比原来更加出色。一个很好的比喻——当年别人是这么给我解释的：通过核裂变，你把原子分裂，得到X量的能量。但如果是核聚变，聚变产生的是威力大得多的氢弹。而按照道家学说，宇宙也是以这种方式运转的。如果你遵循这些法则，你必然能创造出非常棒的东西。

英文原文 AVH: But that was exactly what made it happen. There was no one predominant style of creativity that took over, you know, and we were all very cognizant of that. And we're willing to share, like there was stuff that Ed and I did that Dave later on added to and embellished and it became better than it was before. A good example -- the way it was explained to me was: you can have an atom bomb by splitting the atom, [and] you get X amount of energy. But if you take fusion, fusion creates a hydrogen bomb, which is more powerful. And the universe, according to Taoism, behaves in that fashion. And if you stick to those rules, you're guaranteed you're going to have something really good.

DLR：别人叫我“钻石戴夫”（Diamond Dave），是因为当年我参加了校车接送融合计划，去的都是全黑人和讲西班牙语的学校。而他们去的是里奇蒙高中和帕萨迪纳高中，全是一群白面孔。（笑声）我直到今天仍然只听——如果不是这样你们就指出来——完全专一地只听—— AVH：绝对是。 DLR：（模拟声乐）除非是（模拟声乐）“哟！”如果你刻意地去把它们融合在一起，你其实是在生搬硬套。我认为你必须亲自到某个地方去生活，成为它的一部分。我的圈子——我是极度自我的那种类型。我从未结婚，也没有孩子，所以我的社区就是京都熟食店柜台后面的那个姑娘，还有我在纽约常去吃泰国菜那家店里的两位服务员。雷（Ray）、朗（Long），你们好吗？（笑声）

英文原文 DLR: I'm called Diamond Dave because I was part of the busing program and went to all-Black and Spanish-speaking schools. They went to Ridgemont High and Pasadena High School, which were all a bunch of pale faces. (Laughter) I listen still today to only -- tell them if this isn't true -- only exclusively -- AVH: Absolutely. DLR: (Vocalizing) Unless it's (Vocalizing) "Yo!" If you mix it together consciously, you're kind of trying. I think you've got to go live somewhere and become part of it. My community -- I'm ultra-high spectrum. I never got married, never had kids, so my community is the gal behind the deli counter in Kyoto, and the two waiters at the place I go to get Thai food in New York City. Ray, Long, how are you? (Laughter)

RV：京都的那家熟食店在哪儿？ DLR：我们聊天，互致问候，太太身体好吗？他们问我，你的狗怎么样了？我的狗非常出名。我住在日本时把狗带去了，它棒极了。比我受欢迎多了。没错。我会在早上五点带着罗素（Russell）去散步，它是一只长着浣熊尾巴的牧牛犬，是个甜心外交家。我们散步时，比方说，我会遇到几位老妇人，她们显然是经历过二战的。都快八十多快九十岁了。她们并不喜欢我。无论我在哪里走，看起来都很吓人，在日本就更是如此。但一看到那只狗，她们用日语问我的第一句话就是，“那是只狼吗？”我回答，“不，我才是。”没错。她们问了狗的名字，五周之后她们就会主动问，“罗素在哪儿？罗素好吗？”是啊，是啊。

英文原文 RV: Where's the deli in Kyoto? DLR: And we have conversation and we inquire, how's the wife? They ask me, how's your dog? My dog is very well-known. I took my dog when I lived in Japan and he was great. Much more popular than I was. Oh, yeah. I would go walking at five in the morning with Russell, he's a cattle dog with a raccoon tail, a sweetheart diplomat. And we'd go walking and I would see, for example, a couple of older gals who'd clearly been through the war. They’re in their late 80s. They're not liking me. I look scary no matter where I walk, much less in Japan. But that dog, first thing they asked me in Japanese, you know, "Is that a wolf?" And I said, "No, I am." Oh, yeah. And they asked the name and five weeks later they're asking, "Where's Russell? How's Russell?" Oh, yeah, yeah, yeah.

五声音阶与情感和声：音乐跨越界限

AVH：那么问题来了，罗素喜欢日本音乐吗？日本音乐是建立在五声音阶上的。想想看。 DLR：啊。在我的耳朵听来，那感觉就像乡村音乐。当你开始演奏（哼唱）。我不太愿意转到那个音阶上去。但许多日本传统音乐听起来确实像乡村音乐，因为它是在小调上运行的。他是认真的，虽然说话幽默，但句句切中要害。如果你给许多我们认为是六七十年代的日本音乐配上吉他弹拨，两者能完美贴合。我在那里时看了一支全日本人的乡村乐队，我还看到了一个日本迈克尔·杰克逊，他的表演实际上和原版一样棒。呼-嗬！

英文原文 AVH: So does Russell like Japanese music, is the question. Japanese music is structured on a pentatonic scale. Think about that. DLR: Ah. Well, to my ears, it sounds like country. When you start to play (vocalizing). I hate to move to that scale. But a lot of Japanese classic music sounds like country because it moves on that minor key. It’s -- you know, he’s not kidding around. He says it funny, but he means it money. If you strum a guitar to a lot of what we consider 1960s and '70s Japanese music, it fits beautifully. I saw an all-Japanese country band while I was there, and I saw a Japanese Michael Jackson, who was actually as good as the original. Who-ho!

AVH：这就是我们收录在唱片里的《快乐之路》（"Happy Trails"）的来源，在第一张——还是第二张唱片里。因为音乐其实是没有界限的。只是人们如何去感知它，人才划定了界限。只要你把它置于合适的光芒下或正确的语境中，它就能走向任何地方。大概就是这样。这就是我能补充的所有看法。但也正因如此，它才充满乐趣。它既复杂，却又简单，因为归根结底，你要么喜欢它，要么不喜欢它。 DLR：是的，但也许我们是因为不同的原因喜欢不同类型的音乐。

英文原文 AVH: And this is where “Happy Trails” came from, [which] we put on the record, the first -- whatever, the second record. Because music doesn't really know boundaries. It’s only how people perceive it [that] they create boundaries. And if you put it in the right light or the right context, it can go anywhere. And that's about it. That's all I can add to the picture. But that's what makes it fun. And it's complex, and yet it's simple because the bottom line is you either like it or you don't. DLR: Yeah, but maybe we like different kinds of music for different reasons.

AVH：对，当然。但归根结底，我们在讨论的是尝试在某种流派中进行创造。你大可不必对自己的喜恶如此挑剔和长篇大论，因为归根到底，那只会让人越发困惑。越简单，反而越好。 RV：创作是为了你自己。 DLR：但在另一方面—— AVH：看吧，又来了？这就是造就我们乐队的原因。

英文原文 AVH: Yeah, sure. But at the end of the day, we're talking about trying to be creative in a certain genre. You don't have to be so nitpicky and elaborate in what you do and don't like, because at the end of the day, it's going to become more confusing. The simpler it is, the better it is.
